## Overall finding

The Bharathiar University version is recognizably the same poem/narrative but is **substantially condensed and interpretive**. It frequently smooths, compresses or omits Kalaignar's source-level wordplay, repeated images, separators, direct-speech detail and closing imagery. It is therefore useful as a diagnostic witness but is not suitable as a replacement translation layer.

## Detailed comparison

### 1. English title

- repository: **The Younger Brother Who Asked for a Head**;
- Bharathiar: **The Brother for the Head did Ask!**.

Tamil title authority is `தலைகேட்டான் தம்பி`. The repository title more directly preserves `தம்பி` as the younger brother and the verbal relation `தலை கேட்டான்`. Bharathiar's inverted wording is a legitimate poetic title choice but does not justify changing the repository title.

**Decision: KEEP repository title.**

### 2. Opening wordplay and Kongu praise

The Tamil opening deliberately works through forms visually/phonically related to `கரும்பு / சுரும்பு / அரும்பு / எறும்பு` while praising Kongu and Kumanan. The repository translation attempts to retain that cumulative lexical movement. Bharathiar compresses it into polished praise such as the bevy of belles and honeyed blossoms and does not preserve the same word-by-word sequence.

**Decision: repository is the more source-faithful witness; no reopen candidate.**

### 3. Perunchithiranar's poverty

Tamil says the poet himself became greatly emaciated through continual hunger (`மெத்த இளைத்திட்டார் - நித்த நித்தம் பட்டினியால்!`). The repository translates this as the poet growing terribly thin. Bharathiar says **the son** became lean from daily hunger.

**Decision: repository reading is supported by controlling Tamil; Bharathiar appears to shift the subject. No repository change.**

### 4. Kumanan as rain / donor rhetoric

Tamil explicitly says `மாரிக்குப் பெயர்தான் குமணன்` — Kumanan is another name for rain. The repository preserves this rhetorical equation. Bharathiar instead paraphrases the passage as Kumanan giving wealth like showering rain.

**Decision: KEEP repository wording.**

### 5. Ilaveliman episode / `காவல் மரம்`

The repository preserves the source object as a `guard-tree`; Bharathiar renders it as a `sentinel post`. The controlling Tamil is `காவல் மரத்தில்` and therefore supports a tree, not a post.

**Decision: KEEP repository source-specific object.**

### 6. Kumanan–Ilankumanan quarrel: omitted source imagery in Bharathiar

Bharathiar materially condenses this movement. In particular, it omits or flattens source details including:

- `செங் காய்கள் உதிர் தம்பி யுந்தன் வாயுங் கண்டேன்` — Kumanan's image of the younger brother's mouth shedding red/unripe fruit;
- the fuller catalogue of jewels / palace / elephant / army rhetoric;
- the rooted-growth image of enmity.

The repository retains these source images.

**Decision: no repository change.**

### 7. `என் நாட்டைத் தொழுதுவிட்டுக் காடேகு`

Repository: **“bow to my country and go to the forest”**.

Bharathiar: effectively **“Leave my country and go [to] the woods.”**

The Tamil `தொழுதுவிட்டு` literally carries the act of saluting/bowing and can idiomatically mark taking leave. The repository is lexically literal but slightly unnatural in English; Bharathiar captures the pragmatic sense but drops the source gesture.

**Candidate E4 — LOW / editorial only:** consider a future wording such as **“take leave of my country and go to the forest”** or **“bow farewell to my country and go to the forest”**. This should be decided only in a formal English reopen, not from the secondary witness alone.

### 8. Forest episode / sugarcane-sweet Tamil

The repository retains `கன்னல் மொழித் தமிழ்` as **sugarcane-sweet Tamil** and preserves the statement that lack of a poet is Kumanan's sole unending sorrow. Bharathiar reduces this to a simpler “Tamil sweet.”

**Decision: KEEP repository wording.**

### 9. Head-bounty reaction — breadth of Kumanan's heart

Tamil: `அலையடிக்கும் கடல்வெல்லும் பரந்த உள்ளம்`.

Repository: **“His heart is as vast as a sea that beats in waves.”**

Bharathiar: **“A heart larger than the sea of stormy waves is his.”**

`வெல்லும்` carries the force of surpassing/overcoming rather than mere equality.

**Candidate E3 — MEDIUM:** a more exact future rendering would be **“His broad heart surpasses the wave-beaten sea”** or equivalent. Bharathiar usefully corroborates the comparative force, but the change must be source-authorized in a separate English review.

### 10. Sathanar arrival — omitted similes in Bharathiar

The Tamil compares Kumanan seeing the poet to finding a grove in a desert and meeting a breeze in the evening, and includes `ஓலையில் தமிழை உண்டான் மிகவே!`. The repository preserves these images and the palm-leaf reference. Bharathiar compresses the sequence into Kumanan taking delight in Tamil and omits the two similes and the palm-leaf specificity.

**Decision: repository is substantially more source-complete; no change.**

### 11. Strong correction candidate — `அடுத்தார் பேச்சால் அழிந்த தம்பி`

Tamil sequence:

`அடுத்தார் பேச்சால் அழிந்த தம்பி`

followed by

`எங்கே என்றார் - தொடுத்தார் கணைகள்`.

Current repository English says the poet approached **“the younger brother, ruined by his own words”** and then separately has **“‘Where?’ he asked”**.

Bharathiar renders the key relation as **“the brother, ruined by others' words”**. This agrees with the Tamil `அடுத்தார்` — those near him / associates / people around him — and with the immediately preceding plot in which wicked friends fan Ilankumanan's resentment.

**Candidate E1 — STRONG / source-supported:** repository **“ruined by his own words”** is not supported by `அடுத்தார் பேச்சால்`. A future formal English reopen should render the sense as, for example:

> “Where is the younger brother ruined by the words of those around him?” he asked — and loosed his arrows of speech.

Exact English phrasing remains editorial, but the semantic correction **own words → words/counsel of those around him** is strongly source-supported.

### 12. Strong precision candidate — `வாள்முனையில் செங்குருதி தனைக் கண்டான்`

Tamil explicitly says Ilankumanan saw `செங்குருதி` on the sword-point, after which Sathanar later reveals `ரத்தமல்ல; செவ்வண்ணம்` — it is not blood but red colour.

Current repository English says only **“saw red upon the sword-point.”** Bharathiar says **“the blood on the sword's edges.”**

The deliberate narrative deception/reversal is stronger when the first perception preserves the source word **blood**, followed by the later correction that it is merely red colour.

**Candidate E2 — STRONG / source-supported precision:** a future reopen should consider **“saw red blood upon the sword-point”** (or equivalent), preserving the later `not blood—red colour` reversal.

### 13. `குலை யறுக்கும் செய்தி` — unresolved semantic nuance, no automatic change

Repository renders the reaction to head-cutting as **“What clan-destroying news is this!”** Bharathiar gives the much freer **“What a bitter news this is!”** The secondary witness does not settle the exact semantic force of `குலை யறுக்கும்` sufficiently to authorize a repository correction.

**Decision: INVESTIGATE only if the English layer is formally reopened for E1/E2/E3/E4. Do not change from this witness alone.**

### 14. `காவியுடைக் குமணன்`

Repository renders this as **“ochre-robed Kumanan.”** Bharathiar paraphrases the line in a way that does not preserve the clothing image. The secondary witness provides no basis to displace the source-visible lexical reading.

**Decision: KEEP repository wording pending any independent source-semantic concern.**

### 15. Closing reconciliation

The Tamil closes with the poets circling Kumanan like flies around honey sellers, Ilankumanan falling at his feet, Kumanan granting forgiveness, and the younger brother surrendering the **royal parasol** (`குடை`), which Kumanan gives to Tamil.

The repository preserves all of this. Bharathiar omits the honey/poet-circle image and renders the surrendered object broadly as the **kingdom**, rather than the source-specific parasol.

**Decision: repository is more source-faithful. No change.**

### 16. Bharathiar contextual note

Bharathiar appends:

- `Puranaanuuru: Poems 158, 159 & 162`;
- `Poet: Peruncithiranaar`.

This note is useful as a **secondary research lead**. It is not part of the controlling 1966 canonical Tamil presently preserved in the repository and must not be inserted into that source layer without independent source/provenance evidence.

**Decision: preserve as secondary-witness context only.**
