## Entry 21 → repository item 39 — `தானாக வந்தவரல்ல; தளபதியால் கொண்டுவரப்பட்டவர்!`

Bharathiar title: **He didn't Come by Himself; He was Brought by the Chief!**  
Repository title: **Not One Who Came on His Own; One Brought by the Commander!**

### Comparison

Both versions preserve the historical transition from the three crowned Tamil dynasties through the intervening rulers to the Pallavas, Mahendravarman/Narasimhavarman, Pulikesi, the Vatapi campaign, commander Paranjothi and the claim that the Vatapi Ganapati image was brought to Tamilakam as war-spoil rather than arriving on its own.

Repository `Commander` follows source `தளபதி` more directly than Bharathiar `Chief`.

### Major structural condensation

The repository source item closes with an explicit *Periya Puranam* / Sekkizhar quotation describing Paranjothi's northern campaign, destruction of ancient Vatapi and the gems, treasure, elephants and horses brought before his king. Bharathiar entry 21 closes immediately after the prose claim that Paranjothi brought Pillaiyar from Vatapi and omits that final source quotation.

### Decision

- repository title retained;
- source/English correction candidate: **none**;
- witness classification: **title smoothing + final-source-quotation omission**.

## Entry 22 → repository item 40 — `காஞ்சி மண் காட்டிய கனிவும் கருணையும்`

Bharathiar title: **Love and Mercy Displayed by the Land of Kānci**.  
Repository title: **The Tenderness and Compassion Shown by the Soil of Kanchi**.

### Comparison

Both preserve the Hijra/Mahendra dating frame, Pallava geography, Pulikesi's repeated invasions, Narasimhavarman's victories, Kanchi's Buddhist/Jain associations, the friendship with Lankan ruler Manavarman, the first failed expedition, the soldiers' refusal to sail without Narasimhan, the king's ship-board stratagem and Manavarman's eventual restoration.

Bharathiar smooths and compresses some inscriptional and rhetorical detail, but the story structure and conclusion remain recognizably the same.

Source `கனிவு` is closer to tenderness/kindness than to the broader `love`; source `கருணை` supports compassion/mercy. The repository title is therefore at least as source-specific as the secondary witness.

### Decision

- title replacement: **No**;
- no semantic reopen candidate;
- witness classification: **idiomatic title choice + moderate condensation**.

## Entry 23 → repository item 44 — `இரும்புத் தூணும் ஈக்களின் இறகும்!`

Bharathiar title: **An Iron Pillar and the Wings of a Fly**.  
Repository title: **The Iron Pillar and the Wings of Flies!**

### Comparison

Both preserve the succession from Aditya Chola through Parantaka I, Gandaraditya, Arinjaya and Parantaka II; the Vanavan Mahadevi/Rajaraja family frame; Aditya Karikalan's murder; the competing accusations around Madhurantakan/Uttama Chola; Rajaraja allowing his uncle to rule; and the closing iron-pillar metaphor for Rajaraja's steadiness.

Bharathiar is noticeably more compressed in the historiographical debate and in Kalaignar's rhetorical commentary.

The source title `ஈக்களின்` is plural — **of flies**. Bharathiar singular `a Fly` is a secondary-witness simplification. The repository plural is source-controlled.

### Decision

- **KEEP repository plural title**;
- no source-supported English correction candidate;
- witness classification: **number simplification + structural condensation**.

## Entry 24 → repository item 45 — `தந்தை இராசராசனும், சிந்தை கவர்ந்த செல்வனும்!`

Bharathiar title: **Father Irājarāca and his Captivating Son**.  
Repository title: **Father Rajaraja and the Son Who Captivated Hearts!**

The 2009 witness condenses the source's competing historical arguments and the repeated rhetorical questions around motive, succession and atonement. The repository retains those source-visible turns rather than reducing the item to a chronological summary.

Bharathiar `Captivating Son` is idiomatic; repository `Son Who Captivated Hearts` more explicitly preserves source `சிந்தை கவர்ந்த`.

### Decision

- repository title retained;
- no reopen candidate.

## Entry 25 → repository item 46 — `முன்மாதிரியாகத் திகழ்ந்த மும்முடிச் சோழன்!`

Bharathiar title: **Mummuṭicōḻan Shone as a Model King!**  
Repository title: **The Three-Crowned Chola Who Stood as a Model!**

### Comparison

Both preserve Kalaignar's qualified assessment of Rajaraja: military and administrative greatness alongside refusal to call the period an unqualified golden age; the critique of social/cultural degeneration, northern/ritual influence, sati, caste and restrictions upon Jain/Buddhist currents; and the later domestic/statecraft portrait of Rajaraja among Sembiyan Mahadevi, Kundavai, Vandiyathevan and his queens.

Bharathiar retains `Mummuṭicōḻan` as a transliterated royal epithet. The repository translates source `மும்முடிச் சோழன்` as **Three-Crowned Chola**, making the semantic content visible to an English reader.

The secondary witness also compresses several rhetorical images and the source's extended family/court detail.

### Decision

- title difference: **transliteration vs semantic translation**;
- repository retained;
- no English reopen candidate.

## Entry 26 → repository item 47 — `அந்த வருங்காலமே; அருங்காலமாகும்!`

Bharathiar title: **That Future Will Be the Future Dear!**  
Repository title: **That Future Age Will Be a Precious Age!**

### Comparison

Both versions preserve the unknown birth-year/Sadhayam discussion, Rajaraja's long list of titles, major campaigns, the Rajendra connection, naval power, inscriptions, temple/fort reflection, social-administrative criticism and the closing hope for a worthy future.

The repository remains fuller in campaign names, inscriptional passages, administrative/social argument and the final ethical frame. Bharathiar condenses a number of lists and source transitions.

Source `அருங்காலம்` carries the sense of a rare/precious/excellent age. Bharathiar `Future Dear` is awkward and less lexically transparent than repository **Precious Age**.

### Decision

- repository title strongly retained;
- Bharathiar title classified as **awkward literal/idiomatic rendering**;
- no source-supported English correction candidate.

## Entry 27 → repository item 49 — `களம் பல கண்டனர்; கடற்போரிலும் வென்றனர்!`

Bharathiar title: **They Waged Many Wars; They Won Naval Fights too!**  
Repository title: **They Saw Many Battlefields; They Won in Naval War Too!**

### Comparison

Both preserve the Rajendra–Rajadhiraja father/son comparison, co-rule, Western Chalukya campaigns, Lanka/Pandya conflict, the Ganga expedition, northern victories, inscription/copper-plate evidence and overseas/Kadaram naval success.

Bharathiar compresses geographic and inscriptional detail but preserves the major campaign sequence.

Source `களம் பல கண்டனர்` is literally closer to **saw many battlefields** than to the interpretive **waged many wars**. Source `கடற்போர்` supports naval war/battle; the two English choices here are stylistic rather than contradictory.

### Decision

- repository title retained as the more source-literal rendering;
- no reopen candidate.

## Entry 28 → repository item 50 — `குருதிக்களமே; கொலு மண்டபம் ஆனது!`

Bharathiar title: **A Blood-drenched Battlefield Became a Coronation Hall!**  
Repository title: **The Field of Blood Itself Became the Coronation Hall!**

### Comparison

Both preserve the Rajaraja–Rajendra–Rajadhiraja war frame, Chalukya conflict, humiliation of Agavamallan's envoys, Rajadhiraja's death at Koppam, the younger Rajendra's immediate counterattack and the battlefield coronation, followed by the moral shock at the human cost of kingship.

The secondary witness is somewhat smoother and shorter, while the repository keeps more source sound-effects, polemical commentary and battlefield pacing.

Bharathiar `blood-drenched battlefield` is an idiomatic expansion of source `குருதிக்களம்`; repository `field of blood` stays closer to the lexical image. Both capture the governing sense.

### Decision

- title replacement: **No**;
- no semantic correction candidate;
- witness classification: **idiomatic title expansion + moderate condensation**.

## Entry 29 → repository item 51 — `திருமணங்களாலும் வருவதுண்டு திருப்பம்!`

Bharathiar title: **A Turning Point may Come in the Wake of Weddings!**  
Repository title: **Marriages Too Can Bring a Turn!**

### Comparison

Both preserve Rajendra II and the post-Koppam succession, the emergence of Kulothunga Chola, the Chalukya-Chola dynastic explanation through Rajaraja's/Kundavai's marriage connections, Kulothunga's titles and long reign, and the later administrative/religious references.

The Bharathiar title expands the compact source `திருப்பம்` into `turning point` and `திருமணங்களாலும்` into `in the wake of weddings`; the repository preserves the concise source relationship more directly.

Payload differences are chiefly compression and transliteration; no contradictory historical or semantic reading sufficient to reopen the repository English was found.

### Decision

- repository title retained;
- no reopen candidate.

## Entry 30 → repository item 52 — `படையெடுப்பை முன்கூட்டியே அறிவிக்கும் பண்பாடு!`

Bharathiar title: **Announcing the Advent of an Army prior to the Siege**.  
Repository title: **A Culture That Announces an Invasion in Advance!**

### Comparison

Both preserve the opening turn from Chola to Pandya history, the difficulty of reconstructing early Pandya/Sangam chronology, Mudathirumaran and Mudukudumi Peruvazhuthi, the source image of a king announcing an approaching invasion by drum so innocent inhabitants may know, and the Golden-Handed Pandyan story.

Source `பண்பாடு` is explicitly **culture/conduct/civilised practice**; Bharathiar's title turns that cultural claim into the event-description `Announcing the Advent...`. The repository title retains the source's evaluative word `பண்பாடு`.

### Major structural truncation

Bharathiar entry 30 ends after the Golden-Handed Pandyan episode and the observation that puranas were written while histories were buried. The FINAL-CLEARED repository source continues substantially beyond this point with:

- a list of later/Last-Sangam Pandya rulers already treated in Kalaignar's historical portraits;
- the decision to move forward rather than repeat all of that Pandya material;
- the transition to *Tolkappiyam* as an ancient intellectual treasury;
- the presentation before Pandyan Nilandaru Thiruvin, Athankottasan and the learned assembly;
- the closing invitation to continue the Pandya historical series from Madurai.

The secondary witness therefore represents only the earlier portion of repository item 52 and is **not payload-complete** for the controlling Tamil item.

### Decision

- repository title retained;
- repository English is materially more source-complete;
- no source-supported correction candidate;
- witness classification: **major tail truncation + interpretive title narrowing**.
