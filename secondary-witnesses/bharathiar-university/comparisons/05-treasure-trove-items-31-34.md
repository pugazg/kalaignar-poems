## Entry 31 → repository item 53 — `கடற்கோளில் தப்பிய தமிழ்; கடைச் சங்கம் கண்டது!`

Bharathiar title: **The Tamil that Escaped the Tsunami Erected the Last Cankam**  
Repository title: **Tamil Escaped the Sea-Deluge; It Found the Last Sangam!**

### Title / terminology

Source `கடற்கோள்` is preserved in the repository as **sea-deluge**. Bharathiar modernizes it to **tsunami**. That modernization is interpretive; it is not source wording and does not override the repository title.

Source `கடைச் சங்கம் கண்டது` permits more than one English phrasing. Bharathiar's **Erected the Last Cankam** is interpretive and somewhat awkward. Repository **It Found the Last Sangam!** stays closer to `கண்டது`, though `found` can be read ambiguously in English. Keep the repository title; flag only as an editorial-clarity note, not a correction.

### Semantic differences

The BU version converts `எழுபது காதம்` into **seven hundred miles**. The repository retains **seventy kaadams**, which is safer and more source-faithful because the historical unit conversion is not supplied by the Tamil source itself.

Bharathiar also smooths the unusual source phrase around Pandyan Nilandaru Thiruvin cutting the Pahruli river into an agricultural-growth explanation. The repository preserves the source's stranger rhetorical force instead of normalizing it into a modern explanatory paraphrase.

### Structural / payload completeness

The main narrative remains recognizably the same: Kumari/Pahruli geography, sea-deluge, Nilandaru Thiruvin and Tamil Queen, the `Tolkappiyam` presentation, Middle Sangam poets, the later destruction of Kavatapuram, Adiyarkku Nallar's forty-nine-country passage and Mudathirumaran's determination to establish the Last Sangam.

However, Bharathiar entry 31 ends on printed page 152 after the statement that Mudathirumaran lived through the end of the Middle Sangam and the founding of the Third Sangam. It **omits the repository item's final source note** (scan 270) citing `Rajavali`, `Mahavamsa`, the sixty-year discrepancy and the research dating of the second sea-deluge to 150 BCE.

### Decision

- repository Tamil: **unchanged**;
- repository English: **unchanged**;
- title replacement: **No**;
- witness classification: **modernized title terminology + historical-unit conversion + semantic smoothing + final-note omission**;
- reopen candidate: **none established**; retain the `Found` wording with an editorial-clarity note only.

## Entry 32 → repository item 55 — `நெடுஞ்செழியனும் நெடுநல்வாடையும்!`

Bharathiar title: **Neṭuñceḻiyan and Neṭunalvāṭai**  
Repository title: **Nedunchezhiyan and Nedunalvadai!**

### Title / transliteration

The title is the same lexical identity. Differences are scholarly transliteration and the repository's retention of source-visible terminal punctuation.

### Major opening omission

Bharathiar begins with the literary proposition that the masterworks of the ancients witness the Tamil people's division of life into `akam` and `puram`. That corresponds to the repository's scan **278** material.

The repository item begins one scan earlier, at **277**, with a substantial historical prelude: praise of the Pandyas for the three Sangams, Ayyamperumal Konar, the capital question, Thenmadurai, Kavatapuram, the sea-deluges, Mudathirumaran and the establishment of present-day Madurai. **That complete opening prelude is absent from Bharathiar entry 32.**

### Shared core

Both versions preserve the `agam/puram` frame, Nakkirar and `Nedunalvadai`, Nedunchezhiyan's victories, the queen's longing, the cold `vaadai`, the palace/bed imagery, the moon-and-Rohini comparison, the king's midnight circuit of the wounded soldiers and the concluding return to `agam/puram` as Tamil cultural structure.

The repository is generally more source-complete and keeps more of Kalaignar's rhetorical pacing and line architecture.

### Closing omission

The repository item ends with a parenthetical source note identifying `Nedunalvadai`, Sangam poet Nakkirar and Nedunchezhiyan of Talaiyalanganam, and states that the piece is written in simple style from that literary basis. Bharathiar entry 32 ends with the rhetorical question about `Nedunalvadai` and **omits this closing source note**.

### Decision

- repository title retained;
- no source-supported English correction candidate;
- witness classification: **opening historical-prelude omission + closing-source-note omission + scholarly transliteration + moderate smoothing**.

## Entry 33 → repository item 56 — `பற்று கடந்தால், பற்றி எரியும் வெறியே!`

Bharathiar title: **When Love Exceeds its Limits, There will be a Roaring Fire of Frenzy!**  
Repository title: **When Attachment Goes Beyond Its Bounds, It Burns as Frenzy!**

### Title semantics

Source `பற்று` is broader than romantic **love**: attachment, adherence or strong attachment fits the later contrast between attachment to land/religion and destructive frenzy. Repository **Attachment** therefore better preserves the governing lexical and rhetorical structure than Bharathiar **Love**.

The source wordplay `பற்று ... பற்றி எரியும்` is difficult to reproduce in English. Neither translation can fully carry it, but repository `attachment ... burns` keeps the semantic linkage more directly.

### Payload comparison

Both versions preserve:

- the First/Middle Sangam counts;
- Mudathirumaran's survival after the sea-deluge;
- Mudukudumi Peruvazhuthi / Palyagasalai;
- the Vedic/Aryan-civilisation argument;
- Karikizhar, Nettimaiyar and Nedumpalliyathanar;
- the territorial blessing and naval-force passage;
- the critique of wartime devastation;
- the turn from land/religious attachment to frenzy;
- the closing Madivanan dramatic-Tamil section and the contrast with Pallava Mahendravarman's Sanskrit drama.

Bharathiar smooths several names and explanatory transitions, and it removes the source-visible separator before the Madivanan coda, but no semantic divergence establishes a repository defect.

### Decision

- repository title strongly retained;
- no Tamil or English reopen candidate;
- witness classification: **interpretive title narrowing (`பற்று` → love) + structural smoothing + transliteration differences**.

## Entry 34 → repository item 57 — `உவமை அழகுக்கு உரிய பரிசு என்னவாம்!`

Bharathiar title: **What Reward does the Simile Deserve?**  
Repository title: **What Prize Is Fitting for the Beauty of a Simile!**

### Title semantics

Source `உவமை அழகுக்கு` explicitly refers to the **beauty of the simile**. Bharathiar compresses this into **the Simile**. Repository retains the source's `அழகு` and is therefore more explicit.

`பரிசு` supports either reward or prize. No correction issue arises there.

### Place-name modernization

At the opening of the historical narrative Bharathiar says the Pandyan went to capture the castle of **Kalaiyar Koyil**, while the Tamil source names the fortress `கானப்பேர் கோயில்` and only then explains that ancient Kaanapper is the place later known as Kalaiyar Koyil. The repository preserves **Kaanapper Koyil** in the source position and keeps the historical/modern-name relation visible rather than collapsing them.

### Shared core

Both versions preserve the warfare vocabulary (`uzhinjai`, `nochchi`, `thumbai`, `vaagai`), Ukkira Peruvazhuthi, Vengai Marban and the Kaanapper fortress, the king-and-lover dialogue, fortification imagery, the siege and victory, Aiyur Moolankizhar and the final forge-iron/water-drop simile.

The BU rendering occasionally modernizes or smooths technical images. Its closing simile describes a piece of iron **boiling in the blacksmith's cauldron of fire**; the repository more directly follows the Tamil image of iron heated in the smith's furnace/fire. This secondary wording does not support any repository correction.

### Decision

- repository title retained;
- repository Kaanapper naming retained;
- no source-supported English correction candidate;
- witness classification: **title compression + place-name modernization + technical-image smoothing**.
