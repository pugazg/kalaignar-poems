## Entry 1 → repository item 1 — `பொது உலகம்`

Bharathiar title: **The World is Common**.  
Repository title: **The Common World**.

### Comparison

Both versions preserve the large sequence: primordial void; light; direction; unheard cosmic sound; planets/fire; the earth's thirst and rain; the five elements; conflict/intimacy; new forms of life; evolution; and the closing common-ownership/common-rights declaration.

Bharathiar smooths several repetitions and syntactic turns. The repository preserves more of the source's repeated negative/parallel structure (`no one watching / able to watch / there to see`, etc.) and the explicit build-up of living forms.

### Decision

- title: **KEEP repository title**; `பொது உலகம்` is naturally represented as the noun phrase “The Common World”;
- semantic correction candidate: **none**;
- structural issue: **none**.

## Entry 2 → repository item 2 — `படிமுறை வளர்ச்சி`

Bharathiar title: **Evolution**.  
Repository title: **Stagewise Development**.

### Comparison

The 2009 witness preserves the main chronology and examples: world age, Lemuria/Kumari Kandam, Indus civilisation, ape-man, Stone Age, the Lemur naming discussion, Pavanar/Mathivanan/Sankrityayan, tooth-count examples and the very long timescale of evolutionary change.

The repository retains the source's distinction and final explicit equivalence between `படிமுறை வளர்ச்சி` and `பரிணாம வளர்ச்சி`. Bharathiar uses the broader single word “Evolution,” which is readable but erases the source's deliberate terminological contrast/equation.

The repository also preserves source-specific rhetorical oddities rather than silently smoothing them.

### Decision

- **KEEP repository title**;
- no English reopen candidate;
- Bharathiar title classified as **terminological smoothing**.

## Entry 3 → repository item 5 — `தங்க மனம் வேண்டும்; அது தந்திடும் அன்பு வேண்டும்!`

Bharathiar title: **One Should Have a Golden Heart; One Should Have the Love it Gives!**  
Repository title: **We Need a Heart of Gold; We Need the Love It Gives!**

Both preserve the argument from ancient-human reconstruction through Tolkappiyam's classification of senses, early human life, food, kinship/sexual custom, gesture-language, the growth of spoken language, fire/cooking, conflict and the concluding call for a heart of gold that produces love and peace.

The repository more explicitly preserves:

- the source's `iyal / isai / koothu` triad;
- the quoted tongue-control maxim;
- the playful sequence of cries/sounds;
- the final transition that another story will branch/sprout tomorrow.

Bharathiar is predominantly a close interpretive rendering but occasionally compresses images and source repetitions.

### Decision

- title difference: **stylistic only**;
- no source-supported English correction candidate;
- **KEEP released repository English**.

## Entry 4 → repository item 6 — `கத்தி பகைவுடையது; இரத்தம் நாம் தருவது!`

Bharathiar title: **The Sword is the Foe's; The Blood is Ours!**  
Repository title: **The Knife Belongs to the Enemy; The Blood Is What We Give!**

### Comparison

The two versions follow the same ideological/narrative arc: Nature teaching early man, class inequality and intellectual revolution, Bharathidasan, war and division, Nehru/Manikkavasagar, the medicine-bearing woman, landlord injustice, the call to unite, social-justice/cultural revolution, and the dying warrior's closing exchange.

Repository `knife` is tied directly to source `கத்தி`; Bharathiar's `sword` is an idiomatic martial choice, not evidence for changing the source-controlled title.

### Important secondary-witness divergence

Bharathiar renders the Stone-Age line with the sense that **mercy and culture could not be seen**, whereas FINAL-CLEARED Tamil scan 30 reads:

`கருணையும் பண்பாடும் கற்காலத் தொடக்கத்தில் காணமுடியக் காரணத்தால்`

and the repository English follows that positive source wording (`could be found`). The verified Tamil page record independently preserves the same text after Phase-2 visual verification.

The Bharathiar negative is semantically smoother in context, but it **cannot override the verified controlling witness**. It is recorded as a later-witness interpretive/normalizing divergence only.

### Decision

- Tamil/source correction candidate: **0**;
- English correction candidate: **0**;
- retain the divergence as a **future source-history research note**, not a release defect.

## Entry 5 → repository item 11 — `ஆங்காங்கு அடையாள முத்திரைகள்!`

Bharathiar title: **Remnants Found Here and There!**  
Repository title: **Marks of Identity Here and There!**

### Comparison

Bharathiar begins directly with the Naga-country / Peevalai / Nedumudikilli material. The repository preserves an additional source opening that recalls the previously discussed youth who survived the sea with a piece of wood before turning to Naga Nadu.

Both versions then preserve the Peevalai story, astrological prediction, Poompuhar encounter, Ilanthiraiyan/Tondaiman tradition, sea catastrophe, Naga place-name traces, maritime contact and the historical-research conclusion.

Name/transliteration choices differ in several places (for example `Seethi` versus Bharathiar's diacritic-heavy forms), but the repository follows the FINAL-CLEARED Tamil witness rather than importing the secondary witness's scholarly transliteration system.

### Decision

- Bharathiar pattern: **opening condensation + transliteration-system difference**;
- title: repository `அடையாள முத்திரைகள்` → **Marks of Identity** is more source-specific than `Remnants`;
- no reopen candidate.

## Entry 6 → repository item 17 — `உலக வரலாறு எங்கிருந்து வருவது?`

Bharathiar title: **Where to Start Writing World History?**  
Repository title: **From Where Is World History to Come?**

### Comparison

The 2009 witness closely follows the first major movement: darkness, colour, cosmic explosion/sound, chick-basket image, universe, evolution, egg/hen, mud/wall analogy and the question whether humanity arose in Kumari Kandam or Africa.

### Major structural condensation

Bharathiar **ends at that question** on printed page 25. The FINAL-CLEARED repository item continues for two further source scans with substantial material, including:

- Lemuria/Kumari Kandam and the 12,000-year argument;
- the cited Soviet-scholar claim;
- natural catastrophe and uneven civilisational development;
- Devaneya Pavanar;
- Scythian/Sumerian ancestry discussion;
- Vincent Smith / Sundaranar;
- the culminating argument that world history should be written from the south;
- the `காலப் பேழை / கவிதைச் சாவி` framing.

This is therefore the clearest **substantive omission/condensation** in Batch 2.

### Decision

- repository is substantially more source-complete;
- Bharathiar is not a replacement witness for full payload coverage;
- no repository correction candidate.

## Entry 7 → repository item 19 — `வருந்தத்தக்க வரலாற்று நிகழ்ச்சி!`

Bharathiar title: **A Traumatic Historical Event**.  
Repository title: **A Historical Event to Grieve Over!**

### Comparison

Both preserve Senguttuvan's genealogy/name-confusion discussion, Mogur Palaiyan, Arugan's intervention and revenge, the cutting of the palace women's hair to make a rope for the guardian tree, Senguttuvan's condemnation, release of Palaiyan, and the closing lament over Tamil disunity.

The repository keeps more of Kalaignar's repeated exclamatory rhetoric and the distinction between personal revenge and Tamil cultural honour. Bharathiar smooths that rhetoric into more conventional historical narration.

### Decision

- title: repository is closer to source `வருந்தத்தக்க` than the stronger psychological term `Traumatic`;
- no semantic correction candidate;
- no reopen.

## Entry 8 → repository item 20 — `வீழினும் அவன் வெற்றித் திருமகனே!`

Bharathiar title: **Though Fallen, He was a Victorious Hero!**  
Repository title: **Even in Falling, He Is Victory's Favoured Son!**

### Comparison

Both preserve the battle of Venni, Karikalan and Cheralathan, the spear wound, the Chera king's honour and `vadakkiruthal`, and the extraordinary praise accorded to both victor and defeated king.

### Structural condensation

The repository preserves the source's extended Sangam-poem material and explicit source references, including the Kazhathalaiyar, Vennikkuyathiyar and Mamulanar passages and Purananuru/Akananuru identifications. Bharathiar reduces much of this into narrative summary and shorter quotation/reporting.

The source title's `திருமகன்` image is also flattened into `Victorious Hero` by the secondary witness. The repository's `Victory's Favoured Son` retains that metaphor more closely.

### Decision

- **KEEP repository title and fuller quotation structure**;
- no reopen candidate.

## Entry 10 → repository item 22 — `“பிறகேன் வினா? என்பதே என் வினா!”`

Bharathiar title: **My Question: “Why a Question then?”**  
Repository title: **“Why, Then, the Question? — That Is My Question!”**

### Comparison

Both preserve the repeated `I`, question/answer sequence, universe, long evolutionary journey, egg/hen reasoning, movement from worm/monkey to human, planetary imagery, Maya-land-bridge discussion, human/cultural divisions, and the final backward-looking `நோக்கு` wordplay.

### Secondary-witness divergence 1 — `அரியா நோக்கு`

Bharathiar renders the speaker's look as an **ignorant look**. The FINAL-CLEARED Tamil scan 116 reads `அரியா நோக்காக`, not `அறியா நோக்காக`. The repository translates this as a look **hard to know / rare to understand**, preserving the actual source spelling and the `அரிமா / அரியா` wordplay.

**Repository reading retained.** Bharathiar's wording is recorded as a secondary-witness semantic divergence.

### Secondary-witness divergence 2 — `பார்த்தாமன்`

Bharathiar uses a scholarly/interpretive divine-name form around the Bhagavad Gita passage. The FINAL-CLEARED Tamil scan 115 explicitly prints `பார்த்தாமன்`; the repository keeps a direct transliteration (`Parthaman`). No later-witness normalization is imported.

### Decision

- repository title is closer to source syntax;
- repository `அரியா` interpretation is source-supported;
- no reopen candidate.
