## Payload comparison

### 1. Title

BU: **`A Petty Village Full of Folks Illiterate!`**  
Repository: **`A Backwater Full of Ignorant Folk!`**  
Tamil: **`பாமரர் நிறைந்த பட்டிக்காடு!`**

BU makes two interpretive shifts:

- `பட்டிக்காடு` → **petty village**;
- `பாமரர்` → **folks illiterate**.

The repository's **backwater / ignorant folk** better preserves the source's social-cultural insult without forcing `பாமரர்` to mean literal illiteracy. The later source phrase `கல்லாரும் கற்க வேண்டும்` separately carries the unlearned/learning idea.

**Decision:** repository title retained.

### 2. `நல்லோர்` becomes “literates” in BU

Tamil says the **one or two `நல்லோர்`** rise to oppose. BU renders them as **“literates, one or two”**. The repository renders **“the one or two decent people”**, which follows `நல்லோர்` directly.

**Classification:** BU lexical mistranslation / over-association with the title's “illiterate”.  
**Repository action:** none.

### 3. Burning-fire image omitted by BU

Tamil:

`இழுத்துத் தள்ளுவர் எரியும் நெருப்பில்!`

BU reduces this to being pushed down **heartlessly**, losing the explicit **burning fire**. Repository English retains the fire image.

**Classification:** BU omission / smoothing.  
**Repository action:** none.

### 4. `குட்டி நாய்கள்` and `மாயை`

BU turns `குட்டி நாய்கள்` into **mean dogs** rather than little dogs, and repeatedly translates personified `மாயை` as **fraud**, using masculine pronouns. The repository keeps **little dogs** and preserves **Maya** as a named/personified ambiguity, allowing the ordinary sense of illusion/deception to remain audible.

The Tamil does not independently support replacing the repository's source-sensitive `Maya` treatment with BU's flattened “fraud”. Nor does BU's masculine pronoun supply controlling-source authority for a gender rewrite.

**Classification:** BU interpretive flattening.  
**Repository action:** none.
