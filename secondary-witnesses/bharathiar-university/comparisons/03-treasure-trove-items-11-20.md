## Entry 11 → repository item 23 — `பெண்ணியத்தின் திண்மை கூறும் குண்டலகேசி!`

Bharathiar title: **Kuntalakeci Speaks of Female Fortitude**.  
Repository title: **Kundalakesi, Who Speaks the Strength of Feminism!**

### Comparison

Both versions preserve the Five Great Epics frame, the loss of *Valayapathi* and *Kundalakesi*, Pathirai/Kundalakesi seeing the condemned criminal, obtaining his release, marrying him, the lovers' quarrel, his plan to kill her on the hilltop, her counter-deception and the fatal push, followed by her Buddhist life and debate victories.

Bharathiar is substantially more compressed. The repository retains more of Kalaignar's rhetorical repetition, religious-conflict framing, body imagery, direct speech and the final `பெண்ணியத்தின் திண்மை` / patriarchy contrast.

The title difference is interpretive: Bharathiar's **female fortitude** is idiomatic, while repository **strength of feminism** follows the explicit source noun `பெண்ணியம்` more closely in the poem's anti-patriarchal frame.

### Decision

- title replacement: **No**;
- source/English correction candidate: **none**;
- witness classification: **stylistic title interpretation + structural condensation**.

## Entry 13 → repository item 26 — `விழித்தெழுக; இதோ செம்மொழி விடியல்!`

Bharathiar title: **Awake! Here is the Dawn of Chemozhi!**  
Repository title: **Awake; Here Is the Dawn of a Classical Language!**

### Comparison

Both versions preserve the argument that ancient Tamil history must be reconstructed from literary and material witnesses, the catalogue of heroic Tamil women, the long list of women poets and the concluding call to awaken at the dawn of `செம்மொழி`.

Bharathiar retains `Chemozhi` as a transliterated cultural term; the repository translates the source word semantically as **Classical Language**. Neither creates a textual conflict.

### Important secondary-witness departure — `முரசு` → “winnowing fan”

The Bharathiar witness says a Tamil woman drove away a tiger **with a winnowing fan**. The FINAL-CLEARED Tamil controlling record for scan 130 explicitly reads:

`முரசு கொண்டு புலி விரட்டிய தமிழச்சி`

The repository therefore translates **war-drum**, following the verified source word `முரசு`. The Bharathiar wording appears to normalize the line toward the familiar `முறம்` / winnowing-fan motif, but that is **not what this controlling edition prints**.

### Decision

- repository **war-drum** retained;
- Tamil/source correction candidate: **0**;
- released-English correction candidate: **0**;
- Bharathiar `winnowing fan` classified as a **secondary-witness substitution / likely normalization**, not evidence against the verified source.

## Entry 15 → repository item 31 — `மாண்பு நிறை தாயும் மாசற்ற மகனும்!`

Bharathiar title: **An Honourable Mother and an Upright Daughter**.  
Repository source-controlled title: **The Mother Full of Dignity and the Stainless Son!**

### Source-title/body anomaly

This is the highest-value Batch-3 witness difference.

The controlling Tamil title on verified scan 148 is unambiguously:

`மாண்பு நிறை தாயும்`
`மாசற்ற மகனும்!`

Therefore the repository title correctly preserves **son** (`மகன்`). This is not an OCR/transcription uncertainty.

However, the item body is about **Madhavi and Manimekalai**, and the FINAL-CLEARED closing source on scan 151 explicitly calls them:

`மாதவி, மேகலை யெனும்`
`மாண்பு நிறை தாய் மகள் வரலாறு`

— a history of a dignified **mother and daughter**.

Bharathiar's **mother and daughter** title therefore functions as a later **editorial emendation harmonizing the title with the body**, rather than a witness to what the controlling title page actually prints.

### Comparison

Apart from the title emendation, both versions preserve Madhavi, Manimekalai, Udayakumaran, the Kaya Chandikai disguise, the mistaken killing of the prince, Nedumudikilli's response, Manimekalai's imprisonment/release and her humanitarian/Buddhist work. Bharathiar is more condensed.

### Decision

- Tamil title correction: **No**;
- repository English title correction: **No** — it correctly translates the source-controlled title;
- durable classification: **SOURCE TITLE/BODY ANOMALY + LATER EDITORIAL EMENDATION**;
- future research may seek another Tamil edition/witness, but Bharathiar English alone cannot rewrite the controlling Tamil title.

## Entry 16 → repository item 32 — `கோவூரார் கேள்வியுறும் - குனிந்திடும் தலையுறும்`

Bharathiar title: **Kōvūrār's Question and the Head That Hung**.  
Repository title: **Kovoorar Questions — Heads Bow Down**.

### Comparison

Both preserve the role of poets in Tamil political life, Nedunchezhiyan's vow, Kovoor Kizhar's interventions involving Nalankilli/Nedunkilli, Ilanthathan and Malaiyaman's children, and the final argument against the Chola brothers' civil war.

Bharathiar greatly condenses the source quotations and transitions.

The title is a meaningful witness divergence. Source `குனிந்திடும்` carries **bowing/bending down**. Bharathiar's **head that hung** is not a reason to replace the repository's more direct **Heads Bow Down**.

### Decision

- repository title retained;
- Bharathiar title classified as **semantic smoothing / weaker lexical fit**;
- no source-supported English correction candidate.

## Entry 18 → repository item 36 — `இளையவன்; அவன் ஒரு தமிழ் மகன்!`

Bharathiar title: **A Young Lady, She is a Tamil Lady!**  
Repository source-controlled title: **He Is Young; He Is a Son of Tamil!**

### Title-witness finding

The verified controlling Tamil opening on scan 173 is explicit:

`இளையவன்;`
`அவன் ஒரு தமிழ் மகன்!`

The repository English is therefore a direct translation of the source title.

The Bharathiar title is a **later editorial retitling** that follows the eventual central protagonist, Anni Minjili, rather than the printed Tamil title. The body itself begins with a broader evocation of the heroic Tamil age before moving to Anni Minjili's story; the source title need not be rewritten merely because the later narrative centres a young woman.

### Comparison

Both versions preserve the Kosar background, Anni/Minjili naming frame, Anni Minjili, the cow-in-the-bean-field plot, her father's blinding, her vow, Tithiyan's intervention and the destruction of the guilty Kosars. The repository additionally preserves the source note identifying *Akananuru* 262 and its warning about chronological/place discrepancies.

### Decision

- Bharathiar title: **editorial retitling toward protagonist gender**;
- Tamil/source title correction: **0**;
- repository English title correction: **0**;
- **KEEP source-controlled title**.

## Entry 19 → repository item 37 — `அன்பால் அவனை விலைகொள்ள முடியுமோ?`

Bharathiar title: **Can He be Bought by Love?**  
Repository title: **Can He Be Bought with Love?**

### Comparison

Both preserve Narkannai's poetry and love for Perunarkilli, the conflict between Killi and Tittan over sacrifice/yaga and religious practice, Killi's exile, Sattanthaiyar's loyalty, the Amur wrestling challenge, Killi's victory and Narkannai's one-sided longing.

Bharathiar condenses Kalaignar's anti-sacrifice argument, source similes and the extended *Purananuru* wrestling comparison. Repository retains the explicit source quotation/citation and fuller narrative sequence.

`by love` versus `with love` is idiomatic variation only.

### Decision

- no title replacement;
- no semantic correction candidate;

## Entry 20 → repository item 38 — `நன்றியுடையோர் நெஞ்சில் வாழ்வோன்!`

Bharathiar title: **He Who Lives in the Hearts of the Grateful!**  
Repository title: **He Who Lives in the Hearts of the Grateful!**

### Comparison

The titles agree exactly in sense. Both versions preserve Killivalavan/Kulamutram, the *Purananuru* praise tradition, Alathur Kizhar, the Karur siege counsel, Kovoor Kizhar, Idaikkadanar, Vellaikkudi Naganar, Thayankannanar, the generous Pannan episode and the final Aiyur Mudavanar burial-urn image.

Bharathiar substantially condenses the individual quoted passages and explanatory transitions. The repository preserves the source-specific images — mother tiger, sugarcane/honey, Kaveri agriculture, plough-front and battlefront, Pannan as physician of hunger and the enormous cosmic-scale burial urn — with fuller rhetorical structure.

### Decision

- title: **MATCH**;
- no correction candidate;
- repository translation retained.
