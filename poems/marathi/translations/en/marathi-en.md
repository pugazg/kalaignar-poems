# The Valiant Woman

On the new sands of Tamil land, gifted by the Kaveri,
when the Chera, Chola and Pandya kings had set the field,
asking whose flag should fly atop the temple finial,
as even today ...
war was being waged; in those days,
in one such field,
her father had leapt forth to guard the motherland and died.
Hearing that news, like wax falling into flame,
a little parrot of Tamil land melted!

With eyes like fire, her husband,
who had been away in another town, returned!
With eyes that poured like streams,
she gave him the news of the war!

"Is it because your father fell in battle that you grieve?" he asked.
"No, my love!
If one warrior is lost from the army of this jasmine-girdled land,
is it not like a tear in a fine garment?
I thought: now the enemy will enter without hindrance;
the tears held back within me broke their dam," she said.

He,
like a tiger leaving its cave,
like a volcano roaring out smoke,
took up the sword that would hew the enemy down;
he swore his oath, lifted his radiant face;
"Even if the enemy comes round us in hundreds, I will smash them to dust," he said.
"Without winning back the land, I will not return home!"

The maiden,
with a ringing laugh, said, "Go on, my dear!"
"Whether I return or not,
for whatever may come, give me one kiss now —
here, turn this way," he said!
She gave it — and then he sent
his arrow against the enemy!

so all his comrades in the army cried in wonder!

That pearl among women smiled through her eyes,
hearing, one after another,
of the victories gathering to her man on the battlefield!

Warriors came — "Victory belongs to your husband!" they said!
The king's messengers came! They offered their congratulations!
Women from the neighbourhood came — they read aloud
the long victory scroll and rejoiced! That beauty
shed tears of joy — then,
why did that man come?
To tell the news of death! —
"My precious woman!
My dear — do not be shaken!
Hear the news from the field, the final news," he said —

Alas!
The man who, beneath mango-leaf festoons, on the wedding dais,
said, "We have become one in life" —
took a spear in his chest and fell in a good death —
that woman who must live on though the life-breath has gone,
that Tamil lotus cast in type —
cried aloud — "A cluster of flowers,
this family!
Cruel death has plucked it
without sparing the cluster!" She reviled it —
spoke bitterly of it! "What remains for me now!"
she cried, and cried that the fruit
had rotted away!

Showing eyes that could shame the dew,
speaking words sweet as syrup,
singing poems in the bridal chamber —
are all those days now only earth?
From now on, are these eyes never to sleep?
So she wept! Toward the field
where her husband's body lay, she bowed!

Collapsed in grief, she heard in her ears
the war-drum beaten by the enemy in the speed of victory —
and that was enough;
"Ruined is ruined — let our whole clan
be wiped out if it must!"
she rose and said.
"I shall live for the land of measureless fame,
not for the house!"

Queen among the women of Tamil land, who knew
the way of measuring the naazhi with the circular vessel,
she remembered the pure treasure she had raised in the cradle,
who had gone to learn, without fail,
from the teacher beneath the banyan tree!
She went there — "Amma!" he cried and sprang toward her,
that gold of love with his beautiful child's speech.
"Have Appa and Thatha returned home?" he asked —
"They returned — and have gone on to the town of death.
Sugarcane-sweet one — you too, come," she called.
"What did they bring back?" he asked!
"Honour! Honour! Undying honour," she said —
"My son! You too must come and taste it," she said.

He came — the tender shoot of the clan!
The lamp of the household still burning, she told him:
"Because of the enemy's invasion, our family has become,
little brother, a tree that has shed its leaves!
No sorrow — they went to the field; they died!
And yet, as long as this earth remains,
they have taken the name 'those who guarded honour'!
My son! As long as strength remains in your shoulders,
strike the enemy!
Sing a Parani! This is your
sacred motherland! — run at once!" Thus,
she sprang, embraced her tender son,
combed and dressed him, placed in his hand
a sword stained saffron-red with blood,
and blessed him:
"Go, my son.. toward the battle-front!"
Who is there that would not praise and sing
that scene from the sacred Tamil land —
O noble woman! — in this flower-world?

## Translation notes

- **`மறத்தி` → The Valiant Woman:** explicit translation-layer title; the archival Tamil title remains unchanged.
- **`முல்லைசூழ்`:** "jasmine-girdled", retaining the floral image.
- **`கட்டாணிமுத்தாள்`:** "pearl among women", retaining the image rather than flattening it to a character label.
- **`கொற்றவன்`:** "victorious king", retaining the martial praise register.
- **`பனி வெல்லும் விழி`:** "eyes that could shame the dew", preserving the source image.
- **`வட்டிலினால் நாழிபார்க்கும்`:** kept close to the material image; *naazhi* remains visible rather than being generalized away.
- **`மானம்` / `மானங்காத்தார்`:** "honour" / "those who guarded honour", preserving the repeated ethical-public concept.
- **`பரணி`:** retained as *Parani* — **"A genre of classical Tamil martial poetry composed in honor of a hero who has slain a thousand male elephants in battle."**
- **`செருமுனை`:** "battle-front", retaining the final forward martial movement.
- **`திருமகளே`:** "O noble woman!", treated here as an elevated human address rather than an unsupported identification with Lakshmi.
