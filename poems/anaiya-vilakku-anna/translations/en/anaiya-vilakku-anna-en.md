# Anna, the Inextinguishable Lamp

The English poem below is assembled only from the current reviewed verse bodies in Batches 01–04 after the 2026-09-01 Tamil lexical reconciliation. Batch and scan boundaries are retained as hidden comments for provenance. Translator notes, batch-review prose, repeated decorative Tamil page headers, photographs, and post-poem event caption/date are excluded from the verse.

<!-- batch 01 / scans 7-9 -->
<!-- scan 7 -->

For this Anna poetry gathering, held under my chairmanship,
to proceed with distinction, to flourish in splendour —
to the great Professor, guardian of our people's dignity, who has come to offer his greetings;
to younger brother Veeramani,
a child of the age of awakening
that the volcanic Periyar longed to see;
to the Honourable Ministers;
to dear brother R. M. Veerappan;
to the esteemed Justices;
to the great and honourable scholars of Tamil;
to the women gathered here;
to my student brothers;
to educators and those of the world of arts;
to my comrades of labour — I ask you all
to accept the warm greetings and respects I offer.

If the centenary celebrations begin for Anna,
all the dark clouds swept together by the winds
will pour down as a rain of love,
and grow as a harvest of virtue!

It will swell as a stream of knowledge —
and melt like wax amid flame
at the sight of a poor man's tears!

Because there is a shortage of electricity,
they say the “wife” now tells her husband,
“Buy candles and bring them home!” —
Gone are the days when, in thick darkness, she did all the kitchen work,
cooked mutton and chicken, served the meal,
and, until she could fondly delight in her loving husband,
had only the little oil-lamp for company;
we have reached this new age in which, unless the electric light comes on,
even a smile will not bloom —
for people living in such times, electric lamps and candles are all the same!

<!-- scan 8 -->

Even movements that call themselves All-India parties,
that weave the national colour into their names,
their flags and their principles,
often forget all that and conduct their politics only in Tamil Nadu —
we witness that wonder!

Let this humble man offer one example —
the shortage of electric power is not confined to Tamil Nadu;
in Maharashtra and in many other states too,
here, there and everywhere, power cuts occur!

Yet there, there are no road blockades and shutdowns
bending politics around that issue —
only here, for some newspapers, that itself is their work...
cartoons, mocking snippets —
not a trace, not even an atom,
of the spirit to work with the government and help relieve people's hardship;
agitation, war-song —
politics, politics....
everything is politics....

Even after the light has come, he says town and city alike are still in darkness.
It seems we must first conduct
an eyesight-treatment camp for those wealthy gentlemen!
(Loud applause)

Once “Kudankulam” comes, electricity generation will rise — then
there will be no worry for the people — even after the Prime Minister and Sonia
have said this with assurance, refusing to accept that truth,
he gathers the town and beats the drum that Tamil Nadu will plunge into darkness —
when light comes tomorrow, he will join us and say
it came only “because of him” —

Why quarrel with them? Leaving aside those lamps that go out,
Anna — “the lamp that never goes out” —
is calling us;
let us go there!
Come!

Anna!
Our life!
Our very feeling!

<!-- scan 9 -->

Sovereign of our people's dignity!
Embodiment of awakening!
Beautiful grove
where thoughts blossom!
Heartbeat of the dear younger brothers
who are bars of steel!

(The first centenary begins for you — but
was not the count of your life over before sixty?

In the beauty of your Tamil speech,
you surpassed waterfalls!
And that rain of English words —
a ceremonial fan before your prowess!

You piled up candies of writing like a mountain!
We turned into ants and savoured them!

(The first centenary begins for you — but
was not the count of your life over before sixty?

Magnetism in your eyes!
Gentleness in your speech!
Valour in your deeds!
Wisdom in your way!
Affection — your sibling!
Compassion — its reprint!

A heart that could bear anything!
O treasure bestowed upon the Tamils!

(The first centenary begins for you — but
was not the count of your life over before sixty?

Anna!
You said our Kazhagam, our movement of reason, would form the government!
They said an elder brother's word would surely come true;
it came true; the Tamils tasted it like ripe fruit!
Before you came,
the Tamils had a history! But
only after you came
did the Tamil fully understand his history in its proper form!

<!-- batch 02 / scans 10-12 -->
<!-- scan 10 -->

Even the old “varusham” became “year” in Tamil — and today
the first day of Thai itself has become the Tamils’ festival day!

Under the title *By the Riverbank*,
at Annamalai University, after
the speech you delivered,
even those who had reviled you began to follow behind in reverence.
On the distinction between Aryan and Dravidian peoples,
the great scholar Sundaranar
and Nehru, the Light of Asia,
had set down forceful evidence;
you drew it together aptly
and offered your interpretation, O leader of Kanchi!
From that day onward,
you became forever the lamp that would not be extinguished,
driving away the darkness
that surrounded Dravidian culture —

O one who became a sword for Ayya Periyar’s righteous struggle!
Anna, your form was like the venerable Kural —
*Kamba Rasam* and *Romapuri Rani* revealed another season of you — yet
when Aryan illusion appeared, your brow would rise to drive it away!

At one time, for the Tamils, literature existed
as epics and puranas, as stories of divine incarnations;
yet only after you came
did we set it in motion — as heroic epics, as paintings of love —
for this world to know, to examine and understand, to breathe the fragrance of knowledge!

Was it not the courage Ayya gave,
and the clarity you, Anna, gave,
that enabled me to explain Sangam Tamil,
to write a fitting commentary for the golden Kural,
and to raise before the public forum
the Tolkappiyam of undying fame?
I would say it was you who gave me that courage —

To create the famed *Poompuhar* epic
for Kannagi, the jewel of *Silappathikaram* — and to raise her statue on the seashore —
and when that statue was removed under the force of those who invoked Vastu Shastra,
to condemn their act as madness —
Anna, it was you who gave us the strength.

<!-- scan 11 -->

The venerable Tolkappiyan entered upon an inquiry into knowledge,
and measured it out from the one-sensed living being to the six-sensed human;
standing in Ayya’s path, I too
split it open and showed it — yes;

there is the one-sensed life that knows through the body;
the two-sensed life that knows through body and mouth;
the three-sensed life that knows through body, mouth and nose;
the four-sensed life that knows through body, mouth, nose and eyes;
the five-sensed life that knows
through body, mouth, nose, eyes and ears;
and that which can know through the mind as well —
that sixth sense is rational thought.

Whether people possess all six senses,
or lack one or two of the six,
grammar is not responsible —
as I noted in *Tolkappiya Poonga*;
and because this applies to rational thought too,
I would say only a rationalist can truly discern it!

Asking, “Who is the merciless demon?”
you placed Kamban himself before the court
and loosed many arrows of questioning:
when Rama killed Vali from hiding,
did compassion flow there?
Thus you questioned through reason,
bent the argument with grace —
and cut apart the treacherous net cast by your opponents;

Surpanakha, Ravana’s sister with the pearly smile,
said, “I have fallen in desire for you”; yet
Rama — the elder among the divine incarnations of the Trinity —
and his younger brother Lakshmana
cut off her nose and breast;
and they are called compassionate gods.
But Ravana,
because Sita was another man’s wife,
lifted her away together with the ashram without touching her —
is he alone the merciless one?
In your dictionary, if “compassion” means
that the ascetics of Daruka forest, with the sages’ wives,

<!-- scan 12 -->

formed an alliance and made merry —
would you call that compassion?

And whoever refuses to accept that,
would you call them merciless demons?

Indra, lord of the celestials,
who violated Ahalya’s chastity — and because of him
Ahalya had to lie as stone —
would you describe that, great sir,
as an act filled with Indra’s compassion?
By asking so, was it not you, Anna,
who made heads hang down
and left them shaken?

Hermits, great men, incarnations of Ishvara —
were they hearts filled with compassion?
Asking this, you made even the Goddess of Justice swoon and fall —
you were the advocate — a new poet Nakkirar!

When Lord Siva asked Siruthondar’s wife
for the flesh of her child — and
ate it in handfuls —
would there be a name for that:
“compassionate cruelty”?
The Tamil people are a people with a character of their own!
There was a time when the Tamil people themselves did not know it!
Leader! It was because you arose
that the Tamil became truly Tamil!

(The first centenary begins for you — but
was not the count of your life over before sixty?

Anna!
You were Father Periyar’s commander!
The evils produced in so many forms
by strange men —
you called them a potful of poison!

You are the scholar!
You are the teacher of knowledge!
You stood on the path of equality,
saying that divisions of speech
that define one as poor and another as rich
must not be fostered!

You who stood on the path of equality,
declaring it wrong to nurture division
through speech that defines one as poor and another as wealthy!

Giving it the name “Anna Renaissance Scheme,”
through the great labour of people such as
the Local Administration Minister, who tends its work like the apple of his eye,
village prosperity has been multiplied into many thousands, many lakhs,
water wealth and land wealth have been raised,
so that, in the prosperity of the land,
village and town may stand on an equal footing —
Anna, was it not the feeling infused by your name
that became the first and fullest cause of this vision!

For Salem's iron,
for the Sethusamudram Project,
and for Tamil to receive the status of a Classical Language —
what you expressed with your very gaze!

To achieve them through sweat,
multitudes in Tamil Nadu stand ready,
and step by step
we are winning victories.

To see your face shine
like the full moon
when complete victory is won,
we will stake even our lives.......

Whether in office or out of office,
Anna, this younger brother of yours, this Karunanidhi,
will never forget the one who made him what he is.
Even if he should rise like the finial
atop a tower a thousand feet high,
he will never, as long as life remains in him,
forget that the foundation of that tower
is the crores upon crores of cadre-comrades —
his own brethren.

Was it not for this reason
that one day, speaking of him, you said:
“Whether told to lay his head upon the railway track,
or told to accept ministerial responsibility,
my younger brother Karunanidhi
would regard both alike” —

<!-- scan 14 -->

and when he was confined in solitary imprisonment
in Palayamkottai Prison,
you shed tears and said
that prison was a place of pilgrimage for you —

and when you declared with certainty,
“I have written half the history of the movement;
Karunanidhi will continue and write the other half” —
because those words had become permanent in my heart, Anna,
I can bear the “ram” I raised charging into my chest,
and those who forgot gratitude stabbing me in the back —
but how, Anna, can I bear it and remain alive
when Ettappans betray to the enemy
the Kazhagam we raised with tears and blood?

Anna, until the day I come seeking the place where you are,
when I asked,
“Lend me your heart, Anna,”
the heart you gave, saying,
“Here, younger brother,”
keeps uttering forever
that jewelled saying:
“Let us forget; let us forgive!”

But let us show the world
that in this heart there will never be room
for talk of abandoning our pure ideal.

Those who once deceived Tamils,
claiming they would lift broad shoulders and fight
for schemes that would enrich Tamil Nadu,
have today become servants of servants.

Now the conjurer says
we do not need Sethu at all!
It is no wrong to grow angry
when we see those who would block Sethu
and bring down to ground level
the prosperity it would bring to Tamil land.

Are we not a pride of lions —
should we bow our heads before the schemes of little foxes?
Before raging tigers,
should petty men put on a show?
Where is my Tamilan?
Does he stand like an upright pillar?
Or does he lie there,
without a pulse in his limbs?

<!-- scan 15 -->

Ah, poor fellow;
write him a song
and stage it in the Sangam!
Raise him a statue in gold —

Like two chisels laid above the lips,
the radiant Pandyan bears a moustache;
as though proclaiming that mercy had never come,
the Chola's moustache spreads
as far as the edge of his ears!

Like the trunk of a rutting elephant,
growing toward the eye — with a slight curve —
is the long moustache of the Chera!
There are stories of enemy mountains
being ground to dust
when those moustaches of old quivered!
That valour is needed today —
needed greatly!

Gather strength in deeds of goodness;
cast away harsh deeds entirely;
recover the spirit of the Tamils of old!
Recover the spirit of self-respect!

O our Anna,
forever in penance
to see God in the smile of the poor!

At election time you wrote in the manifesto
that rice must be given to the people at a low price!
If I am to say it
in your own words:

“I said we should give the people
a *padi* of rice for one rupee —
but before I knew it,
Maran wrote in *Murasoli*
that it was Anna's promise.
What I then thought a task like overturning a mountain,
today my younger brother Karunanidhi,
to gladden the hearts of the people of the State,
has announced:
one kilo of rice for one rupee;
and he showers countless rains of flowers upon my grave —
and I too
embrace him with joy
and bless him” —

Seeing this —
beating their mouths and their stomachs.....
the hard-hearted, truthless ones sing abuse;
“Turn that into music, my younger brothers,”
advises the Buddha of Kanchi;
at your feet — **Kazhagam saranam gacchami!**

## Concluding remarks

At the opening of Anna's centenary celebrations —
shall we turn even an atom of attention to India's political situation?
Yes — for it was with the atom that all those problems began —
in that issue watched by all the nations,

between the Left parties here
and the rulers of the Indian empire,
I took a middle position;
for I acted with the conviction
that only a secular, humanist government
has the truthfulness needed to protect the people!
Though those who objected and those who spoke hatred
stood at the two ends, on either side,
I took my decisions
with the future of India in mind;
I kept tying knots of relationship between both sides.
At the final stage, without letting myself be shaken, decisions were taken —
and somehow the two sides ended up standing opposed!
I read of it only in the newspapers, and my heart ached —

Humanist feeling, or religious-fanatic strife —
to answer the question of which side to stand on,
“Come, let us march together,” called
our dear sister Sonia's political camp!
And so;

cherishing and upholding religious harmony,

<!-- scan 17 -->

I remained where humanism is honoured —
let us gather the people
so that a secular government may be formed
at the Centre and in the State;
come, come — I call you.

O great people gathered in this hall,
though a large part of Periyar's and Anna's dreams
has seen fulfilment,
let us raise a resounding voice
to make Tamil too one of the official languages of the Union government!
Instead of raising demands merely for the sake of agitation,
let us choose the right path of struggle for our demands!
Let us insist that the India of the future
grow strong,
grow prosperous!
**Autonomy for the states;
federalism at the Centre!**
