# It Will Surely Be Opened, to Show the Way!

<!-- scan 132 -->

As our journey through history
continues without faltering in its step,
let us sit here for a while
and speak of this very matter.

I have been opening and showing
the treasures packed inside the Chest of Time
with the Key of Poetry;

and while we remain immersed
in a joy touched with anxiety,
wondering whether scenes seen in dreams
will return again to the screen of memory,

we also carry an expectation:
not merely that they return to memory,
but that they stand firm
as history happening before us!

That is why,
up to this point,
I have fashioned twenty-six poems;

I have spoken of the time
when human beings appeared,
and of the time before that;

of the ways in which all living beings arose,
and of stage-by-stage development;

and through the thousands upon thousands of years
that followed one after another,
I have set before you
the friendship Tamil Nadu held with many lands,
its civilisational contact,
its bonds of art,

its clasp of culture,
its sea-trade,
its opportunities to meet many battlefields —

the fruit-sweetnesses
and the bitter tablets alike
that the treasury yields.

But do not rush to the conclusion
that I, your humble servant,
can offer all this
because of some extraordinary scholarship
or because I have attained
the eight great *siddhis*!

<!-- scan 133 -->

I gather evidence.
As the basic labour of that effort,
I honour the books
of my friend Mathivanan —
like a jewel of Kannan.

How many quotations he himself supplies!
How many views
of researchers and learned scholars!

Though I have taken many of them
and filled this chest,
from the very beginning of my giving,
it is his thought
that has stood as the companion
that never leaves my side.

To string jewels,
do we not need a thread of gold?
I have joined the views
of other learned people as well
to that very thread.

Thus far,
from the sprouting of life upon our earth,
we have also seen
that here, in this sacred land of India,
there was Dravidian history
before the coming of the Aryans;

that in the north,
in the Deccan,
in the Indus and Ganges plains,
settlement-cities were laid out in ordered form
even before a thousand years BCE;

that forests were cleared
and turned into settled lands,
cities rich in civilisation were built,
and in structural and architectural arts
the Dravidian Tamils possessed
a skill that astonished those who saw it.

The Aryans, enraged at seeing this,
called the Dravidians “Dasyus”
with hostile intent:

“Those Dasyus' palaces
shine in beauty
with gold and gems;
their mighty forts

<!-- scan 134 -->

and their walled defences
that touch the sky
captivate the mind.

The potter called Kovan
has two life-companions;
and because they bathe in milk —

sinners!
May they fall into the river Sipa!”

Thus, says the source,
does the Aryans' *Rig Veda* curse them.
When this is examined,
it is finally determined,
without even the smallest room for doubt,
that Indian civilisation
before the Aryan arrival
belonged to speakers of Dravidian languages.

In this manner —
a developed civilisation,
a way of life,
trade relations with the countries of the world,
an awareness of art,
bonds of love,

meeting the battlefield,
guarding honour,
honouring humaneness,
removing religious divisions
that breed strife —

this epic chest is formed
as a marvellous treasury
filled with many kinds of jewelled ornaments.

Whenever I open
this epic chest
with the Key of Poetry,

because what we see inside
are the jewels called
the histories of Dravidians and Tamils,

do not therefore think
that this is a political chest.

Inscribe it in your hearts
as a chest of history
that stands beyond politics —

beginning not with
the dawn of living beings,
but even before that dawn,

with the roaming
of the world itself
and the other planets —

<!-- scan 135 -->

how many peoples,
what kinds of minds;

how many kings,
what kinds of governments —

what ended;
what did not end!

what dawned;
what did not dawn!

what bore fruit;
what did not bear fruit!

what bent;
what did not bend!

Not from the Himalayas
to the Kaveri —

but from the banks of the Kaveri
to the banks of the Ganges,

across that long-stretched land,
we shall tell the chronicles
of those who finished their reigns,

and the histories
of those who finished their lives —

so that those yet to live
and those yet to rule
may be shown the way.

For that purpose,
the Chest of Time
will surely continue
to be opened
with the Key of Poetry!
