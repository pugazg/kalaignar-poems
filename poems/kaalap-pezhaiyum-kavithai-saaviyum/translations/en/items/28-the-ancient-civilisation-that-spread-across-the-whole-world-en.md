# The Ancient Civilisation That Spread Across the Whole World!

<!-- scan 136 -->

The ancient civilisation
that spread across the whole world!

As sandalwood gives out fragrance
the more it is rubbed —
as a diamond throws out light
the more its facets are polished —

only by speaking again and again
of the antiquity of the Dravidian people
will coconut saplings rise like teak trees!

Banana saplings will grow like *vaagai* trees!
The moringa bud will become jasmine;
on the thorn, a rose will bloom!

Can a screw-pine frond
be stroked against its grain?
Yet the Tamil alone
can be trampled down and pushed aside!

The Bengali poet Tagore,
who gave the national anthem,
says:

“it was the prosperity and strength
given by the lion-like Dravidians
that removed the weakness
of Aryan civilisation
and kept its foundation from shaking.”

The Soviet linguist “Sagirap,”
who compares and arranges
the Dravidian languages of North India
with the Dravidian languages of the South,
argues forcefully and establishes
that the Tamils spread northward
from the South.

We know that Tamils conquered northern lands;
do we know that Tamil itself went there?

“On the contrary linguistic data indicate that the
Dravidian Languages spread from South to North
rather than from North to South”

<!-- scan 137 -->

G. Zograf
Soviet Indologist in a
Survey of Languages of
India, Pakistan, Ceylon and Nepal.

Even today in Kashmir,
the language spoken by some hill people
has, according to a Kannada historian,
been identified as one of the branch languages
of the Dravidian language family;
this is no empty imagination!

Among the Kurukkar
who live in Bihar's Rajmahal hills,
the folk songs and ancient stories
widely used by them

reveal that they were Dravidian people
who had lived in the far South;
no one has drawn a curtain over that testimony!

Professor S. K. Chatterji says
that the sari and the veshti —
marks of Indian civilisation —
are gifts of the gracious Dravidian civilisation,

in his book *Indo-Aryan and Hindu* (p. 134).

Mother-goddess worship
in the “Harappa” excavations;
a temple to Siva
on the banks of America's Colorado River;

the resemblance of the Adichanallur “skull”
to those of the Indus region;
and the findings of Harappa and Mohenjo-daro —

we shall call all these firm evidence
that even then Dravidian civilisation
held an “original” right in many lands
and spread far and wide.

The worship of the Siva-linga,
which the Aryans mocked,
was itself part of the culture
of the Dravidians they called “Asura”!

Among the world's ancient civilisations,
Professor “Kondratov” of the Soviet land
finds and declares
that the true division would be:

one share in three
to other peoples,
and two shares in three
to the Dravidians.

<!-- scan 138 -->

Following the history
that Australia and Africa
were in those days joined
to Kumari Kandam,

it is also said
that worship of God
and the introduction of weapons
to encircle and defeat the foe
formed a common civilisation
that had begun among the Dravidians
five thousand years ago.

Historians praise
the Egyptians' distinctiveness
in the system of writing,
then ask how they suddenly
came by such distinction;

and say that it was a gift
bestowed upon them by Tamils
through commercial contact!

In an ancient story
said to have been written
by a Babylonian priest,

one called “Onasu,”
a good man who taught civilisation
to the Mesopotamians,

came, surrounded by a well-trained group,
to the city called “Eridu”
and taught civilisation there —

giving them ploughing implements,
and teaching science and art,
architecture,
and the worship of God.

Because this is what is told,
the answer offered
for how that civilisation spread
not little by little,

but like a ball
that leaps the instant it is struck,
is this:
through the relationship
with the Tamils of Kumari Kandam!

That continent too,
even then,
became prey
to the vast mouth of the deep sea —

<!-- scan 139 -->

but afterwards as well,
the Dravidian cultural people
who had lived there
became the great people
of the whole Indian land!

From that mighty tree
of a great people,

many worthy peoples
such as the Tamil people,
and many languages of distinction,
flourished as branches of renown;

with worthy Tamil poets,
and the patrons praised by those poets —
kings who ruled the earth —

with the wealth of the lands
those kings ruled,
and with strength multiplied
to increase that wealth;

with warriors like striped tigers
who lived a life worthy of the world's praise,
and with the heroic Tamil clans
who came in their line —

how did they toss enemy countries
about like balls?
How did they raise valour
so that songs of victory could be sung?

From where are we to seek
and bring all the evidence for these things?

“The proper age for that
is the Sangam age,”
the learned will say;
I know it!

Even in fixing the dates of that age
there are differences of opinion;
yet, according to the evidence shown
by the Tamil scholar Raghava Iyengar,

if we take the fourth century BCE
as the beginning,

then, just as from the east
we name directions
as west, south and north,

we have begun
with the three crowned kings —
Chera, Chola and Pandya;

let us continue in that way,
without faltering midway!
