# Father Rajaraja and the Son Who Captivated Hearts!

<!-- scan 216 -->

Father Rajaraja,
and the son who captivated hearts!

After the terrifying history
written in blood —
“the murder of Aditya Karikalan” —

while the sorrowful background music
still sounded
from Sundara Chola,
Parantaka II,

broken by love for his child
dying in the “Golden Palace of Kanchi,”

and while the causes
behind those tragic events
still loomed like giant question marks,

Madhurantakan ascended
the seat of rule
in CE 970.

One claim said
that he too had been a cause
of Aditya Karikalan's murder;

another denied
that he could have been.

While these remained
matters of argument
upon the pages of history,

an inscription at Kattumannarkudi,
the source says with tears,
lines up the names
of six said to have carried out
the treacherous deed:

Panchavan,
Brahmadhirajan,
Parameswaran,
Devadasan,
Soman,
and Ravidasan;

and identifies four among them
as Aiyars.

<!-- scan 217 -->

Since many scholars who researched it
offer differing pieces of information,

let us too recover ourselves
from the shock
of Aditya Karikalan's murder

and move forward
to the wonder
of how Arulmozhi Varman,
Rajaraja,
turned the wheel of government.

Let us learn
how he built
a golden-age empire.

If Madhurantakan,
called Uttama Chola,
truly had been responsible
for Aditya Karikalan's murder,

then once he established his rule,
would he not have appointed
his own son,

instead of declaring
Arulmozhi Rajaraja
crown prince?

So asks the Tamil Nadu History Committee.

But if Madhurantakan
had done exactly that —

would not the public then have said:

“He made Arulmozhi Varman prince
only to conceal
the great murder he committed;
that too is a stratagem”?

Can we not therefore also infer
that, as an eye-wiping drama,

he set aside his own son
and made Arulmozhi crown prince?

As though his dream itself
had come true,

Uttama Chola obtained
the office he valued as life itself —

and obtained as its shield
Rajaraja and his kin.

<!-- scan 218 -->

It was historian
Nilakanta Sastri,
the source says,
who began the argument
that Brahmins had treacherously
murdered Aditya Karikalan.

Rejecting that,
Sadasiva Pandarathar
cites evidence
that Madhurantakan was innocent.

The evidence both present
consists of inscriptions
and copper plates.

Let us move those witnesses
a little aside,
stand them outside,

and give as our judgment
the release granted
in a climate of doubt.

Then let us follow Rajaraja —

the golden son,
the lion-like Chola,
who established,
the source says,
a Tamil empire
not merely over the south
but across the Indian land.

We can then investigate
how he stabilised his rule —

and know it
with boundless delight.

While Rajaraja
was serving as crown prince,

Madhurantakan trained him
in every art:

administration,
political strategy,

how to embrace those who came near,
and how to draw into his own fold
those who stood in his way.

Perhaps,
the source suggests,
this too may have been
an act seeking atonement
for a wrong committed.

“Let us forget enmity,
let us cast away hostility,
let us strengthen Chola Mandalam.”

<!-- scan 219 -->

With such words turned into action,
with no enemy able to stand against him,

“Climb, climb!” —
advancing only toward
future victories,

Rajaraja took the crown
on the twenty-fifth day
of June, CE 985.

Before coming to the throne,
Rajaraja had already received
sixteen years of training
in administration
as crown prince.

If we calculate his age
from the year
in which he was crowned,

he began direct rule
at roughly forty years of age.

After that,
he ruled for another thirty years.

His son Rajendra too,
the source says,
ascended the throne
only at the age of fifty,
in the year 1012.

While father Rajaraja
was still alive,
Rajendra Chola —
the son who captivated
the minds of the Tamils —
became king.

History raises its praise to the sky
and bows before the claim

that the glory
of the sceptre in the father's hand
had spread northward
as far as the Krishna River;

that to the west
the waves of the Arabian Sea,

and to the south
the island of Lanka,

bowed before
the rule of these two!
