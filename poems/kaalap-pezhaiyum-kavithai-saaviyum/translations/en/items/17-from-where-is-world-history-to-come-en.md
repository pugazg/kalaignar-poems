# From Where Is World History to Come?

<!-- scan 88 -->

Crores — crores — crores —
how many crores of years ago,
perhaps on one year, one month,
one day, one *naazhigai*, one second —

we knew nothing of what stood before us,
nor who stood before us;
darkness — darkness — darkness —

we know the colour of that darkness now:
black!
But then we did not know it —
because we had seen no colour at all!

Black, red, green, yellow,
blue, white —
for us to give each colour a separate name,
no such colour had yet come
and stood alone before us.

What we saw then,
we now understand,
was only the colour black.
Had we then named it ‘white’,
then even now
the colour of darkness
would have been white — white indeed!

A feeling —
as though someone had brushed past,
as though someone stood nearby!

<!-- scan 89 -->

Who was it?
Adam and Eve?
Or was it we ourselves?

Before an answer could be found,
something exploded!
What was it that scattered?
The heart trembled!

Such a colossal sound —
as though a thousand enormities
had been stacked one upon another,
and an enormity larger than all of them
had fallen —

surely we might have heard it
before our ears went deaf?
But we neither heard nor saw it.

Only the mark left by that sound,
like a line scratched across the surface,
became known —
as I said at the beginning —
crores — crores — crores
of years later!

The noise rising
from a basket packed with a crore chicks
can now be heard —
that same sound is heard from beside us,
and clearly from above us and below us too!

Those baskets floated in the sky then,
and they float there now!
The basket we inhabit does too!

There — another basket;
why does it circle us?
No — no —
we are the ones circling it;
we keep circling!

<!-- scan 90 -->

Ah! We ourselves
are spinning around ourselves;

are all those things
that appear far away from us
and keep circling
also baskets holding crores of chicks,
like ours?

Are all the baskets circling?
How are we to know?
We lie within darkness,
not even knowing the colour of darkness,
in a blind condition —

we revolve around ourselves;
it is a movement unknown to us!
No one obtained our consent for it!

Things that exploded and split apart like us —
do they too revolve,
under the name ‘universe’?
Do they circle something else,
or someone else?

Are we alone the universe?
Or is everything together the universe?

Whatever it is, let it be!
What is our task now?
What command has been laid upon us?

Task or command —
even if we do not come forward to fulfil it,
it is not left lying idle,
without even beginning to be carried out:

it moves! it moves!
it keeps on moving.

For however many crores of years,
for that many crores of years,
that ‘movement’ has continued —

and in between,
through some tremors or other,
who knows what, and what all,

<!-- scan 91 -->

changes and changes again.
Only afterwards
are we able to understand them.

Even if we knew beforehand,
we have not been given
the power to prevent them —

and when there is no one there
to do the giving,
how can it be given?
Who would give it?

I and you included — that is, we —
were, and remain,
chicks within one basket
that exploded and scattered
crores upon crores of years ago;
like our own basket!

Here — I shall give you an image.
On a seashore where a cool soft breeze blows,
children blow bubbles of air
and send them floating;

like balloons,
they fly until they burst!
One of them is this basket of sky
upon which we have climbed —
or have been made to climb.

Wind tosses that basket about;
sea-water strikes and rocks it;
and facing every fierce natural assault —
sea-catastrophe,
storm, cyclone,
violent gale —

we hang on,
reaching out and catching
the fragments and scraps
left standing.

And in that hanging,
what temporarily holds us up,
and what strikes and shatters us,

<!-- scan 92 -->

is nature itself,
as far as we have come to know it.

In its creation,
then and now,
we took form —
and so did our baskets;
yes,
baskets of a crore chicks,

baskets accompanied
by the chorus cry
‘kuk! kuk! kuk!’

Was the first basket Kumari Kandam?
The land of Africa?
Something else?

What was the age discovered there —
Stone Age? Golden Age?

When man — that is, you and I —
had not yet appeared,
when we ourselves had not appeared,
what meaning could Stone Age or Golden Age have?

How did we appear at all?
The chick became a hen!
The hen laid an egg!
From the egg came the chick!
Which came first — egg or hen?

Only the explanation called evolution
can unfold an answer to this!
Are not the seeds of plants,
in one sense, eggs too?

First, something called an egg takes shape —
and not merely the hen:
even the human being forms within an egg.

An egg does not mean only a hen's egg,
nor only the eggs laid by birds.
In every drop of semen
there are lakhs upon lakhs of ‘eggs’ —
and from among them,
using only those that are required,

<!-- scan 93 -->

any embryo takes form;
according to the place in which it forms,
on the basis of evolutionary development,
it takes shape as bird,
animal,
crawling creature,
or human being!

If we say man came from the monkey,
we must understand it
as stage-by-stage development,
as coming through evolution;

can we then ask,
‘Why have the monkeys we see today
not turned into human beings?’

As I have said once before:
we mix mud to raise a wall.
After the wall has been raised
from the mixed mud,
the mud left over
can only remain mud;

must that leftover mud itself
somehow become a wall?

All right —
if an egg becomes a hen,
how is the egg itself formed?

The answer is:
as mud is formed,
so too is the egg formed.

Just as clay — or wall-earth — and water
mix together to form mud,
so too certain mixtures are needed
for an egg to form.

From egg to egg,
the nutrients required,
the substances able to form an egg,
lend their help —
and from that egg comes the hen.

In the same way,
through evolution,
from the monkey grew the human being!

Did he first appear in Kumari Kandam?

<!-- scan 94 -->

Or in Africa?
This is a question needed
not only for human history;
it is needed for world history too!

‘The human who arose in Lemuria,
the great continent embracing India,
South-East Asia, Africa and Australia,
was the first civilised human,’

so, in 1974,
the Soviet scholar Kondra Rao is said to have written
in *The Riddles of Three Oceans*;

but because ‘Lemuria’ had broken apart
and today's continents had already taken
approximately their present form
one crore years ago,

it has been announced as the conclusion of research
that, about 12,000 years ago,
during the Ice Age when sea level was low,
the southern land that existed
south of Kanyakumari before the sea took it —
Kumari Kandam —
is more suitably regarded
as the birthplace of humankind,
or the birthplace of human language and civilisation.

Therefore,
against nature itself —
which shows its mighty power
to alter the birthplaces of rivers,
the paths in which they flow,
the sea,
and the city,

through the hand of earthquake,
the speed of storm,
and sea-catastrophe —
against that nature
human beings have stood and lived
for all these ages.

The growth of human civilisation
could not have taken shape evenly,
at the same moment,
in every place in the world.
In connection with this,
Mozhi Nayiru Devaneya Pavanar writes:

‘When skill in crafts
and the ability to speak a refined language
grew among the people of Kumari Nadu,
agricultural abundance,

<!-- scan 95 -->

commercial abundance,
and the arts of city-building
rose correspondingly to excellence’;

and also:

‘At this frontier of time,
when language was spoken in refined form
and civilisation was developing,
the ancestors of the Scythians
and the ancestors of the Sumerians
may have separated and departed
from Kumari Nadu.’

Thus Kumari Kandam is shown
not merely as the place
where human beings first appeared,
but also as the cradle
of human civilisation.

It was on this basis
that scholars such as Vincent Smith
and Professor Sundaranar said
that Indian history
should be written not from the north,
but from the south — from Tamil Nadu.

Now, turning that argument around and extending it,
we must say that the idea grows firm
that not only Indian history,
but world history itself —
the history of humankind
‘from the appearance of man to this day’ —
should be written from the south.

In accordance with that,
I have been opening and showing
the ‘Casket of Time’
with the ‘Key of Poetry’.
