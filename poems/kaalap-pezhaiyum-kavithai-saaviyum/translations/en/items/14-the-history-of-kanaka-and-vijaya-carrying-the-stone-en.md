# The History of Kanaka and Vijaya Carrying the Stone!

<!-- scan 68 -->

Even if the history of ancient Tamil Nadu
was once silk cloth,
what has come down to us for reading and enactment
is only a patched remnant of that cloth —
there is no use hiding this today.

If we speak even of what remains,
our graceful form will be seen;
that, in this world,
we are among the most ancient;
our full form will be understood
in literature, grammar,
and in the wealth of language
sweetening as *iyal, isai, natakam*.

No one has drawn and kept,
firmly and clearly,
all the historical notes of the South.
That is why,
with literature as companion,
and with the help of inscriptions
seen here and there,
we continue to gather and present as history
the truths filtered from them.

History points, approximately,
to the Pandya king called Nediyon —
also called Nilamtharu Thiruvil Pandiyan —
as one connected with the age of Tolkappiyar,
as one who lived some three thousand years ago.

<!-- scan 69 -->

History praises the Chola Karikalan
as a king whose valour never declined;
yet researchers say
that the Karikalan who marched north to the Himalaya
was the second Karikalan.

The peerless warrior Senguttuvan,
son of Imayavaramban Neduncheralathan,
went north twice.
When an astrologer foretold
that his younger brother Ilango would rule,
that brother made the prophecy false:
he became an ascetic on the path of virtue
and gave us the epic of the noble Tamil Kannagi.

The elder brother went to bring stone
for a statue of that queen of chastity;
he broke the crowns of Kanaka and Vijaya
and made them carry the stone back.
See that tale now —
as a one-act play!

### Scene 1

**The palace of Kanaka and Vijaya on the bank of the Ganga.**

**Herald:** O unsurpassed heroes! Compassionate to the poor! Divine men! Oceans of Vedanta! Ornaments of the royal race! Kings of kings of the northern land! Incarnations of God — Kanaka and Vijaya! *Parak! Parak!*

**Kings:** Namaskar!

**Kanaka:** Namaskar!

**Vijaya:** Namaskar!

**Kanaka:** It seems they are all waiting
for the beauty of the swayamvara — he! he!...

**Kings:** Yes! But now that both of you have come,
if one of you wins the swayamvara contest,
how will the two of you divide the woman between yourselves?

<!-- scan 70 -->

**Tamil poet:** We shall see that afterwards....

**Vijaya:** Who are you, fellow? What are you saying?

**Poet:** Tamil! Classical Tamil!
Tamil born with the world!
Ancient Tamil that arose first,
in the age before stone appeared
and before earth appeared!

**Vijaya:** What business have you
in a swayamvara hall?

**Poet:** Hmm — a strange question, O king!
So in a swayamvara hall
there is business only for old men like you,
and none for young men like me....

**Vijaya:** Enough mischief. Tell us who you are!

**Poet:** I am a Tamil poet —
I have come travelling through the northern land....

**Kanaka:** A poet? Do you know how to compose poetry?

**Poet:** A poet who cannot compose poetry —
a warrior who cannot lift a sword —
Tamil Nadu has never yet seen such men.....

<!-- scan 71 -->

**Kanaka:** We shall test that too.
Come then — say a poem at once!

**Poet:** On the bank of the Ganga
the Tamil fought;
on the bank of the Kaveri he gained fame.
If you come in our time to wage war,
we shall break your legs — beware.....

**Kings:** Ha! Ha! Ha!

**Vijaya:** What Kanaka said is worth
hundreds of thousands for every letter, poet!
When your kings engraved
tiger, bow and fish
upon the forehead of the Himalaya,
it seems our kings were never here at all!

**Poet:** Why? Were they to fall at your feet
and offer tribute?
You men of deranged minds, listen!
Today itself I return to the South.
I shall tell the Chera
what Kanaka and Vijaya,
with tongues they cannot guard,
said together with the banquet kings,
not knowing the might of precious Tamil.
You drunkards of arrogance!
Tamil valour has not died!
Aryan kings — we shall meet again.
The one who rules is Cheran Senguttuvan!
Cheran Senguttuvan!!

<!-- scan 72 -->

### Scene 2

**The Chera royal assembly hall.**

**Chera:** Tiger-like warriors,
who bear the sword
and bear fame through Tamil Nadu!
The summons has come for you all!
A fine chance has come
to establish our valour once more
in the northern land!

Some small-minded men, it seems,
have mocked the emblems
engraved upon the Himalaya.
A fool who has never seen a battlefront
mocked the tiger.
A worthless man who puts a price on courage
insulted the bow.
A boor who has never heard tales
of entering the field
mocked the fish.

Uthiran — Visithiran —
Chithiran — Sivethan —
Bhairavan! Do not stare!
These are merely human names!
Kanaka and Vijaya, they say,
are chiefs of these frog-brained kings —
guardians living on the bank of the Ganga
who have thrown a challenge
to the bank of the Kaveri!
We shall teach them revolution —
set out!

**A minister:** O king,
why does an anger not found
in the Chola or the Pandya
burn only in you?

<!-- scan 73 -->

**Chera:** Because I am Tamil —
because my sense of self-respect has not diminished!
The Chola or the Pandya
may be my enemy;
but for that reason
I will not allow northern Aryan kings
to wag their tails,
so long as I have life!

Did they not insult my Tamil?
Those arrogant men!
Ah... O king who gave us the Kural — Valluvar!
They insulted you....

O Sattanar,
who speaks substantial thought
in the royal hall!
O lord of the arena!
They insulted you!

O sea of pearls!
O jasmine grove!
They insulted you!
O lion-land that lives
with drums sounding through all eight directions,
they insulted you!

Mother Narchonai!
They insulted the victory
of Senguttuvan,
son of Imayavaramban Neduncheralathan.

Ilango! Beloved younger brother!
They insulted you!

O Pandya who gave life for justice!
O Nedunchezhiyan who crossed the Aryan army!
They insulted you!

Lion-like bulls
whose classical Tamil wealth never diminishes!
Scholars! Poets! Artists!
They insulted all of you!
They insulted you —

<!-- scan 74 -->

and those who insulted you
are still alive,
as kings in the northern land!

O beautiful capital Vanchi,
surrounded by the Porunai river
and fertile abundance,
they insulted you! They insulted you!
Men of low nature insulted you!

With minister Azhumbil Vel here,
with warrior Villavan Kothai as commander,
and strength waxing like a crescent moon
upon all your broad shoulders —

some creatures who should have remained
by the riverbank muttering mantras
have shouted in fury, it seems!
Warriors — let us show our hand
to those wanderers
who do not know how to guard the tongue!

Let every warrior
in the forts of Thumbi, Vaadi,
Thoranangarpattu and Vettamangalam
set out!

Young bulls!
Tell your beloveds
this may be the final kiss,
and leave!
Are we a society afraid to die?
Are we a people who fear battle,
to surrender to those fly-whisk bearers?
No! No!
Our heads may be cut away in the field —
but they will be cut
with eyes that spit fire!

<!-- scan 75 -->

Our flesh may be torn apart —
but it will be torn
before the heat of self-respect blood has cooled!
Beat the war-drum!
Strike the Himalaya!
The Tamil has not been defeated!
Has not been defeated!
Set out!”

### Scene 3

**Kuyilaluvam.**

*(In the battle between Cheran Senguttuvan and Kanaka–Vijaya, Senguttuvan wins.)*

**Chera:** Villava! What is the name
of this place where victory resounds?

**Villavan:** Kuyilaluvam.

**Chera:** Kuyilaluvam — mark it down!
Every one of these rocks
is a manuscript upon which
pure Tamil valour has written!
Victory alone is not enough, Villava;
we must not let those vain men go.

**Villavan:** They say Kanaka and Vijaya
have fled somewhere,
wearing saffron robes like rishis....

**Chera:** Nothing new....
That is the one habit
that truly belongs to them!

*(Kanaka and Vijaya are brought in dressed as holy men.)*

<!-- scan 76 -->

**Chera:** Who are these men?

**Villavan:** Kanaka and Vijaya.

**Chera:** Kanaka and Vijaya... Kanaka and Vijaya....
Heroes among heroes!
Splendid indeed is your art of war!
Showing your backs —
pulling a veil over yourselves —
attacking from concealment —
turning yourselves into holy men —
now you stand dressed as sages!
Answer me this:
in the Puranas,
how long did the Deva–Asura war last?

**Kanaka:** Eighteen years!

**Chera:** The Rama–Ravana war?.....

**Kanaka:** Eighteen months!

**Chera:** The Bharata war?

**Kanaka:** Eighteen days!

**Chera:** Warriors, remember this:
the war Cheran Senguttuvan waged
against Kanaka and Vijaya
ended in only eighteen *naazhigai*.

**Kanaka:** O king, forgive us....
Forgive us!

**Chera:** Forgive you? I should forgive you?
Come to the soil of Tamil Nadu
and kneel there.
Let that soil forgive you.....

<!-- scan 77 -->

**Villavan:** O king,
should we not return to Tamil Nadu?
It has been thirty-four months
since we set out.....

**Chera:** We shall leave, Villava.
But before that,
do we not have to take stone from here
to raise Kannagi's statue?

**Villavan:** Yes....

**Chera:** We have found
the proper bulls to carry that stone.
Kanaka — Vijaya!
You cast a word against Tamil valour;
now lift the stone!

Tamil scholars,
qualified researchers,
poets of self-respect,
and those eager to know true history
must wander everywhere,
search everywhere,
bring back the hidden lineage of gems,
and set those gems into the crown.
