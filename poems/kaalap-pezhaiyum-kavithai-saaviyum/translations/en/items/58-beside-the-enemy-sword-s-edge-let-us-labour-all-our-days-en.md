# Beside the Enemy Sword's Edge; Let Us Labour All Our Days!

<!-- scan 296 -->

Beside the enemy sword's edge;
let us labour all our days!

Kulothunga I,
who ruled with radiant fame
and unmatched distinction,

won in history
the stamp of such a king.

He maintained friendship
with China,

and, ruling the land,
gained distinction
by sending in CE 1077

a mission of seventy-two people
to the Chinese empire,
the source says.

Karikalan,
Senguttuvan,
Nedunchezhiyan —

the Three Crowned Kings
who had been great rulers
and great warriors —

in intelligence,
skill and courage,
Kulothunga conducted
an energetic rule
the source sets fully beside theirs.

More than that,
he displayed administrative ability
one step beyond,

shown, it says,
by his interest
in public-welfare measures
such as the land-survey system
and the order abolishing customs duty.

Kulothunga,
a hero among heroes,
made the Chola empire prosper.

After him,
however,
a weak government took shape.

Rajaraja II,
Rajadhiraja II,
Rajendra III —

and, in all,
seven Chola kings
ruled after him.

<!-- scan 297 -->

Among those seven,
Rajaraja III himself
became the weak one in rule,

and that history concludes
that he became a cause
of the fall of the Chola empire!

Already during
the reign of Kulothunga II,

the Pandyan king
Maravarman Srivallabhan,
who came to the throne in 1132,

encircled the source's
“Tiruvaranga Rayam”

and sowed the seed
for the Pandyan empire.

The end of that Pandyan empire too,
the source says,
arrived within
the fourteenth century.

Since it has already been noted
that Rajaraja III
was the weak one
among the Cholas,

the great warrior
Manavarman Sundara Pandyan,

as early as 1219,
leapt toward Thanjavur
with an army.

Terrified by the Pandyan attack,
Rajaraja III,

the source says,
left Thanjavur and Uraiyur
burned and levelled to the ground,

and ran into hiding,
thinking escape alone
was enough!

The Pandyan
held a victory celebration
in the coronation hall
at Thanjavur!

Then came the news
that King Sundara Pandyan
was resting
in the fine city
now called Ponnamaravathi.

On hearing it,
the Chola did not rage forth
to oppose him in battle.

Instead,
with both hands folded,
he went to beg
that the responsibility
of ruling the earth
be given back to him.

He ran like a storm
all the way to Ponnamaravathi.

“What news?
Why have you come?”
asked the Pandyan.

The Chola
who had ruled the earth
bent low,
bowed,
fell in supplication
and said —

<!-- scan 298 -->

“Pandyan king!
Leader praised by the world!

I have one request;
fulfil my wish.

My wife has come here
with me.

The two of us
fall at your feet
and ask:

show mercy,
thinking of the future
of our children.

It is enough
if you give back
our country
that you captured!

It would be noble
to return
the Chola land
you seized.

Moreover,
there need be
no disgrace in this
for us —

nor need there be
any for you.”

Seeing a king
who came in the line
of Rajaraja —

a Tamil who would not
bow his head —

now bend his head,
stoop and worship,

Sundara Pandyan said:

“Mother Tamil!
Alas —
forgive this Chola king
who has lost self-respect.”

And within his own heart,
Sundara Pandyan
placed a burning mark
upon himself.

It was he
who worried
about self-respect
and Tamil honour!

This condition
continued again and again:

among elder and younger brothers,
among kin,

the Three Crowned Kings
collided with one another,

made room
for other peoples,

and brought retreat
upon the Tamil race.

Thus the lands
of the Three Crowned Kings,
once great empires,

became minor kingdoms,
then smaller and smaller states,

and at last
ant-sized kingdoms —

<!-- scan 299 -->

For me,
who rejoiced
writing and writing
this once-rising history
of Tamil Nadu,

and for you,
who gained feeling
reading and reading it,

it is better now
not to continue it further.

Let us think
about ourselves.

Let unity
and relationship
among us grow stronger.

Let us labour
all our days —

and let us heap up victory
until every enemy sword
has its point blunted!

(First Part Complete)
