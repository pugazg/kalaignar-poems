# The Common World

<!-- scan 10 -->

Space! Space! Space!
With no one watching —

with no one able to watch;
with no one at all there to see,
space lies spread everywhere!
Light came first —

from which direction?
Unknown; utterly unknown.

We gave a name to the direction it came from!
We worship the direction!

We had never seen space,
never looked upon light —

when we ourselves did not yet exist,
what power had we to see?

In the empty vastness, countless sounds!
At countless moments!

With no one hearing;
with no one able to hear —

with no one at all there to listen!

Crores upon crores of thunderclaps
crashed at a single corner;

red planets,
balls of fire,

<!-- scan 11 -->

rushed roaring,
scattering as they flew.

They say there may be nine, ten planets;
there may even be one or two more.

The world became established as one among them;
only afterwards did conflict begin!

None of this was known then;
only after so many crores of years had passed —

the thirst-stricken world asked for water!
Nature, saying “No,” poured out tears;

calling those tears “rain” instead,
the Earth found consolation!

Cooling the fever of its body,
it gathered the rainwater into ocean expanses.

Water, land, air, outer space,
fire — among the five great elements,

there is endless enmity; there is enduring kinship.
Without even a kiss, new life forms keep arriving;

change even there, change within them,
discovering the new and entering into union —

worms, insects, multiplying birds,
hordes of fierce animals —

bands of patient domestic creatures,
all drawn into the splendour of evolutionary growth.

We humans, who slowly, slowly leapt from the monkey —
let us stand upon the public stage and raise our voice of principle:
“Ownership in common — rights in common;
to call the world private property is wrong!”
