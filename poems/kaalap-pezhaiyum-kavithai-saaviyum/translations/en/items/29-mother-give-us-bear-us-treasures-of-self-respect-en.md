# Mother, Give Us — Bear Us Treasures of Self-Respect!

<!-- scan 140 -->

Mother, give us —
bear us treasures of self-respect!

At the very sound
of the place-name “Talaiyalanganam,”
there rises not merely the memory
of “Talaiyalangadu” in the Thanjavur land;

there rises too
the historical memory
of the soil upon which
one of the greatest battles
of the Sangam literary age was fought.

Those who stood
and fought on both fronts
were themselves
people of this sacred Tamil land.

The second Nedunchezhiyan —
a youth not yet of age —
did not tremble at war;
he came to the battlefield!

Two great kings:
the warriors of the Chera and Chola lines;
with them five Velir chiefs
with great armies,
and warriors beyond number.

The peerless Chera warrior Irumporai,
and the Chola Killivalavan,
strong enough to meet the oncoming foe;

and five enraged Velir leaders —
Venman, Porunan, Ezhini,
Erumaiyuran and Thithiyan —
with their armies.

They thought Nedunchezhiyan
would be stunned at the sight;

that, as though it were a child's game,
they could win a great victory
at the battle-front.

What became of those stout men
who came with such thoughts?
They accepted defeat with dignity —

<!-- scan 141 -->

“We must have done good merit;
we have been reborn,
and we are leaving the battle-front
still alive!” they said,

and reached their own lands
and their own homes.
On the field the Pandyas
spoke to one another with their eyes:

“Even if that fellow called Yama
were to perform penance for long,
he could not defeat
our Nedunchezhiyan!”

★ ★ ★

On the battlefield,
or on the sea called life,

those who possess the resolve
to board the boat called valour
and travel with courage as their oar —

like Nedunchezhiyan of Talaiyalanganam —
need not perform penance for a boon;

as long as Tamil lives,
they too can live!

Stroking his tender fingers,
kissing his broad shoulders,
Tamil poets blessed him.

The Tamil land itself
raised its head and blessed him!

Nakkirar and Mangudi Maruthanar,
lions among the foremost poets,
created *Nedunalvadai* and *Maduraikkanchi*,
works that live like epic songs
fragrant with art.

A day will come
to celebrate that sacred day of creation —
so this Tamil land itself
is said to have blessed them
with all its heart;
and it blesses them even now!

The *Puram* poet Kudapulaviyanar,
when praising this battle,
records that

though Tamils stood arrayed
on both sides of the battlefield,
and the forces of the enemy Erumaiyur
were mixed among them,

<!-- scan 142 -->

Pandya Nedunchezhiyan
won a victory praised by the world;

he made the enemy writhe
like worms on a fishing hook,
loaded defeat upon their shoulders
and drove them away!

He raised the fish flag
high into the sky.

He took into his own hand
the flag of Tamil honour.

When he left for the battle-front
he was a tiger cub —

on the battlefield,
the enemy before him
was only a kitten!

And so,
how does the lion among poets,
Idaikkunrur Kizhar,
sing of this with rising fire?

That is the sight
we must see here,
the splendour
we must rejoice in!

“He whose feet cast off the tinkling anklet
and put on the bright warrior's anklet;
whose brow cast off the child's topknot
and was garlanded with fresh neem
and the long-bannered *uzhinjai*;
whose hand cast off the little bracelet,
grasped the bow,
and stood radiant
at the front of the long chariot —
who is he?”

(*Purananuru* 77)

That is to say:

the little child
who played wearing tinkling anklets
untied them,

put on the warrior's anklet,
removed the small topknot
from his head,

wore the neem garland
and with it
the garland of battle,

<!-- scan 143 -->

and in the hand
that had worn the child's protective bracelet,
held arrow and bow
and stood at the front of the chariot —

that child was
Pandya Nedunchezhiyan,
says the lion among poets!

Is that all?

The Pandya,
like the waxing moon,
utters a vow as well;

his own *Puram* poem,
a song of uprising —
come, let us hear it!

“Those who praise the land
laugh and say,
‘He is young!’ ...

Those angry kings
who speak such belittling words —
if I do not strike them
and shatter the hard battle,

and seize them together
with their war drums,

may those who live
under my shelter,
unable to find another shade,
spread tears and cry,
‘Cruel is our king!’

may I become a ruler
whom the people condemn! ...

may the poets cease
to sing within the bounds
of my land! ...

may the disgrace be mine
of having nothing to give
to those who come in need!”

(*Purananuru* 72)

That is to say:

I shall scatter the enemies
and silence their drums!

If I do not,
let this world place me
on the list of tyrants!

Let the low word strike me:
“he gives nothing
to those who ask!”

<!-- scan 144 -->

Let none of these poets
raise me up in song!

As the Tamil Pandya line
once gave us
such a youth of honour,
such a son of the warrior clan,

let us ask Tamil Mother
to let us behold such sons
throughout this Tamil land!

Mother — give us,
bear us such treasures
of self-respect!
