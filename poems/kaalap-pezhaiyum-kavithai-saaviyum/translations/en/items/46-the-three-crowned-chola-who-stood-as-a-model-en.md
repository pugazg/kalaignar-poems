# The Three-Crowned Chola Who Stood as a Model!

<!-- scan 220 -->

The Three-Crowned Chola
who stood as a model!

The age of Rajaraja's rule —
an age that showed the way
and opened eyes

for the growth of joyous Tamil,
for the training of ethnic welfare,
for the rise of ethnic consciousness,

for cultural maturity,
for the celebration of ancient glory,
for the flowering of an egalitarian society,
for the happiness of the farmers.

Yet I hesitate
to inscribe it in history
as a golden age.

And when I make that hesitation clear,
when I say that the reasons for it
are as transparent as crystal,

courage and integrity
stand beside us as allies.

At the same time,
I do not wish to diminish
the distinction of those pioneering kings

who brought northern territories
under their sway,
showed their strength upon the seas,

formed a vast realm
from Kumari toward the Himalayan peaks,
established the pride of empire,

and laboured to make it flourish
for three hundred years.

Yet in their victories,
in their proud titles,
without seeing even a fault
smaller than a millet-seed,

like the farmer who forgot
to winnow while the wind was blowing,

when the chance came
they failed to secure
the life of the Tamil people —

<!-- scan 221 -->

hearts that did not think of it,
crippled into inability,
without striving,

the Tamil race itself
brought its progress to an end.

That murmuring sorrow
makes it impossible for me
to praise without reservation

the glory of the empire
and the greatness of the Three-Crowned Chola
who built it.

That is the truth —
and even while writing it,
my hand trembles!

The age of the Chola emperors
was a Sangam age
only in the field of politics;

it was an age
that saw many rare victories.

But in the field of life
it did not reach
the dignity of Sangam times,
the cultural dignity of the people.

Not only historians;
people of self-respect
who cherish the truth of Tamil
have grieved over it.

Into the faultless art,
literature and culture of the Tamils,

as a beetle enters fruit
and makes it impossible for us to eat,

there entered customs
brought back when the Tamil empire,
spreading far and wide,
went northward.

Tamil culture became
fruit bored through by the beetle!

One cruelty alone is enough as example:

was there ever in the Tamil land
the custom of a woman
climbing the funeral pyre
with her dead husband
and burning alive?.......

From the queen of Bootha Pandyan
to Chola queens who ruled the earth,

<!-- scan 222 -->

that contagious disease seized them;
was it not Raja Ram Mohan Roy
who was finally able to give the medicine?

Not the funeral pyre alone:

distinctions of high and low
were taught among human beings
born together,

with the threat
that this was the command of the Vedas;

as the harvest of victory
for those “born from the forehead,”

caste restrictions
and Chaturvedi Mangalams arose,

and dams were built
so that the clear rivers
called Jainism and Buddhism
could not mingle equally.

When history shows us
how these came and made us suffer,
does not anguish itself torment us?

The period of the Chola emperors
is the one age in southern history
able to compete firmly
with the Sangam age.

But politically alone
was it a Sangam age —
an age of many rare victories.

In the field of life,
the Aryan scent had already begun to blow;
there began the age of slipping.

Not historians alone;
all those of self-respect
who honour the truth of the Tamil people

stand grieving and speechless
at the change
that had grown into history.

Ancient or modern?
Rational or not?
What accords with our culture?

Before such things
could be examined and judged —

<!-- scan 223 -->

Rajaraja surpassed
the line of those who had come to the throne.

Many flashes of light
called “reward”
seemed to change form,

becoming a moon of cavalry,
then the high noon sun,

until he stood
as the circle of radiance
in Chola history.

He was not old in years;
nor for that reason merely a youth —
he was adamantine.

If he stood holding a sword,
his heroic form looked
as though a crescent of the sky
had been dipped in black
and fixed upon his face as a moustache.

There was such majesty in that face
that even a kiss could not approach it;

as though the forehead of an elephant
had been taken
and fashioned into a chest full of strength,

as though a mountain itself
had appeared as a man —

such was that handsome male lion.

If amid the clamour of work
he gained even half a *naazhigai*
or a full *naazhigai* of rest,

he would hurry
to see the royal women,
five and six together.

On the way,
if through a latticed window
he heard a woman's voice call
“Arulmozhi” or “Arun,”

he would forget himself
and turn toward the voice.

Grandmother Sembiyan Mahadevi
would embrace her grandson;

beside her,
Kundavai Pirattiyar,
pouring affection upon her younger brother,

would ask after his welfare
and discuss the country's friendships —

all with humility,
love and overflowing concern
between grandmother, sister and king.

“Has Devar still not come?”
he would ask Kundavai.

“I have come!”
Vandiyathevan would laugh
as he entered —

<!-- scan 224 -->

his elder sister's husband,
one of the greatest officers of the state,

winking at Kundavai
as though asking,
“What is it?”

Kundavai and Devan
would move a little aside
and speak in private.

Even while that conversation continued,
a group of women,
full of delight,
would arrive.

And when I say “a group of women,”
it was the group
of King Rajaraja's wives:

Ulaga Mahadevi,
Chola Mahadevi,
Nakkan Tillai Azhagi,
Panchavan Mahadevi,
Trailokya Mahadevi,

Abhimana Valli,
Ilada Mahadevi,
Prithvi Mahadevi,
Meenavan Mahadevi,
Veera Narayani,
Vanamahadevi.

Among them,
the blessing that came to Vanamahadevi
was to be the mother
of Rajendra Chola.

He too would inquire after the welfare
of all eleven,
beginning with Chola Mahadevi;

then bow at the feet
of grandmother Sembiyan Mahadevi,
receive a spear from her hand......

and go to the royal council
for deliberation.

Every day,
this was Arulmozhi Varman's royal duty.

And within it,
in the time he set aside for family,

all those meetings!
All those loving conversations!

Everything moved
with the speed of swords
flung upon the battlefield,
with the swiftness of spears!

Rush! Alertness!
And only if nature granted it,
a pause some day!

<!-- scan 225 -->

Thus Rajaraja spun like a top —
performed duty like the sun.

His younger daughter Kundavai,
wife of Vimaladitya,
would come.

There would be no talk
of her domestic life;

the two would discuss
only the “burden of the realm.”

Once a spear is taken up,
on the field
it must do the work of a spear.

Once the sceptre is taken up,
must one not attend to the subjects?

Can one then attend
only to one's own family?

No.

This Three-Crowned Chola himself
stood as the model
that shows it cannot be done.
