# Let Us Drink This “Aryan” Tea!

<!-- scan 78 -->

Before the Chera entered the battlefield
on the bank of the Ganga
to take stone for carving Kannagi's statue,
he had gone there once before,
accompanying his mother
with a great army as her support,
so that she might bathe in that river.

On that first journey itself,
he broke the Aryan kings
who surrounded him,
won victory,
and made his mother's face bloom
like the full moon!

“When, on that day,
in the fierce flood of the great river Ganga,
our king bathed his mother,
the Aryan kings,
who rose against the one man that he was,
beheld the cruel form of battle —
black-eyed Death itself opened its eyes!”
(*Silappathikaram*)

When the casket of time is opened
and past history is told thus
through stories and poems,
I seek only to draw aside
the great curtains that until now
have hidden truths skilfully from our sight,
and show them.

Yet some condemn the act
of explaining that the people called “Dravidians”
are not a race absent from this sacred land,
as a continuing “act of hatred”
meant to awaken once again
a forgotten feeling of racial hostility.

<!-- scan 79 -->

They write letters;
they abuse from hiding.
I have a heart that can bear anything;
yet I do not intend merely
to raise a counter-voice against an opposing voice.
I wish to give a gentle voice of explanation.

That is why I write this.
During a morning walk,
or in a fast running race,
if a small stone or thorn
strikes the foot,

though it may not stop us altogether,
do we not pause,
remove the obstruction,
and then go on?

I think of contrary views in just that way.
My journey continues;
here I stop for a little;
then, with vigour,
the journey will continue again.
This is the explanation I offer
those who seek to stop it!

“Aryan, Dravidian —
why put this tea, long gone cold,
back upon the stove and heat it?
What business is there to accomplish now?”

Some pierce me
with that impudent question.
What can I do?
In a poor man's house,
that very tea is “divine nectar”!

But if one is a landlord
of four thousand acres
of wet and dry fields,
then without a care
for race, language, literature,
art, culture or country,

he may spend his time
looking only upon mounds of money,
hills of wealth,
mountains of gold,
and ranges of diamond and vaidurya!

<!-- scan 80 -->

Coffee, tea or any other drink —
in his mansion it will be stock
that never goes stale,
remaining forever hot!

The moment I say Aryan and Dravidian,
some grow angry
and perform an *archanai* upon me,
as though I had invented something that did not exist.
Why does such wicked anger arise?

Excavation digs out buried skulls and bones,
classifies and orders the histories
of peoples who lived,
and bears witness through deep research.

Iyers, Iyengars, Samas and Shastris —
learned historians themselves —
have published views on these matters.
For those who refuse to see though they have eyes,
who do not wish to hear though they have ears,
who possess awareness yet declare,
“We shall not understand,”

whatever place is fitting for them —
is that not the place they will receive!

What answer will all these great intellects give,
I ask,
to the following statements of their own?

“The Tamils were not merely
the aboriginal people of Tamil Nadu;
they may have been among
the earliest people of the world itself.”

I tell you that this was stated
by P. T. Srinivasa Iyengar
in his book *History of the Tamils*.

“Tamil civilisation is far more ancient
than Aryan civilisation,”
says Sesha Iyengar
in *Dravidian India*.

<!-- scan 81 -->

“Let scholars examine
whether the coming of the Aryans to Tamil Nadu
brought good or evil to the Tamils,”
said Parithimar Kalaignar —
Suriyanarayana Sastri —
in his book *History of the Tamil Language*.

When, with explanation of cause and effect,
I speak world history
from the past to the present,
and when true history is not hidden,

opening the casket of time
will not create some new battlefield.

It is enough
if the old memory
that birth itself creates high and low
withers and dies.

Then, without narrowness of mind,
let us drink this “Aryan” tea!
