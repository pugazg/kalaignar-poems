# Is “Seruppaazhi-Erindha” an Honorific Title?

<!-- scan 157 -->

Is “Seruppaazhi-Erindha”
an honorific title?

Scholars marvel with delight
at the distinguished Chola empires
that stood upright
in the history of Tamil Nadu —

at a Chola history
that gained a foremost place
in Tamil history;

that was filled with flavour,
and, to the same degree,
filled with sorrow;

that praised and celebrated
the Tamil language,
and later pushed Tamil aside
and accepted another language;

that guarded art and civilisation;
that offered distinction
in justice and administration
and showed the way;

that accepted with victorious joy
the gifts poured out
by kings of the northern lands;

that crossed the sea
and entered battlefields,
and ate the fruit of victory
on every one of those fields;

that established reigns
lasting many years
and made them flourish in fame.

★ ★ ★

With the special appreciations
of Navalar Somasundara Bharathiyar
and the historian Nilakanta Sastri,

<!-- scan 158 -->

I had the opportunity to read
Dr. Ma. Rasa Manickanar's
history of the Cholas.

He says the age
of the earliest Chola kings
belongs in relation
to the age of Tolkappiyar,
more than a thousand years BCE.

Among the first line,
he places the Cholas
before the third century BCE —
Sibi, Musukundan, Kandan and Sembiyan —

and in the third century BCE,
one called
“Ilancetcenni who was Seruppaazhi-Erindha.”

Accepting the second century BCE
as belonging to Manu Needhi Cholan
and the first Karikalan,

he calls the first-century Chola
the second Karikalan;

then calls the third-century Chola,
a crowned king,
“Ilancetcenni who was Seruppaazhi-Erindha,”

and joins him with Cholas
such as Nalankilli and Nedunkilli,
whose period he places
from CE 1 to CE 150.

Seeing him set it out this way,
only one doubt arose in me!

You ask what doubt that was?
It was nothing very great;

but because the thought
that came to this humble man
should not trouble someone else next,
I shall explain it.

When all the kings
are called with epithets
that overflow with distinction,

<!-- scan 159 -->

what is this?
Why, for Ilancetcenni Chola alone,
has the title
“Seruppaazhi-Erindha”
been deliberately attached
as though it carried
slightly less worth?

It seemed to me
like a lowering,
a “slide” downward —

as though its meaning
somehow belittled Ilancetcenni.

All this caused confusion in my mind.

Later I looked at *Akananuru*
and cleared up my own confusion;
then I came running
to clear up yours
before it could arise.

There is no need
for even the smallest confusion
about “Seruppaazhi-Erindha.”

Do not think of the word
as `seruppu + aazhi` —
“shoe + ocean.”

Instead,
`seru` means “war,” does it not?

Join that with the dictionary meaning
of `paazhi` as “military camp,”

and with the Tamil explanation
that `erindha` means “conquered.”

Put them together:
`seru + paazhi + erindha` —
Ilancetcenni.

The joy I felt
when I understood this —
was it not joy discovered
in our Tamil,
which is dear as life itself!

The history of sacred India —
particularly because it began
from the middle,

and more particularly
because it failed to begin
from Tamil Nadu —

became like the story
of trying to grow a tree
by pouring water
upon the tip of a branch,
without reaching trunk or root!

Or else it became
like an event in which
hot water was poured
and the root itself was boiled!

<!-- scan 160 -->

It was in Ilancetcenni's time
that the Chola army
had to enter the field
against the Mauryas!

The Mauryas dreamed among themselves
that, with Chandragupta's valour
and Chanakya's cleverness
as favourable ground,

Tamil Nadu too
could be brought down.

They even broke through
the mountains that stood
as obstacles before them
and advanced toward Mogur!

Near the Athur pass
of South Arcot district
lay Mogur,
where the powerful Chola army
had its military camp.

The Chola saw that
if the place established as the `paazhi`
were “struck down” and conquered,
the enemy Mauryas
would bow and flee.

When the enemy host
came with its army,
he sprang upon it
like floodwater plunging
into a hollow,

and attacked on many fronts.

The Mauryas yielded;
they hid with their army!

They withdrew
from the Mogur camp;

they fled
with their heels striking
the backs of their heads!

It was on seeing
that victory won by Ilancetcenni
that Tamil poets,
with rising pride,

fashioned the resounding distinction
“Seruppaazhi-Erindha Cenni”
as a glorious honorific,

crowned that lion-like Tamil
with the title,
and rejoiced,
singing a song of delight!
