# This Tamil Land, Two Thousand Years Ago!

<!-- scan 120 -->

If we turn and look back
at the history of roughly
two thousand years ago,

we see, as though ceremonial lamps
had been lit upon the sea,
ships of every country
standing there in rows;

both eyes shine at the sight.
In art, civilisation and culture
we may stand victorious —

and I give you news of Tamil Nadu
that cools the ear with delight:
maritime trade,
overseas relations across the sea,
art and literature,

a rule and splendour
that entered many battlefields
and wore the garland of victory —

as these gather like clusters of fruit,
I too savour them together with you!

Kannagi's chastity,
Madhavi's dignity,
and, through the words
of the great poet Karikkannanar,

the attachment, affection,
friendship and relations between states
that grew between Pandya and Chola kings —
we have come to know them all
and rejoice.

In defensive fortification,
in the skill of regulation,
there was no contradiction
in Kaveripoompattinam.

And when it comes to wealth of art —
Madhavi, of fame beyond imagination!
The very list of jewels
with which she had adorned herself
is enough to make us dizzy;

let us see at least a few of them
in *Silappathikaram*.
Here they are:

<!-- scan 121 -->

Imagine the moon herself,
adorning herself with jewels,
standing before a full-length mirror —

and hide yourself away
and look!

Upon the feet,
fittingly,
*pariyagam*, *silambu*, *padagam*,

*ariyagam* and the ornament called *sathangai*;
upon her beautiful thighs,
tightly set,
*kurangu-seri*.

A pearl-string
with thirty-two *virisigai*;
at her waist,
a gleaming *mekalai*
showing the beauty before it.

On the shoulder,
a bracelet joined with pure gems,
and the *kandigai*;

earrings of ruby;
a *sudagam* set with diamond.

Upon the neatly ordered forearm,
many kinds of bangles —

diamond bangles,
conch bangles,
gold bangles,
nine-gem bangles,

coral bangles —
so many kinds upon her arm;
and upon her face,
a smile that outshone
all that gold!

A curved ring
like the split mouth of a vaalai fish;
a ruby signet-ring
glowing in rich red;

upon fingers lovely as *kaanthal* flowers,
the radiant emerald-finger ornament,
surrounded by fitting diamonds.

Firm and delicate chains;
on the ornamented arm,
flower-worked *savadi* and *sarappani*;

upon the pearl-garlanded neck,
a chain falling elaborately behind;
a clustered *Chandrabani*
diamond ear ornament;

between indra-neelam stones,
a beautiful conch appearing here and there —

magic or marvel,
these ornaments continuing
as *thoyyagam* and *pullagam*,
beautifying Madhavi's beauty,

are they not truly
emblems that reveal
the refinement of Tamil civilisation?

Is it not like the feeling
of breathing in
a gentle flower-scented breeze
within our hearts?

<!-- scan 122 -->

Is that all?

What was the arrangement
of the stage
upon which that beauty, Madhavi,
danced?

A place was chosen
that did not differ in the least
from the principles
laid down by masters of sculpture.

Bamboo born and grown
on Pothigai hill
was cut to the proper measure,
leaving a *saan* of space
between each node;

it was trimmed precisely,
reckoning four finger-breadths
from the thumb,

and that itself
was used as the measuring rod
for the stage.

Seven rods in width,
eight rods in length,

one rod in height,
with a good interval
of four rods,

with the “standing lamp”
and the “figure curtain” —
all of it arranged
without diminishing artistic beauty —

thus was the dancing stage made,
the chapter declares;
yes,

*Silappathikaram*,
which captures the mind,
declares it!

Ah!
For the mere act of mounting the stage,
there was the dance-master,
the music-master,

the master of *iyal* Tamil,
and then
the flute-master,
the yaazh-master —

how many flawless masters,
how many accomplished experts
in the fullness of art!

<!-- scan 123 -->

That Tamil Nadu
was a land where art flourished;
that it held to honesty
without wavering from its station;

that it upheld the lofty, singular principle
that conduct must be guarded
even more than life —

Madhavi stood
not merely as one example of these.

She also stood as proof
that the field of art itself
is no enemy of chastity;

that after her man
left her and went away,
she embraced renunciation
and stood alone;

that she raised her daughter Manimekalai,
like herself,
as a queen among women
with purity of mind.

This literary history
is not merely an example of all this;

it tells us that
two thousand years ago,

when so many countries
lay immersed in darkness,
Tamil Nadu stood established
as a lighthouse,

casting light
upon the shores of all those lands,
spreading art, literature,
civilisation and culture!

It is as though
tears were sprinkled
upon the face of the sleeping Tamil
to wake him!

★     ★     ★

*Pariyagam* = anklet-bells for the feet

*Padagam* = foot ornament

*Ariyagam* = the ornament called *kaarsari*

*Kurangu-seri* = thigh ornament

*Virisigai* = women's waist ornament

*Kandigai* = neck ornament
