# Nedunchezhiyan and Nedunalvadai!

<!-- scan 277 -->

Nedunchezhiyan
and *Nedunalvadai*!

“The Pandyas saw three Tamil Sangams
and rendered good service to Tamil” —

when, eager to read many works
about the history of those praised Pandyas,

I was troubled by the question,
“What was the capital of the Pandya country?”

I would say it was scholar
“Ayyamperumal” Konar
who cleared that doubt.

In the book he wrote,
he says that in the age of the First Sangam,

many *kaadams* south
of today's Madurai,
ancient Thenmadurai
stood as the capital
of the Pandya country.

After Thenmadurai
was destroyed by the first sea-deluge,

the capital that blossomed
was Kavatapuram —

and there, he says,
stood the Middle Sangam.

When that too
was destroyed by a sea-deluge,

the king Mudathirumaran,
who had not drowned and died in it,

founded the capital
that is today's great city of Madurai!

Now let us see
the spectacle and splendour
of the Pandyan kings
who ascended the throne
and ruled from that capital!

★ ★ ★

<!-- scan 278 -->

The race that,
from that ancient day,
beautifully divided life
into *agam* and *puram*

and has lived by that order —

our precious Tamil race alone,
our thousand-year crop —

is witnessed by the literature
created by the wise!

★ ★ ★

There are many examples,

but Nakkirar strung one
like a garland:

among the *Pattuppattu*,
a song shining like a pearl —

the Tamil nectar
called *Nedunalvadai*!

It speaks of the courage
of Pandyan Nedunchezhiyan;

it speaks too of the heart
of the slender-bangled Kopperundevi,

burning like fire
as she longs through his long absence;

and it becomes a work
that tells how the life called
*agam* and *puram*

stood in Tamil Nadu of that day
as a counter to the doctrine
of this world and the other world!

★ ★ ★

At the battlefield of Talaiyalanganam,
seven kings miscalculated,

thinking a lone young man
had become trapped;

and they lost,
their broad shoulders ruined!

After that,
Nedunchezhiyan,

wishing to live
like inseparable *anril* birds,

<!-- scan 279 -->

accepted as his queen
a beautiful jewel-like dove....

Though the honour-wounds
struck in his youth
by spear and sword
from seven armies

had healed
and turned to scars,

the lovely queen
thought they shone
upon the Pandyan's chest
like golden medals —

and without even thinking,
she kissed them,
every day!

After joining hearts
upon the bridal bed,

time did not grant him
space to hold,
all through the night,

his heart-stealing Anjugam
without letting her leave
the corner of his eye....

The summons
of battlefields
was the great trouble!

Even after
the battle of Talaiyalanganam,

under his leadership
he conquered
Mizhalai Kottam
of the king “Vel Evvi”!

He seized
the crown belonging
to the sword-warrior Muthuvelir,

and captured
their Muthu Kootram as well!

He destroyed the army
of the great warrior-king
“Cheraman Yanaikatchei Irumporai”

and shut him away
in prison!

Thus —

<!-- scan 280 -->

because he heaped
mountain upon mountain
of victory-garlands,

and set out roaring
with army after army
like wave after wave,

Pandimadevi trembled,

like a bird
with clusters upon clusters
of ripe fruit before it,
yet unable to eat,

losing its balance in anguish....

Unable to move
day and night forward,
she shed tears!

The *vaadai* is a cold wind,
is it not?

Even without wrapping cloth,
a woman may wrap herself
in a man's body,

and a man
in a woman's body,

and so defeat
that cold wind!

But what was the condition here?

The Pandyan king,
surging with force,
had gone to the battlefront!

In the heat
that grows courage,

the sky-threatening cold wind
could not prevail
against the king!

But the queen's condition —
alas! pitiable!

Poet Nakkirar sings:

see the suffering
of household fowl
unable to bear
the cold of the *vaadai*!

The hen and cock,
which once wandered
the wide outdoors,

searched and ate food
with delight,
and passed their days in pleasure,

now, unable to tell
night from day,

confined inside
wooden cages,

shift their aching feet
one after another,
it is said!

In such a killing cold,
with love leaping in her heart,

<!-- scan 281 -->

the spotted peahen
rolls upon her bed!

Taking up Tamil
that none can defeat,

Nakkirar fashions
*Nedunalvadai*!

A tall tower
like a mountain-peak —

its flags displaying colours
like rainbows!

Upon stout pillars
of blue-gem hue,

has silver itself
been spread as white plaster?

And the rampart wall —

as though cast from copper,
built with indescribable strength!

Thus Nakkirar,
through classical Tamil,
paints the palace
of Sezhiyan.

And see how,
through song,
he shows the bed
upon which lies

that hibiscus-lipped Pandyan queen,
her whole mind fainting
from thoughts of him!

A fierce war-elephant,
with legs like drums,

after reaching
a full forty years of age,

dies while engaged
in the work of war.

Its two sharp tusks
are taken,
shaped with beauty
and made into a bed.

At its four sides,
beautiful rounded finials,

like the breasts
of pregnant women
swollen with milk!

<!-- scan 282 -->

Hanging strings
of pearls and gems!

Paintings
of mullai and jasmine flowers!

A cover of rare artistry
showing a lion-hunt!

A five-fold bed
with support-cushion,
pillow and reclining cushion,

all fashioned
from swan feathers!

But there is no *avan* —
no “he” —
for her to say *anai*,
“embrace”!

And so the flood
of the queen's grief
breaks through its *anai*,
its dam,
and swells!

Nurses stroked her feet,
saying,
“Mother, sleep!”

“Get away, girls —
have you no other work?”

cried the queen,
furious!

In the painting
of the sky nearby,

she saw the moon
and Rohini
standing close,
never apart.

“Why have I
not received such fortune?

Why do I suffer
uselessly like this?”

A drop of water
peeped out
upon her eyelash;

she flicked it away
with her finger —

but could not
erase and rewrite
her grief;

she suffered on!

Thus,
in the palace at Madurai,

the queen languishes
like a flowering vine
holding nectar,
waiting for its bee.

What, then,
is the condition
of Pandyan Nedunchezhiyan?

<!-- scan 283 -->

Come —
let us see it
in *Nedunalvadai*!

Love did not become
an obstacle
to the Pandyan's sense of duty!

Because of thoughts
of Kopperundevi,

he did not stray
from the goal
he had taken up!

The *vaadai* wind
came to his war-camp too!

Yet he did not forget
the sword-bearing face of war
and run in search
of a bangle-bearing arm!

Here —

at midnight
the Pandyan set out.

For what?

To see the chaste wife
writhing upon the five-fold bed?

No!
No!

In the cold season
that makes even hills tremble,

with the chill wind
blowing from the north,

even though torch-flames
leaned toward the south,

the king,
inside a northern war-camp,

did not turn south
for the sake
of his yearning Pandimadevi!

To perform
the duty of the day,

with sword-bearing warriors
carrying lights
around him,

his upper cloth slipping
from his left shoulder,

he lifted and held it
with his left hand;

and upon the shoulder
of a strong sword-warrior,

he placed his right hand
in comradeship.

At midnight,
with raindrops falling,

he went through
all the places
where many great warriors
lay wounded —

<!-- scan 284 -->

asked after them
with affection,

thanked them,
and embraced them!

All the suffering
they had undergone
through the daytime battle —

dust upon their feet,
hands and bodies
as they drove back
the enemy army —

was forgotten,
it is said,

because the caring gaze
of their leader
fell upon them!

The great queen,
distraught,
searches for her lover;

he,
his legs aching,
walks around the war-camp
fulfilling duty!

If evidence is needed
that the race
which has lived from that day onward

beautifully dividing life
into *agam* and *puram*

is our precious Tamil race —

ah!
is not this one *Nedunalvadai*
enough?

★ ★ ★

(Based upon the literary work *Nedunalvadai*, composed by the Sangam poet Nakkirar in praise of Nedunchezhiyan, victor of the battle of Talaiyalanganam, this has been written in a simple style.)
