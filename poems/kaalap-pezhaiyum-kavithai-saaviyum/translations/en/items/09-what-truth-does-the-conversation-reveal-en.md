# What Truth Does the Conversation Reveal?

<!-- scan 44 -->

I myself have sometimes wondered
whether the previous day's casket of time
was a little too long;
when telling history,
one must travel by the proper road.

Five and three make eight;
even if we add it as three and five,
only the manner of addition differs —
there is no contradiction
in the total!

As an example,
in Sankrityayan's stories
with historical backgrounds,
I fold my hands and invite you:
come see the portrait called “Purudhanan,”
painted against the screen of the year 2000 BCE

Show me your kindness;
after seeing it,
pour out your opinion too!

From the highly regarded book
*From the Volga to the Ganga*,
I shall show you a conversation drawn there;
that is my duty to you!

A conversation between two Aryans —
I give it exactly as it stands,

<!-- scan 45 -->

without adding or subtracting,
without correcting what he has drawn;
value this work of mine in presenting it!

The conversation between the two Aryans in that book.
(Book — pages 101, 118, 119, 120)

“We have done away with the office of Indra.”

“You have done away with it?”

“Yes; for us Aryans of the southern region,
this office had begun to cause fear.”

“Why fear it?”

“Do we not call the commander of the army ‘Indra’?”

“Yes.”

“Yes; Aryans do not regard the commander-in-chief
as master over all their affairs.
In wartime it is true
that we accept his order exactly and act upon it.
But we Aryans consider above everything else
the social assembly in which each has authority
to speak his opinion openly.”

“Yes, yes.”

“But the Asuras hold the opposite principle.
The Indra, or king, of the Asuras
possesses every authority.
He recognizes no social assembly above himself.
Every word that comes from the Asura king's mouth
is law.
Every Asura must accept it exactly.
For one who disobeys,
the punishment is death.”

<!-- scan 46 -->

“Yes, I too have seen it.
Their priests have turned the Asuras into donkeys.”

“Yes — lower even than donkeys,
the Asuras perform linga worship.
The shape of that linga is like
the male organ joined with the female organ.
I accept that these are instruments
for the pleasure of union between man and woman,
and that through that union
our future generations grow.
But is it not sheer foolishness
to make that shape in earth or stone,
set it up and worship it?”

“Yes; what doubt is there in that?”

“That Asura king is a great devotee
of this linga deity.
But I think this too is a disguise.
That Asura king and his priests are not fools.
They are cleverer than we Aryans.
It will take us a long time
to learn to build cities like theirs.
Their market streets,
tanks filled with lotus flowers,
the high flights of steps beside them,
and broad royal avenues —
we cannot see such arrangements
anywhere in our Aryan land.”

What you are seeing is the casket of time
from roughly four thousand years ago —
from 2000 BCE!
Rahul's *From the Volga to the Ganga*
photographs, as it were, the Aryans of that day
regarding the philosophy of the linga-form
worshipped by the Asuras
as the summit of uncivilised life,
as utter foolishness!

<!-- scan 47 -->

If the divine form we saw four thousand years ago
was a cultural form of the Dravidians called Asuras,
and today it is accepted in temple after temple,

does that mean Aryan culture
has now approved the philosophy of the divine form
found by the Asuras?

Is the union of the Avudaiyar and the linga
appropriate only now?

Every day we see deities
with great clustered moustaches
and fearsome, fierce forms —

celebrated as gods of the Dravidian Asuras:
Theevattirayan,
valiant Madurai Veeran,
Kathavarayan,
Muneeswaran,
Saneeswaran,
Kaadan, Maadan,
the dark Idumban —

and clans that celebrate such fierce deities
as their clan gods,
standing as marks, we are told,
of the Asura clan;
we see temples and tanks
bearing their names everywhere,
without exception —
we see and rejoice in them
even at celebrated sacred sites.

Though we may have taken darshan
thousands of times,
have we even once remembered
the story we read
and twisted our faces at what we saw?

My purpose is not to mock gods;
is this not an example proving
that, in this world,
culture remains distinct
even in the question of God?

Even in worshipping the divine,
two different cultures;
that the linga-form belonged to the Asuras,
that the Aryans did not accept it —

yet those who four thousand years ago
said it was uncivilised,
why do they today, across the whole country,
sing namavali to that very form?

Cultures were different —
even in delighting in God there was difference;
between Aryans and Dravidians called Asuras,
there was no agreement in this.

<!-- scan 48 -->

Has that condition now changed?
If not, did Sankrityayan himself
fail to observe the cultural difference correctly?

Is there any temple today
without the linga that the Aryans mocked?
On massive temple chariots —
on temple towers —
which great believers forgot
to set those divine symbols?

In temples of fierce-deity worship,
who came forward boldly
to install an image in linga form?

Even in the making of divine images,
there is cultural difference —
between Aryans and Dravidians called Asuras;

and even there,
one form in the northern region,
another form in the southern region.

There, the linga that marks male and female —
in the Aryan dictionary.....
ignorance, foolishness!

Here, in every temple,
that very form becomes Ammaiyappar,
the Mother-and-Father form;

a form worthy of reverence,
a deity to be worshipped.

Do not roll up your sleeves
and rise to quarrel with me.
Do not leap and bound toward me
and abuse me as a sinner —

this conversation between two Aryans
about the form of God
openly declares
that Aryans did not accept
the culture of the Dravidians called Asuras,
even in the very form of God —

<!-- scan 49 -->

and when we examine
how even then they stood divided,
one way in the north,
another in the south,

we also see that,
in changing themselves to suit circumstance,
Aryans are formidable masters.

Scholar Sankrityayan,
in his own way,
has already taught us this;

and this truth will live
not merely as long as
*From the Volga to the Ganga* lives,

but as long as there remains
such a thing as history
in this world.

Certain! Certain! Certain!
