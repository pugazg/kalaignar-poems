# Sweat Falling from the Brow; the Ribcage Breaking!

<!-- scan 40 -->

In Sankrityayan's collection
of short stories with a historical background —
*From the Volga to the Ganga* —
under the title “Amritashva,”

I recently read on page 54
of the distinctive rule of life
set apart for the wives of that age;
I write with that memory before me.
Historian “Rahul” has drawn it thus:

“When guests and friends came to the house,
it was the custom not only to feast them
and make them happy,

but temporarily to give them one's wives as well,
as an honour.”

The culture that entered in this way,
through the mountain pass,
seems to have taken another degraded form
in the story where Iyarpakai Nayanar
sent his own wife into the bedchamber
for a guest who came presenting himself
as an incarnation of God.

I am only saying what is there;
whether you bear with this,
or refuse to bear it and hate me for it,

<!-- scan 41 -->

you must decide firmly
after reading both books.

The coming of the Aryans,
their clash with the Dravidians
who had lived here from the beginning;

an eighteen-year war without rest
between the Dravidians called “Asuras”
and the “Devas,” the immortals —

learned great men — Iyers, Iyengars,
Samas and Shastris —
wrote such histories even then!

That the Ramayana war itself
was an Aryan–Dravidian struggle
was written by Nehru
before he became Prime Minister of India.

Absorbing all this within itself,
there took shape

the historical work
*From the Volga...*!

I have not bent it to suit my own course;
am I mad enough to call the sky the earth?

Like the ever-flowing life-river Ganga,
I would only undertake penance and ask a boon
that Kaveri too should swell in abundance
for Tamil Nadu;

would I dress myself up
as a distilled liar
and call a dry great river “Ganga”?

That is how I state it emphatically —
always on the strength of evidence —

the story of Aryan culture
coming to swallow and bring down
Dravidian culture!

By a culture that laid siege
three thousand years ago,

<!-- scan 42 -->

we have for many years
been enduring the cruelty
of the fire then kindled.

Thousands upon thousands of acres of land —
said to belong to temple,
said to belong to the Aadheenam —

and upon those lands,
labouring day after day,
pearls of sweat falling and falling from the brow,

ribcage breaking and breaking,
growing crops while the stomach
burns with hunger —

for the poor peasant,
that working labourer,
there is neither means nor opportunity
to cook even a morsel of rice.

If he raises a question and asks “Why?”
there is no one to answer!

Dreaming is of no use;
neither God nor the government
is going to attend to us.

Even when this worry upon worry
takes aim at us
like stones from a sling
and strikes us —
let us bear it
and try waking God himself:

morning, noon and night,
three times a day,

after the cost of food
for you and your family,

save every coin left over each day
in good banks and let it earn interest;

O Lord, spend it every day
so that your children may gain health
and rise step after step
in education rich with knowledge —
then our country will advance well, we shall say.

From today,
let us dig a pit and bury narrow aims;
to climb the hill of fame and stand there —

<!-- scan 43 -->

we need new thought after new thought.
To fulfil our goal,
we need the capacity to act.

To gain that capacity and act,
our gaze must travel in every direction.
As the manure for it,
we must understand our ancient history.

Only when history is in our hands
as sword and shield
can we face the hostile field
that comes crashing against us.

So that our history may shine
as a great lamp of the world,
as a sacred lamp,

it must stand clear in everything,
leaving no room for doubt.

Because the casket of time
will stand beside us for that purpose,
I shall continue to tell
some more stories in poetic form,
from my imagination!

I know there is no gain
in speaking merely of old tales —
yet by speaking of the stories
of our grandfathers and forefathers,
the old stories of history,

we may ward off harm from society.
From the events of past history,

what must be discarded?
What among those memories must be forgotten?

Nothing among them need be destroyed —
only if historical monuments are allowed to live

can they serve to show us the way
toward both the good and the bad.

Recognizing the good,
we may walk further;
if it is evil,
we may turn back at once!
We may reform ourselves too!
