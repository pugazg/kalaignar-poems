# They Saw Many Battlefields; They Won in Naval War Too!

<!-- scan 241 -->

They saw many battlefields;
they won in naval war too!

“If the mother leaps eight feet,
the calf leaps sixteen” —

that proverb became
an unequalled example
for father Rajaraja
and son Rajendra!

Rajadhiraja Chola,
only in the final period
of his reign,

crowned his son Rajendra
and made him king.

Rajendra Chola, however,
in the seventh year itself
after taking the throne,

handed the responsibility
of leading the country

to his younger son Rajadhiraja,
creating a startling turn
in history.

Father and son,
it is said,
ruled the Chola mandalam together
for twenty-five years —

<!-- scan 242 -->

a novelty
not seen until then
in the history of India!

To say still more:
historians divide
into two parts

the wars Rajadhiraja fought
while holding the responsibility
of crown prince.

On the battlefields
of both those parts,

the younger son Rajadhiraja
won fame
as his father's support,
as his equal,

and in one or two,
as one who surpassed his father.

The war of Idaithurai Nadu —
this was the plain
between the two rivers,
Krishna and Tungabhadra,

today's “Raichur”;
there the first victory
belonged to Rajendra Chola!

North of Gangavadi,
west of Nolambadi,

the army of the Western Chalukyas
rose in fury;

he subdued that enemy
like sprinkling water
into a boiling furnace,

and made it praise him
and bow in worship!

Northeast of Hyderabad
in Andhra,

the old “Kollipakkai,”
the source calls today's “Gulbarga”;

there the Chola army
broke the opposition apart
and brought home
victory after victory!

He felled the Rashtrakuta capital
called “Mannaik Kadakkam”

and won the great fame
of being called
“the Chola who took the land” —
*Mannai Konda Cholan*.

In CE 1017,
Rajendra's army
advanced by laying siege
and making war

upon the king of Lanka,
Mahinda V —

<!-- scan 243 -->

that Eelam king
who once before
had turned his back
and fled from Rajaraja.

They defeated him,
recovered the Pandya jewelled crown
he had seized and kept,

and also won the distinction
of holding him
as a prisoner of the Chola country!

Thus the *Mahavamsa*,
the source says,
points to the victory
of the Chola army
in the Eelam war,

and establishes the fame
of the Tamil great warriors!

The Chola empire,
which had made the tiger flag fly
in Eelam,

also sprang with its army
upon the Pandya country!

Vallabha Pandya,
who had been subjugated
in the time of Rajaraja,
king of kings,

perhaps now refused to bow
and began to act in opposition;

hearing this,
the Chola army
broke his back,

the source says,
pointing to the inscriptional phrase
“Kantalur Salaiyil Kalam Aruthaan”!

★ ★ ★

Rajendra Chola,
who had seen many battlefields,

won the heroic title
“Gangai Konda Cholan” —
Chola who took the Ganga —

and established,
as its sign,
the victory monument
called Gangaikonda Cholapuram.

The chief cause of both
was his foremost expedition:
the Ganga campaign!

<!-- scan 244 -->

Beginning with Vengi country,
at the end of the war
at Chakrakottam,

the Chola tiger flag
rose high!

Namanai Konam,
Panchappalli,

a part of Masuni country
on the banks of the Indus,

Ottara country
of South Kosala,
called Maha Kosala,

and the regions
along the banks
of the Mahanadi —

after conquering them,
the Chola army,
says the Thiruvalangadu copper plate
with pride,

collected from its enemies
box upon box
of heaps of many jewels
as tribute.

That same copper plate
also says
that our classical-Tamil Chola,

to cross the Ganga,
lined up many hundreds of elephants
and made them into a bridge,

marched the army across,
reached the opposite bank,
and defeated the enemy!

Pandarathar says
that the Kosala region
called Purva Desa too
fell to the Cholas
as a prize of battle!

The sword that whirled everywhere
crying victory, victory,

whirled too toward Dharmapala
of Dandabhukti country
in the Midnapore district of Bengal;

and that boy's crown
fell because of it!

The Chola army then
stepped onward
into the land
of Ranasura,
king of Dakshina Lada!

Another ruler
of a Bengal state,
Govindachandan,
laid his victory
at the Chola's feet!

<!-- scan 245 -->

Another was Mahipalan,
called an incomparable warrior;

though his rule,
the source says,
spread as far as Kasi,

before the armies
of Rajendra and Rajadhiraja
it flew away
like dust in the wind!

As a sign
of the Vatapi victory,
Pallava commander Paranjothi

brought back a statue —
the Vatapi Ganapati!

Likewise,
the explanation is given
that the statue
brought by the Chola commander

in memory of defeating
many northern kings

is the Ganga Vinayakar statue
at the Nageswarar temple
in Kudanthai!

Among Rajendra Chola's
victories across the sea,

the victory over Kadaram
was the victory
that immersed them in delight.

His *meikeerthis*
and the Thiruvalangadu copper plates,
the source says,
still tell us of it today.

It is said
that pots of Ganga water
were placed upon the heads
of defeated northern soldiers;

as those men came
in ordered ranks,

the two kings,
Rajendra and Rajadhiraja,
stretched out both hands
to receive them!

Now let us show our head
to another enemy!
