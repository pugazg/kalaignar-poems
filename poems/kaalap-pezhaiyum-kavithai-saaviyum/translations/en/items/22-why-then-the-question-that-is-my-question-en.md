# “Why, Then, the Question? — That Is My Question!”

<!-- scan 112 -->

I, I, I alone —
I keep moving forward.

When the tongue bends and straightens,
saying I, I, I alone,

it is not with arrogance —
but with humility,
with great humility.

How did this “I” come to be?
Where did I come from?

Above all these questions
there is one fundamental question —

that is the question,
“Why did I come?”
Only because I came
can I ask, “Why did I come?”

Only by asking
does an answer arise.
Then did the question come first?

Or did the answer come first?
Ask “Why?” — the answer is “Because of this!”

Ask “How?” — the answer is “Like this!”
Which appeared first — the egg or the hen?

An explanation for that
has already been given;
what stands before us now —
is it the question, or the answer?

<!-- scan 113 -->

Can an answer arise
without a question?
What is this called?

Is this the question?
The universe — this is the answer!

Only because this answer already exists —
or because it existed —

could we look at something
and ask, “What is this?”

When there was nothing,
no question arose.
Since no question arose,
no answer appeared.
And so,

there was a state
in which neither question nor answer
was needed —
and I imagine that I too
was in that very state,

clinging to it like a shadow,
not one year,
not two,
not a thousand,

not a lakh,
but crore upon crore —
when, five hundred crore years ago,

this world is said
to have come into being;
and when, eighty crore years ago,

life is said to have appeared —
among all that arose,
I imagine myself as the first life,

and through those eighty crore years
I have floated as air with air,
mingled as water with water,

been heat with heat,
and cold with cold;

<!-- scan 114 -->

been darkness with darkness,
and light with light;

playing with sky and earth,
crawling, writhing,
going on my knees,
creeping,

walking,
and moving forward.
Who can say
that a full stop
has been placed upon my progress?

Even of eighty crore years ago,
Manikkavasagar,
melt and melted in song
of life's growth:

“becoming grass,
becoming shrub,
becoming worm...”

If the full stop
had been placed even then,
what conversation could there be
between you and me now?

In my journey,
shall I call it a walking journey?

A moving journey?
A crawling journey?
A flying journey?
Since “journey” in general
fits them all,

I say only this:
my journey has gone on
for many crore years —
five hundred crore years —

and that within it
I appeared as the first life
eighty crore years ago:
how, and to whom,
can I prove that?

Who among you was there then?
And who among us now
will be there hereafter?

So is “many crore years”
not itself a compromise agreement?

It lacks an exact reckoning;
but is not “an uncountable span of years”
itself an acceptable reckoning?

<!-- scan 115 -->

In the Bhagavad Gita,
Parthaman piled up examples
saying, “I am everything”;
no one should misunderstand
this “I” in that manner.

Perhaps Manikkavasagar,
to travel safely backward
through the stages of development,
began first by saying
“becoming a worm”!

Across these many crore years,
how many, how many lists
of stagewise development
have I been able to see
upon my journey!

A bridge-like land link,
said to have existed until 12,000 BCE,
joining North America
and East Asia,
was submerged in the Ice Age;

and because East Asia
had been connected with North America
until then,
marks of Maya civilisation
having spread there —
these too I can see
as one part of my journey!

From worm to human,
from monkey to human,
I have been carrying this journey
from the time I was a worm —
crawling, writhing —
so that I may tell you too
the meaning of stagewise development,
leaning not on one or two supports,
but on many!

The universe,
where countless planets
including our world wander,
keeps no family planning;

who knows how many more planets
it will bear and bring forth
as children!
Only as the journey goes on
without stopping
will we come to know.

<!-- scan 116 -->

In this journey
of crore upon crore of years,
I have seen
worms writhing in the soil,

birds spreading wings in the sky,
animals roaming in the forest,
creatures that crawl
and creatures that swim,

and the human race —
younger in age than all these,
yet regarded, within our own measure,
as the complete one —

and the divisions within that race,
and the cultures
each of those divisions honours;

seeing,
rejoicing,
grieving,
being darkened by sorrow,
shedding tears,

I carry many kinds of feeling
and continue the journey —
together with you!

As I travel forward,
I turn for a moment
and look behind.

The learned call this
an *arima nokku* — a lion's backward glance;
this humble servant's glance
may even be an *ariya nokku* —
a glance hard to know.

There is no selfish aim in it;
there is a view toward Tamil history.

So do not take it
as a narrow view;
take it as the view
of my principle!

The reason for that view
stands spread before us
as the answer —
“Why, then, the question?”
That is my question!
