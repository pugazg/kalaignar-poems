# We Need a Heart of Gold; We Need the Love It Gives!

<!-- scan 25 -->

Today we take up a sheet,
fill the pen with ink,

and paint in marvellous detail
the condition in which early man lived
six thousand years ago —

what he ate, how he slept,
all that he experienced.

Calling a thousand facilities and opportunities to our aid,
we conduct our research;

then announce its conclusions with ease.
But Tolkappiyar, older even than our master Valluvar —

more than two thousand years ago,
did he not compose the *Tolkappiyam*
with a stylus?

That incomparable grammar of life,
created upon palm-leaf,
begins by saying that a one-sensed life-form
knows through the body,

and ends by saying that six-sensed knowledge
is that which can be known through the mind —
explaining every class of life
and how many senses each possesses.

That great epic,
that painted grammar of life,
that poem which begins with tree, plant and creeper
and speaks onward into human history —

in this earth, a mountain;
on that mountain, a peak —
he who gave us that stainless grammar of tradition,

<!-- scan 26 -->

I would say that kneeling before him,
worshipping and paying homage,
is a mighty duty to be performed
in the very midst of drawing human history!

Six thousand years ago,
man had no clothes,
no power to prepare food of the six tastes,

no sense of love, romance, affection or duty,
no ordered bonds among bodies wrapped in bone and skin,

no habit of thinking about refinement or conduct;
what he saw became the sight,
what he seized became his style of life.

He wandered over forest and hill,
and made friendship too with goat and cattle.

Whenever hunger struck,
he learned to pluck whatever he saw along the path
and eat what he had plucked;

so even if he saw the “magnet stone,”
and even if that stone itself came toward him at first sight,

like owl and horned owl,
each would seek out
whatever prey each could find.

After that — who was he? who was she? ........
who would sing *aararo*
for one yet to be born? ........

So it is in one of Rahul Sankrityayan's stories:
when the beautiful enchantress Diva
and her desirous lover Sur

embrace in delight, drink from each other's lips
and lie there forgetting themselves,
she says:
“My beloved, you are my mother's son!
Even so, forgive me
for not having come close to you until now.”

“Enough — from now on,
do not feel shy with me,” he says;
and afterwards they —

<!-- scan 27 -->

became flood mingling with flood,
veena and its resonance —

then moved apart,
and later each ran off
to play a different veena.

A life slipping along with no bounds,
the custom of the age of gesture
before spoken language —

showing by facial sign
“No,” “You must not,”
and “I cannot desire this” —

that mime-language of performance
was the speech-language of that day.
There is a whole history behind
word-language later rising into sound.

When struck, crying “Ah!” “Oo!” —
calling to one far away, “Come!”;

in fear, trembling and screaming “Ayyo!” —
in overwhelming joy, laughing “Aav!” —

if these took second place and became musical sound,
then *iyal*, *isai* and *koothu*
are surely the basic grammar of every language.

In time, the dominion of the tongue
made even the language of gesture
a minister without portfolio,

and, in keeping with our master's command,
“Whatever else you fail to guard, guard the tongue,”
it seems a rapid-action guard was set
for the kingdom of the tongue!

For the tongue that opened the road
to the heights of civilisation to begin speaking,
the advance that arose forty thousand years ago
was, they say, the cause.

Man, who ate raw flesh to appease hunger,
wanting to taste his food,
learned to kindle fire and cook it.

<!-- scan 28 -->

Only by hunting and killing animals
could he find a way to manage the hunger of the belly —
and even in that hunt,

competition, jealousy, war — new problems one after another;
these clashes that sprouted in the days
of our grandmother and great-grandmother

have not stopped even in the days
of granddaughter and great-grandchild.....
The counsel of the *Aathichudi*,
“Let anger cool,”

enters no one's ears;
why that is, I do not know.

Rather than the people of this age,
who have grown accustomed to a life of enmity,

the people of that age,
who moved uncivilised through cave-life,
seem in many ways better —

so that such a thought
need not arise,

the heart of gold that the earth possesses
must be possessed in greater measure
by woman and man;

that heart, though rare to obtain,

will create love —
will cultivate peace!

Another story will branch out;
its seed will sprout tomorrow!
