## Governing objective

> **Retain Kalaignar's language — voice, cadence, rhetorical architecture, political and historical specificity, literary allusion, repetition, satire, dialogue and direct address — while making the English genuinely readable.**

Readability is not permission to paraphrase away source texture, correct a historical claim inside the translation, modernize the Tamil argument, or substitute translator explanation for Kalaignar's rhetoric.

## Voice-fidelity rules

1. **Direct address stays direct.** Do not replace apostrophe, challenge or invocation with detached narration.
2. **Repetition is meaning.** Repeated phrases, parallel structures and accumulative lists are not to be compressed for elegance.
3. **Oratory remains audible.** Preserve public-speech energy, turns of address, rhetorical escalation and slogan cadence.
4. **Rhetorical questions remain questions.** Do not convert them into explanatory assertions.
5. **Political/historical specificity remains specific.** Names, dynasties, places, movements, institutions, dates and figures remain visible.
6. **Literary references remain traceable.** Sangam works, classical authors, inscriptions and named texts must not dissolve into generic phrases.
7. **Imagery remains Kalaignar's imagery.** Do not rationalize metaphors simply because literal English is unusual.
8. **Satire and polemic remain sharp.** Do not neutralize ridicule, irony, accusation or ideological contrast.
9. **Dialogue remains dialogue.** Speaker changes and quoted speech must remain distinguishable.
10. **No fake archaism.** Historical subject matter does not require pseudo-Shakespearean English.
11. **Source notes and separators remain structurally visible.** Internal `★` breaks, `குறிப்பு:` blocks and parenthetical source notes must be represented appropriately.
12. **Line/stanza architecture remains visible.** English may adjust a line where grammar genuinely requires it, but must not proseify whole verse movements or silently compress content.
13. **Claims are translated as claims.** Translation is not a fact-check layer; contextual correction, if ever requested, belongs outside the translated body.
14. **No silent Tamil repair.** If translation exposes a plausible Tamil source defect, stop and reopen the source-backed archival workflow rather than fixing English alone.

## Names, terms and transliteration

A live terminology ledger should be maintained inside batch review notes and consolidated during the full-work editorial review.

Default principles:

- keep established proper names recognizable in English;
- preserve Tamil literary titles and culturally active movement/political terms when translation would erase their identity;
- translate an institutional or descriptive term when English clarity improves without loss of specificity;
- do not alternate casually between multiple spellings for the same king, place, text or political term;
- when a source wordplay depends on Tamil form, retain enough Tamil/transliteration for the wordplay to remain visible and explain only in a clearly separated translator note if necessary.

No global glossary choice should override the wording/context of an individual source passage without review.

## Batch workflow

For each batch:

1. fetch live `main` and confirm no source/canonical change supersedes this plan;
2. read both canonical item files completely;
3. consult `SOURCE_MAP.md` for exact scans, title witnesses and source-sensitive flags;
4. reread verified page records wherever a line, note, title, quotation or boundary needs source provenance;
5. draft the English translation without changing Tamil;
6. run source-coverage review item by item;
7. run Kalaignar-language/voice review;
8. verify names, dates, figures, literary works, political vocabulary and speaker distinctions;
9. record any non-literal solution or transliteration decision in review notes;
10. mark the batch `reviewed — PASS` only after both items pass.

A translated but unreviewed batch is not eligible for final collection assembly.
