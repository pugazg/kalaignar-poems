## Item 3 — A Story of the ‘Magnet Stone’!

- physical scans: **16–19**

### English translation

<!-- scan 16 -->

Egypt, Maya, Mesopotamia —
pointed to as the beginnings
of ancient civilization —

are pushed behind,
for at the very first glance
Indus Valley civilization

winks and captivates;
and so, asking where its embryonic state lay,
those who conducted severe research

found the region of Kumari Kandam
that had sunk beneath the sea
and declared, “That was its womb-land”;
we too behold it.

Mathivanan raises
a question that leaves us astonished,
and gives the answer as well.

Evolution says
that man arose from the monkey;

why then have the monkeys of today
not become humans as they did then?

When a little plant grows into a great tree,
only the topmost branch keeps climbing higher;

the side branches remain
where we saw them before —

<!-- scan 17 -->

so too does evolution,
this stagewise growth,

become a lesson
and, when reasoned through, give its answer.

Mud trampled and kneaded
becomes the wall we value and preserve!

The mud left lying where it was kneaded
does not become even a little wall —

so it is with the monkey-kind:
though one branch rises
into the human race that sits enthroned,

like the leftover kneaded mud
that remains upon the earth,
the human-ape too remains among humankind.

Before raising a mansion,
must we not dig the earth and lay a foundation!

Scholar Rahul Sankrityayan,
planning in just that way,
did not begin ancient history
with the alphabet's first lesson;

he began from six thousand years ago —
and *From the Volga to the Ganga*,
with its twenty stories,
became a treasury of thought.

Thus, taking the ideas, stories
and incomparable imaginings written by many,
and striving to gather and offer them together,

from earlier days onward,
I have told a few pearl-strung tales;

<!-- scan 18 -->

without sinking you in weariness,
I shall shape the journey onward — come travel with me!

Do not float along in a dream,
thinking that the instant your foot moves,
the casket of time will appear before your eyes

and that we can place the key of poetry in it
and simply open it — do not come with me so!

Do not leap in delight thinking,
“We can scoop up Ganga and Kaveri water,
drink deep and dance” —
only to droop in disappointment afterwards!

There is much evidence
that the threshold where civilization first learned to walk
was our Kumari Kandam!

That is why Anna, explaining Vincent Smith's statement
that even the history of India
should properly begin not from the bank of the Ganga
but from the bank of the Kaveri,

spoke under the title “By the Riverbank.”
Is it enough merely to savour the beauty of that speech?
Should not the Tamil's sense of self-respect rise up,
so that its heat and nerve do not fade away?

There was a time when man lived as a savage —
a Stone Age when men and women alike,
not knowing that showing the whole body could be called wrong,
lived with the whole body exposed;

we see too that they conveyed their thoughts
through gesture.
The first language of early man, then,
was the language of drama;

at the next stage
he began to speak the language of music;

<!-- scan 19 -->

and *iyal* came only after these —
though today we list them as *iyal, isai, natakam*;

in those days drama held first place
and gave expression to thought,
music stood forever second in that order,

and only later did *iyal* move ahead
to claim first place —
that is the history of language!

In that age when only the language of *koothu* existed —
that dark age,
the Stone Age of the past —

without hiding the condition of its people,
I shall continue by telling
a story of the “magnet stone.”

## Item 4 — The Stone Age That Was — A Better Age If It Does Not Return!

- stable item: **4**
- canonical Tamil title: `அன்றிருந்த கற்காலம் - இனி அமையாவிடின் நற்காலம்!`
- contents witness: `அன்றிருந்த கற்காலம் - இனி அமையாவிடின் நற்காலம்!`
- canonical file: `../../../sections/04.md`
- physical scans: **20–24**
- printed pages: **19–23**

### English translation

<!-- scan 20 -->

“Along the road of evolution,
where the human-ape
becomes the ape-man,

as a change to be welcomed with joy,
the ape-man who stood upright and walked
became Neanderthal man;

across the long distance of a long history,
we may grade the traveller who crossed it,

classifying and arranging him as
Stone Age man, Golden Age man,
Copper Age man, Bronze Age man —

and, carrying the construction onward
to Iron Age man,
we may write many stories!

With what exists before us,
and hiding what can be hidden as far as possible,
I have come to dance in the hall —
a dance of the pen.”

Speaking thus, the pen strode with majesty
and, swelling with pride,
opened its cap.

The master of enchanting writing
pours down a rain of words — be drenched!

<!-- scan 21 -->

Let us reckon the time as 6000 B.C.
and consider the civilizational knowledge
the world possessed then!

In the first story set against the history of the “Volga,”
the characters open their mouths and speak;
in my story they will not speak —

because Stone Age humans
had not yet acquired the power of speech!
Only after much time passes
will speech, first no bigger than a mustard seed,
later roar to the measure of an ocean!

I too saw the same rising sun
that the heart-winning writer Rahul saw.

So that my friend too might receive the joy I receive,
I said, “Come, friend — walk with me.”

The two of us went together;
we saw a woman approaching us.

I am posing no riddle;
there was no sari upon her body.

Not even tiger-skin or fox-skin
covered her golden form.

A parrot-beak for her;
a parrot's stare for us!

Why had she worn no garment at all?
Had her waist itself imposed some ban?

O woman walking at such speed,
what answer will you give, dear one? —

I hurried forward to ask;
she grew angry and walked away
like a statue itself set in motion.

<!-- scan 22 -->

Following the woman who had gone,
we hid behind a sheer-standing rock
and watched closely.

An open house with no thatch,
a dwelling lived in with no concealment,
a wasteland that did not even know
how to raise a screen for privacy!

Along the curving street the maiden went;
she entered that house,
bent and rose again,
her pointed breasts quivering as she stood!

Like trunks stretching toward grown sugarcane,
in frantic haste to satisfy hunger,

three tuskers stood with mouths agape;
three men surrounded her.

One tenderly stroked
the maiden's dishevelled head!
Another stopped the man who stroked her,
gesturing angrily,
“Do not ruin the chance we have found” —

and pressing upon her,
“Now satisfy my desire,”
he seized her slender ginger-like waist
and clasped her tightly;

she too wilted and yielded
like the stem of a water-lily!

She forced the banana-blossom bud upon him
and made it into a sharp sword
placed in a coward's hand;

then flung him away with her hand
like a frog that knew not the way.
The third man, who knew the way,
drove him off,

hunted her too,
played out the hunt to its end.....
We two friends watched all of it as spectacle...

<!-- scan 23 -->

A savage scene indeed;
yet even in this age that speaks
of a sacred grammar for chastity,

there are those who practise Stone Age civilization;
they accomplish their business in hiding,
like the molars at the back of the mouth.

Whether they speak everything openly,
or are operators who crouch, spring and gain their reward,

if even a little conscience remains,
they will not stage fearless demonstrations
in the middle of the assembly —

The Stone Age that existed six thousand years ago —
a better age is one in which it does not arise again
even in the year to come!

With a pearl-like woman, this three-man procession —
there is no count of how many times it has occurred.

Even if the woman loses every sound
and becomes a corpse, there is no case;
society itself seems to issue
a certificate of approval for that too;
there is no reckoning at all
for the loss of dignity!

Blessings upon you;
let none of that worm-ridden “revolutionary” talk
be needed here!

Let us gain the wisdom
to place chastity as a common standard for both sexes;
let no one sharpen a knife
so that people may be made to think it a petty thing!

Periyar, Anna and Bharathi
blew like a great storm insisting
that the chastity spoken of for woman
must be spoken of for man as well;

but did they issue an order
that neither sex should care about chastity at all?

<!-- scan 24 -->

The two friends,
after conducting this inquiry of ideas,
finally came to a conclusion.

Equality between man and woman
does not mean giving each an equal share
of the Halahala poison;

it is nectar that must be shared that way.
“Nectar for the devas;
poison for the asuras” —

that is an unfitting judgement of the mythic age;
this new form, flowered in revolution,
rejects that judgement utterly!

When all the debate was over
and they were returning,
one friend asked the other:

“Yes — what is that woman's name?”

“She is one who never spoke;
everything that happened was only the language of drama, friend —

for our own wish, let us give her a name.
Because what we saw was a Stone Age drama;
because she drew the men toward her;

and because the Iron Age is what will come next —
let us call her the ‘Magnet Stone’
that draws iron to itself;

first, let us move away from this place,”
said that friend!

(One who lived in the Neanderthal Valley was called Neanderthal man.)

## Translator notes

- Item 3 `கருநிலை` / `கருவூர்` carries an origin/womb cluster. The English uses **embryonic state** and **womb-land** to keep that rhetorical linkage visible rather than replacing it with a single neutral word such as “origin.”
- `மாந்தக் குரங்கு` is retained as **human-ape** and the evolutionary branch/mud-wall analogies are translated as Kalaignar presents them; the English does not fact-check the scientific or historical claims.
- *From the Volga to the Ganga* is used for `வால்காவிலிருந்து கங்கை வரை`; Rahul Sankrityayan remains explicit.
- `இயல்`, `இசை`, `நாடகம்` and `கூத்து` remain visible as **iyal, isai, natakam, koothu** where the source is making a specifically Tamil-language ordering argument; explanatory substitution would erase the word-order point.
- `சூடும் சொரணையும்` is rendered **heat and nerve**, keeping the charged self-respect rhetoric rather than reducing it to detached “awareness.”
- Item 4's opening `பேனா நாத்தனம்` is rendered **a dance of the pen**, preserving the personified pen and stage-performance frame.
- `கிளி மூக்கு ... கிளி நோக்கு` is kept as **parrot-beak / parrot's stare** to retain the audible wordplay rather than normalizing it into plain physical description.
- The source's sexual passage on scan 22 is translated through its own metaphoric register — tuskers, sugarcane, ginger-waist, water-lily, banana blossom, sword and frog — without inserting anatomical explanation absent from the Tamil.
- `வழக்கில்லை` in the scan-23 passage is rendered **there is no case**, preserving the possible legal sting in the line rather than flattening it into “it is not customary.”
- `கற்பு` is rendered **chastity** throughout this passage because the argument explicitly compares the standard applied to women and men; the translation does not replace it with a narrower modern category.
- `ஆலகால விஷம்`, `அமரர்`, `அசுரர்` are rendered **Halahala poison**, **devas**, **asuras**, retaining the mythological contrast that structures Kalaignar's equality argument.
- The final `காந்தக்கல்` pun is preserved as **Magnet Stone** because the joke depends on the coming Iron Age and the magnet drawing iron; it also links Item 4 back to Item 3's announced story title.
- The parenthetical Neanderthal source note is retained in the translated body.
