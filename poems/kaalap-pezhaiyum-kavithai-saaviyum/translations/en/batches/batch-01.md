### English translation

<!-- scan 10 -->

Space! Space! Space!
With no one watching —

with no one able to watch;
with no one at all there to see,
space lies spread everywhere!
Light came first —

from which direction?
Unknown; utterly unknown.

We gave a name to the direction it came from!
We worship the direction!

We had never seen space,
never looked upon light —

when we ourselves did not yet exist,
what power had we to see?

In the empty vastness, countless sounds!
At countless moments!

With no one hearing;
with no one able to hear —

with no one at all there to listen!

Crores upon crores of thunderclaps
crashed at a single corner;

red planets,
balls of fire,

<!-- scan 11 -->

rushed roaring,
scattering as they flew.

They say there may be nine, ten planets;
there may even be one or two more.

The world became established as one among them;
only afterwards did conflict begin!

None of this was known then;
only after so many crores of years had passed —

the thirst-stricken world asked for water!
Nature, saying “No,” poured out tears;

calling those tears “rain” instead,
the Earth found consolation!

Cooling the fever of its body,
it gathered the rainwater into ocean expanses.

Water, land, air, outer space,
fire — among the five great elements,

there is endless enmity; there is enduring kinship.
Without even a kiss, new life forms keep arriving;

change even there, change within them,
discovering the new and entering into union —

worms, insects, multiplying birds,
hordes of fierce animals —

bands of patient domestic creatures,
all drawn into the splendour of evolutionary growth.

We humans, who slowly, slowly leapt from the monkey —
let us stand upon the public stage and raise our voice of principle:
“Ownership in common — rights in common;
to call the world private property is wrong!”

## Item 2 — Stagewise Development

### English translation

<!-- scan 12 -->

The age of this world in which we live,
they say, is five hundred crore years.

On the basis of the growth of civilisation,
and in the stage-by-stage reckoning of living species upon the earth,

from five lakh years ago
down to roughly ten thousand years,

our old-tiger forebears divided and viewed it
as the BCE age;

they called precisely this the Lemurian age...
The time that remained became the glorious age of civilisation

when, with the civilisation brought forth by Kumari Kandam,
people of the Indus expanse

had risen to a distinguished civilisation.
Since there is no opposing view — if this is taken as

the ten-and-a-half-touch view,
and stamped upon the mind;

then we must accept, line by line,
the notes of the great historians who chart for us

that life appeared in this world
eighty crore years ago,

and that forty lakh years ago
our ape-man walked upon the earth.

<!-- scan 13 -->

We must indeed accept those historical scholars' markings,
line after line.

The age that appeared
five lakh years ago was the first Stone Age!

As far back as fifty thousand years ago,
the beautiful civilisation of Kumari Kandam —

yes, that Kumari Kandam civilisation —
budded there; and yet,

until five thousand years ago,
it smiled and flourished in the Indus expanse!

How did another name,
“Lemuria,” come to our dear Kumari Kandam?

Seeing that lemur monkeys,
which set out at night in search of food,

filled this continent everywhere —
and because, in Latin,
`lemur` was also a name for a ghost that roams by night —

it was by that name that a man called “Clater”
named the continent.

Or perhaps, changing form and raging beyond measure,
it drove a ghost-dance across great Kumari Kandam and shrank it,

shifting the land itself — and so, O Nili,
“Lemuria” alone became the one perfectly fitting name for you —

was it thus that the great professors of history
bestowed that name? I too have such a doubt, I would say!

<!-- scan 14 -->

For all this, I stand upon the historical path
clearly set out by Devaneya Pavanar,
poet-scholar of ethnic consciousness;

and with the help of the excellent book
*From Lemuria to Harappa*,

presented to me in the year ’75
by my dear friend and polyglot scholar R. Mathivanan;

with the writings of Rahul Sankrityayan as background,
and after studying many buried treasures of history —

I squeeze out their essence and offer
this continuing epic in the gait of poetry.
If those who hold differing views tell me by letter,
I shall rejoice; I shall praise them!

No change arrived merely because one woke the next morning.
For the human-ape kind to rise and stand,

not one or two Mahamagam spans —
crores upon crores of time had to pass.
Can an ape become human merely by holding a conference
and passing a resolution?

Evolutionary growth means, through many thousands of years,
keeping the form in the workshop and letting a chisel shape it — does it not?

Lemur monkeys had thirty-six teeth;
the monkey that appeared after them had thirty-four —

this is no tooth-count changed by human arithmetic;
even for that change in number,
how many centuries lay between!

Stone Age humans sprouted thirty-two teeth;
in later times, he says, the number would become only twenty-eight.

<!-- scan 15 -->

Between the ape-creature that stood propped on its tail,
learning to stand and walk upon its feet,

and becoming the human creature
that runs upon an Olympic track —

how many thousands of centuries ran by!
Is this not what they share with us under the names
“stagewise development”
and “evolutionary development”?

(Stagewise Development = Evolution)

## Translator notes

- Item 1 title `பொது உலகம்` is rendered **The Common World** so that `பொது` continues to resonate with the closing political refrain `உடைமை பொது - உரிமை பொது` rather than being reduced to merely “ordinary” or “general.”
- Item 1 preserves the source's play in `பல கோடி இடிகள் / ஒரு கோடியில்`: `கோடி` carries the numerical sense of “crore” in the first line while the second is rendered spatially as “corner.”
- The five-element sequence is translated as **water, land, air, outer space, fire** without replacing Kalaignar's ordering with a standardized philosophical list.
- Item 2 title `படிமுறை வளர்ச்சி` is rendered **Stagewise Development**, preserving the source's own final gloss `( படிமுறை வளர்ச்சி = Evolution )` rather than silently replacing the Tamil title with the English technical term at first occurrence.
- `பத்தரை மாற்று` is preserved as **ten-and-a-half-touch**, retaining the source's wordplay between `மாற்றுக் கருத்து` (“opposing/alternative view”) and the traditional `மாற்று` language of touch/purity grading rather than flattening the passage into plain exposition.
- The name printed as `கிளேட்டர்` is rendered **Clater** from the Tamil witness; it is not silently normalized to an outside historical spelling.
- `நீலி` is retained as **Nili**, preserving the source's ghost/demon allusion and the surrounding `பேயாட்டம்` imagery.
- `மாமங்க` is retained as **Mahamagam**, because the source uses the culturally specific long-cycle image for elapsed time.
- Devaneya Pavanar, R. Mathivanan, Rahul Sankrityayan, Kumari Kandam, Lemuria, the Indus expanse and Harappa remain explicit rather than being generalized.
- Historical/scientific numbers and claims are translated as Kalaignar states them; the English translation does not act as a fact-check or modernization layer.
