# One-Sided Love — 5

<!-- Reviewed English translation from the Tamil final-cleared canonical section. -->

<!-- scan_page: 39 -->

“I too have been watching you these past few days!
Girl, you have reached the state where even honey tastes bitter to you!

If I see this merely as the disorder of youth and pretend not to see,
while you stand at one pole and try to touch the other,

throwing yourself into it like a young calf that knows no fear,
that would be wrong —
and stopping it now is a mother’s duty!

Can a woman whose leg is lame and who walks with a limp
set out to pluck a kurinji flower from a mountain peak?

When the banner atop the great rolling chariot flutters,
and the Chola prince of Uraiyur goes toward the battlefront,
you ran and stood across his path to speak with him —
what will the town say if it has seen that?

If disgrace comes upon the family of a famous great merchant,
your father will have to stand ashamed
and answer before his kin!

We belong to the merchant community —
he was born to a lineage that rules the world!
Do not pine for a love beyond reach
even if ladder after ladder were stacked high,

<!-- scan_page: 40 -->

<!-- full-page illustration; no source caption -->

<!-- scan_page: 41 -->

O jewel-bearing maiden, my daughter!
Your two eyes are swollen like viral fish!

Sleep it away;
they will become sleek fish-eyes again, as before!”

Showing anger and pouring out affection in equal measure,
her mother gave this counsel and went away.
Then Nakkannai cried,
“If I must forget him,
I must give up my very life!”
and clung to Porkodi.

“Whether to forget him or give up your life —
we can think about that again.
First leave aside the bad and think only of the good!
All day long you keep thinking and thinking of Killi,
who is fighting the enemy host,
and swell your tears until they furrow your cheeks!

You suffer because your mother restrains you;
do not think she is breaking your heart!
The blocks placed before temple chariots that are already rolling —
are they meant to stop the chariot from moving?
No, no! They are there so it travels properly along the street!

When the reins of a galloping horse are pulled and loosened,
is it so the horse may stop and collapse in one place?

Think it through, graceful maiden!
Though she may wither in anger and rebuke you,
your mother only warns you

<!-- scan_page: 42 -->

so that your love does not become a waning moon!

If your love grows like the waxing moon
and then pours nectar-rain like the cool full moon,
O daughter of a noble house,
would your mother’s heart turn into iron
dug from the earth
instead of blessing you?

When, during pregnancy,
you made her tongue bitter with trouble,
that blessed mother cured the bitterness with sourness
and performed penance only to see your smile!
Even when you turned her beautiful banyan-leaf belly
into the shape of a trough
and threw her body into disorder,

she lived only to behold your form!
When, from within that jewelled belly,
you kicked this way and that,
she bore the pain
and spent a thousand thoughts
wondering what ornament she might clasp on those feet!

When you tossed your mother’s body about
like a straw in the wind,
then were born into this world, rolled over and cried,
she forgot all her suffering in the intoxication of joy!
And now —

<!-- scan_page: 43 -->

is it a sin for her to teach you something about the road you walk?
The mother who showered you with flowers, forehead-dots and rains of kisses,
counting the years until you would become a young woman —

is she such a fool that she does not know
a parrot may one day peck
at your kovai-red lips?

Your mother is not forbidding you to step into the river and bathe!
She is only saying: know the depth before you enter!
But if, until he returns,
you keep watch on the road,
lose sleep by night and day,
wither away,
and ruin your body,
will the parents who hold you as their very life agree to that?
Your friend begs you — listen at once!
Sleep! Wake refreshed! Be cheerful!
Uraiyur is not going to run away anywhere —
it is only going to end up inside your own room!”
Porkodi said, laughing.

“I am not staying awake!
I did sleep and wake —
listen to my story without fussing!
When I close my eyes and fall asleep,
it is still the thought of him
that presses upon me in dreams!”

“On the great mango tree standing tall in the middle of town,
a bat with thorn-like teeth
swings and sleeps upside down from a branch,

<!-- scan_page: 44 -->

and dreams of tasting the young tartness
of gooseberries in the dense forest
of the Chola king Alisi of Arcot;
just so, my dear,
a dream made me forget myself!
If only I could remain in that dream forever,
we two lovers would stay bound together in delight!
By the fault of my eyes I awoke —
and all that joy dissolved
like dew struck by sunlight!”

♦

Thus,
when Nakkannai told Porkodi the dream she had seen
and drooped in sorrow,
there came the victorious roar of the war-drum,
as though it would shatter the sky to dust!

“Victory for whom? For whom?”
Nakkannai flew out into the street!

★

> “In the tall mango tree within the town,
> the thorn-toothed bat,
> while hanging asleep from a high lovely branch,
> dreamed of the sour taste of gooseberries
> in the great forest of Alisi,
> of the Cholas victorious in war —
> so too, friend, that has passed away....”

(*Natrinai*, poem 87  
Poet: Nakkannaiyar)

### Source explanation

- `முள்எயிற்றுவாவல்` — a bat with thorn-like teeth.
- `மாஅத்த ஓங்கல் அம்சினை` — the tall, beautiful branch of a mango tree.
- `தூங்குதுயில்` — sleeping while hanging.
