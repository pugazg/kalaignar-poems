# One-Sided Love — 9

<!-- Reviewed English translation from the Tamil final-cleared canonical section. -->

<!-- scan_page: 74 -->

In Tittan’s Attani council hall at Uraiyur,
commanders stood with swords drawn from their sheaths.
Veera Venman Veliyan Tittan,
running his fingers along the keen edge of a sword,
laid out the methods of war:
how the warriors’ formation should be arranged;
how they must not fail to teach a fitting lesson
to Katti and Panan, advancing in fury;
and how, like a mountain-python hanging from a tree,
coiling around forest beasts,
trapping them,
breaking their bones joint by joint,
and only then making them its prey,
they must surround the enemy ground from many fronts
and pull the teeth of both Panan and Katti!

At that moment the minister bowed respectfully
before King Tittan and said:
“My king!
Do not underestimate the strength of the enemy.
Panan, skilled in wrestling,
is coming like a lion
to stand beside Palver Katti,
the chieftain of Ganga country!”

“How swollen with arrogance must King Katti have been
to ask me:
‘I shall give you famed jewels
worthy of the wealth of Uraiyur;

<!-- scan_page: 75 -->

give me your daughter in return’?
When the end called death comes to everyone,
what difference if it comes in youth
or comes in old age?
Whenever it comes,
this is my principle:
when life leaves the body without fail,
honour must not climb the funeral pyre beside it!”

Even after Tittan answered in words like tongues of flame,
the chief minister in the Attani hall
did not decide that the matter could end there.
He said to the king:
“Do not cast aside my view
as merely an eleventh among ten.
It would be wise to weigh carefully
the support Panan gives Palver Katti
before reaching a decision.

The poet Kalladanar speaks in a poem
of Panan’s strength.
King of the earth, hear it!

1. Though an enemy fort lies beyond the frontier,
he has the power to destroy it
and drive away its cows together with the bulls;
he bears in his hand a long spear
with a dark, ghee-anointed fitting,
and stands face to face against the foe,
a tiger-cub who never turns his back —
so Kalladanar marvels at Panan’s valour!

<!-- scan_page: 77 -->

2. Mazhuvanar praises him as
Palver Katti of Ganga country,
a great warrior who meets the enemy
with many kinds of spear-forces!
These are warriors who have earned songs of praise;
forgetting the truth that they are marching upon Uraiyur
would be dangerous, my king!” said the minister.

King Tittan did not sweep aside in anger
the counsel of a minister who spoke after deep thought.
With hesitation he nodded and asked:
“Do not bewilder me —
what other path is there to preserve honour?”

“When the great warrior Narkilli is butter
already held in your hand,
who would cry for the ghee called victory
on the battlefront where honour must be defended?
My king, say no word of refusal.
Send a palm-leaf message to your son Killi this very moment!”
said the minister.

“To Killi? To my son Killi?
Narkilli, who mocked me and went away —
will he agree to come merely because I call him?
He will not!
Minister, that effort is useless!” said Tittan.

“Killi has not been waiting for your summons, father!
So that the greatness of Uraiyur suffers no blemish,
so that the honour of the Chola line,
dearer even than life, is not lost,
and so that no disgrace comes upon my sister Aiyai,

<!-- scan_page: 78 -->

I have entered the country
without waiting for your order!
Give your command to your son at once.
I shall drive this bitter enemy clean away and return!”

With a face like the red-flamed sun
then descending through the evening sky,
Narkilli entered the day-court!

“My son!” cried Tittan,
running forward and clasping him to his chest.
He kissed the great warrior’s shoulders
and the crown of his head.
When Killi knelt and bowed at his father’s feet,
the whole assembly sprang to its feet
like a coin flicked into the air.

Light poured from the minister’s eyes.
Calling to Killi,
“O one born to rule Uraiyur!”
he blessed him:
“You have come to the battlefield
to repay Katti and Panan
with principal and a fine measure of interest!”

“Why should an army be needed
to defeat Panan of the enemy country?
Would not the mantras of a smoke-belching sacrificial pit be enough?
Why should warriors be needed
to bring down Katti with his many spear-forces?
Could he not be driven away by performing a sacrifice?”
Killi asked playfully, winking.

Tittan, his head hanging with shame,
held him close and said:
“Let what has happened remain what has happened, my son!
From now on,
let what happens be good!”
Then he turned toward the warriors in the hall.

<!-- scan_page: 79 -->

“Announce to the country
that Killi has returned from Amur!
Let the great drums of our Attani hall thunder
that the enemy will be shocked
and flee in fear!”

As Veera Venman Tittan’s command rang out,
the hide-covered instruments of the Attani hall thundered!
The walls of the Uraiyur palace thundered!
The war-drums in the inland camps thundered!

Though Killi had made himself ready for war,
he hurried toward the palace where his sister Aiyai lived,
wishing to see her —
unaware that Nakkannai had arrived there.

Meanwhile,
a warrior came and said,
“You have been summoned at once
to the Uraiyur palace.”
He left a chariot waiting at one side of the street entrance.

Because evening had fallen,
Kavarpentu sat before an oil-lamp writing a poem.
The warrior called her out
and said the matter was urgent.
In the rush,
without even asking why,
she rolled up the palm leaf on which she had been writing,
fastened a lock upon the house as its guard,
climbed into the chariot and set out.

<!-- scan_page: 80 -->

But the chariot did not go toward the Uraiyur palace.
It ran a full kaadam beyond the boundary of Uraiyur.
The nurse-mother who had boarded it
believing Tittan had summoned her
was startled.

Realizing that the tricksters Katti and Panan
had devised a plot
and dragged her into the enemy camp,
Kavarpentu erupted like a volcano:
“Whoever framed this disgraceful deed —
what definition of valour did he use?”

Panan came near her and said:
“Mother,
it is this Panan’s custom
to seize the cattle first
and only then fight the enemy!
You were the cow in the Uraiyur palace;
the son you raised as your own
has run away from the country!
And did not that incomparable beauty Aiyai too
grow up crawling in your lap?

That is why
we have seized this black cow called Kavarpentu!
Make your calf-like treasure-house of beauty, Aiyai,
marry this king called Katti.
After that,
we shall forgive and forget
our hostility toward Uraiyur!” said Panan.

“You think you can first bend the land
and then bend a woman and carry her away —

<!-- scan_page: 81 -->

but the people of Uraiyur are not all trees!
So forget your plan
to possess noble Aiyai!” she said.

3. “Is forgetting Aiyai,
a fruit no hand has plucked,
such an easy thing?
Paranar describes a maiden
whose beauty-spots are like the fine pollen
of a vengai tree blooming in clusters,
whose dark eyes shine,
and whose breasts rise like hills,
a treasure of beauty in bud.
I too must go in Paranar’s style
and sing Aiyai’s praises
until her face blushes!”

Hearing King Katti speak this way,
Kavarpentu covered her ears.
“Enemies!
Even if you kill me here in this camp,
so be it.
But know this:
I will not allow the life of peacock-maiden Aiyai
to be ruined!” she said.

“We shall see about that too!
Ho! Who is there?
Put her away in a pit!”
King Katti ordered.

> where enemies guard their feud,

### Source explanation

- `இகந்தனவாயினும்` — even though it is beyond the frontier / at a great distance.
- `பகைவர் ஓம்பினர் உறையும் கூழ்கெழு குறும்பு` — a food-rich fort where enemies dwell guarding their hostility.
- `குவை இமில் விடைய வேற்று ஆ ஒய்யும்` — seizing the enemy’s cows together with bulls whose humps are massed and high.
- `சுருணை` — a ferrule / metal fitting.
- `கனிகாழ்` — the spear-shaft rich or anointed with ghee.
- `எழா அப் பாணன்` — Panan who does not turn his back / retreat.
- `நாகிள வேங்கை` — a young vengai tree blooming in clusters.
- `நுண்பல் சுணங்கு` — many fine specks of flower-pollen.
