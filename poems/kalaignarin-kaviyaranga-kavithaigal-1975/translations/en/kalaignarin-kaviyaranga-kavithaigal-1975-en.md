## Item 01 — At the Revolutionary Poet's Poetry Gathering

## Chief Minister Kalaignar's Presiding Poem

<!-- scan_page: 46 -->

Poetry gathering held on 29-4-71
to mark the 80th birthday of
Revolutionary Poet Bharathidasan

Venue:— Puduvai

This morning, as I set out and climbed into the car,
my friend asked,
"Are you going to Puduvai? There is
‘kudi’ there—"

"Why, here in Tamil Nadu itself
there are so many *kudi*s—
are you a man who does not know that?" I said.
He,
eyes widening in surprise,
suddenly alert,
"What kudi? What kudi?"
he asked.

Karaikudi—Kannangudi—
Kundrakkudi—Kumarakudi,
Mangudi—Manakkudi—
Lalgudi—Aykudi—
Pothakkudi—Surakkudi—and
Kallakkudi, which brought me fame—
"Is that enough *kudi*?" I asked....
"Kallakkudi is all right, my friend—in Tamil Nadu,

<!-- scan_page: 47 -->

now that *kalla-kudi* has been stopped, when will the shop
open?" he asked......?

"I am going to Puduvai—I shall ask *parukkidam*
for the statistics—
then we can think and act—do not fly off just now!
I shall go and return," I said in farewell......

I climbed into the car and hurried on—on the way,
at the "Saaram" lodge, I wrote this—
if I delayed even a moment......
my friend would run right into the car—
would he let me write?.....
"Take! Put! Where?
Puduvai?"—would he not take my very life?....

A new distinction—a singular distinction—a great
*kudi*-distinction—a good town;
a great town where masters of poetic distinction
lived so that the very earth might gain distinction.

Once, in nineteen forty-six,
when this humble man too had taken
blows and kicks,
and life itself was about to leave,
the holy soil that embraced me—this soil of Puduvai—
today, to invite me with "Chief Minister, come,"
on behalf of our leadership, our Kazhagam,
there is another Chief Minister here.
When a breeze blows, is it not natural
that I remember an old storm!

Pazhaiya Perumal—the song-writing Puduvai Sivanurai—
and the cultured ministerial dignitaries—

<!-- scan_page: 48 -->

I praise and greet them, and begin
my poetry gathering......

Our Rajaram—
your Ramasamy Ramachandran,
Arumugam, Ramasamy, Subbiah—all these
are names of the Lord; so it is no wrong
to bow and ask for His grace!
Shanmugam too
is of that kind; Avvai
Shanmugam, by his very name,
as though he had brought peace to all the wars of kings;
may Puduvai Shanmugam too suppress the smoke and increase his fame—
I greet him.

Pride upon his face—
Tamil-love in a noble heart—the sound of the *Silambu*
resounding across the land—the beating battle-drum—
I bow to the elder M. Po. Si.

Praising the very Cuckoo of Puduvai,
I place these lines of my poem
before
his grave,
in his sacred presence.

Comrades who are there
to guard me always
as the eyelid guards the eye!
Heroes who pour out blood like oil
to protect Mother Tamil, cool as moonlight!
Warriors who, if told "Cut it and bring it,"
return with it bound!

<!-- scan_page: 49 -->

Friends who call and call me and delight,
as a poor man takes out the money in his box,
looks at it again and again, and rejoices!

Here in Tamil Nadu there is sea-singing Kadal Mallai......
stone-singing epics, sculptures singing beauty!
There is *nanjai* in Tanjai.... and there
a temple for Siva who drank *nanjai*, poison.....
There is Madurai, where they said red paddy could not be exhausted
if cattle were tied to thresh it, and elephants were tied to thresh......
There,
because the Fish once ruled, there is a flag marked with the fish;
for Mother Meenakshi too there is a temple that touches the sky—
there is Nellai, there is Salem.....
there is Kutralam, there is Kovai.....
and the moment they hear "Puduvai,"
there are very many
who take on a new intoxication—

Elders of the Pandya land
who add flavour with rich Tamil,
poets who have mounted the stage to thunder jewelled verse!
Accept my greetings;

"Let us dance! Let us sing the *pallu*!
Let us dance, saying we have attained
joyous freedom!"

The one who declared it before it had been attained,
the king who spoke with the tongue of a battle-lion,
and the younger one to him......
like a young Marudhu.....
the moment he began;

<!-- scan_page: 50 -->

"Wherever one looks—Sakti, brother!
The seven seas are her colour!"—
when Bharathi said, "Sing," the warrior sang at once,
a poet Bharathi praised as *paar ati theeran*......
The long name "Kanaka Subbu Rathinam," stretching a *katham* away,
he neatly made
"the servant of poet Bharathi"—Bharathidasan......
He entered the field to guard Tamil,
tying his *kacchu* snugly about him......

An upright bearing,
knitted brows,
the sound of a waterfall—a moustache
like a sheaf of paddy stalks;
in the craft of writing verse,
Bharathi had mastered *rasavatham*, alchemy—look at this man!
He had mastered *athirasavatham*......
*Athirasavatham* indeed......
so much sweetness in his song......

He takes us to the slopes of Sanjeevi Parvatham
and tells us to hear a musical conversation:

She: "I shall give you a hundred kisses without hurting."

He: "Out of desire, give me one kiss as an advance!"

"The little stream, the field raised behind a bund,
the good canal that stores river-water, all ordered in their place;
the shoulders that plough and plough the paddy-mud and bring forth yield—
whose shoulders are all those shoulders of abundant labour?....
*Viṇukkuṟiyā?*
An erupting volcano?

<!-- scan_page: 51 -->

Are these lines not enough
to garland the Revolutionary Poet,
to set him alongside the world?....

"If this country exists only for one man's need,
it is better that such a country perish."
Is this not the song of a whole human being? Hearing it,
the man who weeps is half a man......a deficient man......
a half-deficient man......

"The world is a dark room;
even the man who says caste exists—exists there, does he not?"
he begins,
and ends:
"A few who roll up whatever
falls into their hands."

He is a
*suruttu* poet—a smoke-
*suruttu* poet—this man
is a *purattu* poet;
a poet who turns every blind custom
upside down......

Noise, tumult, demonstration, procession—
is poetry meant only for these?
Will not the Cuckoo of Puduvai sing
so the world may live in peace?
(I shall tell you what he sang.)

"We shall make a new world;
we shall uproot the wicked war-making world."

The poet of peace calls out to the world......
Is it good that the world flourish through peace?
Or good that it wither through religious division?

<!-- scan_page: 52 -->

The poet heard the thought Gandhi cried aloud,
melting like wax within a dream.....

"The teller of lies is a coward—
the one who plunders another's land is a great bandicoot;
the one who thinks that all the people of the world
should prosper—that one is wise!"

A poet of ideas; a somewhat dark poet;
a poet of the age; a poet of all three times—
he is
a waterfall-song of savour for Tamil;
what a wonder, so perfectly found!

"Like the face of a maiden who sees a guest arrive,
the jasmine bud has blossomed—what a sight......
Whether it sees one it knows or one it does not,
to smile is simply its habit......"

Hear this now; a woman delightful to the eye
is dancing......and to delight the tongue,
Dasan sings.....

"Feet where anklets coo, treading out the rhythm;
her lotus-hand floating in the sky! And more—the fingers
that signal like fine buds of flawless champak;
those who see them behold stems of red coral!"

Another:

"Nothing at all blocks the throat;
her companion is caught in the reckoning of a little sulk;
the woman goes near and says, ‘Aththaan.’"

Humour that is his alone!
Flavour that belongs to his poetry alone!
The great poet speaks of him
as a poet made for simile......

<!-- scan_page: 53 -->

Can one give merely one or two examples......

1. "Like lime mixed in the water of a tub,
   darkness's
   binding came undone."

2. "When rice is ladled, let the ghee be poured
   so that it runs
   like the sap that oozes from a yielding banana-stem!"

Revolution—or peace?
Surging simile—or thought? The sweet honey
that gives the intoxication of Tamil?......
Whoever argues
that one surpasses another.....
I will not agree; I will say
one surpasses the other—and the other the one!

How much he gave!
In the dark house
he lit the family lamp;
he poured the nectar of music, and for it
won the Pandiyan prize... for us there is
the Kurinji tract too; while conducting the journal *Kuyil*;
poetry;
in the smile of beauty he captivated the Tamil movement......
he gave peace a good verdict......he
let the woman of poetry wither in memories of love;
with death's unexpected kiss he parted her......

Saying, "I shall see my teacher Bharathi,"
he flew away......
If the two poets met in the world of the dead,
what do you think
they would have spoken?....

<!-- scan_page: 54 -->

"People say that today
we are not in Tamil Nadu......
shall we too believe that?.....

‘When the words "the land of refined Tamil" are uttered—
sweet honey comes pouring into my ears.’

I waited, heartsore, longing greatly
to make this heart-stealing song a reality—today,
have I seen the marvel, brother?..... Anna
gave our fathers' land the name Tamil Nadu!
We live there!
Is it not true?"

To Bharathi, who walks about in ecstasy,
Pavendar offers a greeting and says:

"The pulp within ripe fruit—the juice
that rises in mature cane;
the honey upon the dewy flower—the taste
that rises in boiled syrup;
the milk a fine cow pours—the cool tender water
given by the coconut palm;
I shall call them sweet—yet Tamil,
I shall call my very life, behold!"

"That is what I wrote...
Today all Tamils have taken Tamil
as their own dear life......
and have set out to spread it
in all eight directions...
Ah! We did not die—we live in our motherland!"

"What you said is true," I told him!
Bharathi embraced his own Dasan,
and Bharathi laughed;
the two of them sang:

<!-- scan_page: 55 -->

"Our life and our wealth—
O conch, proclaim: imperishable Tamil!"

Let us too sing it......
let us behold an ascendant Tamil Nadu......
let us weed out the stinking quarrel of caste.....
let us hold festivals
for mighty Bharathi and his Dasan.
At the hour of festival......let us listen
to the ideas for which their festivals stand.

To Bharathi, who asked,
"Would one sell one's eyes to buy a painting?"
Dasan declared that
women are a cluster of unplucked guava-fruit.

"Even the horse shut away in a solitary stable
has permission to look out—
must the woman in the house
be made to blush and fear?......"
he asked......

"To attain love is the nature of life—
is it something a bed can confine?—Girl,
begin!" he said—"take pleasure,
take! take! take! in the mango-grove!"—
he rejoiced.

"Let us open a path for love,
and find a door that can close the path to the womb—
what fault is there in that?"
he said.

Do you know when?
Only now do we,
saying "family planning,"
"Do not become like Kuchela!"—

<!-- scan_page: 56 -->

preach it
on behalf of the government!......
The great poet had spoken the progressive thought
that child-control was needed
thirty years ago.....

A man, father to four or five children,
lay trembling on the front platform.
The woman—his wife—comes; here,
listen to the poet's gait,
flowing in flood like the Ganga:

"My dear wife came slowly to me.
‘Are you weary?’ she asked; with flower-soft hand
she touched me.
‘There is dew out in the street,’ she said; ‘Ah,’ I replied.
I understood her heart—what need had the woman
for words!

Ten months passed; one day at noon,
when the creditor came and tormented me,
while my whole mind shook thinking of my eldest daughter's fever,
an old woman came to me and said,
‘By Muthaalammai's grace, at an auspicious hour
your wife has borne a child.’
In the midst of infectious disease, poverty, creditors' harassment
beating on and on—child upon child!"

That
Bharathidasan—
"he gave woman her honour;
looking upon human beings trampled like earth,
he said: ‘Do not sell yourself!’"

<!-- scan_page: 57 -->

"Even if crores of enemies call and lure me,
I will not touch it,"
he sounded in a trumpet-voice......
In the heart of pitch-darkness
he stood as a good ray of light......
The man who lies idle without doing service,
he called a tortoise......
In cluster upon cluster
he lit chain-lamps of poetry......
As Bharathidasan, heir to Bharathi......

For the brave, for poets, and for every other
gifted genius—the lineage that stands behind Tamil itself
becomes the qualification......
Today,
wherever we look, it is the lineage of Bharathi;
we see descendants in Bharathidasan's path.

They string pearls of words......many poets
                                                (many poets)
string pearls of words;
even toothless poets......(Election)
When it comes to the vote, they would give it
to Bharathi and Bharathidasan without another thought......
Would the two poets
come to rule......
our kingdom of poetry!
They must yet come to rule......
already they rule our hearts......

Long may he live!
Long live his fame!

## Item 02 — At the Parambu Hill Festival for the Great Patron Pari

## At the Poetry Gathering
### Chief Minister Kalaignar's Presiding Poem

<!-- scan_page: 58 -->

5—5—71

At the poetry gathering held in connection with the
festival for the great patron Pari at Parambu Hill on 5—5—71,
Chief Minister Kalaignar Karunanidhi presided over the gathering
and sang this presiding poem.

For the *madhavar*, a share—
the woman, for Him, a share—
[*mādh-oru-bhāgan*—Lord Siva!]
for the *madhavar*, a share:
in the joy attained at this festival
by the head of the Kundrakkudi monastery,
is there not a share for the *madhavar* too?
For, once this is over,
Sivaganga is waiting to drag me away..........

Greetings to that *madhavar*........
Greetings to the *Anbilar* here........
If there are any who are without love for me,
greetings to them too—
to the great Marudhu-hearted Mayor Madurai Muthu,
and to comrade Thennarasu, the little Marudhu,
greetings—

They call you a Tamil renunciant—
I do not accept it—
whatever you may renounce,

<!-- scan_page: 59 -->

you declare that Tamil alone you will never renounce—
you are no renunciant from Tamil!
Revered Adigalar, accept my greetings—
This afternoon:
        without sleeping, in Madurai itself,
        I fashioned this poetry-gathering song........

There,
        as though the Sangam that once was in Madurai
        had risen and come here—
        O assembly of poets!
        I offer greetings to all of you—
amid the countless tasks I already have,
this is the poem that makes me *kirukku* away;

why summon me to scribble so,
to show the great love that has its own *kirukku* for me?—
the great ascetic *kirukkar*, Adigalar, called me here.
Leaving Chennai, which has learnt the trick
of milking without a calf at hand,
I have come to Parambu Hill,
where the flag of the Kundrakkudi Adheenam flies.

All the beauty that belongs to a hill comes through rain—
but the beauty belonging to this hill comes through King Pari;
even the taste within the jackfruit spring here,
I would call a gift bestowed by him.

In his realm, they say,
a cat would be the protection inside a mouse's burrow—
this patron would not place upon his own wealth
even the value given to weeds in a field—
the lord who gave his ornamented chariot
so a creeper might have a place to cling.

<!-- scan_page: 60 -->

We have read in stories:
the mischief-making Narada went before Kandavel and said,
"For your love, I know a maiden—Valli,
a beauty of form, a round moon!
Once you see her, you will devour her with your eyes."
And Murugan,
leaping up to find that peahen,
mounted his speckled peacock and set out.
Hear Avvaiyar's imagination!
Hear another imagination here.

After travelling a long distance
without finding the slender maiden,
he came near Parambu Hill and asked
an old toothless woman—
Murugan, whose moustache had not yet sprouted—
"Where is Valli?"
The old woman answered at length:
"Climb the hill! You may see her!"

Long-speared Murugan climbed,
rising over the hill with the speed of a spear.
"Where is Valli? Where is Valli?" he wandered.
To Vadivelan they said,
"Come......see......"
and showed him: the whole hill was full of *valli-kizhangu*,
Valli tubers!
What mischief in those roots......
"She tricked us by calling an edible tuber Valli!"
Murugan cried of the old woman,
and flew away disappointed.

But we may return without disappointment:
if one sees Pari's Parambu Hill,
fragrant with the flowers that Pari fostered—
so they say—
he bore a name praised by the world—and, for Tamil,

<!-- scan_page: 61 -->

he held a bond with Kapilar that raised its glory.
For the earthly king Pari,
the Tamil poet-king Kapilar was his life-friend.
As though two moons had split apart
and been born as maidens,
the great patron Pari
begot two exquisitely beautiful statues as daughters.

Many kings came seeking them as brides;
when they could not have them,
they brought armies against Parambu Hill and were defeated—
like bees bursting out when a honeycomb is broken,
the Three Crowned Kings
broke Parambu Hill's barrier
and drank away Pari's life.

When they came asking, there had been no giving of the women,
and war followed; afterward,
searching and grieving, asking kings in all four directions
until he was exhausted,
in the end,
        Pari's daughters became the wives
        of King Kari's sons,
        through Kapilar's effort!
His friend who had ruled a land—
once Kapilar, lord of Tamil verse,
had completed the singular duty he owed Pari,
that great poet who ruled the written leaf thought,
"Nothing now prevents me from joining my Pari,"
and, facing north, gave up his life.

Today, if a dying man has a friend,
there are dear friends who hurry
to put a stone upon his head!
And if a friend is defeated by an enemy,

<!-- scan_page: 62 -->

there are "good men" in our country
who betray him and become Ettappars—
does not the ripened friendship
of Kapilar and Pari teach good sense
even to those ungrateful ones?

In the Tamil tradition that gives
food-gifts, gold-gifts, vehicle-gifts—
nine kinds of gifts—
Pari gave three hundred villages to those who came;
I saw the greatness of his fame
moving like a roaming southern breeze.

It is not enough, O patrons,
merely to praise patrons with the mouth;
the hearts of those who praise
must themselves shine with generosity.
To say, "Only if there is a heap of gold stored up
can one give to the poor who come with folded hands"—
that is an excuse.

If two boiled tubers are all one has,
to give one to a poor person writhing in hunger
is itself a great gift.

This Tamil government here says:
begging for food is unworthy;
let beggars rise by learning
and mastering an occupation!
With that aim, planning to open many homes,
working to encourage useful labour,
I ask that your hearts pour forth in love.
You became Pari by giving a chariot to the mullai creeper—
give buttermilk to one poor child,
and you become half of him!

<!-- scan_page: 63 -->

To make the Pari festival splendid,
O poets in this poetry gathering,
pour forth all the glory of many-hued Tamil—

Mother Tamil's
        antiquity — *thonmai*
        simplicity — *elimmai*
        generosity — *vanmai*
        strength — *valimai*
        divinity — *iraimai*
        novelty — *pudhumai*—
how many kinds of *mai* there are to sing!
Many kissed this earth in the election!

All of them sing only "mai—mai"!
Russian *mai*—chemical *mai*—
those who, through a *mai* that does not respect
the people's verdict, deny the truth—*unmai*—of victory,
in this world—*immai*—and the next—*marumai*—
in this *mai* and that *mai*,
their fate is defeat forever......

"Mother Tamil's antiquity—*thonmai*,"
like tender coconut-water given by the coconut palm......!
A poet here will offer it......
I shall call him the young Chera poet......

Under the theme "Tamil's simplicity—*elimmai*,"
hear now a song bright with vision...
our dear friend Savarlal,
who composes nectar-like verse,
will rain poetry here!

<!-- scan_page: 64 -->

On the theme "generosity—*vanmai*,"
a poet of thought will sing—
I shall tell you one truth—*unmai*:
        he is a poet with two names, Muthu Sambandam—

On Mother Tamil's "strength—*valimai*,"
poet Ayyakkannu will sing!

Next—"divinity—*iraimai*"!
The singer—Singara Vadivelan!
You may have heard the song,
"O Singara Vela who lives at Sikkal—"
in the *sikkal*, the knot, of the theme
        "divinity in Tamil,"
we shall see his distinction.

Next, "novelty—*pudhumai*":
        Pari the king of the creeper...the fort's
        flag-king—the mullai
        creeper-king—he was
        a crowned king.
In this age when the republic flourishes,
there is another crowned king here......
        he is the poet Mudiyarasan.......
        soon,
        a crowned rule!

Afterword:
Take all the thoughts I spoke in the foreword
as though I have said them again in the conclusion!
Let us make the world understand
Tamil's "antiquity—*thonmai*"—

<!-- scan_page: 65 -->

let us use its "simplicity—*elimmai*"
to feed Tamil knowledge to everyone—
let us guard Tamil wealth
from falling into generosity-less violence;
let us proclaim that our sweet Tamil
is a "spear" to our enemies......
        let us show its strength—*valimai*!
Rather than seek divinity—*iraimai* only in Tamil,
let us say it is finer to see it
in the smile of the poor Tamil!
When Tamil spreads
as the language of instruction,
we too shall learn in Tamil
the journey to the milk-white moon
and create many "novelties—*pudhumai*"!

                                                Greetings.

## Item 04 — “Chief Minister Kalaignar's Reply Poem”

<!-- scan_page: 67 -->

Sung at the unveiling of Periyar's statue
in Erode on September 17.

Because Chakravarthi Rajaji, the great sage,
freely penned a poem called "The Arrack Era"
in the magazine *Kalki*,
all sorts of Swatantrites rejoice.
Those who drank from "bar to bar,"
having obtained permits,
now speak the very opposite—like the Buddha!

"The heart is diseased,"
"I need two units,"
"Here, look at the doctor's certificate"—
the whole town knows the drinking story
of the Swatantrites here!

For every rich daytime masquerader,
a permit is needed to buy fragrant liquor!
One justice for show—
another justice for the poor sinner:
a new justice of the *Panchanga Shastra*!

When the suffering poor drinker
drank varnish and died—
and when wealthy gentlemen,
struck by intoxication,
downed "whisky" and rum—
did the stench not pierce
even *Neḷrōji* Street?
Did the country itself not see?

Today, seeing our friend Rajaji write
"the profound scholar Anna,"
I was truly overcome, sir!

There was another day:

<!-- scan_page: 68 -->

when the government textbook about Anna appeared,
did he forget how happily he wrote,
"Does everyone who dies at a young age
become an incarnate being?"

In Orissa, under his party's rule,
a flood of liquor ran—
why then did his heart
go barren?

He praises me as
"a blessed treasure of compassion"—why?
I bow to him!

The boon was not given by him;
the people of the country
gave me their loving hand!
I received a boon—
I received strength from the heart
given by Periyar and Anna!

Even now I say it—listen!
If a law is made
to bring complete prohibition
throughout India,
I shall bow my head, fold my hands,
stand praising it as an excellent act,
and hurry to put it into effect.
Even after that,
I shall make a rule
that no longer permits some
to drink in the dark
saying, "heart disease"!

If you need the full details,
I shall give the list later:

<!-- source_separator: horizontal-rule -->
