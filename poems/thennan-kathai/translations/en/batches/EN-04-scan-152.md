## English translation

<!-- source scan 152 — direct continuation from scan 151 -->

Crying, “The enemy is gone!” with a burst of laughter,
he turned back along the road.
From hiding, the Aryans loosed a single arrow.
He cut away the arrow that struck his chest,
raised his sword, turned, and cried,
“Who is the coward?”

Rama's band, trapped with no way to run,
will be reduced to cotton, with not even flesh left to search for!...
My land is sugarcane, man! To you
who have plunged it into misery—
this sweep of the sword is lightning, man!—
so saying, he whirled it,
the famed one who could encircle
and face even a cyclone!

Vibhishana, tasting the Aryan fruit,
saw that now the situation was dire!
A spear in his hand!... He hurled it
toward his elder brother's back!
That spear, flying from concealment,
pierced his mountain-rock back, drove through,
and thrust its head out through his chest—
tears poured!
Like the Himalaya toppling,
Ravana fell!

“The one who killed me is my younger brother,
with palmyra-tree shoulders—in his hands
he bears the Brahmin's feet—
there is medicine to save my life!—but
hearing that my own brother
fought from hiding, I die.”

Hearing this, the people of Lanka
became as though dead!

★

## Translator review notes

1. **Batch boundary:** scan 152 only. No wording from scans 145–151 is repeated except the minimal narrative continuity implied by the opening.
2. **151→152 continuity:** the opening is treated as the immediate continuation of EN-03's `the villains disappeared`; no false new episode heading or narrative reset is inserted.
3. **User-controlled lexical source:** the translation follows the corrected scan-152 forms, including `கணை வெட்டித் தள்ளி`, `ஓடுதற்கு`, `சூறாவளி`, `எனைக் கொன்றவன்`, `பனைமரத்`, and `தாளுடையான்`. Superseded visual readings are not restored.
4. **`ஓரம்பு`:** rendered `a single arrow`, keeping the compact one-arrow sense without importing outside narrative detail.
5. **`பஞ்சாய்ப் போகும்`:** rendered `will be reduced to cotton`; the violent compressed image is retained rather than explained away.
6. **`கன்னலடா எந்தன் நாடு`:** rendered literally as `My land is sugarcane, man!`; the unusual metaphor is preserved rather than normalized into sweetness or fertility prose.
7. **`வாள்வீச்சு மின்னலடா`:** rendered `this sweep of the sword is lightning, man!`, preserving the direct boast and image.
8. **`சூறாவளி தனையும் சூழ்ந்தெதிர்க்கும்`:** rendered conservatively as `could encircle / and face even a cyclone`; no extra heroic attribute is added.
9. **Vibhishana:** the Gate A–C standard English form `Vibhishana` is retained.
10. **`ஆரியக் கனி சுவைக்கும்`:** rendered `tasting the Aryan fruit`, retaining the source metaphor rather than paraphrasing allegiance.
11. **Spear image:** `மலைப்பாறை முதுகு` remains `mountain-rock back`; the following `கண்ணீர் கொட்டும்` is left as `tears poured!` so the English does not over-specify the grammatical subject of the compressed Tamil image.
12. **`பார்ப்பனரின் தாளுடையான்`:** rendered `he bears the Brahmin's feet`, retaining the accusatory bodily image without explanatory interpolation.
13. **Final speech:** Ravana's quoted final speech remains self-contained and closes before the Lanka-reaction couplet.
14. **Terminal structure:** the final `★` is retained and no translation text follows it inside the poem body.
15. **Page-151 editorial omission:** EN-04 contains no scan-151 source text and does not restore or allude to the deliberately omitted term.
