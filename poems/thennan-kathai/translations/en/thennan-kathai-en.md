# தென்னவன் காதை — English Translation

Short beards, stubby beards, long beards, white beards—
beards of every such kind......
Spreading out a bazaar of Puranas, tasting the sour old brew,
intoxicated, dancing—the throng of earth-god priests...
At the edge of a realm once without stain or scar,
slaughtering cattle, goats, human beings,
piling mounds of corpses for sacrifice!
By an Aryan invasion?... No...
By swaying waists—by sidelong glances—by the rishis'
draught of sensual nectar—the Southland had gone dark;
there, like a lamp newly lit,

rose the king of Lanka!
To tell his story... every sinew
would leap, ayyo! He who won fame in all eight directions,
the king of southern Lanka, forefather of the Dravidian people!
Our grandsire, who held fast to ancient Tamil.

★

Lanka, a doll within the eye.
The deep seas, its dark pupil! The great warrior
who brought glory to the Tamil land.
A lion in form, gold at heart! He lived declaring
that none could equal him in the music of the yaazh—
a man of sacred Yalpanam, a mountain-like Tamil!
His companion, flower-tressed, where bees come to rest,
Mandodari—jewel of womanhood—
her speech could make the veena answer back!...
Ravana's yaazh-music was defeated only
before the fruit-sweet words of his chaste wife
in their bedchamber!
As eyelids are doors that guard beautiful eyes,
so was Ravana to Lanka!
And she to him!
In the forests, along the lines of our borders, the Aryans,
seeking sacrifices for their gods—this cannot be,

this cruelty!—he drew his curved sword!
Calls from the forest hermitages kissed
his feet; and the laughter of maidens in their prime—
a split palmyra spathe!
"Would I touch the Aryan scorpion?"—
that bull raised his voice in defiance!....
Every forest where jackals lurk, every land
where deceivers roam—
guard! guard! Against those forces,
Ravana—the rooster that tells the hour! the rooster!

★

Tamil queen Muthunagai, Ravana's sister,
blew the conch in defiance
of the ascetics of Dandakaranya!
She sprang forth like a tigress, a maiden
of a warrior clan;
in the eyes of the Veda-chanters
flowed the Ganga!
They had understood that the Tamils
could be defeated only by treachery, by intrigue—
not by strength of shoulder.

And so Rama the king set out,
with the younger one, Lakshmana,
and his wife Sita
following behind.
Bent on the crown after bringing ruin upon his brother,
reviled through the land as a betrayer of trust,
that wretch called Rama had taken to the forest.
One day,
while wandering by the forest edge, he saw
Muthunagai, like a stalk of sugarcane,
asleep from weariness
beside a grove of fruit trees.
Lust had robbed the scoundrel of reason.
The pure woman slept, her lotus-flower eyes closed;
wickedly, to violate her,
he went near with his brother at his side.
Sita Devi—an accomplice standing guard!
Muthunagai, who had slept without a guard,
rose blazing to defend the fire of her chastity....

Like a fruit tree, one woman alone—two monkeys!
What could she do?
As the price of her chastity, she lost a hand! She lost her nose!
Muthunagai lost the teeth that poured out pearly laughter!
Alas, she lost a breast—yet
she did not lose the steadfastness of chastity!
Even if she lost her head, a Tamil woman who would guard her honour—
Ravana's younger sister!

★

He heard the ear-searing news
that his guileless sister had been made Surpanakha—
he looked as though a wound had opened in his eye.
The king of the earth who rules the seas—
is he not Muthunagai's elder brother?
Even if no wider bond of kinship existed, what of it?
Is she not a citizen of Lanka?
He rose for revenge—his eyes storing red
like the young morning sun...
Hearing that the king was coming,
the beautiful-eyed Sita Devi,

wanted a winsome deer
and sent her husband away!
“While your elder brother is gone, little brother,
are you thinking of casting a net for your sister-in-law?
Go after the elder one, you too—she drove him off,
and waited for the Southerner to come.
The warrior came like a chariot, fire flying from his eyes;
he saw Sita come to the street entrance,
pouring honeyed words:
‘The elder and the younger have both run off—
you, the man suited to me, have come—
I give myself to you, she said, and embraced him,
Sita Devi of the bangles,
like a spreading jasmine vine upon Ravana's broad shoulders!
You are the wife of the man
who brought harm to my sister, dearer than life!
Rather than give your body to crows, vultures,
and lustful bears—
I will not even allow a hand to touch you.
Board the ship, he said.

The wife of a man born to a lineage
that had put honour aboard a ship—
saying that he should accept her body as a gift,
thinking that by leaning upon his strong chest
she would somehow gain a boon—
climbed aboard the vessel and stood
behind the lion of majestic gait,
like a she-jackal!

★

“He will take the captive Sita Devi,
seat her upon the throne and make her crowned queen;
if Sita bears a son,
he too will have a share in Lanka's sceptre;
a land for the Brahmins, like palmyra fruit—
if it comes without labour,
what harm if ten thousand Sitas are lost!”

But things did not happen as the Aryans had imagined.
The king of Lanka arranged a prison
for Sita, the sleight-of-hand trickster!
As for that crowd proclaiming “Veda” and “sacrifice”...
he said: break their rib-bones
as if chopping firewood, and come!

The victorious king had a brother, Vibhishana—
a parasite upon a vengai tree,
waiting to bring down his elder brother and rule.
Into the hand of a fifth-column force called Hanuman
he placed a firebrand and said,
destroy the great city of Lanka ....

Across southern Lanka—a forest of fire, a forest of corpses!
Through the cooperation of corrupt Vibhishana,
the foxes—the Aryans—found a way
for their clan to flourish!

The cruel one who, through the stratagems of darbha grass,
killed his elder brother Vali, the Tamil warrior.

Sugriva—the one who asks a price for self-respect—
fell at the feet of the eye-rolling Aryans.

★

That treasure who had placed his trust in his younger brother,
the lion who had ruled while the whole earth trembled,
saw the enemy armies coming over the waves of the sea.
My own brother, Vibhishana! Come, set out with me,
he said!
Do not play with a cobra raising its hood—
you will quake like a debtor's heart.

Elder brother, come; let us go together and surrender,
he said—and stood singing the praises
of the Aryans who display jars of liquor.
The Southerner saw his younger brother's monkey-show;
his eyes became pools:
"You who came to destroy the clan!
Handle of the axe!.... Thank me
for not cutting out and flinging away your cruel tongue.
Go!"
And he strode toward the battlefield!

★

A noble one steeped in the same womb—
mighty in the sleep of thought, the handsome
younger brother called Kumbhakarna—
like the sword worn at his elder brother's waist,
sprang after him toward the place where armies clashed!

The youngest brother came and stood before him.
To defeat the Aryans who live with the aidai tied,
gathering an army is useless—it will end in ruin, he said!
I am not the brother who lives
by setting up a shop of treachery.
Birthplace of ruin, get moving!—
and he stormed away like a flood that had burst its sluice.

★

“My dear warriors! Comrades rising high!
I am Ravana, who rules
this southern Lanka today—

men of debased trades—men stripped of culture—
those who live by deceit—
and those who cling to them:
I have donned the array of battle
to destroy them all in half a second!
My tiger-like friends!

I had thought the wicked priests had no army!
Making maidens their encampment,
they have massed every force they need.
With young maidens of kovai-fruit lips,
guava cheeks, bodies like blankets,
all the kings of the race
have leaned toward the rishis!
The sages' wife, showing a full-moon face,
has tormented crowned kings—the forts
where the Tamil flag once flew
have all become monasteries for Aryans
with their folds held out—alas!!

Rama, who schemed harm against his dear younger brother
and ran to the forest;
base Sugriva, thick-skinned beyond even a buffalo,
his companion—the one who brought death to his elder brother!
And another—my own brother,
who, with the traitor called Hanuman,
set fire to Lanka!
If all of these stand against me,
must there not be some mystery in this?

They say I must give Sita a son,
and then hand Lanka's sceptre to that very son—
the evil company of Rama
lays down conditions like firebrands!
My consort, a golden maiden like a ball of flowers—
fresh Tamil beyond even poets who weave verse—
my great queen, a cool moon! a block of diamond!

Why should the bard who plays that yaazh
need a ruined veena called Sita?
Before long I will open the way
for our own land to touch the Aryans' feet and bow!
Warriors who show your valour through your eyes!
Swear upon the language and set forth—
let us return after taking vengeance on the porcupines!”

Thus thundered he of hill-like shoulders!
Everyone roared on hearing it!...
In the battlefield smelling of slaughter,
the armies on both sides spill blood!
Those who died with heads smashed,
those whose guts were torn open—
Tamils—one clan!

Hiding by hills, hiding by trees,
the enemy Rama fought like a rat!
Such a method has never been seen in archery—
Take up the bow and arrow, man!—
Kumbhakarna rose in fury!... Seeing him,
To Rama, bounding away, he cried:
Coward, turn back!
“Is life sugarcane?”—he stood roaring like an elephant!

Before he had finished speaking!...
Lakshmana too
rained a volley into his back.

The pure Tamil who had led the army
saw his beloved younger brother
fallen like an elephant!

With flame flying through every thought, every atom,
Cats!... stand still! he cried,
and nocked... the war-arrow!
Was it rain pouring from cool clouds?
Was it thunder shaking in summer?
He took on storm-speed—a new speed!
Like a tiger struck by a spear—like an arrow set free—
like a flood whose dam had broken—the man
whose chest had taken a thousand sword-blows fought!....
With heels striking their napes,
the villains disappeared.

Crying, “The enemy is gone!” with a burst of laughter,
he turned back along the road.
From hiding, the Aryans loosed a single arrow.
He cut away the arrow that struck his chest,
raised his sword, turned, and cried,
“Who is the coward?”

Rama's band, trapped with no way to run,
will be reduced to cotton, with not even flesh left to search for!...
My land is sugarcane, man! To you
who have plunged it into misery—
this sweep of the sword is lightning, man!—
so saying, he whirled it,
the famed one who could encircle
and face even a cyclone!

Vibhishana, tasting the Aryan fruit,
saw that now the situation was dire!
A spear in his hand!... He hurled it
toward his elder brother's back!
That spear, flying from concealment,
pierced his mountain-rock back, drove through,
and thrust its head out through his chest—
tears poured!
Like the Himalaya toppling,
Ravana fell!

“The one who killed me is my younger brother,
with palmyra-tree shoulders—in his hands
he bears the Brahmin's feet—
there is medicine to save my life!—but
hearing that my own brother
fought from hiding, I die.”

Hearing this, the people of Lanka
became as though dead!
