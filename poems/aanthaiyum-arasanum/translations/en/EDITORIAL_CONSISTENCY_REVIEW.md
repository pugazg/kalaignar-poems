## Editorial decisions reviewed

### Title / named referent

**Andhai and the King!** is retained. Within the poem, Andhai/Andhaiyar is the named poet who becomes Pisirandhaiyar; translating the title as “The Owl and the King” would replace a personal-name referent with a common-noun reading unsupported by this poem's narrative.

### Place-name wordplay

**Kozhiyur / Uraiyur** are retained as names. The `உறை` / sword-sheath wordplay is represented in the verse as `urai—its sheath` rather than replacing the place name with explanatory prose.

### Names and source variation

- `கோப்பெருஞ்சோழன்` → **Kopperuncholan**;
- `பிசிராந்தையார்` / `ஆந்தையார்` → **Pisirandhaiyar / Andhaiyar**;
- `புல்லாற்றூர் எயிற்றியனார்` → **Pullarrur Eyitriyanar**;
- scan-20 `புல்லலூர் எயிற்றியனார்` → **Pullalur Eyitriyanar**.

The Pullarrur/Pullalur difference is intentionally visible because the FINAL-CLEARED Tamil source preserves that edition-level variation.

### Literary / historical terminology

- `புறப்பாட்டு` → **Puram songs**;
- `முப்பாலும்` → **the Three Parts**, keeping the source's compressed reference without asserting an unprinted expansion;
- `வடக்கிருத்தல்` → **sitting facing north / northern fast**, with the poem's own explanation retained in the English body;
- `நடுகல்` → **hero-stone**, preserving the commemorative stone sense;
- `பூவரசு` → **Poovarasu**, avoiding an unsupported editorial choice between possible referents.
