# Lend Me Your Heart, Anna

This English poem is assembled only from the **reviewed translation verse** in `batches/batch-01.md` through `batches/batch-05.md`. Batch/source boundaries remain traceable through hidden comments. The publication/context note on scan 13, scan 26 printer imprint, and scans 27–28 back matter are outside the poem body.

<!-- batch 01 / scans 13-15 -->

Softer than the softness of a flower-petal,
a sacred heart — a loving heart —
a mother's heart that gathers one close! His
flower-petals speak Tamil —
mango, jackfruit, banana — the three fruits themselves are outdone —
his eye-flowers turn spear, turn sword
if even one harm comes to Mother Tamil!

Though his foot-flowers wilt, his arduous journey does not halt;
his hand-flowers clasp together the younger brothers, the Kazhagam!
Those very flowers forgive the enemies
and make their heads bow in shame, like ripened paddy!
Let democracy's flower sway,
let socialism's flower give fragrance,
with mother-tongue Tamil itself as life's garden —
O breeze that comes dancing,
O flower-crown! O crown of fame! In the heaps of blessings
that come seeking you — each day
I shall fly as the bee that comes singing.
For you I shall give up myself; for the song
my one crore Tamil youths stood singing,
you were the Great Leader..

Listen as I sing the greatness
of the man of action, the warrior,
whose Tamil fervour, packed into the drum,
made the precious life of youth swell!
The wounds dealt by the *tangu sani vēl* —
soothing them with the fomentation called love,
his red-fruit mouth applying medicine —
listen as I sing his nobility!
Where is that battle-mighty arm; where that jewelled chest?
Where, where the patron's shoulders
that would never show their back, whoever came upon the battlefront? —
hear how he asked the younger brothers! Hear! Hear!
While Kanchi stood, Kalinga trembled —
O maidens joined in love — *kazhar-chchenni* —
while Kanchi stood, Kalinga was lost:
"Open — let the war of the field be sung!"

To wake the love-dazed women from sleep
he set himself to action and sang:
a parani of the battlefield.. the *Kalingattu Parani*,
a praise-parani of immeasurable valour — I
mounted a parani of poetry, a Kanchipuram Parani,
and stood singing then;
today
I sing a parani of grief....!

As meaning within a poem, as sweetness in sugarcane,
as light in a ray, as flower in a grove,
as coolness in the moon, as richness upon the earth,
as the babbling tongue of the roaming waterfall,
as music mounting the wandering wind,

as dancing foam upon the wave-risen sea —
the poet in whom the fragrance of art gathers and rises,
Anna, chief among poets — when I sing his sacred praise,
ah! the tears the Earth-maiden pours!
A flood!
A flood!
A mighty flood!
In nineteen hundred and thirty-eight,
if a few sowed the seed
for a language revolution to arise in the homeland:
the peerless chief-warrior of the leader of Erode —
our Anna entered the field, wearing Kanchi!

Withering in prison for two, three months,
giving Tamil as the staff that would bear us,
by those rare three letters he became Anna...
That day, in fifty-two,
in Chennai, I told you —
do you remember?

<!-- batch 02 / scans 16-19 -->

There is a distinction in three letters,
the fragrance of *Muttamil* is there..
Three kings, three flags, three fruits —
Tamils who made the three drums resound — in the Tamil they lived,
*vāzhvu* — “life” — three letters;
*anbu* — “love”, the ground of that life — three letters..
*arivu* — “knowledge”, the companion of love — three letters;
*kādhal* — “love” that rises among the knowing — three letters...
the fierce *vīram* — “valour” lovers praised — three letters...
*kaḷam* — “the field” that brings forth valour — three letters...
*veṟṟi* — “victory” seen by entering the field — three letters;
and serene Anna, who spurs us all toward that victory —
Anna — three letters:
I told you, know this!

Tamil's roar to every direction,
a rain of words everywhere;
for those without means, those without a way,
seeking a plan by which they may live,
he circles like a bee, mounting the platform!
And when Anna writes,
every page, every home — Tamil;
the whole land... fragrant there with newness...
“Ēdā thambi! Ettā pēnā....”
“Sharpen not the knife — sharpen the mind.”
A casket of thought! A box of camphor!
“A corpse upon a tree-branch;
a spear in a burnt wound!”

Could we ever forget those heart-capturing words?
    “The jobless by the roadside —
    in the hearts of the jobless, perverse thoughts —
    O King! That is the sign of the times!”
    To whom but Anna could come this gait of grace?
    This gait of reason?
    “The line rose; the hill sank” —
    was that not an editorial Tamil Nadu will never forget!
    “If this army falls, what army will win?”
    “He who has a younger brother fears no army!”
    Though the *Manonmani* text uttered such peerless lines —
    was it not Anna who engraved them
    upon our hearts!

“The jasmine in another man's garden too has fragrance” —
what beauty, teaching the civility of politics!
“Let us forget — let us forgive” —
what greatness, bearing an opponent's taunt!
    Who taught us this?
    As one raises a wall before painting the picture,
    Anna said it was good to conduct politics
        with grace and civility —
        forgetting that,
    if four or five men leapt away,
    Anna saw them — and laughed!
Because one mother's womb could not bear
to give birth to everyone,

he called them, “You younger brothers,
born of separate mothers!”
He showed love sweet as fruit,
as rock-candy, as honey-syrup,
and embraced them like a bee settling on a dew-flower.
    Sethu Pillai, master of words,
    Somasundara Bharathi too — in a war of words
    he left them spellbound — poor souls; caught them!
The Justice Party, behind the walls of a towering fort —
a party of the moneyed caste — must not remain so:
    by that principle
he brought it into the street!
“Cast away the white men's titles!” —
at the Salem conference he moved the resolution.
    Unable to withstand
    Anna's battle-form,
those without principle fell,
    like blind men without a staff!
Resolute Anna brought the resolution
to change the name to *Dravidar Kazhagam*
at the conference where warriors gathered!

When Mahatma Gandhi, by the path of righteousness,
removed British rule,
rejecting Periyar's declaration of it as a day of sorrow,
Anna began his battle!
“This is a day of joy! This is a sweet day!
Indians reduced to bone and skin,
through the grace of loving Gandhi, today

have risen, cutting the shackles of slavery!
Let us lift the drum of freedom and make it thunder!”
So Anna said, that very day...
From that day, in the bond between Anna and Ayya,
    a scratch appeared;
in the year forty-nine it turned into separation.
Like the full moon breaking out through cloud,
he made the *Munnetra Kazhagam* bud forth!
Unable to remain together with the Leader,
    when he came out
    with the majority of comrades,
our unshaken Anna said that day —

it —
it feels, *amma*, as though he said it only yesterday!
“The Munnetra Kazhagam has no Leader —
the chair where my Leader sat will remain empty!
Someday he — the Leader — will come and sit in it..
Until then, I shall wait,” he said.
Like a flower-garden in bloom
    shedding its morning dew,
the waiting crowd that heard him
    shed tears.
“O drops of tears! O pupils of the nation's eye!” he called them,

and marshalled them to shed red blood!
Has anyone ever heard of such a thing?

<!-- batch 03 / scans 20-21 -->

Has anyone ever spoken of such a thing?
Within eighteen years, a movement —
like a tiger crouching, then springing — came to rule the land?
Not even two years had passed since my Anna took the reins —
and many noble deeds had taken effect!
To give uplift to all the farmers of the plough,
to heap up for them a life of beauty — he set himself to the task!
The President of the Republic, of soaring renown,
who came to inaugurate the World Tamil Conference,
“I have seen a matchless sight, I have seen majesty;
in all my life I have never seen anything like this,”
said in praise — for the land to applaud — of Anna's glory!
    Brahmananda Reddy of Andhra too:
    “Ah! You are the true Gandhian himself!” —
    he adored him thus — before even a year had passed!

    “You are making prohibition rigorous; in many
    states they have washed their hands of it and tied pots upon the trees!
You are the Mahatma's own manifestation.”
Giri — does that not mean a mountain? — over that
whole mountain our Anna became the cloud!
That is the meaning of the greeting V. V. Giri gave.
A world-renowned great scholar stood praising him — that day,
the one who had stood as the summit of the South —
what did he say
of Anna?

The scholar of ancient Tamil, the man of Pallavaram —
stood entranced by Anna's ancient-Tamil speech —
a thousand were the words of praise that Maraimalai gave.
Pasumalai Bharathi — Pandyan in bearing — him
the whole land calls *Navalar* — he said:
“To guard the Tamil land fallen through the *sazhakkar*,
a leader without stain in his heart has been found!”
Thus did he speak Anna's greatness that day!

Two thousand years ago too, as young as she is today,
Mother Tamil — though, so that she might live
for years upon years, a hundred-thousand crores, say,
she gave birth to poets who made a thousand books —
our mother, goddess of the arts,
gave birth to Valluvar as her foremost son.

    If flower — the lotus alone;
    if king — Karikalan;
    if book — the *Tirukkural* alone:
    the book of virtue praised in such words
    he gave; and all humankind rejoiced!
Beside mountains that strike the sky —
like poison-trees rising, whether our eyes see them or not —
in the life of a cultivated Tamil people,
all those doctrines that wounded the back
sprang up there!
In the Tamil land where the Kural rang out, seeing no castes,
midway there came, *amma*, castes by the many crores!

<!-- batch 04 / scans 22-23 -->

Sanctuaries fragrant with knowledge disappeared;
they came to see temples where a thousand gods dwell.

People with no sense of language, creatures of mere mouth-sense —
whether Tamil died or lived, what was that to them?
In the great land where the path of virtue had stood as the goal,
they sowed other paths; they heaped up error.
Like a field gone without rain, the Tamil heart changed.
Envy — desire — anger — hurtful speech:
as though no work could be done without these four,
some Tamils carried on!
“There is no world for those without wealth” —
in the land that had amassed wealth and flourished,
today darkness thickened; misery planted its flag there.
Mother Tamil withered — a song of sorrow
Mother Tamil sang; in scorching fire
Mother Tamil danced...
She ran.. she ran.. not one path could she find!
Leaving the town of earth, he lives in the town of fame —
the great sovereign of the town of poetry, the Master of the Kural!
Going there, she said, “Precious son!
I have come with one request.”
    “What is it, *amma*?” asked the Kural-one.
“In Tamil Nadu, wherever one digs, gold comes forth;
you must be born again,” she said.

    “To dig for gold?” he asked.
    “To make Tamil hearts, all Tamil life,

gold,” she said.
“What has happened today?” he asked.
“Danger has come to the mountain-like language,” she said;
“The poor man has no hut to go to,” he said;
“Not knowing how to raise the rod sternly and cast it gently,
the killing staff has risen,” she said; and she told him too
of those calf-like in intellect talking useless tales.
Wiping her weeping eyes,
nectar-tongued Valluvar asked,
“Amma, where shall I be born?”
She kissed the crown of the son who bowed,
and with arms like the hanging roots of a banyan.. embraced him.
“A ploughed field shall not dry for want of seedlings

anymore” — thinking so,
like a farmer whose heart rejoices,
her whole heart swelled and leapt;
to pour gruel into the parched stomach —
“O rock-candy! O honey-syrup! O Tirukkural!
    Be born in Kanchi!” she said.
He was born as our Anna;
    as the king of reason,
as the breeze from Pothigai mountain,
as the fresh fragrance
    of Tamil feeling budding into flower,
as the meaning within a *pathigam* —

<!-- batch 05 / scans 24-26 -->

as the *purappāṭṭu* of the ancient Tamils,
Anna came and rose — like a ray
rising on the eastern horizon —
to sing his fame,
I too, the younger brother he raised,
taking the Tamil he gave,
have come here — this is the truth —
They call him Leader; they call him philosopher;
they call him actor; they call him king of drama;
they say he possessed the power of speech and the savour of writing.

They call him man; they call him ruby; they call him Minister of the State.
They call him mother; they call him guardian of precious speech;
they call him politician — all of these,
if there is no time to say them one by one — out of the love in the heart,
let them call him by the single word
“Anna” — for so
his mother gave him the name too.
To honour that lineage of mothers:
a statue for Avvai;
a statue for Kannagi, who nurtured virtue;
a statue for Bharathi of the unbending heart,
and for Bharathidasan of the unbowing head;

a statue for Veerama Munivar;
a statue for Caldwell and Pope;
a statue for Kambar;

a statue for the Tamil who bravely sailed the ship;
a statue for Tiruvalluvar, to spread the Kural to all eight directions.
By setting up ten statues — so the world might know
the attachment Anna held for the Tamil people;
when a statue for that Anna
was set up in Chennai..
he stood with only his index finger raised..
We thought: our Anna is giving an order.
Alas! Only today do we understand
that with one finger he was saying,
“I shall live only one more year!”
Our Anna.. O king of hearts...

You told us,
“He who has a younger brother fears no army”;
leaving us behind, why did you continue
that great journey...?
We grew in the warm glow of your eyes!
Why did you turn all our eyes into ponds?
We thought you yourself were our shade; but you
went beneath the earth by the sea seeking shade — is that just?
Saying, “I am the good pearl,”
are you sleeping on the seashore?
Why have you curled up the tongue
that poured out resonant music?

With the movement of your finger, in the world of letters,
you worked wonders; why
have you folded that finger?

I have seen the great beauty
of you thinking with your eyes closed.. today,
what cruelty is this — the earth has closed over you,
stopping us from seeing you!
You found an end to cruelty; why did you go,
subjecting us to cruelty?
You said we need a heart that can bear anything:

what heart have we, Anna, to bear this too?
Enough of taking the sea breeze
on the shore, Anna!
Rise and come, our Anna!
You will not come; you will not come;
we know nature's conspiracy, Anna. Until
I come seeking the place where you are,
lend me your heart, Anna..
When I come, I shall bring it in my hand
and place it at your foot-flowers, Anna...
