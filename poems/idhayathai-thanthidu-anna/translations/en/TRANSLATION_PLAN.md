## Translation objective

Produce an English version faithful not only to what Kalaignar says, but to **how Kalaignar speaks and writes**.

> **Retain Kalaignar's language — voice, cadence, rhetorical architecture, political memory, literary allusion, repetition and grief — while making the English genuinely readable.**

Readability is not permission to paraphrase away source texture.

## Voice-fidelity rules

1. **Direct address stays direct.** Do not turn intimate/public address into detached biography.
2. **Repetition is meaning.** Do not remove repetitions simply because English style finds them redundant.
3. **Oratory must remain audible.** Preserve calls, rising sequences, slogans, questions, apostrophes, contrasts and catalogues.
4. **Political vocabulary is not decoration.** Do not neutralize movements, leaders, struggles, conferences or political memory.
5. **Literary/classical echoes remain visible.** Keep specific Tamil figures, genres, works and allusions specific.
6. **Imagery remains Kalaignar's imagery.** Do not replace source metaphors with familiar English substitutes.
7. **Elegiac intensity must not be softened.**
8. **Source oddities are not translator licence.** Do not silently correct Tamil before translating it.
9. **No fake archaism.** Elevated English may be used where needed, but not pseudo-Victorian diction.

## Per-batch workflow

For every batch:

1. read verified Tamil page files;
2. read the same range in the reviewed Tamil assembly;
3. check `SOURCE_MAP.md` continuation notes;
4. draft by rhetorical unit;
5. compare back to Tamil for omissions/additions;
6. run a separate Kalaignar-voice pass;
7. record difficult choices outside verse;
8. mark `reviewed` only after all checks pass.

Do not move to the next batch with unresolved meaning or voice issues.

## Proper names, quotations and lineation

- retain readable Roman forms for names/titles without silently substituting modern institutions;
- retain `Anna` as an active form of relationship/address;
- keep compact slogans memorable rather than explanatory;
- do not mechanically mirror every Tamil physical line, but preserve stanza/rhetorical structure;
- notes may explain historical/literary references or unresolved source forms, but must not become an essay.
