# English Translation — Batch 02

Source: scans **16–19** / printed pages **14–17**.

This batch continues directly from Batch 01's closing question, `நினைவுண்டா உங்களுக்கு?` — “do you remember?” — and carries Kalaignar's answer through the repeated three-letter motif, Anna's remembered sayings, movement history, the Anna–Ayya split and the formation of the Munnetra Kazhagam.

## English translation

<!-- scan 16 / printed page 14 -->

There is a distinction in three letters,
the fragrance of *Muttamil* is there..
Three kings, three flags, three fruits —
Tamils who made the three drums resound — in the Tamil they lived,
*vāzhvu* — “life” — three letters;
*anbu* — “love”, the ground of that life — three letters..
*arivu* — “knowledge”, the companion of love — three letters;
*kādhal* — “love” that rises among the knowing — three letters...
the fierce *vīram* — “valour” lovers praised — three letters...
*kaḷam* — “the field” that brings forth valour — three letters...
*veṟṟi* — “victory” seen by entering the field — three letters;
and serene Anna, who spurs us all toward that victory —
Anna — three letters:
I told you, know this!

Tamil's roar to every direction,
a rain of words everywhere;
for those without means, those without a way,
seeking a plan by which they may live,
he circles like a bee, mounting the platform!
And when Anna writes,
every page, every home — Tamil;
the whole land... fragrant there with newness...
“Ēdā thambi! Ettā pēnā....”
“Sharpen not the knife — sharpen the mind.”
A casket of thought! A box of camphor!
“A corpse upon a tree-branch;
a spear in a burnt wound!”

<!-- scan 17 / printed page 15 -->

Could we ever forget those heart-capturing words?
    “The jobless by the roadside —
    in the hearts of the jobless, perverse thoughts —
    O King! That is the sign of the times!”
    To whom but Anna could come this gait of grace?
    This gait of reason?
    “The line rose; the hill sank” —
    was that not an editorial Tamil Nadu will never forget!
    “If this army falls, what army will win?”
    “He who has a younger brother fears no army!”
    Though the *Manonmani* text uttered such peerless lines —
    was it not Anna who engraved them
    upon our hearts!

“The jasmine in another man's garden too has fragrance” —
what beauty, teaching the civility of politics!
“Let us forget — let us forgive” —
what greatness, bearing an opponent's taunt!
    Who taught us this?
    As one raises a wall before painting the picture,
    Anna said it was good to conduct politics
        with grace and civility —
        forgetting that,
    if four or five men leapt away,
    Anna saw them — and laughed!
Because one mother's womb could not bear
to give birth to everyone,

<!-- scan 18 / printed page 16 -->

he called them, “You younger brothers,
born of separate mothers!”
He showed love sweet as fruit,
as rock-candy, as honey-syrup,
and embraced them like a bee settling on a dew-flower.
    Sethu Pillai, master of words,
    Somasundara Bharathi too — in a war of words
    he left them spellbound — poor souls; caught them!
The Justice Party, behind the walls of a towering fort —
a party of the moneyed caste — must not remain so:
    by that principle
he brought it into the street!
“Cast away the white men's titles!” —
at the Salem conference he moved the resolution.
    Unable to withstand
    Anna's battle-form,
those without principle fell,
    like blind men without a staff!
Resolute Anna brought the resolution
to change the name to *Dravidar Kazhagam*
at the conference where warriors gathered!

When Mahatma Gandhi, by the path of righteousness,
removed British rule,
rejecting Periyar's declaration of it as a day of sorrow,
Anna began his battle!
“This is a day of joy! This is a sweet day!
Indians reduced to bone and skin,
through the grace of loving Gandhi, today

<!-- scan 19 / printed page 17 -->

have risen, cutting the shackles of slavery!
Let us lift the drum of freedom and make it thunder!”
So Anna said, that very day...
From that day, in the bond between Anna and Ayya,
    a scratch appeared;
in the year forty-nine it turned into separation.
Like the full moon breaking out through cloud,
he made the *Munnetra Kazhagam* bud forth!
Unable to remain together with the Leader,
    when he came out
    with the majority of comrades,
our unshaken Anna said that day —

it —
it feels, *amma*, as though he said it only yesterday!
“The Munnetra Kazhagam has no Leader —
the chair where my Leader sat will remain empty!
Someday he — the Leader — will come and sit in it..
Until then, I shall wait,” he said.
Like a flower-garden in bloom
    shedding its morning dew,
the waiting crowd that heard him
    shed tears.
“O drops of tears! O pupils of the nation's eye!” he called them,

and marshalled them to shed red blood!
Has anyone ever heard of such a thing?

## Translator's notes

1. **Three-letter sequence:** Kalaignar's rhetoric depends on a chain of Tamil words presented as `முன்றெழுத்து` / three-letter words. English equivalents do not preserve that letter-count. The translation therefore retains the key Tamil words — *vāzhvu, anbu, arivu, kādhal, vīram, kaḷam, veṟṟi,* and *Anna* — with compact English glosses while repeating **“three letters”**. This preserves the architecture of the wordplay instead of replacing it with an explanatory paragraph.
2. **`Muttamil`** is retained for `முத்தமிழ்`, the culturally specific idea of threefold Tamil. It is not flattened into a generic “Tamil culture” phrase.
3. **`Ēdā thambi! Ettā pēnā....`** is retained in transliteration because the verified source form is unusually compressed/lexically difficult. No smoother remembered or expected Tamil slogan has been substituted before translation.
4. **`எடெல்லாம் வீடெல்லாம் தமிழ்`** is rendered as **“every page, every home — Tamil”** from its rhetorical context, while the verified Tamil source form remains untouched.
5. **`Manonmani`** is retained close to the source form `மனோன்மணி`; the translation does not expand the literary reference inside the verse.
6. **`பனிமலர் வீழ் தும்பியதாய்த்`** is rendered conservatively as **“like a bee settling on a dew-flower”** to retain the source's flower/bee imagery. The Tamil expression is unusual, and no wider interpretive paraphrase is inserted.
7. **`தீர் அண்ணா`** is rendered as **“Resolute Anna”**. This carries the compact source epithet without rewriting the verified Tamil form.
8. **`Dravidar Kazhagam`** and **`Munnetra Kazhagam`** remain in Tamil political form rather than being neutralized into generic “association/party” language.
9. **`Ayya`** is retained as the source's relationship/title word in the Anna–Ayya passage. The verse itself does not replace that address with an explanatory name.
10. **`அம்மா`** in `இருக்குதம்மா` is retained as *amma* because it carries Kalaignar's spoken, intimate-oratorical texture here; translating it mechanically as “mother” would misrepresent the rhetorical address.
11. **`கண்ணீர்த் துளிகளே! நாட்டின் கண்மணிகளே!`** is rendered **“O drops of tears! O pupils of the nation's eye!”** to preserve the eye/tear image embedded in `கண்மணி`, rather than reducing it to the generic “dear people”.
