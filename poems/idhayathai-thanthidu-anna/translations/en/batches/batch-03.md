# English Translation — Batch 03

Source: scans **20–21** / printed pages **18–19**.

This batch begins by answering and extending Batch 02's closing question, `யாரேனும் கேட்டதுண்டா?` — “Has anyone ever heard of such a thing?” — with a second public question, `யாரேனும் புகன்றதுண்டா?`. It moves from the rapid rise of the movement and Anna's governance into praise by public figures, then turns through Tamil scholarship and literary memory toward social critique.

## English translation

<!-- scan 20 / printed page 18 -->

Has anyone ever spoken of such a thing?
Within eighteen years, a movement —
like a tiger crouching, then springing — came to rule the land?
Not even two years had passed since my Anna took the reins —
and many noble deeds had taken effect!
To give uplift to all the farmers of the plough,
to heap up for them a life of beauty — he set himself to the task!
The President of the Republic, of soaring renown,
who came to inaugurate the World Tamil Conference,
“I have seen a matchless sight, I have seen majesty;
in all my life I have never seen anything like this,”
said in praise — for the land to applaud — of Anna's glory!
    Brahmananda Reddy of Andhra too:
    “Ah! You are the true Gandhian himself!” —
    he adored him thus — before even a year had passed!

    “You are making prohibition rigorous; in many
    states they have washed their hands of it and tied pots upon the trees!
You are the Mahatma's own manifestation.”
Giri — does that not mean a mountain? — over that
whole mountain our Anna became the cloud!
That is the meaning of the greeting V. V. Giri gave.
A world-renowned great scholar stood praising him — that day,
the one who had stood as the summit of the South —
what did he say
of Anna?

<!-- scan 21 / printed page 19 -->

The scholar of ancient Tamil, the man of Pallavaram —
stood entranced by Anna's ancient-Tamil speech —
a thousand were the words of praise that Maraimalai gave.
Pasumalai Bharathi — Pandyan in bearing — him
the whole land calls *Navalar* — he said:
“To guard the Tamil land fallen through the *sazhakkar*,
a leader without stain in his heart has been found!”
Thus did he speak Anna's greatness that day!

Two thousand years ago too, as young as she is today,
Mother Tamil — though, so that she might live
for years upon years, a hundred-thousand crores, say,
she gave birth to poets who made a thousand books —
our mother, goddess of the arts,
gave birth to Valluvar as her foremost son.

    If flower — the lotus alone;
    if king — Karikalan;
    if book — the *Tirukkural* alone:
    the book of virtue praised in such words
    he gave; and all humankind rejoiced!
Beside mountains that strike the sky —
like poison-trees rising, whether our eyes see them or not —
in the life of a cultivated Tamil people,
all those doctrines that wounded the back
sprang up there!
In the Tamil land where the Kural rang out, seeing no castes,
midway there came, *amma*, castes by the many crores!

## Translator's notes

1. **Paired opening questions:** Batch 02 ends `யாரேனும் கேட்டதுண்டா?`; scan 20 begins `யாரேனும் புகன்றதுண்டா?`. The English preserves the call-and-response cadence as **“Has anyone ever heard of such a thing?” / “Has anyone ever spoken of such a thing?”** rather than treating the second question as a fresh paragraph of exposition.
2. **`பதுங்கிப் பாயும் வேங்கை`:** rendered as **“like a tiger crouching, then springing”** to retain Kalaignar's kinetic political image rather than reduce it to “rose rapidly.”
3. **`ஏரார்ந்த உழவர்`:** rendered **“farmers of the plough”** so the agrarian image remains visible. The line is not neutralized to the bureaucratic “agricultural sector.”
4. **Prohibition passage:** `கை கழுவிக் கலயம் கட்டிவிட்டார் மரங்களிலே` is kept close as **“washed their hands of it and tied pots upon the trees”**. The verse does not insert a prose explanation of the practice; its vivid political contrast is allowed to remain Kalaignar's.
5. **Giri wordplay:** the verified source prints `கீரியென்றால்`, followed shortly by `வி.வி. கிரி`. The English renders the immediate pun as **“Giri — does that not mean a mountain?”** and keeps the following cloud-over-mountain image. This is a translation of the source's own local wordplay, not a change to the archived Tamil reading.
6. **Maraimalai / Pallavaram:** the translation follows the source's sequence of descriptive labels and the explicit `மறைமலை`; it does not add biographical identification inside the verse.
7. **`பசுமலை பாரதி - பாண்டியன் தோற்றம்`:** retained closely as **“Pasumalai Bharathi — Pandyan in bearing”**. No external identification is inserted into the poem.
8. **`Navalar`:** retained as a Tamil honorific/title because the source uses it as a meaningful public designation.
9. **`சழக்கரால்`:** retained as *sazhakkar* because this verified source form is semantically difficult and should not be silently normalized into a more expected Tamil word before translation. The surrounding claim is translated; the uncertain lexical residue is made visible.
10. **Tamil hyperbole:** `நூறாயிரம் கோடி என ஆண்டு பல` is kept as **“for years upon years, a hundred-thousand crores, say”** rather than converted into a numerically tidy English abstraction. The extravagance is part of Kalaignar's voice.
11. **`மலர் / மன்னன் / நூல்`:** the compact parallelism is preserved as **“If flower / if king / if book”**, with the source's named answers intact.
12. **`அம்மா`:** retained as *amma* at the close because, as in Batch 02, it carries spoken-oratorical intimacy rather than a literal address to “mother” in English.
