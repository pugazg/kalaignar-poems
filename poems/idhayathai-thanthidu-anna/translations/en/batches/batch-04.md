# English Translation — Batch 04

Source: scans **22–23** / printed pages **20–21**.

This batch continues directly from Batch 03's closing caste/social-critique line. It intensifies the catalogue of moral and social decline, personifies Mother Tamil in grief, then moves into her dialogue with Valluvar and the imagined rebirth that becomes Anna. The final phrase remains deliberately open because it continues into Batch 05.

## English translation

<!-- scan 22 / printed page 20 -->

Sanctuaries fragrant with knowledge disappeared;
they came to see temples where a thousand gods dwell.

People with no sense of language, creatures of mere mouth-sense —
whether Tamil died or lived, what was that to them?
In the great land where the path of virtue had stood as the goal,
they sowed other paths; they heaped up error.
Like a field gone without rain, the Tamil heart changed.
Envy — desire — anger — hurtful speech:
as though no work could be done without these four,
some Tamils carried on!
“There is no world for those without wealth” —
in the land that had amassed wealth and flourished,
today darkness thickened; misery planted its flag there.
Mother Tamil withered — a song of sorrow
Mother Tamil sang; in scorching fire
Mother Tamil danced...
She ran.. she ran.. not one path could she find!
Leaving the town of earth, he lives in the town of fame —
the great sovereign of the town of poetry, the Master of the Kural!
Going there, she said, “Precious son!
I have come with one request.”
    “What is it, *amma*?” asked the Kural-one.
“In Tamil Nadu, wherever one digs, gold comes forth;
you must be born again,” she said.

    “To dig for gold?” he asked.
    “To make Tamil hearts, all Tamil life,

<!-- scan 23 / printed page 21 -->

gold,” she said.
“What has happened today?” he asked.
“Danger has come to the mountain-like language,” she said;
“The poor man has no hut to go to,” he said;
“Not knowing how to raise the rod sternly and cast it gently,
the killing staff has risen,” she said; and she told him too
of those calf-like in intellect talking useless tales.
Wiping her weeping eyes,
nectar-tongued Valluvar asked,
“Amma, where shall I be born?”
She kissed the crown of the son who bowed,
and with arms like the hanging roots of a banyan.. embraced him.
“A ploughed field shall not dry for want of seedlings

anymore” — thinking so,
like a farmer whose heart rejoices,
her whole heart swelled and leapt;
to pour gruel into the parched stomach —
“O rock-candy! O honey-syrup! O Tirukkural!
    Be born in Kanchi!” she said.
He was born as our Anna;
    as the king of reason,
as the breeze from Pothigai mountain,
as the fresh fragrance
    of Tamil feeling budding into flower,
as the meaning within a *pathigam* —

## Translator's notes

1. **Continuation from Batch 03:** scan 21 ends with the arrival of countless castes in a land where the Kural had sounded without caste. Scan 22 continues the same critique. The English therefore does not reset the subject or soften the transition.
2. **`வாயுணர்வின் மாக்கள்`:** rendered close as **“creatures of mere mouth-sense”**. The expression is deliberately not polished into a generic “people without awareness,” because Kalaignar's contrast is between language-consciousness and bodily/mouth-consciousness.
3. **The four vices:** `அழுக்காறு - அவா - வெகுளி - இன்னாச் சொல்` is retained as the compact sequence **“Envy — desire — anger — hurtful speech”**. The list's Kural-like moral cadence is preserved rather than expanded into commentary.
4. **`புவியூர் / புகழூர் / கவியூர்`:** the place-name wordplay is preserved as **“town of earth / town of fame / town of poetry”**. The English keeps the repeated `ūr` idea through “town” instead of reducing the lines to a prose statement that Valluvar is dead and famous.
5. **Valluvar dialogue:** the exchange is kept as direct question-and-answer speech. Mother Tamil's distress is not paraphrased into narrative explanation.
6. **`சென்றமையக் குடில் இல்லை ஏழைக்கென்றான்`:** rendered conservatively as **“The poor man has no hut to go to”**. The verified Tamil is compressed and unusual; no alternate remembered wording has been substituted into the source before translation.
7. **`கடிதோச்சி மெல்ல எறிய`:** translated as **“raise the rod sternly and cast it gently”**, retaining the source's paradox of severe display and gentle execution. Kalaignar's following reversal — the killing staff rising — remains explicit.
8. **`அறிவில் கன்றனையோர்`:** retained as **“those calf-like in intellect”** rather than smoothed into “the ignorant.” The animal image belongs to the source.
9. **`உச்சிமோந்து / ஆல விழுதனைய கைகள்`:** rendered through the physical intimacy of **kissing the crown** and **arms like the hanging roots of a banyan**. The source image is kept rather than replaced with a generic maternal embrace.
10. **Field/seedling image:** `உழுத வயல் நாற்றின்றிக் காயாது` is carried into the following farmer simile as **“A ploughed field shall not dry for want of seedlings anymore”**. This preserves the agricultural logic of the source and its hope of renewal.
11. **Vocatives:** `கற்கண்டே! தேன்பாகே! திருக்குறளே!` remains **“O rock-candy! O honey-syrup! O Tirukkural!”**. The sweetness imagery is intentionally not domesticated into “dear child.”
12. **`பதிகம்`:** retained as *pathigam* because the line is part of a continuing Tamil literary catalogue. A loose generic “poem” would erase the specificity. The phrase continues directly into scan 24 / Batch 05.
