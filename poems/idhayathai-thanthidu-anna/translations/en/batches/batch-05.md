# English Translation — Batch 05

Source: scans **24–26** / printed pages **22–23** plus the unnumbered final poem scan.

This final translation batch begins by completing the open literary sequence from Batch 04, moves through Kalaignar's accumulating names and roles for Anna and the statue/cultural-memory catalogue, then turns abruptly from public commemoration into intimate grief. The closing heart-plea is kept direct and restrained because it is the title-bearing emotional resolution of the poem.

## English translation

<!-- scan 24 / printed page 22 -->

as the *purappāṭṭu* of the ancient Tamils,
Anna came and rose — like a ray
rising on the eastern horizon —
to sing his fame,
I too, the younger brother he raised,
taking the Tamil he gave,
have come here — this is the truth —
They call him Leader; they call him philosopher;
they call him actor; they call him king of drama;
they say he possessed the power of speech and the savour of writing.

They call him man; they call him ruby; they call him Minister of the State.
They call him mother; they call him guardian of precious speech;
they call him politician — all of these,
if there is no time to say them one by one — out of the love in the heart,
let them call him by the single word
“Anna” — for so
his mother gave him the name too.
To honour that lineage of mothers:
a statue for Avvai;
a statue for Kannagi, who nurtured virtue;
a statue for Bharathi of the unbending heart,
and for Bharathidasan of the unbowing head;

a statue for Veerama Munivar;
a statue for Caldwell and Pope;
a statue for Kambar;

<!-- scan 25 / printed page 23 -->

a statue for the Tamil who bravely sailed the ship;
a statue for Tiruvalluvar, to spread the Kural to all eight directions.
By setting up ten statues — so the world might know
the attachment Anna held for the Tamil people;
when a statue for that Anna
was set up in Chennai..
he stood with only his index finger raised..
We thought: our Anna is giving an order.
Alas! Only today do we understand
that with one finger he was saying,
“I shall live only one more year!”
Our Anna.. O king of hearts...

You told us,
“He who has a younger brother fears no army”;
leaving us behind, why did you continue
that great journey...?
We grew in the warm glow of your eyes!
Why did you turn all our eyes into ponds?
We thought you yourself were our shade; but you
went beneath the earth by the sea seeking shade — is that just?
Saying, “I am the good pearl,”
are you sleeping on the seashore?
Why have you curled up the tongue
that poured out resonant music?

With the movement of your finger, in the world of letters,
you worked wonders; why
have you folded that finger?

<!-- scan 26 / unnumbered -->

I have seen the great beauty
of you thinking with your eyes closed.. today,
what cruelty is this — the earth has closed over you,
stopping us from seeing you!
You found an end to cruelty; why did you go,
subjecting us to cruelty?
You said we need a heart that can bear anything:

what heart have we, Anna, to bear this too?
Enough of taking the sea breeze
on the shore, Anna!
Rise and come, our Anna!
You will not come; you will not come;
we know nature's conspiracy, Anna. Until
I come seeking the place where you are,
lend me your heart, Anna..
When I come, I shall bring it in my hand
and place it at your foot-flowers, Anna...

## Translator's notes

1. **Batch 04 → Batch 05 continuation:** Batch 04 ends `தமிழ் உணர்வின் புதுமணமாய்ப் / பதிகத்துப் பொருளாய்ப்`; scan 24 continues `பழந்தமிழர் புறப்பாட்டாய்`. The English therefore carries the sequence directly into **“as the *purappāṭṭu* of the ancient Tamils”** rather than treating scan 24 as a fresh sentence.
2. **`புறப்பாட்டு`:** retained as *purappāṭṭu* because it belongs to Kalaignar's dense Tamil literary register. A generic “ancient poem” would erase the source's specificity.
3. **`கீழ் வானுதித்த கதிர்`:** rendered **“a ray rising on the eastern horizon.”** The phrase is kept as a dawn/rising image; no additional historical symbolism is inserted.
4. **Role catalogue:** `தலைவர் / தத்துவமேதை / நடிகர் / நாடக வேந்தர் / மனிதன் / மாணிக்கம் / மாநிலத்து அமைச்சர் / அன்னை / அருமொழிக் காவல் / அரசியல்வாதி` is kept accumulative. The English does not condense the catalogue into a biographical summary.
5. **`அண்ணா`:** retained as `Anna`, not reduced to a personal name. The source explicitly gathers the preceding roles into this single relationship-word.
6. **Statue catalogue:** Avvai, Kannagi, Bharathi, Bharathidasan, Veerama Munivar, Caldwell, Pope, Kambar and Tiruvalluvar remain named as in the source. `தீரமாய்க் கப்பலோட்டிய தமிழர்` is translated descriptively as **“the Tamil who bravely sailed the ship”**; no external identity is inserted because the source does not name that person here.
7. **Raised finger:** the English preserves the poem's retrospective turn from **commanding index finger** to **one remaining year of life**. The interpretive realization is Kalaignar's own in the source; no external statue explanation is added.
8. **`இதயமன்னா`:** rendered **“O king of hearts”** to keep the heart/king compound audible. This prepares the poem's final heart-plea without replacing the vocative with a generic endearment.
9. **Echoed slogan:** `படைக்கஞ்சாத் தம்பியுண்டென்று` is translated in continuity with Batch 02's **“He who has a younger brother fears no army.”** The recurrence is intentional and is kept recognisable.
10. **Sea/pearl/shade imagery:** `நிழல் நீ தான்`, `கடல் நிலத்துக்குள்`, `நான்தானடா நன்முத்து`, and `கடற்கரையில்` remain a compressed seashore image-complex. The English does not insert a place-name or burial-site explanation that is absent from the verse.
11. **Body-of-language imagery:** tongue and finger remain physical: the tongue that poured resonant music is “curled up”; the finger that made wonders in the world of letters is “folded.” The death imagery is not softened into abstract “silence”.
12. **`எதையும் தாங்கும் இதயம்`:** rendered directly as **“a heart that can bear anything”** so the following question — what heart can bear this? — lands with the same repetition.
13. **`இரவலாக`:** rendered as **“lend me your heart”**. The source carries the sense of borrowing/receiving on loan; “lend” preserves the temporary nature that is completed by the promise to return the heart when the speaker comes.
14. **`கால்மலரில்`:** rendered **“at your foot-flowers”** to return to the flower/body imagery established at the poem's opening rather than replacing it with a conventional devotional phrase.
15. **Printer imprint:** scan 26's printer imprint is excluded from the English poem body. It remains publication matter, not verse.
