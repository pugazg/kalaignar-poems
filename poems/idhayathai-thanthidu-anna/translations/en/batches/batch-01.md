# English Translation — Batch 01

Source: scans **13–15** / printed pages **11–13**.

This batch translates poem verse only. The publication/context note printed above the poem on scan 13 remains outside the verse translation.

## English translation

<!-- scan 13 / printed page 11 -->

Softer than the softness of a flower-petal,
a sacred heart — a loving heart —
a mother's heart that gathers one close! His
flower-petals speak Tamil —
mango, jackfruit, banana — the three fruits themselves are outdone —
his eye-flowers turn spear, turn sword
if even one harm comes to Mother Tamil!

Though his foot-flowers wilt, his arduous journey does not halt;
his hand-flowers clasp together the younger brothers, the Kazhagam!
Those very flowers forgive the enemies
and make their heads bow in shame, like ripened paddy!
Let democracy's flower sway,
let socialism's flower give fragrance,
with mother-tongue Tamil itself as life's garden —
O breeze that comes dancing,
O flower-crown! O crown of fame! In the heaps of blessings
that come seeking you — each day
I shall fly as the bee that comes singing.
For you I shall give up myself; for the song
my one crore Tamil youths stood singing,
you were the Great Leader..

<!-- scan 14 / printed page 12 -->

Listen as I sing the greatness
of the man of action, the warrior,
whose Tamil fervour, packed into the drum,
made the precious life of youth swell!
The wounds dealt by the *tangu sani vēl* —
soothing them with the fomentation called love,
his red-fruit mouth applying medicine —
listen as I sing his nobility!
Where is that battle-mighty arm; where that jewelled chest?
Where, where the patron's shoulders
that would never show their back, whoever came upon the battlefront? —
hear how he asked the younger brothers! Hear! Hear!
While Kanchi stood, Kalinga trembled —
O maidens joined in love — *kazhar-chchenni* —
while Kanchi stood, Kalinga was lost:
"Open — let the war of the field be sung!"

To wake the love-dazed women from sleep
he set himself to action and sang:
a parani of the battlefield.. the *Kalingattu Parani*,
a praise-parani of immeasurable valour — I
mounted a parani of poetry, a Kanchipuram Parani,
and stood singing then;
today
I sing a parani of grief....!

<!-- scan 15 / printed page 13 -->

As meaning within a poem, as sweetness in sugarcane,
as light in a ray, as flower in a grove,
as coolness in the moon, as richness upon the earth,
as the babbling tongue of the roaming waterfall,
as music mounting the wandering wind,

as dancing foam upon the wave-risen sea —
the poet in whom the fragrance of art gathers and rises,
Anna, chief among poets — when I sing his sacred praise,
ah! the tears the Earth-maiden pours!
A flood!
A flood!
A mighty flood!
In nineteen hundred and thirty-eight,
if a few sowed the seed
for a language revolution to arise in the homeland:
the peerless chief-warrior of the leader of Erode —
our Anna entered the field, wearing Kanchi!

Withering in prison for two, three months,
giving Tamil as the staff that would bear us,
by those rare three letters he became Anna...
That day, in fifty-two,
in Chennai, I told you —
do you remember?

## Translator's notes

1. **`Kazhagam`** is retained rather than flattened into a generic "party" or "association" because the Tamil uses the movement-word `கழகம்` as an active political belonging.
2. **`tangu sani vēl`** preserves the verified but lexically difficult source sequence `தங்கு சனி வேல்`. Rather than silently normalize it to a more expected Tamil reading and then translate that invented reading, this batch retains the phrase in transliteration while translating the surrounding wound/healing imagery.
3. The Kanchi/Kalinga passage deliberately keeps the verified `கழற்சென்னி` as **`kazhar-chchenni`**. The surrounding syntax and martial-literary movement are translated, but no unsupported lexical correction is introduced for this source form.
4. **`Parani`** is retained as the Tamil literary genre term. Kalaignar turns the heroic/victory genre into `அவலப் பரணி` — rendered here as **"a parani of grief"** — and that genre-turn is central to the rhetoric.
5. **`one crore Tamil youths`** retains Kalaignar's Indian numerical idiom rather than replacing it inside the verse with "ten million".
6. **`wearing Kanchi`** stays deliberately close to `காஞ்சி பூண்டு`. The Tamil is compressed and allusive; the translation does not expand it into an unsupported historical explanation.
7. `அய்ம்பத்திரண்டுதனில்` is rendered rhetorically as **"in fifty-two"**. The translation does not use the source's unusual printed form as licence to rewrite the verified Tamil.
