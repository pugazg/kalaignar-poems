# New Path

<!-- scan 128 -->

### New Path

(Presiding poetic address by
Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi
at the agricultural *kaviyarangam* titled “New Path,”
held at the Moorthy Art Hall, Kumbakonam,
on 13 August 1968.)

Minister for Agriculture,
whose administrative strength enhances the Tamil government here!
Poets who have come with the swordsmanship of verse
to display the great prowess of farming!
Seeing the emerald gems produced by her own child,
Mother Earth rejoices: “This is *nanjai*, wet cultivation.”
Seeing the emerald grains of paddy produced by Thanjavur,
she rejoices again in that fertile *nanjai* land.
At Thanjavur's tail-end, where Puhar lies,
on Thanjavur's coast, where Poompuhar lies,
none here will exceed my words; therefore,
with no thought of complaint — *puhār* —
let us remember the ancient Puhar festival,
the earlier Karthigai festival—

<!-- scan 129 -->

before the rains themselves stood bewildered.
Not for one day or two, but many days,
though the seasonal rain failed,
you stood like the pole star,
lifting Thanjavur's victorious brow. Long live!
Since the world is sustained by what descends from the sky,
is not rain itself nectar?
Is it not rain that becomes food for those who eat,
and itself food for those who drink?
Is it not rain that destroys,
and then becomes support and raises up those it has ruined?
Though the world cannot exist without water,
our forebears knew that water itself could not be ordered without rivers.
They found rivers — and so they found rice.
How many rivers upon this earth
made patterned paddy smile,
spread green silk,
and burned away the demon of poverty!
Good people who stand by the house,
by the forest,
and by the election vote —
come for a while to the riverbank;
come today to the bank of my song!

Seeing the maiden Kaveri dance at Kudamurutti,
Veera Cholan comes rowing,
saying, “This is the place to fall in love —
this is Kollidam, the place for love!”
He bends toward her saying, “Give me your hand” —
and the Vaigai bends;

<!-- scan 130 -->

he murmurs, “Your teeth are pearl-gems” — Muthar.
Kaveri winks:
“My sister-in-law is coming;
hide yourself, dear!”
He replies, “Let Manniyar come.
Is she a Kallanai dam across our love?
Kamini, Bamini, Namini — we are lovers;
yes, now tell the others that our wedding is coming.
Will the fire of love cool, my dear?
Come near — will that fire cool?”
She says, “No one should think wrongly of us;
no one should seek to divide our love;
even if others do not sever our bond, dear,
my shyness holds me back.”
Veera Cholan quickly embraces her:
“Come, let us first drink from the Palar.”
The turmeric-faced maiden, leaning on his lap, asks,
“When is the wedding, dear?”
“It will be grandly held under the leadership of the Periyar,” he says.

Do not think I merely joined rivers together
to weave a love story!
Wherever possible let us set their courses right
and increase the wealth of the rivers.
“The ploughman is the linchpin of the world;
though men toil, farming remains foremost” —
let us rise and thank them,
with Valluvar himself offering his blessing.

<!-- scan 131 -->

His life blossoms in the mud!
The farmer is a king:
a crown of seedlings rests on his head.
The farmer is a warrior:
there is always a “war” in his field — a haystack-war.
The farmer is a painter:
every field is a picture he has drawn.
The farmer is a god,
for he protects living beings.
Let us honour the sacred wealth
that labours beneath the feet,
with the *kuruvai* crop before us.
From the day the land was born you have laboured;
you give your very life for the country's welfare.
As long as strength remains in the body
you lie out in the fields.
You reckon in the old measures — *kalam*, *marakkal*, *padi* —
and when the man who ploughed calculates his account,
you purse your lips and say,
“Not even an *uzhakku* remains.”
Because we asked why this condition persists,
we found a path toward rising progress.
A path found should not remain only on paper;
nor is it good for it to end with a *kaviyarangam* song.
People of Tamil Nadu lying at home, wake up!
On this Independence Day, rise with urgency!
You roam forest and field,
you circle among goats and cattle;
if old methods of cultivation are changed,
there is a way to reap a much fuller yield.
The dark condition in which only an *uzhakku* remained
will disappear like dawn whitening the east.

<!-- scan 132 -->

I am not calling you
to raise a crop in one night like Nandan.
Under the karuvel tree the “mirasudar” shouts,
“Kanda! Kadamba! Karuppa! Munia!”;
cattle are tethered,
the plough breaks the human back,
the manure is what those animals kindly provide,
and the bull goes round because the goad pierces it
while water is poured.
Into this inherited mode of farming
we are joining science,
so that it may move with the speed of a spinning top.
In Western countries educated people sow and harvest;
they plough with scientific machinery.
Here, educated people weep at the thought of growing crops
and fall into queues searching for jobs!
What other work is higher
than the work that gives food?
The old meaning of
“all who learn stand at the head-end of those who plough”
was that the learned bow before the ploughman.
Should we not give it a new arrangement?
“All who learn shall be at the head and tail of those who plough” —
the educated themselves shall farm,
at the head-reach and at the tail-reach!
We have stood behind for many days.
Let us take heart to achieve self-sufficiency in food.
Our government stands in front to reach that condition.
When we say increase production in the country,
some leave that aside
and increase production in the home!

<!-- scan 133 -->

Pearl, jewel,
little bird, casket,
eye, nose —
stop producing children beyond count under such pet names.
Instead produce food:
paddy,
varagu millet,
pearl millet,
sugarcane,
black gram,
pulses,
sesame,
horse gram.
Increase food; produce it!
At this *kaviyarangam* created to make that clear,
a number of farmer-friends have come
to plough with the plough of poetry.
I welcome them.

Do not think a man called Karumbayiram
will sing about sugarcane merely because of his name.
He will sing as sweet as sugarcane;
we can taste it like ants.
There was *durai*-rule before —
before the year forty-seven:
white colonial *durai*-rule.
Twenty years after freedom,
today it is Anna Durai's rule.
That day was the rule of the foreign *durai*;
today it is the Aduthurai variety.
Our poet will sing about it.

<!-- scan 134 -->

For forests to fragrance,
for fame to endure,
for dignity to rise,
for the nation daily
to labour,
for the hand's home to be full —
Aduthurai Twenty-Seven!

(Poet Karumbayiram finishes his poem on
“Aduthurai-27 Paddy.”)

Chairman Kalaignar:

Born in Aduthurai, has this Twenty-Seven
through love of English become “A.D.T.-27,” sir?
Likewise there is an “I.R.-8” paddy
that defeats another in abundant yield.
We have heard of poets who, before taking a breath,
can produce three hundred or four hundred lines;
here is one who can deliver verse in the blink of an eye.
“Kannimai” — “blink” — is his very name.
He will pour melody about paddy number eight, I.R.-8:

(Poet Kannimai finishes his poem on “I.R.-8 Paddy.”)

Chairman Kalaignar:

Next:
another poet sings about maize.
His name is Nanjappa!

<!-- scan 135 -->

We shall say, “Give us a little Tamil, sir,”
and listen to Nanjappa's song
to drive famine away!

(Poet Nanjappa finishes his poem on “Maize.”)

Chairman Kalaignar:

Next,
a poet comes carrying a *kambu* staff —
do not fear that he has come to start a quarrel.
He pours a poem
about methods of cultivating *kambu*, pearl millet.
His name is Radhakrishnan:

(Poet Radhakrishnan finishes his poem on
“Cultivation of Pearl Millet.”)

Chairman Kalaignar:

Next —
have you seen the wonder of growing *ragi*
in a lotus pond?
A poet named Chellapandian from Thamaraikulam
brings the methods of cultivating ragi.
Listen with pleasure and put them into practice:

(Poet Chellapandian finishes his poem on “Ragi.”)

Chairman Kalaignar:

Next,
under the name “Moovendhar,”
in the lineage of the poet-king,

<!-- scan 136 -->

Tamilarasu sings,
weaving a silken poem about hybrid sorghum.

Chairman Kalaignar's concluding poem:

Minister for Agriculture!
Good farming people!
Dear farmers of Tamil Nadu!
You have heard six men speak of abundant yield;
you have heard this humble man sing on behalf of the government.
After hearing all this on August Fifteenth,
do not sit carelessly saying, “Let us see what happens.”
The Tamil government here is a government for agriculture,
a new government rushing to work swiftly
to increase the harvest.
Loans will be provided without delay;
fertiliser distribution will not stop even for a moment;
credit and pesticide will be available
to destroy the lives of crop-destroying pests.
This is not merely the fellowship of song;
there is also the help of cooperation.
For double-crop wet land in Thanjavur
we have granted water-cess relief;
we have exempted dry land from tax
and become medicine for the farmer's smile.
We have plans to do much more —
we need only your wholehearted cooperation.
Landowners must have in their hearts
an abundance of generosity
toward those who labour.

<!-- scan 137 -->

Labour and ownership must join
so that the food situation may flourish.
Under the leadership of honourable Gandhi
we drove out the foreigner.
There are no foreigners among us now;
it is wrong for our own people to behave like foreigners.
The man expert in hoarding
kills many lives.
The man skilled in smuggling
surpasses even Yama.
The man who says, “Do not give out the paddy,”
becomes a cruel beast.
Today let farming people
win “freedom” from such men.
For this purpose let us find a New Path.
On August Fifteenth let us proclaim the vow:
Abundant harvest is the ideal of Anna's government —
let us pledge ourselves to it with certainty!

Greetings.
Long live!

## Translator notes

- The river-love passage deliberately strings together South Indian river/place names — Kollidam, Vaigai, Manniyar, Kallanai, Palar, Periyar and others — as puns inside romantic dialogue. The names are retained rather than replaced with generic river terms.
- `nanjai`, *kalam*, *marakkal*, *padi*, *uzhakku*, *kuruvai* and *kambu* are source-active agricultural terms. Short contextual glosses are supplied in English without altering the Tamil witness.
- The Aduthurai/A.D.T.-27 and I.R.-8 passages preserve the source's humour around modern crop-variety names and English abbreviations.
