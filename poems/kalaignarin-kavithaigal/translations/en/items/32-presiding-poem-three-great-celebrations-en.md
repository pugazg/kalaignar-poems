# Presiding Poem at the Three Great Celebrations Poetry Assembly

<!-- scan 297 -->

Tamil, granted to us as life itself!
Even the moon bears a stain,
but sweet Tamil,
you are a ray of light without blemish!

Jaggery is needed
to sweeten Pongal;
Tamil of the Podhigai hills,
to make you grow,
what we need in the conch-resounding battlefield
is a good heart.

Tamil that flourishes as valour!
Tamil that yields as victory!
Laugh so the whole land shakes:
let talk of defeat belong henceforth
to the enemy, not to us!

People born from who-knows-where
have come to divide up Tamil life into shares.
The Tamil host must gather without bank or boundary.
We must make the blind and mute awaken and speak,
and make them march toward the field.

For that spark to rise,
you are the *purappāttu* living in our word and deed,
the war-song of Kalinga,
the ancient song of the elder people
who arose before stone and earth,
unvanquished even by the sea's engulfing flood —

<!-- scan 298 -->

the Tamil of mango, jackfruit and banana,
giving flavour to the new song I sing today.
Live long!

We have seen the three great seas,
seen the threefold Tamil,
seen the three great kings,
seen three flags flying,
seen the glory of the three Sangams,
tasted the three great fruits —
now let us behold the Three Great Celebration!

Is there a day
when we do not think of Anna,
Periyar and the Kazhagam?
Today let us take a vow
to guard the policy that is our very eye.

Gold? Wealth? What use are they?
Counterfeits wander only for those!

Erode was once like a head full of lice;
had there been no Erode comb
to clean and beautify it,
Tamil Nadu itself
might by now have gone bald!

We know his birthday,
but not his star.
He never worried about stars either.
Yet if one asks his *lagna* —
it must surely have been Leo!
Before that lion,
jackals could not wag their tails.

He was a surging volcano;
its lava rushed toward the citadel of Vedic orthodoxy.
He was a forest fire;
in it the great serpents of Sanatana order

<!-- scan 299 -->

were scorched.
He was a wild flood breaking its banks;
in that torrent
the mud houses of old almanacs
vanished without a trace.

He was a war-sword;
in a single sweep,
priestcraft lost its breath.

If even small singing streams,
when they rage,
can make ruin hard to stop,
he was the Periyar — the great river!
If that river rises in anger,
will not the taproots of epic and purana
be torn out?

Like a singular radiance
born in molten gold,
making the red dawn itself blush,
Periyar surged up.
Crores of young bulls of modernity
stood firm and declared:
our blood itself is the stake.

The man of this land shall live here,
a man of fame who can outshine any land.
The Tamil who had bowed his head —
see, he stands upright now!

Those already winnowed out
are the little fox-pack now.
With rational thought he condemned
piety, religion and purana:
“ruin! ruin! ruin!”
A new idea arose — a revolutionary idea.
If even a worm becomes a tiger when trodden on,
what will a tiger become?
Yet some still do not understand!

<!-- scan 300 -->

But consider this:
pigs caked in filth,
mangy dogs,
herds of donkeys —
all have the right
to run and play in streets
where “high-caste” people walk.
Yet the last Shudra or Panchama,
said to have been born from the god's feet,
becomes pollution merely by walking there.
Such was the unjust “justice” called Manu's law,
with no one to reject it
before Periyar was born in Erode.

That is why he said:
strike after seeing the snake —
and the skull of that bitter doctrine
split under the blow.

For Valli and Parvati,
for Bama and Radha and all their kind,
you conduct marriage festivals every year.
“What became of last year's marriage, ayya?”
Ayya asked.
Who has yet answered that question of reason?

One day in Kailasa,
Parvati went to bathe,
gathered the dirt from her body,
gave it life,
and stationed that form as guard
so no man should enter
while she bathed unclothed.
Shiva, who had swallowed the deadly poison,
hurried in.
The form stopped him as a man.
Shiva grew angry
and struck off the dirt-born figure's head.

<!-- scan 301 -->

Parvati, seeing it, blazed in fury
and ordered her husband Shiva
to restore life to the child born of her dirt.
An elephant happened along the street;
its head was cut off
and fixed upon the dirt-born body.
That, the story says,
is elephant-faced Pillaiyar.
You know it, do you not?

If Mother Parvati carried enough dirt on her body
to make such a large child,
how many days must she have gone without bathing?
Was it not the old man of Erode
who planted such questions
and turned purana into an object of ridicule?

Was it not Periyar too
who cleared the weeds of ideas,
saying that the crop of common ownership
can grow only in a social field
from which weeds have been removed?

You who measure and declare that an “atma” exists —
where then did the tale of the next birth come from?
The question roared like *Murasoli*;
Sanatanists withdrew like turtles.

Seeing plants move in darkness,
blind minds cried “ghost, demon, spirit”
and died in fear.
There is no ghost, no demon — madmen!
Who lit the Self-Respect flame saying,
“Do not believe what swindlers tell you”?
Periyar!

Why did he affectionately call Kamaraj
a “green Tamil”?
So the backward classes might live!

<!-- scan 302 -->

Why did he wish
that Kazhagam rule should continue for all time?
So that the dreams he had seen
might be fulfilled step by step!

Was it not Periyar
who made Tamil feeling,
lying like the Lord of Srirangam,
spring up and thunder in all eight directions
and perform many valiant tasks?

Street stage, art stage, music stage, cinema stage —
everything once rang with
“ammāmi, aththimbēr,” a kind of *jala-tarangam*.
He changed that,
made it a Tamil stage, a stage of our own.
Is it not fitting to hold a poetry assembly
for the man who did so?

He had a beard like Tagore of Bengal,
a gaze raised like a charging tiger,
a body like a red hill,
and in eyes white as conch
two black beetles —
light remained in them until death.

Even a spinning top rests after circling;
he, even in extreme old age,
still went round like a top.

As volcano and burning flame,
as nature's own dance,
as thunder making opposition tremble,
as the great torch of ethnic consciousness,
as a sharp sword cutting down degradation,
as Ingersoll who denied even God,
as Socrates forever speaking in Athens,
as Voltaire with a diamond heart asking “Why?” —

<!-- scan 303 -->

our Father Periyar lived
and filled every atom of our thought!

A rain of reason would fall —
beauty flow, darkness go!
Clear speeches would shine,
ability sparkle!
Like the opened sea,
the mountain peak,
the sound of waves,
the cloud embracing them —
reason's rain would fall.
Arrogance would perish.
Slavery would end.

When Ayya spoke,
for Kazhagam people it was like eating *adhirasam*;
for villains, like an atom bomb exploding!

He did not die young;
he lived to fullness of age and then rested.
Yet if the Taj Mahal suddenly sank into the earth,
would we console ourselves
by saying, “It was built many years ago”?
If the Thanjavur tower fell,
would it be easy to bear
by saying it had grown old?
He was as tall as the Thanjavur tower,
a maker of history like the Taj Mahal.

A hundred years for that sun of awakening!
And for our Anna —
for the Kazhagam he created —

<!-- scan 304 -->

today is its thirtieth year!

Can a storm wind give birth
to a flower-soft breeze?
Such a child was born:
our Anna, the gentle wind,
a breeze that drove away even violent storms
with a smile.

Calling, “O fallen Tamil Nadu!”
he classified the greatness in which we had once lived
and gave us a deep treasury of ideas.
It was a treasury of thought —
not some broken-eared chest imagined by a black-money man
to overturn the Kazhagam,
but a camphor casket given by Kanchi Anna!

As Periyar's right hand,
as a motorboat carrying the poor in knowledge to shore,
as a sunbeam forgiving the errors of the small,
as a master who made fanatics bow,
he conducted politics with method and principle
and planned the removal of the begging bowl.
Some who once sat with “Sri” before their names
now wander the street abusing us.
If we fight a pimple, our own face is spoiled;
apply civet calmly and the boil will fall away.

Even when he differed in thought from E. V. R. Periyar
and wept as he parted to form another Kazhagam,
our Anna thundered to the enemy
that the two would remain like a double-barrelled gun.
Today, gathering cast-off pieces,
base men run with swords
to rip open the womb of the mother herself;
soon they will be crippled!

<!-- scan 305 -->

It feels as though it happened today —
that sight of the cow parting from the Kazhagam calf.
As though a spear that could pierce a hill
was aimed and tore our chest,
Anna, who would never leave us,
left us that day. Alas!
Eight or nine years have passed.

Avvai has a song called *Kondrai Vendhan*;
our king of principle has an imperishable song!
He showed in his new Tamil style
both the gait of the swelling sea
and the soft walk of maidens.
He trapped those who spoke evil in debate.
At the moment of taking snuff,
with a smile he ground his opponents to powder.
He drove out those of dusty character.
He placed a golden crown on Mother Tamil.

Anna held a pen —
a plough upon paper
to alter the fate written on the heads
of deceived Tamils!

Betel, areca and lime reddened his mouth;
if Hindi came to strike Tamils,
his eyes reddened.
He sent *agrasanadhipathi* away
and let “assembly president” walk the stage.
Yesterday's *prasangam* became “speech.”
*Namaskaram-kimaskaram* bloomed into good Tamil “vanakkam.”
Once a flag-hoisting ceremony exists in pure Tamil,
what work has *dhvajarohanam* there?
If “Truth alone triumphs” is said in Tamil,
does *Satyameva Jayate* grow angry?

<!-- scan 306 -->

The Sankaracharya says even worship
cannot be conducted in Tamil nurtured by the Sangam!
To meet the battle method of the Kanchi seat,
the leader from Kanchi did not leave us untaught.
If worship for the Lord in the temple
cannot be in the mother tongue,
we shall beat the drum and say:
we do not need that Lord either!

We are brothers to the young breeze called Anna;
at the same time,
we are grandchildren of the great storm called Periyar.
Those who impose the northern language
must not forget our history.
If they tread on the feet
of the fighting Tamil people,
our feet will tread upon
the head of language domination — take warning!

We are soldiers of a forty-year language war,
not heroes who obtained exemption
by saying, “What has it to do with us?”
Even when our young shoots
became victims to gunfire
and flowered as corpses in the middle of the street,
even when they entered fire
and became lamps that cannot be extinguished,
some who luckily escaped the nationwide earthquake
of the language revolution
by running and hiding
now speak for show:
“I shall abolish Hindi,”
and purchase valour at market price.
Even a party that sang off-key
in support of Hindi during the Emergency
now says Hindi must not come.
When cats themselves roar like leopards,
if we leopards rise in anger,
will there not be a war that shakes the earth?

<!-- scan 307 -->

Prison and torture —
for a Kazhagam man these are old stories!
Hindi for government,
Sanskrit for worship of God —
as long as even one Tamil remains alive,
he will not accept it!
Source-visible `ஓதிய மிலார்` —
moringa branches cannot escape
if they come to strike iron pillars!

Do not imagine that because
Periyar and Anna are gone
you may run the country as you please.
Had they been here,
they would have stood as dams
against our river of feeling.
Waxen trees!
You might then have escaped without melting away.

Beating one's chest before a bull without a nose-rope
is the work of fools.
Even Anna, who asked us to be patient
like earth that bears those who dig it,
if he were alive today,
would surely thunder,
“Rise in fury, brother!”

Is government being conducted now?
Is it not jungle rule that continues?
How to destroy the opposition —
that alone is the thought.
Wherever one turns,
only slander against us;
cruel cases, murder cases —
they think the Kazhagam will tremble
if they file severe charges!
Will a temple tower fall
because a chicken pecks it?

<!-- scan 308 -->

As Ganga and Yamuna flood
and enter the northern plains,
illicit liquor floods
and pours across Tamil land!
They beat their chests saying,
“I shall drive out the liquor demon.”
Is he some demon without a form
to be chased away?
He is here,
laughing as an organiser of the ruling party!

Rape, murder, theft,
tear gas, firing,
bribery and corruption,
interference in administration,
interference even in justice —
proof exists by the thousand, one upon another.
We would need a separate poetry assembly
just for all that.

Covering everything up,
they act like Buddha and Jesus
and ask us to believe them!

Have you ever heard ministers before
speak of auctioning cattle?
Here speaks the cattle-man Velan:
if bullock-cart processions take place,
the carts will certainly be smashed,
and the bulls auctioned!

If the farmer who weeps
turns into a spark of fire,
will he not dare,
in opposition to auctioning cattle,
to auction the ministers themselves?

Because they have imagined Tamils
to be a sack of gooseberries,
the pressure in the chest grows.
We must cut it down!
When will all Tamils unite?
When will the domination of evil men
be driven back?

<!-- scan 309 -->

I know the question that rises.
There is black soil in the land,
weeds in the field,
chaff in paddy,
and a traitor among Tamils —
not far away, but near!

They want office,
a palanquin ride every day.
For that,
if the enemy's feet must be washed,
they race ahead to do it.
The pleasure such Vibhishanas find
is like the pleasure of prostitutes
who embrace a man's corpse for money!

Therefore,
do not fear Tamil traitors.
They are rusted nails.
Those nails cannot even hold
pictures of Anna and Periyar;
how can they bear their principles?

Let cowards move aside.
Let heroes continue.
We shall beat the drum
and raise the cry of the people;
we shall remove degradation.

Ayya lit the lamp of reason.
Anna's hand protected it
from being extinguished by wind.
Again a great wind blows today.
Anna's hand is not here;
in its place let thousands,
lakhs, crores of hands stretch out.
Every one of those hands

<!-- scan 310 -->

belongs to Anna —
the hands of pure brothers,
the hands of cadres!
All of them are Anna's hands.

`Agaram` is the first letter of Tamil;
Anna's name is the first letter for Tamils.
That leader of the first letter gave us
the Munnetra Kazhagam — the Progressive Federation.

If progress is to reach Tamils,
the Three Great Celebration alone is not enough.
To guard this great land,
to guard our language,
today itself we must take a vow.

We must gain Periyar's courage.
We must understand Perarignar Anna's clarity.
We need firmness that does not fear great opposition.
Death comes at six or at a hundred —
let what must happen, happen.
We need a heart of sacrifice
that does not count the sweetness of life.
We must open a brave chest
to bullets that pierce through.
We need the resolve of the revolutionary poet:
“The flower-grove where the mango-cuckoo sings —
a prison that thinks it can cage us.”

We must roll frauds away.
We must nurture newness.
We must crush counterfeits.
We must move as warriors of fighting spirit.
Periyar and Anna must live forever
in our hearts.

❀
