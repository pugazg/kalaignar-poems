# Views of Society...!

<!-- scan 318 -->

Presiding poem delivered on 7 January 1980
at the poetry assembly held for the Golden Jubilee
of *Ananda Vikatan*.

Greetings,
poets who have taken the thread of imagination
and woven poems
on the Tamil loom — greetings!

Elders who have come to this hall
with enduring love for Tamil — greetings!

Swarms of bees who have flown here
to taste the honey of song!
We are fragrant flowers;
we welcome and salute you.
We are people who do not abandon affection,
people who do not forget human fragrance.

He was worthy of undying fame;
was there any undertaking of his
that did not flourish?
Arrogant words were scarce;
Vasan's heart was one
no one could hate.

<!-- scan 319 -->

Vasan's head never rose in conceit,
and that head never bent
before the unworthy.

★ ★ ★

In how many ways we nurture
Tamil, sweet as sugarcane!
Who nurtured *Vikatan* itself
through pearl-like humour,
essays and stories,
like a fresh cluster of flowers?
He is not with us today.
Yet the wall he built remains,
and the pictures remain;
let us drink them deeply
with the eyes of the heart.

★ ★ ★

Is *Ananda Vikatan* fifty years old?
No — there is a correction:
fifty-two is the truth!
Truthful Vikatan,
I ask only to tease:
what profit is there in reducing your age?
Are you still wandering the cinema world
looking for another opportunity?

The older you grow,
the more diamond-hard you become.
Taking on colourful roles,
you pour out thought.
Have you not heard
the cool moon, blazing sun and stars
claim ages of crores upon crores of years?
Live long like them!

O jewel of the eye, dark jewel,
golden jewel that makes us laugh with thought,
new jewel, jewel of fame!
With manners like a cluster of many gems,
you delight your reader-farmers
like a mound of harvested grain.
Lady, the excitement we gain from seeing you —

<!-- scan 320 -->

we do not gain even from a Kanchipuram sari
spread before our eyes!

After a musician's concert,
the vidwans turn the pages of *Vikatan*
to read the review.
A scholar feels proud
only when *Vikatan* gives his book a review.
Even a cartoon,
in Vikatan's pages,
under Mali's hand,
came alive and spoke.

Kalki's stories,
springs of multiplying word-wealth,
captured the mind.

Even when policies differed,
Vikatan showed in practice
that culture is needed in the way disagreement is spoken.
Even a pond may carry a little moss;
so too there may be a fault sometimes,
but not a crime.

When we said,
“Tamizh Nadu for Tamils,”
Vikatan answered,
“A rat-hole for rats!”
Perhaps Vikatan,
though it once ran a rationalist contest,
was also an expert in almanac astrology!
For just as it predicted,
we now see Tamil Nadu as a rat-hole —
yet there are no rats in it!
Eight-legged insects, snakes,
scorpions and centipedes
make an unbearable racket.
The insect called caste has eight legs —
no, no, I have said it wrong:
it has more legs than one can count!
Should we not seek out and destroy
these many-legged creatures that came halfway in?
The violence that writhes like snake and scorpion —
how few are the lives it devours? Tell me!

<!-- scan 321 -->

★ ★ ★

Balasubramanian is like a prop-root
to the banyan tree.
With affection he asked me
to sit as chair of this poetry assembly
so Vikatan's Golden Jubilee might be held with vigour.
He did not say,
“I will value you only if you hold office.”
Even if I lie in street dust,
he said, the red-black *kundrimani* seed
will not lose its colour;
he invited me with kindness,
black and red together.
I call Balu's character,
without like or dislike,
a lamp of light,
and praise it in wonder.

★ ★ ★

Do not be shocked!
I shall call *Ananda Vikatan* “Gandhari.”
Without even the help of a grinding-stone roller,
she gave birth to a hundred children!
Because they were not born from burning stomach-pain,
all those children acquired the qualification
to run separate magazines of their own!

★ ★ ★

As we say “Honourable Sastri,”
“Honourable Pillai,”
“Honourable Minister,”
Vikatan has never possessed
that kind of “weighty” honour at all.

<!-- scan 322 -->

The reason?
No one weighs Vikatan
and sends it to the old-paper shop.
When it is *bound* and carefully preserved,
where is the “pound” or kilo weight to speak of?

In women's hands,
Vikatan is a lover to their eyes;
unwilling to part,
they embrace it against their silk saris.
For men, Vikatan is a beautiful maiden;
they are enchanted even by
the “jacket” beauty of its cover.

Today Vikatan is the bridegroom,
so teasing wedding play is customary.
Do not grow angry for nothing!

Vikatan is a doctor:
when it is nearby,
every patient receives some comfort.

Vikatan is a wanderer:
you can see it on every train and bus.

Vikatan is a madman.
Why?
Wherever it is,
ten people keep circling around it!

Here too are ten people
who have come to sing sweet Tamil.
Even if you take them as ten people
circling around me — so be it!

<!-- scan 323 -->

Am I not mad myself
when it comes to Tamil?
Even if I were born a dog,
I would wag my tail
before a servant of Tamil.
Even if I wandered as a rabid dog,
would I not bark only
“Tamil! Tamil!”?

Tamil, my mother!
Tamil that raised me
as a cradle-lullaby!
Tamil sweet as a jackfruit segment
mixed with flesh, life and honey!
Until my breath dissolves into the sky,
I shall do my duty
to the soil in which I was born.

But while serving nectar-tongued Tamil,
our gaze toward social progress
must also remain sharp.
That is why Vikatan eagerly invites us
to take a **“view of society.”**

★ ★ ★

“Society” — poor thing,
a pitiable word!
By putting the blame upon it,
thousands upon thousands of injustices
are delivered by shameless villains.
Those orphan children then go
to law school in colleges of anarchy!

So that the laws they study
do not go useless,
they rapidly turn into exploding bombs.
If asked, “Is this not violence?”
the answer rises sharply:
“What better way did society teach us?”

<!-- scan 324 -->

Poor society — pitiable society!

When a burglar carrying a crowbar,
or a boy working scissors through a festival crowd,
is caught red-handed,
he cries:
“God, what can I do?
Blind society made me a thief!”

Poor society — pitiable society!

A man worships Mādan and Kādan as gods,
tears the heads from human buds,
and gives human sacrifice to a sorcerer.
If we cry, “You great sinner!”
he too replies:
“What can I do?
This destructive society made me like this.”

Poor society — pitiable society!

Even the robber
who breaks the arms and legs of ancient statues
and carries on international trade with them
plainly declares:
“Cruel society drove me
into this degraded trade.”

Poor society — pitiable society!

Because no green light was shown,
“I lit this light suited to my desire,”
says the lady of the red light.

<!-- scan 325 -->

If we spit,
“Shame! Is this a livelihood?”
she spits back:
“Shame on this ‘society’!”

Poor society — pitiable society!

★ ★ ★

Yet, taken as a whole,
some truth does shine here
in full light.

If we build no foundation of social progress,
then raise a mansion of socialist economics
and place international-political finials upon it,
it will crack and collapse.
Understanding this difficulty,
Vikatan asks us to look upon
“society.”

A gathering of human flowers
placed a sacred fence
around its own garden;
that fence is called society.

Human beings were not
the first life on earth:
worms, insects, fish, monkeys,
animals and birds came before.
Does the phrase “fragrance of a previous birth”
make sense to you now?
The “next birth,” and the birth after that —
are they not really the forms
that human beings may take
after crores upon crores of years of change?

<!-- scan 326 -->

The human species arose upon the earth.
I say with certainty:
at that time there was one caste alone.
After discovering fire,
restraining water,
and taking up family responsibility,
society was born.

The society that was born spread wide
and became a great society
with embankments of division.
“Birth is equal to all lives;
distinction comes from differing deeds” —
groups formed in harmony with this Kural.
Then small-minded men entered across the path
and created castes opposed to moral law.
Love fell;
quarrels multiplied.

The result?
In the darkness of ignorance,
man lived by eating man.
Today, in the light of science,
man lives by killing man!

Society is a garden of ordered flowers,
but every separate flower
must give fragrance.
A tiny drop becomes a great flood;
should every drop be a drop of poison?

Yet poison is drunk till dawn
as though it were honey — they call it liquor.
There are laws in the country to prohibit it;
there are also schemes here and there
to keep the mixing going!

Adulteration itself becomes a trade.
Clay pellets are married into grain.
Those who learn this art
and lose their moral standing
are the very people who sell
our country's currency for a good price —
and sell the currency of business honesty too!

<!-- scan 327 -->

If we ask,
“Can you do such poisonous deeds
that ruin health and nation?”
they move their tongues and say:
“What can I do? It is a defect of society.”

Poor society — pitiable society!

Goats, cattle, pigs and even donkeys
may brush against one's clothes
and circle the temple.
But if only a child of God
from the Harijan community touches them,
they cry “Tiruneelakanta!”
If asked,
“Is this cruelty right?”
they answer:
“It is justice that has existed in society from olden days.”

Poor society — pitiable society!
A society that must fall under our gaze!

I do not believe the story
that Shiva opened a third eye in his forehead.
Even so,
I wish that story would come true here
to burn these degrading social evils
to ash.
Let us open the eye in our forehead!
For the country to live,
for suffering to end,
let our gaze fall strongly upon society.

<!-- scan 328 -->

Poets who stand tall
like mountains of imagination,
raise your voices as one!
Let us protect rights!
Let us honour relationships!
Let us make truths speak!
Let us make evil hearts grow clear!

## Translator notes

- The title witnesses are exact; the ellipsis and terminal `!` are retained in English.
- The poem's Golden-Jubilee humour depends on Tamil/English magazine culture and wordplay: `கனம்` (“weight/honour”), *bound*/*pound*, the cover as a woman's “jacket,” and Vikatan as Gandhari with a hundred magazine-children. The English keeps these jokes explicit rather than replacing them with unrelated puns.
- Vasan, Mali, Kalki and Balasubramanian are retained as source-named figures without external biographical expansion.
- The repeated `பாபம் சமுதாயம், பரிதாபத்திற்குரிய சமுதாயம்!` is kept as **“Poor society — pitiable society!”** because repetition is the poem's governing satire.
- The source term `சண்டாளத் தொழில்` in the antiquities-smuggling passage is rendered “degraded trade”; the Tamil source is not altered.
- Evolution, caste, untouchability, alcohol, adulteration, crime and social causation are presented as Kalaignar's argument in this poem, not as a fact-check layer.
