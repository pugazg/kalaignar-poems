# There Are Some Countries!

<!-- scan 457 -->

There are some countries!
....
From village rule to national rule,
the child called democracy
is never even “born”!
While it still writhes in the womb
like a worm,
they abort it and rejoice!
If, by some mistake, it escapes,
is born and begins to crawl,
they cut off its head
and make “soup”!

Let the dignity of people's rule become dust!
Become dust! Die away!
Or else become barren forever—
for that, they stand and pray
for divine grace!

There, a change of government
happens in half a second!
On the ruler's chest,
bullets become garlands—
ornaments and necklaces!
On the next man's chest,
a heavy shield is pressed;
if he too lies down,
who knows whether he will rise!

Guns themselves
serve as the pillars
that hold up the tents of rulers—

<!-- scan 458 -->

⟦continuation from previous page⟧

they do the work;
they “finish the job”!
Coronation ceremonies
are conducted at the front
by drawn swords themselves!
A royal anointing means
bathing the ceremonial halls
with the red water
brought in pots of blood!

There are some countries!
There,
the child called democracy
is never “born”!

One who rides an elephant beneath a royal parasol—
his very intestines are pulled out,
made into a garland,
and beautifully placed
upon the next sacrificial goat!
Those who raised him up and praised him
will take even his bones!
“This is our beautiful sceptre,” they say!
The one who holds that sceptre—
when will he burn
like a stack of straw?
Who can know?

If it is monarchy,
perhaps its meaning is:
“End that rule!”—
and finish it with a cruel club!

<!-- scan 459 -->

⟦continuation from previous page⟧

Ended and ended,
yet never ending—
there are some countries
that remain monarchies!
There,
the child called democracy
is never “born.”

The dalliance and pleasure of dictatorship!
On the pleasure-bed of autocracy,
its exclusive entertainments—
in the middle of that revel,
if anyone merely says “mm,”
it means prison!
If anyone asks “why,”
it means exile!

Dictatorship is a banana tree—
its own shoot will bring it down!
I do not know why:
the banyan tree of democracy
never sends down roots in that soil!
Why? I do not know.
They have cut the trunk itself
and pulled out the taproot!

On the flanks of India,
two countries sprouted!
There,
only in fields of blood
do crops of government grow!
“Mujibur's life is ended”—
and blood ran from all our eyes!
Yet one day dawned:
Bengal's earth

<!-- scan 460 -->

⟦continuation from previous page⟧

flowered! Flowered!
The world rejoiced!
They hailed him as Bangabandhu—
and then rolled the head
of that golden *bandhu*
across the ground like a ball,
and played in delight!

Now here is another ball game
in the arena of Bangladesh!
The skull-ball of Ziaur Rahiman,
rolling past the “goalkeeper,”
was caught in a net of conspiracy,
struck the iron post
and broke apart!

Dictatorship—
it is a banana tree;
its own shoot will bring it down!

A little breeze of democracy
blew in Bangladesh.
Even that is being blocked,
and the work of spreading
a jewel-red carpet
for a military durbar
moves swiftly forward!

There are some countries!
There,
the child called democracy
is never “born”!
If, by some mistake, it is born and begins to crawl,
they cut off its head
and make “soup”!

This, today,
is the painful news
Bengal gives us!

## Translator note

The source's central metaphor is the unborn/killed “child of democracy,” set against coups and blood-soaked coronations. `வங்க பந்து` activates a Tamil `பந்து` wordplay between *bandhu* (kin/friend, in Bangabandhu) and “ball”; the English retains *bandhu* and explicitly carries the ball image. The source-visible name `ஜியாவுர் ரகிமான்` is rendered conservatively as “Ziaur Rahiman” rather than silently normalised to an outside spelling.
