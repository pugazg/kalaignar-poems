# “On Arignar Anna’s Path”

<!-- scan 362 -->

As part of the Three Great Celebrations,
at the Kalaignar Marriage Hall in Anna Arivalayam,
on the evening of 16 September 1994,
Leader Kalaignar presided over the poetry assembly
and sang this poem.

Greetings!

August 15 —
the birthday of freedom.
September 15 —
for the Tamil,
the birthday of breath.
September 17 —
for the Tamil,
the birthday of heat.
And because on that very day
self-respect too was born,
we were born;
yes — the Kazhagam was born.

Joining all these together,
to explain a principle strong as iron,
we hold the pearl-like festival —
the Three Great Celebrations.

<!-- scan 363 -->

At this celebration,
my greetings to the poets
who have come to sing in the assembly,
and to the great Tamil people
who have come to see it.

One day all the flowers
gathered together,
held a conference,
and passed a resolution:

“Poets!
From now on,
do not summon our names
as similes in your poems!”

Looking toward the earth,
pleading with her eyes,
the white moon made a request:

“Enough of placing me in melody
and singing of me.
Leave me free in the sky!”

The clouds assembled,
held a procession,
and carried a petition:

“Poets!
Do not any longer show clouds
as similes for the hair
of enchanting beauties!”

<!-- scan 364 -->

Deer and peacocks,
and many others,
staged a blockade
and issued a demand:

“Poets! How long
will you drag us in,
shut us inside the cage of poetry,
and enjoy yourselves?
We want freedom!”
They leapt and bounded as they spoke.

Everyone in our gathering of poets
stood astonished.
“Why this demand?
Why this struggle? This procession?”
we asked,
looking toward the antelope,
the forest peacock,
the moon in the sky,
and the many-coloured flower.
Their answer was:

“By singing our praise
and showing us as similes,
again and again,
you have bored even us!”

Deer and honey, peacock and cuckoo,
sky and sea, sun-ray and moon,
loft-pigeon, paired parrot,
mango, jackfruit and banana,
dancing creeper, singing waterfall,
agil, cloud, mountain and statue —
all of them now announced
that without their cooperation —

<!-- scan 365 -->

“Very well,
you tell us yourselves:
what name is there
that never grows tiresome?”
We stood and asked.

There is one name
that never grows stale!
Think of it —
a name untouched
by sourness or bitterness!
A name that lives forever
as literature!
A name that has become
an incomparable history!

You call us flowers;
we wilt.
That name never wilts;
it spreads fragrance forever!

You call us the moon;
we wane and become a crescent.
That name, know this,
gives cool light
without diminishing!

You call us clouds;
only when wind blows
do we dissolve into rain.
Find one name
that without any wind
pours a rain of compassion
and, as the central substance of your poem,
brings it greatness.

<!-- scan 366 -->

“That name is the precious name
Anna!” —
so the waterfall, the breeze,
the beautiful flower
and all the familiar things
rejoiced within
and cried out in one voice.

Only those who do not know him
need Anna introduced to them.
Yet every day we see
this story acted before us:
even those who do not know him at all
believe they can move through the country
only by pretending
to know him very well!

When flowers and all the other
objects of simile themselves
become distressed
and protest:
“Do not drag our names into everything
until you make them tiresome,”
what is the name
that never tires us?
The name engraved
in golden letters in history,
a name that removes weariness
and gives delight —
Anna, Anna, Anna —
is it not that very name?

Even filthy operators
utter Anna’s name —

<!-- scan 367 -->

three hundred times before every breath —
and say they will fashion weapons
from iron that has not even ripened!
Anna does not refuse them,
not even for the sake of argument.
Perhaps our Anna still believes
one must possess a heart
that can bear anything!

They say Periyar’s ideas
were bitter medicine;
Perarignar Anna placed that medicine
inside sweetness
and gave it gently.

“Break Vinayaka,” Periyar declared,
and broke him!
“Do not needlessly break Vinayaka;
do not break coconuts for Vinayaka either” —
Anna stood holding this Kazhagam principle
like a lamp in his hand.

Yet those who today issue
Vinayaka Chaturthi greetings
make the skies shake
by shouting the name
of that brilliant Anna!

Ministers eat ritual earth,
roll inside Mariamman temples,
wrap themselves in neem-leaf garments,
celebrate goddess festivals —

<!-- scan 368 -->

and then say,
“We are driving out superstition.”
Instead of moving away in shame,
“Shame! Shame!”
the office-seeking chameleons
who go nearby to perform *paada puja*
are themselves called
lamps of rationalism!

Using Anna’s name,
they untie the knot so beautifully —
is this justice?

From childhood Anna lived
for many years in Kanchi.
History says
he never went to see
the elder of the monastery
in the neighbouring street.
Today, a visit to see the junior pontiff
is printed in eight-column headlines!
Anna, look at the suffering undergone
by this “evolutionary growth” of rationalism!

Once we heard Navalar explain
the difference
between those who merely saw Anna
and those who lived and moved with him.
Today we hear that very man
lean toward the side of those who only saw him
and sing a prefatory hymn!

Those who yesterday performed penance
longing for their face
to appear beside mine in a photograph,
today, Anna,
when they see a photograph
in which I sit beside you —

<!-- scan 369 -->

try to cover it over with ink!
All these petty men
want to rule the state.
Ah, Anna —
now I understand the meaning of your smile!

“You need a broad heart, little brother,
a cool heart that can forgive
even ungrateful sinners,”
you tell me.
Yes, Anna —
is that not why
I asked to borrow your heart
and received it?

Rationalism, equality, socialism,
ethnic consciousness, social justice,
love of language —
because they came to rule
in the name of all these,
new commentaries are now offered for each!
Those who received office from them,
and those who received many kinds of favour,
nod their heads:
“Yes... yes.”

If the demon Bakasura of Bharata
had teeth two *kathams* long
and gaps between them three *kathams* wide,
the temple-priest’s disciple
who beats the drum saying “yes, yes”
to Padavettamman —
what difference is there
between him and these people?

You stand as a statue,
and smile.
They slash principle with a killing sword;
you endure it.
Why? We understand that too, Anna:
there are younger brothers
who have sworn to labour without tiring
on Anna’s path —

<!-- scan 370 -->

standing as the flame of Self-Respect.
They will not go astray.
To the common people
who are deceived by counterfeits,
they will make truths understood
and falsehoods rejected.
You trust this, Anna.
So that your trust will not be wasted,
we shall walk on, Anna!

## Translator notes

- The canonical and contents title witnesses are exact. `அறிஞர் அண்ணா` is rendered as **Arignar Anna**, retaining the familiar Tamil honorific rather than flattening it into a generic “scholar.”
- The poem’s opening is the 1994 Three Great Celebrations frame: Independence Day, Anna’s birthday, Periyar’s birthday and the birth of the Kazhagam are translated as the source presents them.
- The flowers/moon/clouds/animals “strike” against poetic simile is retained as an extended comic conceit whose resolution is that Anna’s name alone never grows stale.
- Periyar/Vinayaka, temple ritual, Kanchi monastery, Navalar and photograph passages are translated as Kalaignar’s polemical source claims; no outside political reconciliation is inserted.
- The line about having borrowed Anna’s heart deliberately echoes the anthology’s earlier elegiac language and is retained as an internal literary recurrence.
- Scan 371 is an item-owned blank/show-through verso. Its provenance marker is retained even though it has no edition text; structural scans 372–373 belong separately to the `கண்ணீர்த் துளிகள்` anthology divider and are not part of this item.
