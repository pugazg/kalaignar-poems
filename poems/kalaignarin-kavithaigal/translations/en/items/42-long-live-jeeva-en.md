# Long Live Jeeva

<!-- scan 382 -->

The good Tamil that makes our name fragrant is fragrant;
the name Nanjil is fragrant;
Boothapandi, fragrant with song, is fragrant;
and the mouth that sings the fame
of the noble Jeeva
is fragrant with honey!

At an age when the mist
of restless youth
had not yet cleared,
he set his heart on public life
and placed the nation first!
Though the garden was full of flowers,
like a bee that flew far away,
he said, “Service alone is my life,”
and flew throughout the land.

A few days he would stay at home;
many days he would languish
inside a prison cage!
On the stage he would beat the drum;
through the summer of life
he would keep travelling!
He would give us the flower *Thamarai*;
from it we would drink
Tamil nectar!

<!-- scan 383 -->

He would speak with arm raised —
and there
we would see a fierce storm blow!
He would call, “Come!” —
and at that yearning
we would thrill!

Where are those mountain-like shoulders?
Where is that strength that never diminished?
Where is that noble character?
Where is that nature that laboured for the nation?
Where is that face filled with courage?
Where is the light poured by those eyes?

That striped tiger
whose very blood
breathed the fragrance of revolution,
that Tamil poet
firm in principle —
has cruel death robbed him away
and rejoiced?
So the people of the land ask
and cry out in anguish!

Long live Jeeva!
Long live forever!

*Nam Nadu*, 21.3.1963

## Translator notes

- The canonical title has no terminal `!`; the contents witness does. The English title follows the canonical opening.
- The opening repeatedly uses `மணம்` / fragrance across Tamil, Nanjil, Boothapandi and the mouth praising Jeeva. English retains the repetition rather than replacing it with varied synonyms.
- `தாமரை` is both “lotus” and the title *Thamarai*; the English keeps the publication/title form in italics while retaining the flower metaphor.
- The final `வரிப்புலி` is rendered literally as “striped tiger,” preserving the revolutionary-animal image.
