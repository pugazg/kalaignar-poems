# Fountain of Imagination

<!-- scan 413 -->

*(Written spontaneously by Kalaignar after seeing a beautiful fountain in an Orlando lake while touring the United States in 1971 at the invitation of the U.S. government.)*

In a rare lake in the city called Orlando
I saw a lovely firework-like fountain.
To the rhythm set by the coloured lights,
without missing a beat,
it dances—
what can I say of the grace
of that fountain-dancer’s performance!
One sight: as though milk-white ice
were rolling across a floor of green grass—
and while you are still looking,
*pat!* it changes into another sight!
As though this is how the Apollo here once launched,
arms of water climb the sky,
ripping open the dark.
In a goldsmith’s workshop,
yellow light spreading gold—
then, like an arrow loosed from a bow,

<!-- scan 414 -->

it shoots upward with a whirr.
When people of Stone-Age belief
cut down a living creature as sacrifice
before their gods,
red colour leaps and hisses
like the blood that spurts out.
On the Margazhi festival days in our towns,
women spread designs before their thresholds,
and in each street lay pumpkin flowers
upon the *kolam* they have drawn—
I saw a sight just as rich for the eyes.
Luckily—
I had the chance to enjoy it alone;
that is how I escaped!
Had a woman been beside me,
she would have counted every colour
in the fountain—and the very next day
asked for one sari of each kind!

## Translator note

The poem moves rapidly between dance, rocket, goldsmith, sacrificial-blood and Tamil *kolam* imagery. `அப்போலோ` is retained as Apollo in the source's U.S.-travel/space-flight context; the closing gendered sari joke is translated as source humour rather than modernised.
