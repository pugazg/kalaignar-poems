# Panneerselvam!

<!-- scan 374 -->

In 1940, when Dravidian commander
A. T. Panneerselvam travelled by aircraft to London
to serve as adviser to the Secretary of State for India,
the aircraft fell into the Oman Sea and sank.
This is the tear-soaked portrait
that Kalaignar, while still young,
wrote about the departed Panneerselvam
in *Murasoli*, then published from Tiruvarur.

Those who say the people of the world
sit in a dark room with both eyes closed
and roll about —
let ignorance sink!
To make that truth known,
Periyar’s drum thundered.
Along that path came
a man... a beloved one... a comrade,
our precious Selvam...
a warrior of the knowledge movement.

When, in the good light of the sun,
the dusk says, “My time has come,”
and withdraws —
if, over there,
the morning sun that rose in the east
suddenly falls into the sea,
will not the world be shaken?
Will it not wither and waste away?

Just as this land was stepping forward
to cast off darkness and gain light,
a death-cloud of delusion
hid the young sunbeam!

O scion of the clan
with long shoulders that can lift mountains!
Lion-bull who came out of the cave
with a killing sword
to tear out the entrails
of the pack of little foxes!

<!-- scan 375 -->

O mountain-shouldered one!
O hill of ruby!
Your speech was a segment of ripe fruit;
your glance, a sweep of cool mist!
You have gone —
but will your fame go with you?

Panneerselvam!
Where is your milk-soft face?
Where is your Pandyan moustache?
Have the desolate waves
of the hunger-maddened sea
finally grown still after eating you?

“Let them eat... let them eat and rejoice...
let the people of the Dravidian land
think it so forever” —
where have you hidden yourself?

Born in wealth
and raised in wealth,
how did you learn
the character of the worker?

O noble one!
The tears we shed and shed
would conquer a thousand Oman seas!

Unblemished golden Dravidian land!
For all the people drunk on the liquor of old custom
to wake and rise —
to see that day
will be the offering to your work!

Though opposition circles us
and rages like a cyclone,
we have set out declaring:
“Victory is ours!”

We are not paupers —
we are descendants
of the guardian of Thanjavur!
Even if we must fall,
we shall sing of your deeds
and fall!

## Translator notes

- The canonical title compounds the name as `பன்னீர்ச்செல்வமே!`; the contents witness separates it as `பன்னீர்ச் செல்வமே` and omits the exclamation. The English title follows the canonical personal-name reading.
- The contextual note and the Oman Sea reference are translated exactly as this anthology witness frames A. T. Panneerselvam’s 1940 death; Phase 4 does not replace that source note with an outside biographical account.
- The poem’s young-sun, death-cloud, lion, mountain and sea imagery is intentionally heightened elegy and is retained rather than reduced to prose memorial description.
- `திருவிடத்தார்` / Dravidian-land rhetoric and the Periyar knowledge-movement frame are translated as Kalaignar’s youthful source voice.
