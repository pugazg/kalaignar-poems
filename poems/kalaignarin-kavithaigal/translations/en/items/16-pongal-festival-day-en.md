# Pongal Festival Day

<!-- scan 175 -->

A presiding poetic address by
Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi
at the *kaviyarangam* titled
“Pongal Festival Day,”
broadcast by Chennai Radio
on 12 January 1970.

★

The waters of tanks, ponds and canals
had all turned to vapour;
a storm of drought had come,
and crops had reached the point of death.
Seeing that condition, the Tamil Government
sprang into action and swiftly cooled the torment of heat.
Painted clouds conceived,
came over the earth
and gave birth to the child called rain.
The old government order called food control
was withdrawn.
So, on Pongal — this festival that is an epic of joy —
let all the cuckoos that once called begin calling again;
let Mother Tamil walk over mountains of flowers
scattered by her people;
let every person in our State,
amid the tastes of mango, jackfruit and banana,
eat sweet Pongal to the full.
To make that possible,
we labour as far as our strength permits.
And, to sharpen the mind a little—

<!-- scan 176 -->

Radio said,
“Come to the whetstone of the *kaviyarangam*.”
Since I possess a mind,
I have come;
I have come because I possess a mind,
to have it given a new edge!

Around me are refined Tamil poets
who praise in ordered measure,
ready to take the jackfruit of thought
that lies rooted deep
and set down its sweet bulbs one by one.
I have not mastered all the *yappu* arts
of *thalai* and *thodai*;
like a woman's arm without bangles,
I shall shape and offer a few songs.

Bear with me, bulls among poets!
On this beautiful Tamil Pongal day,
under the theme “The War-Drum of Love,”
here are six of you who have come to sing, sirs!
As the old line says, “With Guha we became five,”
count yourselves, with this son of Tamil,
as seven.
Seven of us are here on this stage;
crying “Rise, Tamilian!”
we shall hoist the flag of Farmers' Day.
To guard the land of the warriors,
we shall beat our chests and swear our pledge!

Today we cry, “Let the milk boil over!”
Is it fresh cow's milk? No, no!
It is the *pāl* given by the charioteer of classical Tamil:
*Arathuppāl*, *Porutpāl*, *Kāmattuppāl* —
and beyond those *pāl*,
what other milk/part should boil over?

“I asked her to bring sugarcane for me to taste”—
but she lifted up the child,

<!-- scan 177 -->

fruit and nectar together, and gave it to me;
that is joy.
“Bring turmeric,” she said.
“What for? Let me rub it across your face!” I said.

Seeing the happiness in my house,
let the neighbours not chew ginger in envy;
uproot it instead,
and tie it to the Pongal pot as custom demands!
There is Pongal even for the bull that butts;
and Pongal too for the strong-shouldered hero
who goes out to seize the bull.

“The man who conquers the bull
is the bull-like man fit for me,”
said the maiden with palmyra-bright teeth:
such lines of literature
bring the old days back to mind.

When light arrives, darkness recedes;
sculptures cut by the chisel gleam;
peacocks dancing in delight spread their plumes;
a young man pulls close, asking for clear honey;
the maiden gives him a single drop
and flies away!

On Pongal day,
so that fresh joy may arise everywhere,
the sun has risen.
To approach all things through love and win,
the Rising Sun has risen high today!

Do you see the green flood,
like a green mat spread before us?
Hear the laughter of the little river singing *sindhu*!
Can the roaring forest-torrent,
charging like a wild tiger,
be allowed to burst beyond its dams?
See the Government standing beside every effort
to cultivate through agricultural credit.
To supply fertiliser cheaply,
a giving hand is needed;

<!-- scan 178 -->

Anna, judging its worth,
left that hand with us before he went.
Some shake their heads and refuse to accept us,
pointing only to holes.
I say: every hole in the *nadaswaram*
exists for good music.
If you are the fingers that move,
even the wind becomes melody today.

If, in factories rising to touch the sky,
some exist only to live for themselves,
our country becomes a head infested with lice.
The medicine I have studied for that is socialism.
Until that condition itself arrives,
it is right, in the present condition,
to find ways for working people to live.
Let us open our mouths and pledge it today.
We have not locked away the principle of common ownership;
nor have we spoken in rejection of it.
That ideal now inside books
must, little by little,
come inside the country.
Before every idea hidden in song
can spread as socialism,
let industry take shape in a fitting way;
let cooperative forms multiply in our land.
On Pongal day let us take up a new path.
Call this sweet path an interim path;
later we shall make even stones ripen.

By the farmer's sweat,
the mother-field became pregnant;
from her womb came a daughter,
the maiden called the grain-ear,
cherished by all.
Like a bride in the wedding hall,
she stands with bowed head
on Pongal day.

<!-- scan 179 -->

Those called Tamils rejoice;
they leap crying “thai, thai!”
Is not *Thai* the month that brings cool radiance?
Does not *thaiyal* mean woman?
Is not *thaiththal* — stitching — also a kind of work?
Some stitch us with thorns;
some stitch caps for us!

Those called Tamils rejoice;
they leap crying “thai, thai!”
They call me and say, “Poet, stitch!”
I say stitching is not my profession;
burying is my profession.
To bury evil is a deed that removes evil;
to bury helplessness is a sweet labour;
to bury ignorance is the work of the wise;
and to bury my face at the feet
of the noble one who gave his life
so that the voiceless might live —
that is my profession.

Two hundred thousand people who received *patta* titles bless us.
We plan so water may be given without obstruction.
For those to whom credit once seemed an unreachable fruit,
we make a special effort so the development bank
may lend without hindrance.
To end the grief of wandering about,
wondering whether an official's eye would ever fall upon them,
one day every month we require officials
to go into villages and render *Manu Neethi*.

We are changing the old condition
in which electricity went only
to the houses of wealthy men glittering with gold;
we labour so that, by 1972,
electric connections may reach everywhere
in my Tamil sacred land.
For praising all this on Pongal day,
I ask for no garlands in heaps,
no prizes.
Poets of this assembly,
if you sing one beautiful song,

<!-- scan 180 -->

I shall stand upright
like the Pandyan who destroyed the enemy field.
In a land where a woman with a winnowing tray
could face a tiger from its cave,
shall we stand bewildered before violence?
We shall overthrow it.
Let us cast away smoky ideas
and honour new thought on Pongal day.
Let us call the greying thought of old men
mere dye for the hair;
and is it not wrong
to accept the inflated claims of liars
without examining them?

Learned in literature,
masters of sweet-Tamil poetry,
our poets will sing on several themes
and offer supporting staffs
for Tamil life.
Selfish greed is the taproot of internal enmity;
violence grows as its branches.
From lack of unity
comes inequality.
And the cause of all these,
we shall say, is poverty of understanding.
A fine poet, rich in substance,
will give us thoughts of the Buddha.

Some great men say
this is the precious hour
to create a movement that drives poverty away.
Is the demon of poverty an ox
that moves at such a slow pace?
If you give it eight steps,
do not let it take even one back!
Chase it and it returns;
threaten it and it returns;
but if truth's roar knocks out its teeth
and casts it down lifeless,
even if you roll it over, it will not rise.

They say imagination thrives in poverty,
that pearl after pearl of poetry is born there.
They give, in clusters, examples:
Sathimuththa Pulavar,
Saththa Kavirayar,
and wandering *panars* of every day.

<!-- scan 181 -->

Yet there are many stories too
of poetry born in prosperity.
What of King Athiveerarama?
What of the southern Pandyan king?
Was he not a lion among poets
with sugarcane-sweet speech?
Those who say poverty multiplies poetry
are often the great wealthy men
who will not permit prosperity to the poor.
To reveal all this through learning,
let the gifted Ki. Va. Ja. come!

(Poet Ki. Va. Jagannathan completes his poem on “Poverty.”)

**Chair — Kalaignar:**

Poverty has gone; suffering is cured!
Excellent, excellent — let us praise the friend's poem
with dark eyes widening in delight.
But meanwhile we see another cruelty enter:
the selfish illusion that enslaves.
Against greed as it comes,
with a desire for battle let arrows be loosed;
the distinguished poet Abdul Kafur
will come carrying sharpened Tamil.

(Poet Abdul Kafur completes his poem on “Selfish Greed.”)

**Chair — Kalaignar:**

Again and again selfishness returns;
he lashed it with classical Tamil.
Good, good — good indeed!
Next—

<!-- scan 182 -->

let us take internal enmity in hand.
Like a fruit hidden beneath a leaf,
it cannot be seen by the eye;
it appears inside friendship,
taking a new form every day;
even a scorpion would blush at the poison it mixes.
Sword against sword is open enmity;
shoulder joined to shoulder,
while betrayal is done — that is internal enmity.
There is a brave man in this *kaviyarangam*
to seek that enemy
and strike it down the instant it is found.
His name itself is Thooran.
A man with moisture in the heart,
he will speak a poem of leaping valour.

(Poet Periyasamy Thooran completes his poem on “Internal Enmity.”)

**Chair — Kalaignar:**

Internal enmity ran away,
searched for another place to enter among us,
withered when it could not,
and danced only to submit!
I draw the line clearly:
like poison mixed skillfully into milk,
it first peers in;
then, like a banyan's aerial roots,
it spreads here and there.
Therefore bolt the door against internal enmity;
guard your distinct character.
Next:
those who murder the principle
“United we live” and crow over it—

<!-- scan 183 -->

who will oppose their acts?
With Tamil still as young as it was then,
Tamil sweet as jaggery when tasted,
there is one to string a poem.
Can there be six tastes without salt?
Can there be this arena without Subbu?
The man of Kothamangalam comes
to peck away at disunity.

(Poet Kothamangalam Subbu completes his poem on “Disunity.”)

**Chair — Kalaignar:**

For long we have explained
that unity must exist within difference.
Those who do not understand writhe;
they explode like bombs.
In Kerala yesterday I saw,
wherever my eye fell,
a coconut palm rising from earth
to compete with the areca climbing toward the sky.
So too, in the Soviet land,
those placed low did not lose
when they competed with those placed high.
To make the stench of high and low wither away,
Thangarasan will offer a poem of strength.

(Poet Thangarasan completes his poem on “Inequality.”)

**Chair — Kalaignar:**

Crying out, “Why all this high and low?”
to fill in that foul nursery-bed,
we say violence is not the way;
we say the good path spoken by Mahatma Gandhi is the way.

<!-- scan 184 -->

It is Soundara, who guards the house by gentle means;
let her know her turn has come and sing.

(Poet Soundara Kailasam completes her poem on “Violence.”)

**Chair — Kalaignar, concluding:**

You have completed the *kaviyarangam*;
and within it I circle
like a bee that has drunk honey.
Am I Sadaiyappa Vallal,
who loosened gold for Kamban,
to read again what you have written
and award prizes? No, no.
Some listeners sit like tamarind pounded into a lump;
but can one ever cast aside a stick of red sugarcane
once one has bitten it?
Poets who have distilled and set out
honeyed nectar in this arena,
accept the greetings of this humble man
on this beautiful Pongal Festival Day.

Greetings.
Long live!

## Translator notes

- The source repeatedly exploits Tamil homophones and morphological play around `பால்` (*pāl*: milk / section or division), `தை` (the Tamil month *Thai*), `தையல்` (woman/sewing associations), `தைத்தல்` (to stitch) and `புதைத்தல்` (to bury). The English keeps the Tamil forms where the argument depends on their sound rather than inventing an equivalent pun.
- `உதய சூரியன்` (“Rising Sun”) carries both literal solar imagery and the political emblematic resonance present in Kalaignar's rhetoric; the translation does not neutralise that double register.
- `பட்டா`, `மனுநீதி`, *nadaswaram*, *yappu*, *thalai* and *thodai* are retained/transliterated where a generic English replacement would erase the cultural or rhetorical function.
- References to socialism/common ownership, cooperatives, land titles, rural administrative visits, electrification and agrarian policy are translated as the source's own political claims, not independently fact-checked or harmonised.
