# Freedom Fighters

<!-- scan 72 -->

Freedom
Fighters

A presiding poetic introduction by
Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi
at the *kaviyarangam* titled
“Freedom Fighters,”
broadcast by Tiruchirappalli Radio
on 15 August 1967.

★

My life-breath Anna, mothers and fathers
born of Tamilakam ringed by the sounding sea,
golden-radiant brothers, renowned poets,
all guardians of precious Tamil —
receive my greetings on this sacred day.

Ah...
Where is the glorious land where the Cheras lived?
Why did the kingdom of the valiant Pandyas fall?
In the tireless land where the Cholas once moved,
how did cowardly doctrines gather so thick?
Questions burst in a grieving heart.
Let us drink deep — give life itself for the nation!

<!-- scan 73 -->

The empty sermon that says “we do not want freedom”
is the howl of jackals.
Bulls seized by love of country,
tear away the panic-stricken men;
let us inscribe a newness
at the summit of a tiger's life!
Thus bristling, surging and rising,
the warriors for freedom given by Tamilakam
have come, at the radio station's invitation,
to be born again in fragrant verse;
the mothers have arrived — and I am their leader!

Let us rise to hear, in the language of poets,
the story of Tamils in whose eyes valour smiles,
who sang the anthem of freedom;
let us press their feet-flowers to our eyes and worship.
First...
“They told us: curb the hunger for freedom;
put down the fire of independence.”
Against them,
a lion-bull thundered, “Beat the drum aloud!”
and fell to death at the hands of the wicked —
let us hear that history.

“The sky pours, the earth yields;
why tribute to a king?”
“Once-ruling race, besieged again —
dying worm, cast off the crown!”
Such fire-spilling breath
was Kattabomman's life-breath.
Because it cooled upon the hanging rope,

<!-- scan 74 -->

we call that place Kayathar.
The history of that ground is being served;
let us feast upon it.

(Poet Tiruchitrambala Kavirayar completes
a historical poem on Kattabomman.)

Chairman Kalaignar:

We thought Kattabomman had died;
this Chitrambalam has given birth to him again — live long, well done!
Along that path,
there are mothers still here
to bear child after child;
we stand eager to lift them up and rejoice.

To end the bondage that gives ruin
and win freedom,
“My son, give your head,” said a mother —
and gave it.
The two Marudhu brothers, tiger-sons,
became fragrance in the garland of freedom they had strung;
like an arrow loosed, with speed,
Poet Natarasa takes words ripened in fire,
weaves verse,
and sends the two Marudhu brothers walking before us again —
very well indeed:

(Poet T. Ku. Natarasan completes
a historical poem on the Marudhu Pandiyars.)

Chairman Kalaignar:

Our friend has brought both Marudhus here
and given us a history rich in valour.

<!-- scan 75 -->

Now, to show the great poet Bharati,
as though the two Marudhus were born as a single Marudhu,
poet Murugusundaram comes forward;
let us listen.

“Bharati did not rise high
by looking at old style, old verse, old Tamil books
and merely writing from them.
If the world has not known
the radiant beauty of Bharati's poetry,
the fault lies with the world.
For the weary and for Tamils who had not studied,
he peeled the fruit ripe,
and with segment-sweet Tamil
gave poetry — Subramania Bharati,
the moon that came singing to end the long sleep,
a word-camphor that came to perfume the forest.”

<!-- scan 76 -->

So sang Bharathidasan,
making peacock-Tamil dance before me;
what will this poet sing? Let us see.

(Poet Murugusundaram completes his poem on Bharati.)

Chairman Kalaignar:

Like a waterfall, did he not pour
upright verse upon Bharati? Good, good.
Yet once,
a waterfall's mischief
made Tamil Nadu bear a great storm of shock!
V. V. S. Aiyar, that truth-filled man of action,
was caught in the fierce whirl of a waterfall
and vanished one day;
today another poet
has come to raise him again.

He rose to destroy the foreign forces
that built walls around freedom,
sealed it in,
and stood guard over it;
he rose, disappeared midway —
and here he comes again,
brought to life by the beautiful,
precious classical Tamil of Abdul Rahman:

(Poet Abdul Rahman completes his poem on V. V. S. Aiyar.)

Chairman Kalaignar:

In Tamil Nadu, where ancient wisdom lives in an ancient people,
we have now seen V. V. S. Aiyar,
born of a lineage that wove fame,
a son of a house steadfast in freedom —

<!-- scan 77 -->

and next, the radiant leader
who launched a ship upon the sea off Tuticorin:

“My spotless wife,
in whom fortune, fame and all excellence abide!
Of my dear life, of the life within my heart,
I gladly gave you one lasting half;
the remaining half
I gave beneath the feet of our great nation —
this is the truth!”

With this *agaval* he opened his heart —
a worthy great one, a leader of Tamils.
To sing of the abundant V. O. C.,
our young poet-friend Ilanchezhiyan
comes here with sweet Tamil words,
a good man who dwells in the heart:

(Poet Ilanchezhiyan completes his poem on V. O. C.)

Chairman Kalaignar's concluding poem:

They lived in prison; they pulled the oil-press —
to drive out the white rulers of this land,
many received shortened lives and suffered killing.
Like a sword leaping out beyond its sheath,
without attachment to life, they fought.
On this day when we have assembled to speak their greatness,
to speak the greatness of all who gave their lives for freedom,
I do not possess poetic wealth like these men;
I have not learned to sing poetry.

<!-- scan 78 -->

But because I sing the story of warriors who broke shackles,
I too sing without shackles;
because they slapped their thighs and felled traitors,
my song too slaps its thigh!

Let my poem
go without *yāppu* if it must — but our country,
our language, honour and feeling
must not go without protection.
Holding only to that principle,
even if I have had no opportunity to study prosody,
I will not stand like a tree that never bears fruit;
I turn the feelings soaking my heart into lines
and say one thing for the axes that change their principles to understand:

How many lives, how much blood,
given without end to win freedom!
After digging this earth and labouring so greatly,
after obtaining gold of ten-and-a-half purity,
is it right, instead of showing craft,
setting it as jewellery
and giving it to women like paintings,
to throw it into a rubbish pit?

In a grove where birds cry,
a fine rose plucked while thorns scratched —
should it give fragrance
upon the heads of sword-bearing warriors,
in the hair of modest women,
or should it be handed
to the cramped fist of a wicked monkey?

We shall cherish and protect the freedom we won;
we shall add honour to the warriors who won it.
If one or two relatives alone
live in prosperity — is that freedom?

<!-- scan 79 -->

The learned and the unlearned,
those who toil in the fields,
helpers, smiths, small workers,
good women and jewel-like students —
for all to delight in a life free of evil:
that is freedom.

Let this principle shine sweetly;
let sluggish doctrine disappear;
let us sound it today in rhythmic Tamil.
Golden hill, Tamil, Mother!
We have spoken of the greatness of your lion-sons;
so that Tamilakam may reap victory everywhere,
grant us your grace here, Mother!

Greetings!
Long live!

✤

## Translator notes

- `பத்தரை மாற்று` is retained conceptually as “gold of ten-and-a-half purity,” a traditional purity/value metaphor; the source's rhetorical continuity with earlier items is preserved.
- Names, quoted historical rhetoric and patriotic claims are translated as Kalaignar's source presentation rather than externally harmonised or fact-checked.
- Parenthetical notices marking the other poets' completed contributions are part of the source structure and remain explicit; their poems are not present in this canonical item and therefore are not reconstructed here.
