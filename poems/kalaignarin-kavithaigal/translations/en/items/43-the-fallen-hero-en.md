# The Fallen Hero

<!-- scan 384 -->

Sung in mourning when
Thoothukudi hero K. V. K. Sami was murdered.

★

“Joy in the grove, joy in the breeze — and then
I shall whirl my sword
to guard my own land!”
So roared the young bull.
While he was still
a flower of youth not yet opened,
he stood like a bright lamp,
raising a radiant face and saying,
“I shall scorch the enemy!”

A flower-maiden waited
to reach out, embrace and sport
with that still-unembraced body.
They called him a pearl
of the working people's clan,
a fruit rich with beauty and substance;
they called her
a flower wet with dew.

When would the two
become one?

“Put the drawn sword
back in its sheath for a while, beloved,”
she said,
blocking the lover who raised his sword
and rushed toward the field
where the war-drum roared.

“Do not think, woman,
that you can sacrifice me,
who leap toward liberation,
to your love!
I shall stand in the ranks
that have risen to give life
back to my sacred Tamil land,
ruined and mocked as low
by low men.
I want neither jeweled mansions
nor honeyed speech,” he said.

<!-- scan 385 -->

“Have you come to hate me altogether?” she asked.

“I will not say
I have no attachment to you.
But until the day comes
when there is no snake-pit
in the garden of the southern Tamils,
the lesson of love
is one I cannot study,” he said.
She stood trembling.

“Then let both of us
stand together on the battlefield.
Even if we are extinguished,
let the same fate come to us both.
What answer have you to that, beloved?”

“Lovers sometimes
follow such a course!
But... it will not suit me, my gold!
When I enter the field
and strike the advancing enemy,
my heart may hesitate for a moment,
wondering what has happened
to the slender woman
who entered my heart.
Within that moment
my army may falter.
No, woman!
Bid me farewell, my dear!”

“You refuse only marriage;
there is the flower-bed,
like a leaf spread out for a meal —
come there to this woman!”

“Let the milk-rice
remain in the pot.
Though I am hungry now,
I will not ruin you and go!
Give me leave without delay,
my swan!”

“Is even the invitation
of mango-fruit cheeks forbidden?”

“It is forbidden.
I go carrying an army;
even if I return only as a corpse,
you must still be able to marry.
My love —

<!-- scan 386 -->

if I leave you merely ‘tasted’
and go away,
what profit is there for me?
The great loss would be yours!”

“No! One kiss...!”

“No...! After I return,
a whole shower of them!”

“Do not leave me crying, beloved...”

“We shall be happy all our days!
Put away your worry!”

“When you have won victory in battle,
give me leave to rejoice
that I too have won victory in love!”

“After we change the fate
of the people of the South,
who show a thousand colours
with every movement
yet wander without cohesion,
then, beautiful peacock,
there will be no bar
to our exchanging marriage garlands — know that!”

“Win and return quickly!
I shall take pearls
born from the roaring sea
and string a garland for you,
my lord of the southern Pandya land!
Until you return
I shall remain right here;
otherwise this very seashore
shall be the grave made for me!”

“Show me that pearl of a smile
that was never born in a shell.
I shall return,
singing the goodness
of refined Tamil
that lives within my thought,”
he said and parted,
leaving the maiden alone
on the white sand of the shore.

<!-- scan 387 -->

The great sea gives us pearls
through its beautiful shells;
its dancing waves give us salt
and raise us up.
Would anyone hate the sea
because its water is useless
even for rinsing the mouth?

So too,
the spear-eyed maiden
did not hate the noble great warrior
who laboured for the country
merely because
he had not embraced her at once.

The lion who had taken leave
went to see the leader,
spoke with him,
understood the situation,
and stood as commander
of the war being waged for justice.

At the head of a great host
the good warrior called everyone
to join against the corrupt men
who ruled the land.
Every street became a camp;
in the southern Pandya region
there was no quarter
without a battle-post.
Only the day for war
remained to be fixed.

“No one is equal to his shoulders!
Show our swords the way!”
So people gathered
in group after group.

News spread in every direction
that the Tamil people,
who had stood from the earliest age
upon this ancient earth,
the people of the South,
had risen.
A flood of ethnic awakening
made all directions stare in astonishment.
Battalions of youth,
ranks of working people —
all joined the formation
and sent word
to the commander-in-chief:
“Name the day!”

The leader held as his rule
the Kural's law:
act only after knowing
one's own strength
and the enemy's strength.

<!-- scan 388 -->

“The war will begin soon;
be patient,” he replied.
He rejoiced,
knowing only a few days remained
before the army would march.
Who knew that before then
such a cruel wonder would arise?...

Near the camp,
he stood alone,
absorbed in thought
about how the army should be led.
Then six or seven men
with the nature of jackals
ran forward to spring upon him.

“Othiyamilar!
Creatures useless for anything!
What do you want?” he asked.

“We want your life,” they said.

“I am already going to the field
to give this body
to dogs and jackals,
to vultures and hawks
that only wear human shape.
If before then
you have a craving for carrion,
take it away!
But know one thing:
you may be able
to take the life of this one man;
you cannot destroy
the feelings I have raised,
or the true warriors!” he said.

Those snake-men
without ears to hear Tamil
circled him with a *pada-pada* rush.
They hacked away
the warrior's limbs
like hanging roots of a banyan tree.
The fingers of the hand
raised to block the sword
fell to the ground
*pol-pol*, one after another.
As though setting explosives
to split a mountain,
they dared even
to split the great warrior's skull.
His head shattered;
he collapsed upon the earth.
On the lips his cousin-longed-for bride
had yearned to kiss —

<!-- scan 389 -->

on that chosen jewel-like chest —
the sweep of the sickle!
Before she could bury
her flower-face there,
a flood of blood.
The killers who came
for his life ran away;
the noble man gave
his last breath too
to the country
and lay down
in a cradle from which
sleep would never lift.

The beautiful woman,
standing by the sea-wave
with her pearl garland,
heard that the picture
had been shattered.
Those great sinners
had cut the garland itself!
The lion who was to lead the army —
limbs severed,
writhing and fallen
through betrayal —

“Alas!
Did I live all these days
only to hear this?
The mouth that told me,
‘Stand upon the white sand, woman!
I shall hurry back and marry you,’
has closed?
Have those legs
of majestic stride gone weak?
I lived believing
that those eyes which poured fire
would pour coolness
when you married me,
my fragrant honey!
They say those *chandala* creatures
have cast you
into the mire of death!
Why should I live any longer?” she cried.

She tore off the pearl garland
and hurled it into the sea.
Then she too,
remembering her beloved,
went down
to the bottom of the deep ocean.

## Translator notes

- The title witnesses are exact. **The Fallen Hero** renders `மறைந்த மாவீரன்` as an elegiac title without adding an outside biographical designation.
- Item 43 is translated as the anthology's dramatic elegiac narrative. Its lover-warrior dialogue, sexual/food metaphors and assassination sequence are not softened or converted into a historical fact-check layer.
- `எச்சில்படுத்தி` uses the source's food/tasting metaphor for sexually using and leaving the woman; the English “merely ‘tasted’” keeps that intentionally uncomfortable register.
- The source-visible `ஓதிய மிலார்` is retained conservatively as **Othiyamilar** rather than normalized from another edition. `முடை` is rendered contextually as carrion.
- The onomatopoeic `படபட` and `பொல பொல` are retained as sound effects in transliterated form.
- Near the close the canonical Tamil's lineation separates `சண்டாளப்` and `பிறவிகள்`; semantically they form the caste-marked abusive phrase `சண்டாளப் பிறவிகள்`. English renders it as “*chandala* creatures” and records the source's derogatory rhetoric without endorsing or silently sanitizing it. No Tamil source layer was changed.
