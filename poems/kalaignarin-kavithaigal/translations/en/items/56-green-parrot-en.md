# Green Parrot

<!-- scan 410 -->

Put a chilli fruit
on the head of a tender bottle-gourd,
set jasmine leaves as wings at its sides,
and place a screw-pine leaf behind as a tail—
green parrot!
A creation like you! A creation like you!
Round head! Red beak!
Little wings! Long tail!
Sugarcane-sweet parrot—
is my description wrong?
You refuse to bless me after hearing the portrait;
even hearing words of praise,
you will not praise me back.
I know the reason, little parrot!
Because I put you in a cage,
you writhe like a worm on a hook.
I shut you in prison and took delight;
now I understand—
prison is cruel, prison is cruel!
Forgive me, forgive me!
Golden-speaking doll, forgive me!
When my prison ends, I shall break yours;
through prison I have learned prison’s pain.
Little enslaved bird! Lovely darling!

<!-- scan 411 -->

I have gained experience—gained experience.
When I return, I shall give you a life of freedom!
Fly, fly, fly high!
Your bound feet will be freed; at once
fly upward as far as you can reach!
Beautiful-hued girl, fly everywhere!
I shut you in the cage, parrot!
Forgive me! I have understood
the cage’s pain. Because I have understood
the cage’s pain, you caged parrot—

<!-- scan 412 -->

I shall open it and set you free. But, parrot,
people who know the cage’s pain
have come here to rule.
They put us in cages to die!
Why, parrot? Why, parrot?
Give me an answer too, green parrot!
“You caged me,
so they caged you,” you will say—
well said, parrot;
but whom did you cage
that I caged you?

## Translator note

The poem deliberately turns a playful parrot portrait into a prison-and-political-rule allegory. Repetition around `சிறை` / prison and `கூண்டு` / cage is preserved rather than condensed.
