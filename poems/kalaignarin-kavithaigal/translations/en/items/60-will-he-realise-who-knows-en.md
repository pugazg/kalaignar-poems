# Will He Realise? Who Knows?

<!-- scan 418 -->

Will he realise?
Who knows?
Sibling,
he abuses me to his heart’s content—
he abuses me to his heart’s content—
expecting that I,
taking a word heated in a furnace of fire,
will not fail to burn him back;
so he abuses me to his heart’s content!
To sit in an office
that shines like silk,
he tried many paths,
found one path—
and abuses me to his heart’s content!
“Just watch how I attack him!
Can Karunanidhi escape?
I shall obey your every word,
even scratch your feet!
I shall not dream of self-respect!
Show mercy and grant me a post!”
He clasps
the feet of those in the Garden;
that is why
he abuses me to his heart’s content,
whirling a tongue of fire!
To answer chaff,
do we need leopards from the thicket?
If I answer such people,
that very answer becomes a scheme
for them to gain a post!

<!-- scan 419 -->

That is why
he abuses me to his heart’s content;
he clasps their feet!
I know too that the “ten-headed ones”
stretch out a hand,
saying a post is not needed—
money-aid will do!
Let them go, let them survive,
let their business prosper;
let them abuse me for it—
I utter not one opposing word
and leave them be!
To attack an elephant is a tiger’s valour;
to attack a “white-nosed horse”
is shameful, is it not?
That is why I say: let some abuse me;
I utter not one opposing word
and leave them be!
Is it wisdom
to use a spear
to bring down an ant
that a finger can crush?
To keep what one has gained
from being snatched away,
and to leap and leap
for the sake of gaining a post,
he abuses me to his heart’s content,
and clasps their feet!

> “Beside a minister who plots ruin,
> there gather foes worth seventy crores.”

Though I failed then
to grasp the meaning of that Kural,
so that pain may not surround us again—
will he realise it today?
Who knows?

## Translator note

The poem's repeated attack/office-seeking refrain is structural and is not compressed. Quoted source expressions such as `பத்துத்தலையோர்` (“ten-headed ones”) and `வெண் மூக்குக் குதிரை` (“white-nosed horse”) remain source metaphors rather than being assigned an outside referent. The Tirukkural is translated for sense while remaining visibly a quotation.
