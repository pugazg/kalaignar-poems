# I Shall Walk on Our Ayya and Anna's Path!

<!-- scan 293 -->

I shall walk on the path
of our Ayya and Anna!

My sibling, do you know
one truth?

The joy I did not find
in the lap of the mother
who carried and gave birth to me;
the joy I did not find
on the shoulders of the father
who lifted me and raised me;
the joy I did not find
even when my two sisters
stood like organs of breath
keeping me alive;
the joy I did not find
in the affection of the companions
who hold my hand
and pour love upon me;
the joy I did not find
in the speech of my children,
speech that defeats flute and yāzh
and even the Kural that praises them —
I find that joy
when your mouth blossoms
and cries, “Long live!”

Whenever I call, “Come,”
you treat it as an order,
carry it out,
and stand before me.
Before my lips can even move
to say, “Enter the field,”
you have finished the duty
and stand upright.
Then I see it.
I wear joy

<!-- scan 294 -->

throughout my whole heart.
And I am ashamed —
my face itself is ashamed —
that I cannot repay
your love in equal measure.

Seventy-one today —
how many events,
mixtures of pleasure and pain,
in this simple man's life!

Those who know my path know:
it is the path of Periyar and Anna.
Yet how many crores of taunts,
words, resistance and battles
this heart has carried and carried,
and still it stands erect,
still it stands firm!

Not every stone struck by a chisel
becomes a sculpture alive with spirit.
My thanks
to those who carved me into one!

Not every iron bar
hammered in the fierce heat
of the forge
is shaped into sword or spear.
To those who forged me
into sword and spear,
I scatter flowers of thanks
with both hands full!

When you praise me,
a mere sandal beneath the foot,

<!-- scan 295 -->

I rejoice.
But when I see agreements
that would again make the land
a colony,
I grimace.

At the bogeyman threats
of Hindi fanatics,
a volcano and an earthquake
rise in my heart!

When “social justice”
is shown only
as an eye-wiping trick,
my wounded heart throbs.

There, through a political settlement
reached by Arafat,
Palestinians have half a freedom!

In South Africa,
through white darkness,
a black dawn!

Here, Eelam alone
still burns
without being extinguished!

India became two,
then became three.
In what remains,
how many castes,
how many quarrels!

If secular principle dies,
the nation will crumble.

In the search for humaneness,
parties lose the human being himself,
and reel from scene to changing scene.

<!-- scan 296 -->

Amid all this,
on this birthday,
this is the vow I take:
joining with my sibling,
dearer to me than life,
who says,
“Why fear while I am here?” —

to guard the country,
to guard the language,
to guard the rights
of the people of our race,
I shall continue service
without even an atom of time
for weariness.

I shall keep walking —
on the path
of our Ayya and Anna!

## Translator notes

- The canonical title ends with `!`; the contents witness does not. The English follows the canonical title and preserves the Tamil contents witness separately in metadata.
- `அய்யா` is retained as **Ayya**, the movement's familiar honorific for Periyar, rather than flattened into a generic “sir.”
- The Palestine, South Africa, Eelam, social-justice and political-geography passages are translated as the poem's contemporary claims and rhetoric; Phase 4 does not update or fact-check them.
- The opening family-versus-movement-sibling contrast is intentionally intimate and hyperbolic. The English keeps that structure rather than reducing it to a political summary.
