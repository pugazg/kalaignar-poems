# On Anna's Path

<!-- scan 286 -->

Presiding poetic address by
Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi
at the Anna *kaviyarangam*
held at Kalaivanar Arangam, Madras,
on the evening of 15 September 1975.

O Tamil! Honey! Golden fruit!
Nectar! Love! Beautiful art!
Grace! Knowledge! Lamp of thought!
Grove! Fame! Jewel of gold!
Sacred treasure! Form! Coolness of the moon!
Tree! Shade! Comfort that gives shade!
Mother — holding fast to your feet,
I have come to sing the presiding poem.

To labour without weariness on Anna's path,
to let music resound and continue the journey,
raise aloud the vow
to guard the Kazhagam like the eye,
until it strikes the sky!

It feels as though it happened today —
that sight of the cow separated
from the calf of the Kazhagam.
As though a spear able to pierce a mountain
had been aimed and driven through the heart —

<!-- scan 287 -->

Anna, who would never leave us,
left us that day. Alas!
Seven or eight years have passed!

Avvai has a song called *Kondraivendan*;
our king of principle
has a song that can never perish.

The stride of the swelling sea,
the soft walk of women —
he showed both in his new Tamil style.
He trapped those who spoke evil
inside their own words.
At the very moment he took a pinch of snuff,
his smile would reduce opponents to powder.
He drove away those of rotten character.
He placed a golden crown upon Mother Tamil.

Anna held a pen.
On paper it became a plough,
to change the written fate
of deceived Tamils.
In our time,
to make those who had wept smile,
it became the sceptre of the government he gave.

On the racing-horse platform of public speech,
he offered guava-sweet Tamil.
When others scorched the Tamil language,
he became the root-force of the language struggle,
entered the field,
awakened those who watched,
and possessed the grace
to praise even those who insulted him.
He destroyed the frenzy
of linguistic domination.

*Palthāram patchanam* —
*pāṇi grahaṇam* —
*vaduvara graha-pravesam* —
*bandhu-mithrar* —
*namaskaram* —
*ubhaya kusalopari* —
*nāmāvali* —
*dhvajarohanam* —

<!-- scan 288 -->

should colourful Tamil,
which gives taste to the tongue,
wither beneath a Sanskrit invasion
whose words cannot even enter the tongue?

Betel, areca and lime
would redden his mouth.
If another language surged forward
to attack Tamil,
his eyes would redden.

He let the *agrasanadhipathi*
walk among us as **avai-thalaivar** — chair of the assembly.
The old *prasangam*
became today's **sorpōzhivu** — public address.
*Namaskaram*, *kimaskaram* and all the rest
bloomed as the good Tamil **vanakkam**.
Once a pure-Tamil **flag-hoisting festival** had arrived,
what work was left there
for *dhvajarohanam*?

If truth alone triumphs,
must it ask *Satyameva Jayate*
for permission?

I would call Anna a rich man:
his friends were the wealth he possessed.
I would call Anna an ascetic:
his whole life wore the saffron of renunciation.
I would call Anna a god:
even traitors now show him incense and lamps.
I would call Anna a Krishna:
even Putana lifts him and gives him milk!

He made our hearts his throne.
Some unknowing people
opposed him at first,
not understanding him.
Only later did they realise:
he had come as the Marx of the South
to teach true equality.

<!-- scan 289 -->

The tale of a fox becoming a horse,
the tale of Narada becoming a woman,
the tale of Krishna breaking the hanging pot —
old tales once praised by the town.

He was the great scholar given by Periyar,
a man who treated those tales
as straw in the fire.

Kamban portrayed Ravana
as a demon without even the substance called compassion.
Anna refused to let that portrayal pass.
With moving pictures drawn in words,
and questions that pierced like needles,
he dragged the births of heaven down into the street.

Was the great sage
who abandoned Menaka
after the hunger of his body was satisfied
a form of compassion?
Was Indra,
that incomparable great king
who by trickery tasted Ahalya,
a form of compassion?
Kambar!
Was Ravana alone
the hard-hearted guardian?
Who upon this earth
can answer the questions Anna raised?

Taking Anna's writings,
I weave a little story:

On **One Night**, under **Moonrise**,
the **Good Younger Brother's** **Servant Maid**
runs toward **Chandramohan**.
In the **Flame of Love**,
she sees the **Gateway to Heaven**
and drinks deep of **Kambarasam**!

The parents who saw it
grew fiercely angry
and went to court
to establish justice.

<!-- scan 290 -->

“Should a fence be built
to stop love?
Must I forget the man
who tied the *thāli* around my neck?
Should a mother tie the *thāli* for her daughter?”
So argued **Kumarik Kottam**.
What would a garden of money
become before her?

**Needhidevan Mayakkam** —
the Judge-God's Delusion — was dispelled;
he stood upright,
blessed them,
“Live well,”
and went away!

Some say God exists;
some say he does not.
I have no worry about God!
“One caste, one God” —
to accept that, he said,
is true theism.
For one person not to deceive another —
that, he said, is atheism!

I say what is good:
for us, God is
in the smile of the afflicted.
Make this your principle, he said;
raise the flag of reason, he said.

Was it not Anna
who showed through familiar Tamil similes
that the Kazhagam is the true communist?
Was not the path he showed
the good path for abolishing poverty?
For rights to increase,
for truths to stand firm —
was not what he taught
a new way?
For the country to flourish,
for good Tamil to grow —
was not each movement of his tongue a drumbeat?
Is he not, every day,
the ruler who still rules us?

Among the days of the week
you may choose Wednesday.
But of the days Anna lived,

<!-- scan 291 -->

which one shall I choose
and remember?

Shall I remember Trichy,
where he called Navalar,
“Come, thambi — come take the leadership”?
Shall I remember how,
to think through an unsolved problem,
he would go to the Professor's house?

Shall I remember the day
when, after eighteen years
of separation that neither heart desired,
we went with him
to see Periyar's face again?

Or the seashore,
where he placed a ring upon me,
saying,
“Though many arrows strike,
may this *kanaiyāzhi* guard you”?

Whatever I remember, our Anna,
your form rises before us,
a sacred form of greatness.
Thinking of it,
we take comfort.

You thundered,
“A man with a brother-army
need not fear an army.”
You would say,
“One tree alone cannot become a grove.”
Yet you know the story
of how one solitary tree here
itself became a grove.

The Puranic people say
a Saturn transit is good for some.
Is that true? Is it false?
In our Kazhagam,
a Saturn-*departure*
has indeed brought good results, Anna!

Because we labour on your path,
like the brittle branch of a drumstick tree,
the enemy will snap,
shatter,
and scatter like cotton.

We walk carrying the lamp you gave us.

<!-- scan 292 -->

The Tamil people add the ghee,
set the wick,
and help us.
In hard rain,
without letting it go out,
without letting it tremble in the wind,
we cup our hands
and guard the hand-lamp.

Give us one boon, Anna!
I ask it as though in penance.
For the people at the foundation,
achievement after achievement
has been built up.
Anna,
if only once
you could see with your own eyes
how tirelessly we walk upon your path,
all our worry would fly away.

O cool full moon,
come without refusing.
If you grant this boon,
it will be as though we received
the boon of deathlessness.
Joyful Tamil Nadu will dance;
all our sorrow will disappear.

### Kalaignar's conclusion

Why a conclusion today?
“Long live Anna!”
is for us
both the pearl-address
and the final address.

So let all of us say:

Long live Anna!
Long live Anna!
Long live Anna!

Greetings!

❀

## Translator notes

- `அண்ணா வழியில்` is rendered **On Anna's Path**. The repeated `வழி` carries both literal path/journey and ideological-political lineage.
- Scans 287–288 deliberately catalogue Sanskritised public/ceremonial vocabulary and Tamil replacements. The source forms are kept in transliteration where the contrast itself is the point; `அக்கிராசனாதிபதி → அவைத்தலைவர்`, `பிரசங்கம் → சொற்பொழிவு`, `நமஸ்காரம் → வணக்கம்`, and `துவஜாரோகணம் → கொடியேற்று விழா` remain explicit.
- The rationalist/Kamban/Ravana passage and the mythological examples on scan 289 are Kalaignar's source polemic; the translation does not harmonise them with external epic or religious traditions.
- The sequence beginning `ஓர் இரவு சந்திரோதயத்தில்...` stitches together names/titles associated in the source with Anna's writings. The English highlights them as titles/conscious literary allusions rather than treating the chain as an ordinary literal incident.
- `கணையாழி` in the seashore memory is retained in transliteration in the key line because the Tamil activates both a ring and the surrounding `கணை` (“arrow”) wordplay.
- `சனிப்பெயர்ச்சி` is used as a political pun on Saturn's transit/departure. The English makes the transit/departure turn visible without claiming an exact homophone.
- The personal memories of Navalar, the Professor, Periyar and the seashore ring are translated as Karunanidhi's first-person recollections in this poem.
- The closing lamp, government-achievement and prayer-to-Anna imagery is preserved as elegiac/political rhetoric rather than neutralised.
