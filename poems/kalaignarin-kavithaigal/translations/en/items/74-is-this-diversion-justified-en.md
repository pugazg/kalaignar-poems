# Is This Diversion Justified?

<!-- scan 453 -->

Rolling the earth itself up like a mat,
the wicked Hiranyaksha hid inside the sea!
The gods were startled
and stood praying to Thirumal.
Thirumal took the form of a boar
and entered the surging sea!
He brought back the globe of the world
after destroying the asura!

In joy, Bhuma Devi thanked
the grunting boar
and said with feeling,
“I have fallen in love with you.”
The Lord in boar-form agreed at once.
What happened next...?

> “Like one flood joined with another flood,
> the two became veena and music.”

To the God of gods, Thirumal,
and to his divine consort Bhuma Devi,
was born an asura with a warrior nature!
His naming, they say,
was Narakasura.

The man and woman belong to the clan of gods—
yet the son born to them belongs to the asura clan!
One feels like asking, “How can this be?”
But once we begin asking questions like this,
must we not also ask
who could ever roll this earth itself up like a mat?

When the world was rolled up like a mat,
would not the seven seas too
have been inside this very earth?

<!-- scan 454 -->

⟦continuation from previous page⟧

The demon who lifted the earth with all seven seas—
in which sea did he hide that earth?
We say, “The one who taught letters is God.”
If raising the questions “Why?” and “How?”
is itself opposition to God,
can one who does not know how to explain a doubt
remain a great teacher?

If the struggle between asuras and devas
was the Aryan-Dravidian struggle—
not only Ayya Periyar and Arignar Anna,
but also, the poem says,
the Light of Asia, Nehru, wrote so—
do scholars reject it?
If they do,
would that not mean rejecting old history itself?

The research book written
by the famed historian Srinivasa Iyengar
again and again says
that the Deva-Asura war of those days
was an Aryan-Dravidian struggle.
Is it just to divert that meaning elsewhere?
Then—can the Diwali purana be accepted?

## Translator note

The poem first retells the Hiranyaksha/Varaha/Bhuma/Narakasura story and then turns its internal logic into a rationalist challenge. Its Aryan-Dravidian historical interpretation and the attributions to Periyar, Anna, Nehru and Srinivasa Iyengar are translated as source claims; Phase 4 does not independently verify them.
