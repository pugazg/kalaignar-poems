# "Chithirai Festival" — Presiding Poem!

<!-- scan 333 -->

On Tamil New Year’s Day, 14 April 1990,
Chief Minister Kalaignar delivered on Madras Television
this presiding poem under the title
“Chithirai Festival.”

★

To the Tamil people filled with heartfelt love,
to the distinguished ones who have come to create lofty poetry,
and to my siblings dearer to me than life —
I heap my greetings and good wishes!

The television station sent word:
“Come and offer New Year greetings.”
The time when people said,
“Spit! Spit!! Why this fellow?” has changed;
now a time has come when they send for me —
is that not a wonder?

For the assembly of poets gathered here,
I am the leader!
A joke — a fine joke!
Without drinking the mother’s milk called grammar,
whenever hunger struck,
I drank the bottled milk of modern verse —
such a child am I!

And they have invited me
to preside over a poetry assembly!

<!-- scan 334 -->

When I wondered why,
I realised this:
I will not ask for *Vaikasi* —
“vai kāsu,” “put down money” — for my poem.

So perhaps they invited me
to bless Chithirai!
They ask me to give my blessing
to the Chithirai maiden
who has received her share in this poetry assembly;
to the Chithirai maiden born of Panguni,
they ask me to give my blessing.
Perhaps they dared invite me
because I will not offer a *Purattasi* —
a “purattu āsi,” a fraudulent blessing!

With a half-sari that does not slip from the breast —
*mār kazhiyā* —
the maiden Aavani,
a woman of a traditional household,
looked at me and said,
“Ah, is it you?” — *Aani*!
Then she danced and stamped, *thai, thai*,
flashing eyes that poured fire.

Why?
Because she said the first day of Thai
is the true Tamil New Year!
That may indeed be true;
but to keep her from melting in anger,
I told her:
if Tamils have two New Years in one year,
is that not twice the joy?
On the cool face of the tender maiden
I saw moonlight.

<!-- scan 335 -->

She embraced me!
Do not be startled and ask,
“Another woman too?”
It is only a *month* —
not a woman!

More than calling this merely a New Year,
I rejoice that this is the centenary year
of the Revolutionary Poet.
Ambedkar, the new man, arose,
lifted by the hand those who had lain
as though buried in pits,
set them walking,
and spread sunlight across their faces —
that history, on this colourful festival,
we must salute!
We must nurture new thought!

To the one who gave the Constitution
of the sacred Indian nation
as a treasury of thought;
to the one who stood tall as the Vindhyas
and checked the domination
of fanatics drunk on language —
yes, to lion-hearted Ambedkar
and lion-hearted Bharathidasan,
in this year when we celebrate their centenaries,
may the evil powers that torment us
turn and flee in defeat!

The sage Vallalar of Vadalur said:
“Do not sink into caste,
religion and the mire of scripture,
O human beings.”
We have not honoured his words!
We only raise arches that hide the sky
and hold festivals for him — without shame!
And for Valluvar,
the great one who said
that all lives are equal by birth —

<!-- scan 336 -->

we offer honours alone,
but refuse to put his words into practice.
For the daughters and sons we ourselves beget,
we search for marriage alliances
inside the circles of relatives and caste.
What profit is there
in preachers advising the whole town
about a principle they themselves will not live?
Why does the body burn with anger
when the truth is spoken?
If wealth born of thought
is called a reforming principle,
to leave it without action
is a great betrayal!

There are Tamils in Tamil Nadu
who learned, long before Ettappan’s age,
to praise, worship and sing hymns to betrayal.
Even if Kattabomman spits at them,
those calculating men do not care —
those “geniuses” who live clutching
the enemy’s feet!

To shout “principle!” and “doctrine!”
from a hilltop,
to swear “Even if my throat is strangled,
I will not yield,”
and then do nothing
to make even one of those claims real —
what use is it to climb a platform
and pose before the people as a great warrior?
Only when word and deed are one
will a government flower
and hang heavy with fruit!

Because yours is a government
that does what it says
and says what it does,
we shall labour to increase prosperity;
we shall say that, like the linchpin of a chariot,
the nation must stand behind the plough;

<!-- scan 337 -->

that beautiful cities and villages,
and a society without longing and deprivation,
will arise when industry grows;
that pure-minded plans are needed
to eradicate many cruel diseases
and build a clear, healthy life;
that if rulers move like a pouncing tiger,
hoarding rats will be crushed;

that those whose backs were broken,
who drifted in education and employment
like boats without oars,
will in the coming age gain prosperity
through revolutionary changes in reservation;

that the poor young girl educated through eighth standard,
who once sighed that family life
was a fruit beyond her reach,
shall have that despair changed,
and that five thousand rupees to help place the *thali*
on her neck is a boon obtained without penance;

that for those who have lost the head of the family
and cry out, crushed by poverty,
the sum given by the government
shall become a precious amount
that wipes their tears;

that children shall receive nutritious food with eggs;
that women must not be treated as mere hens;
that thirty per cent reservation is a first-stage concession,
and that in the end men and women must receive
an “Ardhanarishvara” share of rights —
that is, half and half, fifty per cent;
that only when women’s rights are secured
will the rights of the soil in this speaking land endure;
and that in parents’ property,
half shall belong to the daughter too —
a proud law of equal right;

<!-- scan 338 -->

that poor pregnant women,
inter-caste couples,
those who marry widows,
persons with disabilities,
and leprosy-afflicted beggars
must not stand bewildered,
without wealth or dignity —
we shall stand as patrons
and honour those lives;

that on three festivals each year
five kilograms of rice shall be free;
that not only Adi Dravidars and tribal people,
but also the most backward and denotified communities
struggling at the very bottom
shall receive house-sites,
acquired and given free;
that we shall work until there is no small village
without drinking water;
that enough of living
with the sky as roof and the earth as mat —
we shall continue daily the plan
to build sturdy houses for fishers,
Adi Dravidars and tribal people;
that those who became impoverished
while defending the country and language
shall receive welfare funds and opportunities;
that students going to school
shall receive generous bus-fare concessions;
that government employees, factory labourers,
teachers, weavers,
all working people and farmers —
people as dear as life —
shall have their just demands heard,
and we shall leap to remove their suffering.

Not merely words sweet as jaggery —
this is a government
that gives deeds a form for its words!

<!-- scan 339 -->

That is why we say:
let us grow thought — let us act —
let us increase prosperity and guard character;
with the light of truth in the heart
and resolve strong as steel,
let us win our rights;
let us learn the world.

I am a poet who gives freedom to poetry;
I have given the themes.
Those who come to sing upon them are
poets who praise victorious Tamil,
poets at the summit,
poets who have learned
and circle the world!

From the golden heap
of good Tamil they bring,
let the learned gain their chance;
let evil be gone,
let truth become ours;
let us plant the flag
of literary Tamil upon this earth!
With scientific Tamil,
let us touch the edge of the sky!

<!-- scan 340 -->

First —

Arasu Manimekalai.
If this government were Manimekalai,
with the inexhaustible bowl
we would scoop and scoop without decrease
and serve nectar every day
to everyone!

Never mind.
Even without the inexhaustible bowl,
to embrace five crore Tamil people
and give them a good life,
we think every day.
Now let us hear Arasu Manimekalai’s rare poem
about that thought!

Next —

Poet-King Ponni Valavan.
The Kaveri has another name: Ponni!
While we are thinking
how to act and solve that very problem,
here comes Ponni.
Let us see what thought she gives us!

Next —

Nirmala Suresh.
Do you know her theme?
It is about *seer* — order and excellence!
A theme suited to a woman!
Without thinking of increasing
wedding *seer-varisai* gifts,
we hope she will give good ideas
for increasing the country’s excellence.

<!-- scan 341 -->

Next —

our high-priced poet-king,
“diamond and pearl” — Vairamuthu!
His theme is “Let us guard character.”
If, after guarding character,
increasing excellence and refining Tamil Nadu,
we let it become an auction item for scoundrels,
honour will go;
even our name as human beings will go!
Gandhi was the gift of time;
Arignar Anna the gift of Kanchi;
and with them, to stand on the path
whose character Periyar shaped,
and to open the eyes
of the wailing *Othiya-milaar* men,
he will sing.
Let us drink its flavour!

Next —

Poet Tamilanban,
a reader of news on television,
a man who breathes classical Tamil.
What will he say so that rights may be won?
Let him read!
Let rights breathe!

Next —

Kavikko Abdul Rahman,
the man of laughter in a poetry assembly —

<!-- scan 342 -->

not one who stands beside us
only when things glitter,
and at times of commotion
moves many leagues away!
This steel poet comes to say:
“Let us learn the world.”
Will he learn it,
or merely tour it?
Let us watch and see!

### Concluding words

Even if I say nothing in conclusion,
no harm will come;
yet I wish to say one thing.
So that the lion-like gathering of Tamil youth
may think well and act,
I shall recite one passage
from my book *Sangath Tamil*.
Receive it!

Great men who build mansions
and live lulled by gentle breezes!
Money, money, money —
you bathe every day
in the worker’s sweat
and heap up wealth!
Like vultures that eat corpses,
you keep swelling your bellies!
You push the poor
into lives wrapped in darkness
and run after possessions,
with no mind that says “enough.”
O you who have made
increasing wealth alone your goal in life,
I ask you one question.
Answer me!

<!-- scan 343 -->

What do you eat from your leaf-plate?
A ruby stir-fry?
An emerald curry?
Do you fry diamonds,
and eat them with a cat’s-eye-gem *aviyal*?
Do you make rice
out of pearls and coral,
pound it, put it in the cooking pot
and fill your gut?

Arignar Anna once stood on a public platform
and asked this question, rich in meaning,
of men who pile up wealth like a mountain.
We have never forgotten it;
we still ask it today.
We proclaim across the earth
that the fragrance of common ownership must spread!
This cry is not new.
Poet-lord Nakkirar
beat the drum of commonality
in a *Puram* poem.
Listen!

For the king who longs
to bring the whole earth encircled by the southern sea
under the shade of his white victory umbrella,
asserting exclusive rule;
and for the unlettered rough man
who roams the forest day and night without sleep,
hunting animals as one throws a stone
to knock down fruit —
for either of them,
to guard their dignity,
are not two pieces of clothing,
one below and one above, enough?
And to remove hunger without stupor,
is not one *nazhi* of grain enough
for each to eat gruel or Pongal
in this ocean-girdled world?
Thus, if the truth is
“what we eat is one *nazhi*,
what we wear is two garments,”

<!-- scan 344 -->

is it justice that many wither in poverty
while a few dance in the pride of abundant wealth?
When all born into this world
have the same needs of body and mind,
why, without thinking this through,
should one man heap up possessions like a flood?
Why should many die
in lives hollowed out like pits?

Let us destroy private ownership
that says “everything is mine,”
and find the right way to drive out poverty;
let us share food and protect all life,
lay down the principle of common welfare,
and make joy on earth —
that is the use of having taken birth!
Nakkirar gathered this thought and gave it;
good people through this ancient world
have continued to give it ever since.

> “For the one who rules the sea-girt south
> beneath a single white umbrella,
> and for the unlearned hunter
> who sleeps neither midnight nor day,
> what is eaten is one *nazhi*;
> what is worn is two garments;
> all else is alike.
> The use of wealth is giving;
> if we say ‘we shall enjoy it ourselves,’
> many things will fail us.”
>
> — *Purananuru*, poem 189

This is a song I sang
when I was out of office!

<!-- scan 345 -->

Office is a garland of thorns;
literary work is for me a strand of jasmine.
Office is a deep pit;
public service is my Podhigai hill.
If you ask, “Honour or crown?”
I am a man who values honour alone.
If you ask, “Crown or head?”
I am not a fool who grins,
“Keep the crown; that is enough!”
That is why I said,
“Let us offer a hand to relationship
and raise our voice for rights,”
and was ready to renounce office,
spending more than ten years that way.

Office is only an opportunity to serve!
Office, splendour and pomp
will all burst like an inflated balloon.
Only service rooted in character
will endure like the sky!
Some who do not know this
writhe like fish thrown upon the bank,
thinking office alone
is the purpose of birth.

Including them too,
I offer New Year greetings,
thank the scholars, poets
and comrades of every section,
and take my leave.
Accept my respectful greetings!

## Translator notes

- The title preserves the canonical verified opening’s straight quote glyphs and terminal `!`; the contents witness uses curly quotation marks and no terminal exclamation.
- Scan 334 is built from Tamil month-name sound-play: `வைகாசி / வை காசி`, `புரட்டாசி / புரட்டு ஆசி`, `மார்கழி / மார் கழி`, and `ஆனி / ஆ நீயா`, plus the dance syllable `தை தை`. The Tamil forms are retained where an English substitute would invent a false pun.
- The social-welfare catalogue is translated as the poem’s 1990 governmental programme rhetoric; Phase 4 does not fact-check or modernise those claims.
- `அர்த்த நாரீஸ்வர` is retained through the poem’s own explanatory gloss, “half and half — 50 per cent.”
- `ஓதிய மிலார்` is a final-cleared source form already treated conservatively elsewhere in the anthology; it is retained here rather than silently normalised.
- The final common-ownership passage quotes *Purananuru* 189 as embedded by the source. The English is a translation of this anthology witness, not an imported external edition.
