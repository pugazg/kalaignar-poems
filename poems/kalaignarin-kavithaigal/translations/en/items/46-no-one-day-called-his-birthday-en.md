# There Is No One Day Called His Birthday!

<!-- scan 396 -->

There is no one day
called his birthday!

He is the king of hearts
of this southern-tip Tamil Nadu.
Our Anna — lord of a golden age —
who surpassed even sweet jaggery
and flowers opening their petals
with the savour of his words
and the fragrant perfume
of his writing!

Even the serpents that come hissing,
setting decaying ideas
like venomous fangs,
he makes coil themselves,
with the heart that bears anything
serving as his snake-charmer's flute —
such a great-hearted man!

For Anna, whose name shall not fade,
archanai after archanai is offered every day!

In restraint he is like a tortoise;
he endures as though mute!
But if some evil comes upon the Tamils,
he leaps up like fire!

Like a hen brooding over her clutch—

<!-- scan 397 -->

he, for all of us—
the very way we marshal our ranks today
is surely the training he gave us!

From coral-red lips
what spills are pearl-words!
In eyes where anger does not dwell
what gleams is diamond-light!

There is no one day called his birthday:
every day
that nectar-Tamil is born
is his birthday!

## Translator notes

- The canonical title reads `என` (“called / as”), while the contents witness has `ஏன்` (“why”); the English follows the canonical opening and does not combine the two witnesses.
- `அர்ச்சனைகள்` is kept through the religious image of repeated *archanai* offerings because the poem deliberately turns everyday remembrance of Anna into a ritual metaphor.
- Across the scan-396→397 join, the final-cleared Tamil has the source-visible sequence `அடைகாக்கும் கோழி போல / அவனைத்தான் நம்மையெல்லாம்-`. The English preserves the broken rhetorical syntax rather than silently changing the Tamil to a smoother presumed reading.
