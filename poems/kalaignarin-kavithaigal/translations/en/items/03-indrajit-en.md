# Indrajit

<!-- scan 43 -->

**Indrajit**

Across the earth valour leaps — in women's
eyes chastity speaks;
the shadow of the flag that rises into the sky
conquers the enemy; every mode of the country's music
displays honour;
countless powers bloom in this fortress of warriors —
white clouds hold the royal parasol,
while the broad sea performs penance,
kissing coolly the golden-walled fort.
Their own lives are not the greater thing
to the people who live here!
"To guard the people's lives is the grammar of great Tamils" —
so speaks the guide of southern Lanka's great clan;
lamp of self-respect — leader of the Tamils;
Ravana, who lives forever upon the page of fame!

Lightning-waisted great queen Mandodari was his wife.
The two of them,
longing to hear a child's sugarcane-sweet speech,
without performing harsh austerities,
without bathing in the Ganges,
created a fruit — singing poetry in the bedchamber!
A new flower discovered in a waterfall of love —
a beauty of a man — a pearl they gained
by plunging into an ocean of delight —

<!-- scan 44 -->

they touched him to their eyes and rejoiced;
they forgot their worries.
A cradle inlaid with rubies
would shatter beneath the child's foot;
the very next day would become a festival
at the sight of the son's valour!
If he drooled from his mouth,
mother and father would drink it as nectar!

He crawled through the hall — behind him
the great warrior followed, singing Tamil:

"They dig into the earth
and lift out nailed-gold and jewel-clusters;
can all those equal a child,
praised like the Kural?
The slack-jawed ones scorn household life!
What can I say of their wisdom?
Any epic or literature that denies
the great bliss a child gives — I call it cactus!
I call it a forest twig useless even for a fork!
Is not a child's kiss the gathering of the three fruits?
Is not its smile the taste of Muttamil!"

So he cooed and drew his graceful woman into his arms.
"The child is watching," the maiden would say and move away.
"He strings pearls with the pearls of his laughter!" he would say,
then, "May you have speech! May you have a good heart!
Come, my darling!"
and upon the scarred chest struck by arrows
he would gather his gold into his embrace.

Like a wave rising high
upon the foaming sea in season,
he planted his foot upon the earth
and leapt —
"to catch the mountain-cloud," said

<!-- scan 45 -->

the great queen, pouring cool words!

"Will the wealth born to a warrior of speed
live like a crow or chicken?
Will he listen if one says,
'Cling lovingly to life'?
Without fearing death he leapt upon the white cloud!
So from now I will call him Meghanada!"

The festival of naming the prince of sacred Lanka —
on that day when peacock flocks danced
to the sound of anklets,
he held the beautiful child close
and shed tears of joy, saying,
"Long live the lamp of our lineage, born to rule this land!"
All the women stood smiling;
a great day!

Like the splitting of a palmyra spathe,
the warriors read valour upon the young prince's face!
Like grain sprouting in the pāli of a wedding house,
Meghanada grew and grew upon the earth,
like a mountain!

Before twice eight years were complete,
he entered the forest,
captured two lions
and kept them at the palace like watchdogs!
In his twentieth year
he broke an iron pillar with one hand!
He built a fortress no enemy could enter in Lanka.
He established a beautiful hall for literary Tamil.
Then Indra, king of the devas,
fought him and was defeated —
and so he became worthy of the name Indrajit.

"Sweet one! My nectar!
Our son's greatness has returned to my heart
a new rising, a new feeling, a new youth —

<!-- scan 46 -->

fruit-sweet one! In memory of it,
give me an old kiss! —
when I stand alone I cannot bear
this trouble called love!"

Saying this,
Ravana, guardian of the Tamil field,
found her jasmine-teeth and held her close.

"My life! My beloved! I will tell you one thing;
a many-coloured flower, when it matures,
becomes fruit —
we too are walking toward that stage,
is it not true?
Thinking of all the songs we sang
in moonlit days,
it is best that, in good time,
we seek a companion in love
for our son, our cool nectar."

Thus spoke bee-haunted flower-tressed Mandodari!
Hearing it, Ravana nodded yes —
but did not let go —
the two of them won that night together;
they rose when dawn's light struck.

At that moment a companion came running:
a mango-like young woman, she said,
was standing in Meghanada's palace,
shy as a water-lily creeper!

The parents stood astonished — yet
the king rejoiced as though tasting athirasam.
"Life of my life! Go and see your daughter-in-law!
Is she a garlanded king's daughter? — a tender vine?
A dark-tressed beauty? — one suited to our darling?
Learn all this and return in a moment," he said.

She went — she came back!
"What is it, mirror-eyed one! Your golden feet
hardly touch the earth — you are flying!
Tell me, sweet Tamil!"
Thus the man beside her stretched his ear in excitement.

<!-- scan 47 -->

The queen of Lanka came forward
to tell exactly what was there:

"As though sandalwood were turned and carved into a statue,
then lips fitted with the colour of burning flame,
beneath brows like Indra's bow
two beautiful blue gems set within eyelids,
a sacred mouth fragrant with classical Tamil made to move,
and life breathed into it
so it might forever sing praise —
what wonder is this?

Queen of beauty! Nectar-speech! Love-speech!
There is no word in the manuscript of familiar Tamil
that can show a comparison!
I will tell you one thing.

[Large reclining-figure illustration]

Do not be startled when you hear it —
she is the enemy's daughter!
A red paddy-ear our son brought away
without scattering, without spilling!
She is the daughter of the Naga king
who once tried to face us with an army
and escaped alive!
Her name is Sulochana —
a sweet-filled
segment of jackfruit! One day —

<!-- scan 48 -->

as that good maiden wandered
beside the garden of a jewelled tower,
Meghanada went there like a cat
that had spotted a sweet cake!

'I suffer from a love that will not cool, beloved,'
he said, lowering before her
the head that bows to no one,
standing there to beg alms of love!

'Because I lacked the courage,
I did not begin first —
pure one, I heard your name
and for many days I have not slept.
Look at my neck, which wears no tiger-claw;
bring it at once!
From now on I want no one else —
if I have this love, that is enough,' she said!

Is your son not a lion?
Would a surging tiger give birth to a cat?
He lifted the gold itself
and carried her upon his shoulder;
chill delight spread through every limb
and she clung to him.
With the new strength her body gave him,
he crossed the fortress wall of the Naga land!

This, king, is the story the maiden told;
know it," she said, heart rejoicing!

Ravana too said it would be best
to celebrate through the streets
where fierce-handed chariots parade
and make Sulochana
the daughter-in-law of the island of Lanka;
and she said, "Good!"

"Return my daughter!" demanded the Naga king.
Meghanada tore apart his furious palm-leaf message.
"I will cut off his head," said the Naga father-in-law,
waiting to repay the insult!

Meanwhile, to destroy Ravana,
the northern Aryans undertook a degrading intrigue —
a conspiracy-play behind the curtain
to finish the Dravidian!

<!-- scan 49 -->

One younger brother who had seemed a dove
turned into a wicked eagle —
for Rama, who had sent
a fifth-column force called Hanuman,
Meghanada found a way to teach a lesson!
He cut the tail of the one who tried to burn the city,
gave his hide a taste of punishment,
and drove him away!

The flood of betrayal surged upon Lanka!....

"A grove has no beauty without flowers!
A poem has no beauty without meaning!

<!-- scan 50 -->

A tongue has no beauty without grace!
Therefore it is best to go down and speak with Rama,"
said cruel Vibhishana,
the one who had "become seven with Guha"!

Hearing it,
"You have well spoken the grammar that guards the tongue,
uncle!
But after war has begun to guard the country,
to kneel in order to guard our own heads
is not our custom — it is no act of honour.
Walk on," said Meghanada,
with a heart that knew no trembling.

The impetuous northerners began the war!
Whenever hardship came,
the great people of this land laughed at it
and leapt into the battlefield;
the deed of the brother who came
like a disease that kills its own blood
shook the whole mind of resolute Ravana.

"Refined gold! Eye that I fathered!
I am sending you to war!
Go and return, my son," he said.

"These battle-itching shoulders
have found their food!
For the wicked we will build
a ladder to the city of death!"
The bull rose roaring.

"To guard the motherland I will give my head —
I will teach a lesson to sinners
who open the road for the enemy-country's dogs!
I will return, my mother!
Send me on my way!
I will bring the news of victory," he said
and flew away.

His companion came before him.
"I set out for war," he said.

"Not even half a year has passed since our wedding —
son of kings for whom ruling the earth was not enough,
who rule the sea as well!
To this simple woman whose love
cannot find a way across its own banks —
is it just to say that the war-drum
has sounded for a journey? Tell me!" she said.

<!-- scan 51 -->

"It is not beautiful to pour tears like hanging roots!
We were born in a lineage
that ploughs with the bow and raises a crop of valour.
We bow in life to the learned,
not to enemies!

Women should weep to stop the deceit of men,
not to stop the event of entering the battlefield!
Damage may come to body, wealth and life —
not to the culture of ancient Tamil!
When dawn comes,
give me leave to depart," said
the vengai longing to see the face of war.

"When you bent me like a creeper,
threw me across your shoulder
and crossed the long fortress wall,
I was not afraid — yet when I think of tomorrow's dawn,
why does my heart tremble so, beloved?"

She wept. He said:

"This is a land where a child hearing thunder
claps its hands and laughs!
A Tamil land that does not know
the word 'impossible' —
can you speak without reading this, green parrot?
Lay me in your lap."

With the words shed by that fearless heart as comfort,
the woman, unable to find any road to consolation,
clung like a beautiful flower
to the bull-embracing chest of her man.

"Kiss this hard chest
where the mud of blood will settle," he asked;
one or two became a hundred —
she poured down a rain of kisses!
Even the moon that had hidden
peeped out to watch —
peerless warrior Indrajit became shy!

They heard he was coming to war.
"He will come like a river without a dam," they said.
"The charioteer of his chariot is our man!

<!-- scan 52 -->

No need for useless fear — cast it away!
I will go into the town and return as a spy!"
Thus Vibhishana slipped away in hiding.

"Without your brow shrinking,
without your blue eyes clouding,
conquer the long enemy, beloved!
Great treasure of this motherland rich in worth!
The unlearned fools set out like swarming termites
to show what their hands can do —
my king! Guard the honour of the clan!"

So beautiful Sulochana Devi,
for the warrior already clothed for battle,
placed the garland, marked the tilak
and stood blessing him!!

"Upon this earth I will let no enemy enter —
my ten-and-a-half-touch pure gold!"
He received one "seal" and went forth!

"At the place called Nikumbala is a great encampment.
There are weapons there capable of destroying whole masses!
That is Indrajit's great army;
we must destroy it by strategy."
Vibhishana said this in the camp of the worthless men!

They all danced in excess of joy!
With his army, the tiger-like Tamilian
entered the Nikumbala encampment!
Weapons known to no one,
he took and placed into the warriors' hands!
The war-drum roared outside the camp!
The camp shook as enemies arrived!
The warrior suddenly trapped inside the cave
spat smoke like a volcano, enraged!
Enemies surrounded him everywhere.

"This is the work of my shameless uncle!
He has completed his share of betrayal —
I will sever his head!"

<!-- scan 53 -->

He whirled in his chariot like a revolving sword!
His eyes appeared as flame.
With the strength of a thousand elephants he resisted!
Like a temple tower walking,
like a mountain moving,
he stormed like a cyclone,
came like a surging sea —
were these the eyes that spoke tenderness
to fish-eyed women?
They were sparks of fire
to destroy the alien lineage utterly!

Were these hands that embraced a golden statue?
They were hands that reaped the heads of the arrogant!
Everywhere, he!
In everything, he!
He was the sunbeam that tears the night!

Just as he was about to finish the battle
and cut off Rama's head,
the charioteer's conspiracy began at once!
The wheels had lost their linchpins —
in that instant the chariot lurched and fell,
and the four horses sprang in panic!
The whole undercarriage scattered apart!

Meghanada fell through deceit —
and poison-hearted Vibhishana cut off his hand.
There was no time even to think
of the treachery his uncle had done...
with both arms pouring rivers of blood,
he fought!

Clenching a spear in his mouth,
he tormented the enemies.
All the enemies gathered in a mass.
Even with both hands lost,
his furious power of battle
made the enemy itself praise him —
everyone except one sinner, Vibhishana!
Before the prince!

<!-- scan 54 -->

Unable to bring down the enemy by spear or arrow,
with feet and head alone
he scooped away many lives!

On learning that her beloved's hand
had been severed on the battlefield,
flower-creeper Sulochana too understood —

"Where is the arm that leapt to embrace me?
Sinner that I am — how shall I bear to see my beloved?"

Lamenting, she ran there.
She saw one man standing among a thousand,
displaying Tamil valour:

"Beloved!" she cried —
"My love," he answered —
and for a moment he weakened!

Then Lakshmana, the great coward,
loosed an arrow that did not miss —
and to the cry "Long live the motherland!"
he swayed upon the battlefield
like a vessel without a sail.

The base-natured Naga king,
who had nursed an old grudge,
now settled the ancient vengeance
by cutting off his head!

"I have gained a place
on the golden page of the green-Tamil people;
green parrot, I am coming!"
he said —
and only his head fell into her hands...
Sulochana, that priceless jewel,
died as well!

"I have no other life left
to lose in this war," said
the king of Lanka and collapsed —
Alas, only to die after him
had Mandodari opened her long lashes!
