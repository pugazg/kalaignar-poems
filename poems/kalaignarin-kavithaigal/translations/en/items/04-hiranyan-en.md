# Hiranyan

<!-- scan 55 -->

Hiranyan

Upon the neck of Earth, the woman,
the Southland rolls like a strand of pearls;
a lovely vīṇā taken up and plucked!
A grove of art where gatherings of poets weave garlands of verse;
Lanka, like a hand into which the people of the world
heap tribute and offerings!
In those old days when the honey-sweet sound of Tamil,
delightful to the ear,
spread Tamil valour throughout the world....

A warrior whose shoulders were like twin mountains,
a body rich in splendour;
honey to the Dravidians — moonlight to them —
a weapon for sweet Tamil, a great one who shone!
He had a moustache like a sheath in which war-swords slept!
He had an unquenchable longing
that the Tamil land should live in a way the world would praise;
and whenever he saw the Aryans march bearing grass,
there was the roar of a lion in his voice!
To this unwavering Tamilan
his parents gave the name **Perunthagai Vēndhu** — the noble king!
The base-minded Aryans called him **Hiranyan**!
A king who understood all three divisions of the *Tirukkural*;

<!-- scan 56 -->

he guarded his rule so that neither the Book of Wealth
nor the Book of Virtue strayed beyond the bounds of governance!
The final book, the Book of Love,
he found with his sweet queen!
And as its fruit....
so that baby-speech surpassing flute and yāzh might unfold,
a jewel-casket appeared within the queen's womb —
and the people of the country roared in joy!

A young bull with eyes of beauty and a face that spoke poetry;
because his crescent forehead surpassed even feminine beauty,
his parents called him **Pirainuthalān**, the crescent-browed one;
the backward Aryans called him **Prahlada**!
At a time when they had found no path by which to destroy the king,
if the banner of the clan should sprout in the warrior-king's line,
they said, ruin would come upon the Veda-chanting clan itself;
and like fire flaring in a sacrificial pit
the Aryans boiled up to devise a plot!
They made their jackal-plan and dispersed —
those grass-bearers who had come to turn the country into a forest!

As the king's son grew day by day,
changing with every passing hour....
a jackal crossed their path saying,
"I alone am skilled enough
to teach him swordcraft, archery and the arts of learning."
The king flared up:
"You coward who would fear even
to stroke a screw-pine leaf against its grain!
You fool who does not know how to hold a sword
even to cut a banana tree!
Go back the way you came!"

"It is not good that he should go away with a wounded heart;

<!-- scan 57 -->

show no difference of caste when giving education, O king!"
The crowned queen stood sulking as she said it,
faulting the victor's "narrow outlook";
she had learned a false definition of broad-mindedness....
She asked that milk and eggs be offered to a snake!
The mark of a narrow outlook — the sacred thread upon the chest!
A crooked heart from which little-jackal deeds arise —
an Aryan doctrine-text in the hand!
Yet, in the name of a large-minded outlook,
she asked him to accept the emissary of Aryanism
who had come close only to destroy from within!
Because answering word for word against
the round-faced great queen was not his custom,
the great warrior who showed tiger-speed and storm-speed in battle
agreed in an instant and went away.

To the wavering crowd that lives thinking
Tamil **vāḷ** — "sword" — means merely a monkey's **vāl**, its "tail";
that **vēl**, "spear," means the karuvēlam tree;
and **kaḷam**, "field of battle," means only a place where paddy is heaped —
there came as their ally,
a creature the source mocks as `ōthiyamilār-ōṇān kuñju`,
who sat like an incomparable teacher and delivered lessons;
the prince sat obediently before him and studied.
The teacher's eldest daughter, Asha Devi,
stayed nearby and made friends with him!
Even the womb of Aryanism carries intrigue —
who on this earth has not been caught in its whirl?
He began with the source-form chant, **"ari namōthu sindham"**!
He told with relish the tale in which a fox became a horse!
The maiden ripened into fruit; the prince became a parrot!
He taught the tale of the red-matted one who wears the moon,
and the sacred sport he played with a devotee's wife....

<!-- scan 58 -->

The tale of Brahma, maker of the three worlds,
uniting with his daughter Tilottama....
The tale of Kannan overturning the pots of young maidens
by the seashore, riverbank, forest-edge and house-yard....
He taught every fevered tale of erotic desire!
The girl became a pawn in the gambling game her father played!
Without tiring, she taught the young prince the arts of pleasure!

Believing his son would return a warrior from Vivekapuri,
the king asked,
"Have you finished archery? Elephant-riding?"
The son replied with scorn:
"Why, father, all that useless misery?
I have tasted the pleasure given by Inbavalli,
in whom every beauty is gathered....
I have found the path to behold
the lotus-feet of the Lord!"
Hearing his son speak so,
Hiranyan sprang at him in fury:
"With shoulders eager for combat,
I have stood firm as diamond within the palmyra trunk,
shaking the directions!
I took up the sword to split the mouths
of those base men who, upon a people that worships Nature,
smuggle in an idol under the name of God!
Show me, then — who is your god?
If not, in a forest torrent.... in mid-ocean.... in blazing fire....
I shall plunge you down, grind you to powder
and scatter you upon the earth...."
His words flew like a gunshot,
cutting one thing cleanly into two;
the prince, who had returned with the brains of a goat,
stood frightened!
Had the queen not stepped between them there....
Tamil honour would have remained;
Hiranyan's son would have died....

<!-- scan 59 -->

"If by tomorrow night you do not show me God,
the mother who bore you as a burden....
and the beloved who has turned hard as stone....
will cry in terror as you mount the funeral bier!
No son — no warrior —
no more glory to my line because of you!
Go! Go! Disappear!"
He stood lamenting....

Even when armies clashed like mountains,
even when enemies swarmed around him like locusts,
he was the great warrior whose mind never shook;
a golden page in the history of martial Tamils.
But hearing that his son had gone over to the Aryans,
the prince slipped away in hiding, broken and afraid!

Before the Vedic priest seated beside the sacrificial pit,
he told all that had happened:
"My life has reached the point of departure....
Ask your gods to come!"
"Do not fear — come!" said
Asha Devi, green-parrot girl,
drawing him onto her lap
and raining kiss after kiss....
To change his mind,
she wrapped him against her breast and held him!
Having completed every kind of lesson
in the pleasures of desire,
she began a new display of arts;
she composed a new dictionary of sexual delight!
She played and rejoiced —
a wasp that transforms the senses of the worm it seizes!
She stung him.... with kovvai-red lips!
The prince born to rule the great land
where Avvai's Tamil is sung
subdued himself within her grip!

<!-- scan 60 -->

They judged that this was the moment for a rare conspiracy....
a plan to make Aryanism endure....
and in the council of the sacrificial pit
it received immediate approval!
Prahlada, unaware of it,
went before his father and declared,
"I shall show you the Lord!"
"Where?" roared the Hiranyan-lion.
"In this pillar!" he said;
it was a word spoken at Asha's prompting.

"In the pillar? — shame, shame!
Had you said, 'He dwells in the hearts of the pure,'
the world of reason would not deny it!
Fool — in this pillar?
In the pillar...?"
With a thunderous roar,
he raised his iron-shod foot to smash the pillar to dust!
By the traitors' arrangement
the whole pillar had been filled with magnetism —
a fact even the guards did not know!
The son who had lost his rational sense stood there too;
the villains' arrangement had vanished from his mind
under the pleasures the maiden had given him!
The raised iron shoe clung to the magnetic pillar;
the broad-shouldered one sank into astonishment!
Then a deceiver hidden there,
disguised in the form of a lion,
so that no one might know him as an Aryan,
thrust a spear into the breast of the southern sun!
Blood surged out in torrents!
They cut open his entrails; cut out his tongue;
hacked the body; destroyed the form....
Then they sold a story for people to believe:
"Our Lord came in the form of Narasinga!"
A thick fog closed over it —
the mouth that had thundered against Aryanism
was silenced;

<!-- scan 61 -->

this was the sacred theatre of Aryanism!
The son too, understanding it only as
"the god himself took my father's life," disappeared into its fold!
He did not know the full shape of Aryanism —
he stood eager to dance upon the throne!
That treacherous son is not alive today;
so what?
In this country, along his path,
are there not countless Prahladas
at the feet of Aryanism?
