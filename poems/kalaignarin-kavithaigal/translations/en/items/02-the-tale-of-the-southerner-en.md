# The Tale of the Southerner

<!-- scan 34 -->

**The Tale of the Southerner**

Short beard; squat beard; long beard; white beard;
with beards of every kind like these...
a crowd of pūsuras, spreading out a bazaar of Puranas,
tasting the sour old grove, getting drunk and dancing.
At the edge of a realm once without stain or blemish,
they slaughtered cows, goats and human beings,
heaping mounds of corpses for sacrifice!
By an Aryan invasion?... No...
by a swaying waist... a sidelong glance... by the sages'
wine of pleasure — in the southern land gone dark —
like a lamp newly lit
rose the king of Lanka!
If I begin to tell his history... every muscle dances, ayyo!
The lord of southern Lanka whose fame reached all eight directions,
forefather of the Dravidians!
Our grandfather who held fast to love of ancient Tamil.
The island of Lanka like the pupil within the eye,
the deep seas its dark iris! The great warrior
who brought renown to Tamil soil.
A lion in form, a heart of gold! He lived
with none his equal in the mode of the yāzh —
a man of sacred Jaffna, a mountain-Tamilian!

<!-- scan 35 -->

His companion, flower-tressed where bees would settle,
Mandodari — jewel of womankind — a speaker
whose words could pluck an answering veena!... Ravana's yāzh-music
was defeated only before the fruit-sweet speech
of his chaste wife in the bedchamber!
There are eyelid-doors to guard beautiful eyes;
so was Ravana to Lanka!
And she to him!

In the forests, along our border-lines, Aryans
sought sacrifice for their god; saying
such cruelty must not be, he drew his sword!
Invitations from forest hermitages kissed his feet;
the laughter of those young women flowered like a palmyra spathe!
"Ha! Would I touch an Aryan scorpion?"
That bull raised a cry of resistance!.....
Wherever foxes filled the forest, wherever deceivers roamed the land:
guard! guard! To those armies
Ravana was the cock that announced the hour! The cock!

Tamil queen Muthunagai, Ravana's sister,
blew the conch against the ascetics of Dandakaranya!
She sprang forth like a vengai, a woman of a warrior clan;
the Ganges seemed to stream from the eyes of the Veda-chanters!
Having understood that strength of shoulder could not
defeat the Tamils, they turned to treachery and intrigue:
with the younger Lakshmana and Sita, his wife, following behind,
the king called Rama set out,
a man who, after wronging his brother and seeking the crown,
was scorned throughout the land as a betrayer of trust
and had gone away to the forest!

<!-- scan 36 -->

One day,
as he wandered by the forest, sugarcane-like Muthunagai
slept from weariness beside an orchard of fruit trees.
The vile man, his reason lost to lust,
went near the pure woman sleeping with lotus eyes closed,
intending wickedly to violate her — his younger brother
beside him as accomplice.
Sita Devi stood there as a ready hand for guard!
Muthunagai, who had fallen asleep without a guard,
flared up to protect the fire of her chastity...
one woman alone like a fruit tree — against two monkeys!
What could she do?

As the price of chastity she lost a hand! Lost her nose!
Lost the teeth that scattered Muthunagai's pearls! Alas,
lost her breast — yet her chastity
did not lose its ground!
A Tamil woman who would guard honour even if her head were lost,
Ravana's own younger sister!

The ear-burning news that a guileless sister
had been made into Surpanakha reached him —
he looked as though a wound had opened in his eye.
The king who ruled sea-girt earth — was he not
Muthunagai's elder brother?
Even if there were no spreading bond of kin,
was she not a citizen of Lanka?
He rose to take revenge,
storing in his eyes the redness of the young morning sun...!

Hearing that the king was coming, beautiful-eyed Sita Devi
demanded a winsome deer
and sent her husband racing after it!

<!-- scan 37 -->

"In the elder brother's absence, do you think
to cast a net for your sister-in-law, younger brother?

Run after the elder one, you too!" — she drove him away
and waited for the Southerner to arrive.

With eyes throwing sparks, the warrior came like a chariot.
He saw Sita, pouring honeyed speech, come to the street entrance.

"The elder and younger have both run away —
you, the one suited to me, have now come —
I give myself to you," she said,
and twined herself around Ravana's broad shoulders,
green-bangled Sita Devi like a spreading jasmine creeper!

"You are the wife of the man who harmed
my own life-blood sister!
Rather than give your body to crow and vulture
and lusting bear,
I will not even let my hand touch it.
Board the ship," he said.

The companion of the man born in a lineage
that had loaded its honour upon a ship said,
"Let my body accept the gift,"
and leaned upon his strong chest —
thinking that somehow she would gain the boon,
she boarded the vessel and stood,
a she-fox behind the lion of proud gait!

"If he carries Sita Devi away as prisoner,
sets her upon the throne and makes her crowned queen,
and if Sita bears him a son,
that son too will have a share in Lanka's sceptre.
If a country for Aryans can come
like palmyra fruit, without labour —

<!-- scan 38 -->

what harm if ten thousand Sitas go?"
But the Aryans' thought did not come to pass.
For Sita the conjurer,
the king of Lanka arranged a prison!

"Those who proclaim 'Veda!' and 'sacrifice!' —
go break their ribs
as you would split firewood!" he ordered.

The victorious king had a younger brother, Vibhishana...
a parasite upon a vengai tree,
waiting to bring down his elder brother and rule.
Into the hand of a fifth-column force called Hanuman
he placed a firebrand:
"Destroy the great city of Lanka," he said...
all southern Lanka became a forest of fire, a forest of corpses!
Through the cooperation of corrupt Vibhishana,
the dwarf-fox Aryans found a road to advance their race!

<!-- scan 39 -->

The cruel man who, by the scheming of the darbha-grass men,
had killed his elder brother Vali, a Tamil warrior —
Sugriva, who priced away self-respect —
fell at the feet of the eye-rolling Aryans.

The gold who had trusted his younger brother,
the lion who had ruled until the whole earth trembled,
saw enemy forces riding the waves of the sea
and called, "Own brother, Vibhishana! Come at once!"

"Do not play with a cobra lifting its hood —
you will tremble like a debtor's heart, Anna!
Let us go together and surrender; come!"
Thus the younger brother stood singing the praise
of Aryans who displayed their liquor jars.

The Southerner saw the monkey-show; his eyes
became pools.
"You who came to ruin the clan!
Handle of the axe!
Thank me that I have not cut away your wicked tongue —
walk out!" he said,
and strode toward the battlefield....!

The noble one steeped in the same womb,
mighty in the sleep of thought,
the beautiful younger brother Kumbakarna,
like the sword hanging at his elder brother's waist,
sprang after him toward the place where armies clashed!

The youngest brother came and stood in his path.
"Gathering armies to defeat
the Aryans who live with matted hair is useless —
all will be ruined," he said.

"I am not a brother who lives by setting up a shop of betrayal!
Birthplace of destruction! Walk on!"
He surged away like a flood that had burst its embankment!

<!-- scan 40 -->

"My dear warriors! My comrades rising high!
I am Ravana, who rules this southern Lanka today —
men of base occupation, men stripped of character,
men who live by cheating,
men who lie at their feet —
I have put on the armour of war
to destroy them all in half a moment!

Friends like tigers! I had thought
the wicked priests had no army!
They have made maidens into encampments
and gathered every army they need.
With young women of kovai-fruit lips,
guava cheeks and blanket-bodies,
all the kings of our race
have leaned toward the sages!

Showing full-moon faces,
the wives of ascetics have tormented crowned kings —
fortresses where the Tamil flag once flew
have become monasteries for begging Aryans, ayyo!

There is the thick-skinned one, thicker even than a buffalo,
who harmed his dear younger brother and fled to the forest;
that degraded Sugriva is his companion —
the man who brought death to his elder brother!

Another is Rama.
With the traitor called Hanuman
stands my own brother who set Lanka on fire!

If Rama and all these men oppose me,
must there not be some mystery in this?

They demand that I
give Sita a son and then hand Lanka's sceptre
to that very son —
firebrand-like conditions
from wicked Rama's band!

My own companion is a golden daughter like a flower-ball,
fresh Tamil beyond even the reach
of poets who weave verse —

<!-- scan 41 -->

my great queen,
cool moon! block of diamond!
For this yāzh-player,
why a ruined veena called Sita?

Not even for a few days will I open a path
for our land to touch the Aryans' feet and bow!

Warriors whose eyes themselves display valour!
Swear upon our language and set forth —
we will avenge ourselves upon the porcupines and return!"
Thus thundered the war-drum
of the man with hill-like shoulders!

Hearing him, everyone roared!....

On the battlefield smelling of death,
the armies on both sides poured blood!
Those with shattered heads,
those with torn bellies —
all were Tamils, one clan!

By mountain-side, by tree-side, hiding,
enemy Rama fought like a rat!

"Never on this earth have I seen such a method —
can bow and arrow not reach?"
Kumbakarna flared in anger.
Seeing Rama leap and flee,
he cried, "Coward, turn back!"
He roared like an elephant:
"Is life some sugarcane to cling to?"

He had not finished speaking...
from behind him
Lakshmana poured a shower of arrows!

The dear younger brother fell like an elephant.
The pure Tamilian leading the army saw it!
Every thought, every atom of him scattered fire.
"Cats!... stand where you are!"
he shouted and loosed the war-arrow!

Was it rain pouring from a cold cloud?
Was it thunder cracking in summer?
He took on storm-speed — a new speed!

<!-- scan 42 -->

Like a tiger struck by a spear — like an arrow released —
like floodwater freed from a dam —
the man whose chest had borne a thousand swords
fought!...
With heels at their napes, the cowards hid!

"The enemy is finished!" he laughed aloud
and turned back along the road.
From hiding, the Aryans shot a single arrow.
He cut away the shaft lodged in his chest
and, lifting his sword, turned:
"Who is the coward?"

Rama's band, trapped with no road to run,
would soon have no flesh even left to search for!....

"My land is sugarcane-sweet;
to you who have trapped it in misery —
this sweep of my sword
is lightning!" he cried and whirled it.

The man famed for facing down even a cyclone!

Vibhishana, who had understood
that the Aryan situation had become desperate,
had a spear in his hand!... He hurled it
toward his elder brother's back!

That spear, flying from hiding,
pierced his mountain-rock back,
thrust its head out through his chest
and poured tears!

As though the Himalaya itself had fallen,
Ravana fell!

"The man who killed me is my younger brother,
shoulders like a palmyra, feet at the Brahmin's feet —
in his hand there is medicine to save life!
But I die having heard
that my own blood-brother fought from hiding."

Hearing this, the people of Lanka
became as though dead themselves!
