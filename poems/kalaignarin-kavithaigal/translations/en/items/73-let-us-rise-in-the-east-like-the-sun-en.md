# Let Us Rise in the East Like the Sun!

<!-- scan 450 -->

Let us rise in the east
like the sun!

Each day, when they turn the pages of the newspapers,
Tamil people writhe at news that stings like scorpions.
A rule that destroys the lives of the poor like a sword—
and what a sight: those who carry the papers
show not even one drop of shame!

Every day comes news of theft in temples;
even the idols of the Lord are stolen—
a red fire in devotees' hearts.
Cholera too, they say, spreads
as food for the hunger of “Death”;
in police stations, rape and killings
continue day after day!

Those who are in government
certainly need protection;
we are not so ignorant as to say they do not.
But for that,
must thousands upon thousands of police
line up and stand like motionless tall trees,
doing penance along every road?

The people of Kumari groan
under the damage of floods;
without even a mustard-seed of concern in the heart,
a flower-strewn path is laid
for the Chief Minister's car.
They certainly know how to curry favour—
but will the country prosper?

<!-- scan 451 -->

⟦continuation from previous page⟧

Full-page advertisements
for schemes that never happen;
a proclamation:
“I bear the Dravidian lamp!”
Those who lie Nandi-like
for money, office and concessions say,
“Good, good—you alone are the Mother of Dravidianism!”
What shame!

Bus fares make eyes bulge;
the quantity of ration rice shrinks sharply;
the cruelty of prices rises into a giant form;
Tamil people breathe out fire in long sighs!
No—people turned to embers!

With fertiliser prices rising,
and diesel, petrol and cooking-gas prices rising too,
the eyes of farmers burn with heat;
so do the eyes of those who labour beside them,
and those of traders!

Professional tax, land tax, crop tax—
taxes in pile after pile.
Seeing the acts of rulers who bring such distress,
people have risen today saying,
“Even patience has a limit!”

Bribery and corruption—crores upon crores—
spread their beds, sing and dance in delight;
without even a little shame,
they sit in the Fort and scold others!

To watch and endure all this—
is this Tamil Nadu,
or a burial ground, a cremation ground?
So, vowing that we shall endure no longer,
all our siblings,
so that the country may live
and decline may end,
will join shoulders
with the ranks of our allied parties
and set out!

<!-- scan 452 -->

⟦continuation from previous page⟧

To teach a lesson to the deceivers
who abandoned in midstream
the people who trusted them and voted,
first comes the November protest—
a struggle on the path of nonviolence!
The next stage,
as decided by all parties:
the arrow we launch will be a total shutdown!
We can lie down no longer.
As a people's uprising,
let us rise in the east like the sun!

## Translator note

The poem moves from a newspaper catalogue of public grievances into explicit opposition mobilisation. Temple theft, police-station violence, flood response, prices, taxation and corruption are retained as the poem states them. `காக்காய் பிடிக்கின்றார்` is rendered “curry favour,” while the source's image of supporters lying “Nandi-like” for money, office and concessions is kept rather than smoothed away.
