# Mother Art’s Foremost Son!

<!-- scan 376 -->

A gaze cool as mist,
speech sweet as fruit,
a talent all his own —
a musician-poet
who gave beauty with sweetness!
Whenever people called,
he came to serve their laughter —
a good volunteer!
A Tamil who made Tamil flourish!
A generous giver
whose rain-like hand protected conferences!
A Tamil bull who held
that the one great fortune
was the fortune of labour!
Sole king of the world of art —
his heart itself an ocean —
what cruelty is this,
that leaving us shaken,
he has entered a sleep
from which he will not rise?

“Laughter alone is medicine
for human life —
mix it with the honey of thought
and drink it every day;
while I am here,
that feast shall never run short!”
So he spoke and lived —
alas,
he has fallen into consuming fire!

<!-- scan 377 -->

He made the poor laugh;
he made cowards stand upright;
and leaving all of us crying aloud,
he has gone to the world of death!

In cinema he made the fragrance of knowledge spread,
and stood as the nation’s strong right arm —
our **Nalla Thambi**, our “Good Brother”!

When a crippled thought rose
saying, “Let great wealth be hoarded,”
he broke the point of that thought
with **Panam** — *Money* —
the poet who gave us that work!

He turned the widow
into a **Manamagal** — a bride —
and let the birds of love
fly into a new sky,
a warrior of action!

Amid the stones thrown by villains
and the blows of words they dealt,
he kept explaining his principles
through *villuppaattu*,
in a way that made
even bullying troublemakers bare their teeth and laugh —
what a master he was!

Old Nandan goes
to the temple where dancing is done;
new **Kindan** goes
to the shrine where knowledge is sought.
We rejoiced that the Othiyamilar-hearted,
seeing you,
ran away.
Today,
having lost you,
our hearts melt!

Where is the sight
of you storing a smile
at the edge of your lips,
welcoming us with flower-like eyes?
Where is that small smiling face,
its lips red with beauty,
chewing a green leaf
like a parrot’s wing,
with fresh shell-lime and betel nut?

Did you become fire
so that Tamils might float on water,
O leader of art?

<!-- scan 378 -->

In this vast great Tamil land,
where waterways cut through the soil,
O king of art who lived with distinction —
if we had known a storm would blow
and uproot you,
countless crores of hands
would have held back that storm!

“I shall trip opposition
with a young smile
and sit beside my leader Anna,”
you said and rose.
Before that could happen,
you fell into death’s lap!

My life! My nectar!
What thought was in you
when you gave up life?
Did you say,
“I piled up a mountain of gold;
today I lie at the foot
of a mountain of dust,”
and then climb instead
to the summit of the mountain of fame?

New jewel! Pearl of fame!
You travelled on an artistic journey
to **Rusiya**, where common ownership was sprouting.
Did you conceive another journey like that
and set out on this great journey,
leaving a great blame upon us?

O art!
O imagination!
O beauty!
O knowledge!
O love!
O virtue!
Who is there now,
a good man like you?

You are there —
and your fame is there,
forever in the hearts of Tamils!

## Translator notes

- The canonical title separates `தலைச் செல்வன்!`; the contents witness compounds `தலைச்செல்வன்` and omits the terminal exclamation. **Mother Art’s Foremost Son!** follows the canonical elegiac sense.
- The poem is Kalaignar’s elegy for Kalaivanar N. S. Krishnan. Film/work titles embedded as puns — `நல்ல தம்பி`, `பணம்`, `மணமகள்`, and `கிந்தன்` — are kept recognisable in bold with brief English glosses rather than dissolved into ordinary prose.
- `வில்லடிப் பாட்டு` is retained as *villuppaattu*, the bow-song performance form used for reformist public explanation.
- `ஒதியமிலார்` is a final-cleared source form retained conservatively, consistent with earlier anthology translations.
- `ருசியா` is translated as the source-visible “Rusiya”/Russia reference in the poem’s common-ownership art-tour memory; no outside biographical wording is substituted.
