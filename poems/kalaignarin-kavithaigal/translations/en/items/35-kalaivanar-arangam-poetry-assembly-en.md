# Kalaivanar Arangam Poetry Assembly

<!-- scan 329 -->

On many themes about Anna,
poets have come taking words
sweet as jackfruit segments
to inscribe poetry
like letters on stone,
poetry time cannot erase.

At Kalaivanar Arangam,
today — an assembly of poet-lords!

I hear the mocking laughter of some:
“What use is a poetry assembly to the Kazhagam?
Have they no shame at all to hold a festival?
We stand here victorious,
swinging the sword of valour;
why do these useless men need
a Three Great Celebration?”
So an arrogant voice sounds over there.

I shall give one explanation
to them and to you.
Like a thief who puts out the lamp,
enters a house,
grabs what he can,
and runs away without being caught,
they have won the Aruppukkottai
and Nellai constituencies!

That is precisely why I said earlier:

<!-- scan 330 -->

under this “Idi Amin” rule,
we should not desire by-elections
even to the tip of a sesame seed!
Yet our dear siblings said with one voice
that in a battlefield
it is glory to receive an honourable wound.
Therefore,
against the army of money-rule,
our army of democracy clashed!

If even the great poet Kamban hesitated
to praise as a great warrior
Rama, the honoured Ramachandra
who hid and felled Vali,
then even in death,
was not victory Vali's?

And even if he ruled afterwards,
was not his brother Sugriva still a traitor?

Therefore,
those who fall bearing honourable wounds
will still be listed among heroes.

Even when brought low,
a Tamil will never lower his head
by losing self-respect.

He will not forget
the distinct culture
of gratefully honouring mother and father.

Because Anna came as mother to Tamils
and Periyar as father,
the DMK, which stands as their child,
without forgetting even a little of its debt of gratitude,
today —

<!-- scan 331 -->

heals with a smile
the wound received at the battle-front
and prepares the road to future victory.

Thinking Tamil,
wetting the heart with Tamil —
our Tamils once exercised mastery
over the sounding sea.
Yet like holding spit in the mouth,
brushing filthy teeth,
then rinsing and swallowing that same spit back,
Tamils themselves consented
to destroy their nectar-like ethnic character,
artistic greatness and literature,
and spat foul saliva back into the body.

The gut filled with filth must be washed clean.
For that we need
a good laundry-worker called medicine.

Bodily purity comes through medicine;
purity of the heart comes through truth.

Making a road for that truth,
placing explosives beneath the fortress of deceit,
for sixty years without fear
fought Periyar —
our Anna's leader!

Accepting Ayya Periyar's leadership,
Anna too set out
to break the chains of slavery.

Under the chairmanship of Ma. Nannan,
a rationalist of character
born, it seems, for the office
of judge in a *pattimandram* —

<!-- scan 332 -->

in yesterday's debate held here,
the team of younger brother Durai Murugan lost.

In one sense,
that was just!
One team argued
that politics was the greater cause
of Anna's lasting fame.
The other team established
that social service was greater.

Because this gathering accepted
the argument put forward
by younger Selvendran's team,
the courteous judge, Mr Nannan,
leaned toward society
and gave that as his verdict.

If politics is like taking paddy
that has come home after harvest,
husking it into rice,
placing it in the pot
and cooking a meal,
then social work is this:
watering the field where that paddy grows,
preparing the soil,
adding manure,
and removing the weeds growing nearby.

Understanding this,
Anna performed social work
and took politics into his hands
as a weapon for social progress!

## Translator notes

- The title witnesses are exact; **Kalaivanar Arangam** is retained as the venue name rather than translated into a generic hall.
- The Aruppukkottai/Nellai by-election, “Idi Amin” rule, money-rule versus democracy, and future-victory passages are translated as the poem's political rhetoric and claims.
- The Rama/Vali/Sugriva comparison is preserved as Kalaignar deploys it, without devotional or epic harmonisation.
- The spit/gut/medicine cleansing metaphor repeats imagery found elsewhere in the anthology; the English retains its intentionally harsh bodily register.
- `பட்டிமன்றம்` is retained as *pattimandram*, the Tamil public debate form. The closing paddy-field analogy distinguishes politics from the prior social labour that makes political progress possible.
