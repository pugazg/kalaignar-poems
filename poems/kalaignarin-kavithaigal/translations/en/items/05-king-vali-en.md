# King Vali

<!-- scan 62 -->

King Vali

Golden hill of noble thought, good man of Kishkindha;
unmatched master in wrestling and sword-fight!
Tara, Tamil woman, guards the husband she has chosen;
a maiden who brings delight with sugar-candy speech!
Pearls seem picked from her teeth!
A bow seems drawn across her brows!
Even the moon learns from her
a new art of giving light!
By strength of shoulder, by strength of sword,
all the enmities that gather rank upon rank
are crushed to dust — trouble disappears!
In giving, he is Vēl Pāri!
In falling at the feet of Tamil and paying homage, a Valluvar!
Under the rule of that mighty man there is no poverty;
therefore there is no suffering.
Yet is it not nature that even the good bear one blemish?
A mountain-serpent that comes to kill; a savage beast;
the total form of boundless selfishness without principle;
a complete fool —
the ill-named Sugriva happened to be born his younger brother!
A minister called Hanuman was his great companion;
a religious monkey fighting for the order of Manu!

<!-- scan 63 -->

Into sugarcane sweet at every joint
he planned to mix neem-oil atom by atom;
to destroy southern Tamil, fine as millet-flour,
he became the traitorous ally of directionless Aryanism.
"If you bring down your elder brother,
the throne will lie at your feet — understand that, Sugriva!"
Thus he urged the fool on,
and the two who had waited for their moment spoke together.
They sent a brute called Avunan against the elder brother.

"There is no one who can defeat me in wrestling;
let any who deny it come and compete!"
Beating his chest, Avunan stood in challenge.
The great king who ruled the earth,
lamp of the martial Tamil clan,
welcomed him with a smile and sounded the war-drum!
"How many days will this wrestling last?
Both are great warriors," said one crowd.
"He looks even stronger than the king;
will some harm come to our ruler?" worried another crowd.
"He looks like a mountain;
his two shoulders are like mortars.
The king will surely lose," another crowd faltered.
But before any shock the people expected could occur,
the great warrior Avunan fell senseless from a single blow!
The next blow.... "Ammaadi!".... "Appaadi!"...!
After that there was no one to catch him....
he hid himself inside a mountain cave!
Sugriva, whose intrigue was not yet complete,
said to his brother:
"It would be good to capture Avunan,
bring him back and make a spectacle for the country!

<!-- scan 64 -->

Elder brother, follow him!"
He stood pointing out the cave.
The brother who would refuse to believe
that his younger brother's whole heart was a mountain of fire
walked toward the cave!
So that the lion who entered the cave
would die without returning,
the hostile schemers planned — and acted!
They brought elephants, rolled boulders into place
and sealed the cave-mouth!
They thought, "We have destroyed the lion."
They laughed, "We have attained the throne."
Taking their seats in state, they began to proclaim:

"People of great Kishkindha!
The news that your father, my elder brother, has died
is a terrible wonder!
Even to think of it pains our hearts!
By fighting craftily and feigning flight,
Avunan the warrior has sent that incomparable hero
to the other world — alas! Misery! Misery!
There is no use in weeping! There is no one to console us!
Worshipping the Lord is now my only work;
I shall become an ascetic."
So saying, he shed false tears in streams!

"Do not go, young lord! Do not go!
Take the right to rule the earth;
accept the duty of protecting us!"
The uncomprehending people of the land lamented and blocked his way.
"I cannot refuse, O minister! O Maruti!
Bring the jewelled crown — put it upon my head!
I tried to avoid responsibility — but will the people let me?
I cannot bring myself to reject my brother's country!"

<!-- scan 65 -->

The people standing around rejoiced
at the words of Sugriva, master of intrigue.
Anjaneya flew like a whirlwind,
bustling through the work of coronation —
and at that moment, like a storm-wind,
like a great fire, Tara Devi burst in!

"A hare's horn is the crown upon your head, Sugriva — know that!
You joined with Hanuman so that an alien doctrine
might spread through Tamil land,
and you brought down your own elder brother!
Exception to every rule that defines a younger brother!
Traitor to trust! There is no feeling in your blood!
I would say you do not have even the attachment
a bee bears toward a flower
for this pure Tamil land.
Had you told my husband you wished to rule,
would he not, that man of gold, have given it freely?
A battlefield coward without even the strength
to win it by fighting!
You have sat as charioteer of an empire like a chariot —
shame! shame!
If you have courage, lift your sword!
Answer the opposition of this plume-wearing woman
and then rule the country!
Where is Hanuman, whose thighs tremble?"

To Tara who thundered thus,
base Sugriva said:
"Even if you are wife to a great warrior
who fights until armies tremble,
you are still a woman whose waist sways as she walks!
Inside the room!
A hen brooding eggs has no work in the public hall —
go! go!"

"Who did you say that to?"
Tara sprang up
and began pouring words like tongues of flame:
"Cockerel-chick hatched from the egg
of that brooding hen!

<!-- scan 66 -->

Useless male!
Little jackal! Tiny ant!
You whose thick tongue dares insult womankind!
Go and ask the race of dogs to teach you the meaning of gratitude!
Get out of here now!" she lunged.

"You decided there was no one to restrain your arrogance!
Because I showed you respect as crowned queen,
you mistook it for fear!
You have given me the occasion to invoke the law —
guards, this insolent woman must be put in prison —
let the work be done at once!"
With his two hard eyes he gave the order.

"Stop there, younger brother!
Newcomer to the rule of the land!
You took a woman for something feeble —
know that she can spring like a leopard!
You leap in delight thinking,
'We sealed him in the black mountain cave
behind a door of rock' — good!
This is no dream, younger brother; I am your elder brother —
the king of Kishkindha whose time has not yet ended!
See how I kicked away the boulder
with which elephant, Hanuman and loving younger brother
had sealed me in!"
Roaring so, King Vali entered.
Seeing his elder brother,
the younger fled into hiding with Hanuman,
wandering the forest rejoicing that they had saved their lives.

One day there,
the "great warrior" Rama, who had lost his wife,
came to rest with his younger brother Lakshmana.
Sugriva received them and served tender coconuts.

<!-- scan 67 -->

"Had this been the house of the rishis,
there would have been a sweeter drink.
Never mind — it will do," said Lord Rama, drinking.
Then he said:

"My wife is a young-breasted, drum-waisted woman —
a sculpted beauty who enters the field of desire and wins!
All the pleasure one sees in soma-intoxication,
I would say, can be found in her glance!
Her name is Sita! Breasts like red-lotus buds!
She is not with me now.
Find where she is and help bring her back
to satisfy my hunger."

Sugriva replied:
"We shall give help — we shall give an army — but one thing!
My elder brother is an incomparable warrior;
none equals him in sword-fight or wrestling!
There is no one to defeat him in the casting of the **vāḷi**;
from that he won the special name **Vali**!
If you tell me a way to take his life by deceit,
his country will come under my control —
and after that I shall help you."

They devised a plot to seize the sacred land;
dark-hearted Sugriva set out for Kishkindha!
Grace-faced King Vali, amid the chamber's delight,
stroked the curling hair of the life dear to him and found pleasure.
Outside,
a shout whose meaning was not yet clear:
"Will you come out to fight?"
Hearing the cry, the tiger rose from the bed
and was astonished to see his younger brother standing opposite!

<!-- scan 68 -->

The younger brother knew all his elder's strength;
yet seeing him slap his shoulders without fear,
the queen withered in doubt. But what of it?
Could she restrain the warrior?
She gave him leave and sent him forth.
With tears flooding her eyes,
she fell upon the silk-cotton bed!

"Wrestling? Bow-fight? Sword-fight?"
the king asked.
"Wrestling is what I want," Sugriva said.
They collided like two mountains,
both roaring like thunder!
King Vali calculated that the fight would end in moments!
He lifted his younger brother in his hands —
Sugriva became a hand-ball to Vali,
spun round and round;
in another instant life would leave him —
victory stood with Vali.

Hidden in a thicket was the great warrior Rama.
He took aim at Vali's chest and loosed an arrow.
Like a flood bursting a dam,
a torrent of blood rushed from the noble chest;
like a spring exploding from split earth,
the fountain of blood rising from his heart struck the clouds.

"Who is the warrior who attacked me from hiding?
Ah! Rama? Messenger of dharma?
Look at Tamil blood pouring upon the earth!
Why do you wander uselessly wearing the name of warrior?
To receive a wound on the front is valour —
to rise in that splendour is honour —
the Tamilan is one who says honour is greater than life!
Whoever denies it is an enemy!
The younger brother who keeps company with that enemy —
the world of reason will turn away rather than look at him!....

<!-- scan 69 -->

Hero who won fame by breaking a rotten bow!
Champion who seeks another's help to recover a noble woman!
Because there are some Tamils
who believe everything white is milk,
you strut about as a mighty hero!
A coward without courage to stand face to face and fight —
if the grain is lost while only chaff remains,
can life still be of use?
If I die and you remain,
will valour spread?"

Thus King Vali questioned —
with his questions he sounded a war-drum!
Before he could finish, he fell — a corpse!

The news spread — a red fire caught.
Red-lotus Tara Devi came and collapsed!
"My king! When I am here for you to embrace,
have you embraced Mother Earth?"
She wept; she wailed.
With her tears she defeated
the river of blood that ran from him —
and went with her husband!
Rama saw the culture of Tamil land —
and bowed his head!
