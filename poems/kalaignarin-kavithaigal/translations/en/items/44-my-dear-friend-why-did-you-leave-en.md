# My Dear Friend! Why Did You Leave?

<!-- scan 390 -->

Written by Kalaignar when Kaviyarasu Kannadasan died.

★

My dear friend!
You who gave comfort to the heart
with poems of young summer —
why did you stop
your heart's own beat?

You blew like a breeze;
you also scorched
like fire inside my heart!
Even then,
our friendship shone
like the inseparable *anril* pair;
it never became
an extinguished lamp, my friend!

Kannadasa!
Dear one who sweetens
all my thoughts!
You were a flower-garden of poetry —
and the earthquake called Time
has shattered you
and levelled you to the ground!

You were a child
who stretched out your arms
and leapt toward every side
where someone held out a hand to caress you.
Did you leap in just that way
into the lap of the woman called the grave,
to drink the milk of peace
and fall asleep?

You were the cuckoo
who rejoiced singing
music for the movement —

<!-- scan 391 -->

once, they drugged you into a daze
and parted you away.
However many arrows of attack
you loosed afterward,
this heart of mine that bore them
was still your mother!

Even when you scolded,
because you scolded in Tamil,
I accepted it
like a sweet *pittu*.
Sweet poetic Tamil!
Why have you left me behind
in the midst of this separation?

Ah!
In that field of youth
we planted seedlings called love!
Believing our friendship
a crop for a thousand ages,
we dreamed a thousand crores of dreams!
Someone came for the harvest —
and cut away only you!

Your mind was never steady;
yet your fame became permanent!
To bring about this wonder,
sweet Mother Tamil
stood beside you!

My friend!
My dear companion!
You who sang
so many lullabies —
Mother Nature has now
rocked you and put you to sleep!
You who received
so much praise —
is Mother Nature's own caress
sweeter to you now?

You forgot me!
You hid yourself from us!
Yet because we cannot forget you,
you have filled every corner
of our hearts!

## Translator notes

- The title witnesses are exact. `பிரிந்தாய்` is rendered as “did you leave?” to retain the elegy's direct spoken grief without adding an explicit object absent from the Tamil.
- `அன்றில்` invokes the traditional image of inseparable paired birds; the Tamil term *anril* is retained rather than replaced with a generic modern species name.
- `பிட்டு` is kept as *pittu*, preserving the source's wordplay between a scolding received as Tamil and a sweet morsel.
- The movement-song / later-arrow passage is translated as the poem's autobiographical friendship rhetoric; Phase 4 does not convert it into an external political-history gloss.
