# In a Changing Town

<!-- scan 311 -->

Under the title **“In a Changing Town,”**
on 10 August 1980,
Puducherry Radio arranged an Independence-Day poetry assembly
at the Puducherry Kamban Kalaiarangam.
Under Kalaignar's chairmanship,
six poets sang on the sub-themes
**equality, labour, surroundings,
home welfare, education and unity.**
This is the presiding poem he delivered.

Greetings
to the Honourable Chief Minister of Puducherry,
to the other ministers,
to the Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the Assembly,
to the respected state organiser
who plans and carries out his tasks,
and to this overflowing hall —
to all my life-blood siblings
who would give even their lives
to protect classical Tamil,
I offer my greetings!

Greetings
to the elders of rich Tamil
who have come to hear this radio *kaviyarangam*;

<!-- scan 312 -->

greetings too
to the smiling poets
who have come to sing in many modes.

It is a puzzle
that Puducherry Radio invited
this poet who writes
without caring much for *etukai* or *monai*!
Accept my thanks for that!

In the land where the volcanic poet of Ettayapuram
forged chartered poetry,
in the land where Bharathidasan,
the poet who made an earthquake of revolution,
was born,
we have gathered for a poetry assembly of freedom
that cuts away the destroying chains of slavery.

I salute the motherland
that lets freedom flash
in golden letters of history,
yet still carries poverty
upon its head.

Even after the fox-natured foreigners
left the country,
the benefit of our own rule
has not reached all who suffer.

In Puducherry,
like bottles emptied after drinking,
poor people lie here and there
in the middle of streets,
in fields,
by wells,
their bellies scorched,
walking corpses!

<!-- scan 313 -->

When will the tongue praise this
as a golden land?
There is no clothing
to protect human dignity,
no house in which to shelter.
What use is an honourarium
to mud, mire,
the insects and worms of the drain?
They do not need one!

Did we churn freedom
from the ocean of struggle
only so that self-respect might disappear?

Because they had nothing,
in Dindigul,
loving people who worship in Allah's name
rushed out of line to receive charity.
In the cruel mortar of death
they were ground to powder,
struck by the pestle called crowding,
and left this long world.

They ran seeking veshtis and saris,
driven by tormenting poverty.
They shattered like veenas,
and hung asleep
on the ladders of death,
without a lullaby.

Women of the mother-clan, flowers;
children, clusters of buds —
in the graveyard garden
they bloomed as plants of death.

Is freedom merely
to see crops like those of barren soil?

<!-- scan 314 -->

Is liberation something won
so that a few may live
while many wither?

Let us at least now make a rule
so those who have nothing may live
and hardship be removed.
Let us bid farewell to “fate.”
Let us march against
the supposed fruits of previous birth.
Let us, by righteous means,
break down the fortress of domination
that places barriers before life.
Let us make happiness swell
like a flood from an opened sluice.

Puducherry Radio has built this assembly
called “In a Changing Town,”
with poets steeped in Tamil.
Let us eat without weariness
the fruit of poetry
from this waterfall of honeyed taste!

Puducherry is the pilgrimage land
that stamped on me
a seal of sacrifice.
This is the sacred ground
where I died and lived again!
In this divine place
where many bathe in sacred water,
they once beat me down
thinking, “the fellow is finished.”
They lifted me and threw me into a drain.
Because the mosquitoes of death did not bite,
Puducherry kept me alive
and made me bear new trials.
Had it swallowed me that day,
I would have no trouble at all today!

There are beautiful towns
across this cooling earth;
in a changing town,
is there a greatness of newness?

<!-- scan 315 -->

Across the cooling earth
there are lovely towns,
waterfalls and rivers,
and deep seas in abundance.
A fawn leaps;
a tiger-cub rests in its mother's lap;
in the grove a cuckoo sings;
in delight a peacock dances.

Nature's abundance,
beauty's smile,
good and pleasant thought —
change in everything!
We need courage to act,
gentleness in action,
and we must shatter division.

The talk that birth makes one high or low
has been destroyed by the secretion of reason —
so we say.
Yet people do not forget division,
and religions do not renounce collision.

They let equality dance on the stage,
then secretly plot to break its leg,
and excuse themselves:
“It is God's doing — what can we do?”
Strike such a crowd with the whip!

To whom is the sermon
“You must labour” addressed?
To the many crores
already broken by labour upon labour?
They bake in the forge's hot fire;
they die in the deep mud of the field;
they perish in smoke-belching factories;
they perish among waves without fearing storms.
They turn deserts into groves.
They strike rocks to lay good roads.
Yet sweet sermons are preached at them:
how much more must they labour and tear themselves apart?

Labour according to strength,
comfort according to need —
people worn out by ceaseless work
need that at once!

<!-- scan 316 -->

Without it,
advice and sermons are bundles of wet firewood;
they cannot cook the socialism we need.
For the country to live,
we must prepare that *Nalapakam*
in practical life.

Surroundings, home welfare, education, unity —
so these do not become empty exteriors,
so victories may accumulate,
we must understand the greatness of labour
and establish equality without high and low.

**In a changing town —**

even an onion will take Draupadi's vow,
for peel after peel
another sari of skin appears!
Even an onion takes Draupadi's vow.

**In a changing town —**

a lump of mud praises itself as Karna.
Karna's heart melted with compassion;
a mud-clod melts in rain —
so is the clod Karna too?

**In a changing town —**

even the grinding-stone roller
will call itself the Lord
and demand worship;
in the temple it will make a commotion:
“Am I not also the Sivalinga?”
That equality is laughable.
Not every oil-press stone becomes a Sivalinga!

<!-- scan 317 -->

**In a changing town —**

a man who eats six times every day
will call himself a labourer,
because, he says,
he eats until sweat from his forehead falls to the ground!
Beat the great drum:
call him a madman!

**In a changing town —**

counterfeits become originals,
brass becomes gold.
To overthrow these lies,
in this changing town,
I call the poets steeped in Tamil.

Come into the poetry assembly,
you whose metrical chains caress the ear,
whose beauty surpasses an ivory statue,
whose heart is filled with delightful Tamil,
carrying the flame of love!

## Translator notes

- The title witnesses differ only in Tamil spacing: canonical `மாறி வரும்` versus contents `மாறிவரும்`. The English follows the canonical witness and preserves the contents form separately in metadata.
- `எதுகை` and `மோனை` are retained as *etukai* and *monai*, traditional Tamil sound-patterning terms, rather than replaced with an inexact English prosodic label.
- Kalaignar's Puducherry assault/survival passage is translated as first-person autobiographical rhetoric in this poem; no outside biographical harmonisation is added.
- The Dindigul crowd-death passage, poverty claims and political-economic critique are translated as source claims.
- The closing onion/Draupadi, mud-clod/Karna and grinding-stone/Sivalinga sequences are deliberate comic analogies attacking false equivalence; their Tamil cultural references remain explicit.
