# A Backwater Full of Ignorant Folk!

<!-- scan 435 -->

A backwater full of ignorant folk—
and on its panchayat board, most of them are the same!
If the one or two decent people there grow alarmed
and rise to speak against them,
they will drag them down
and throw them into the burning fire!
Are they not a gang grown fat and roaming free?
A backwater full of ignorant folk—
and on its panchayat board, most of them are the same!

★

Knowing that little dogs will bark,
they throw scraps of bread,
shut their mouths and trap them in the net!
A certain “Maya” in the middle of the hall
speaks meltingly—and hides the truth!
A trick learnt from the womb itself!
A backwater full of ignorant folk—
and on its panchayat board, most of them are the same!

There is no useful plan to make the village prosper;
Maya's work is to cultivate corruption
so that she alone may live in splendour!
Say, “This will not help,”
and they will flay your skin!

<!-- scan 436 -->

Every day, a brand-new law—
and still, because sleep has not cleared,
they beat the drum of swagger!
A backwater full of ignorant folk—
and on its panchayat board, most of them are the same!

★

They say chaste Kannagi once blazed in fury
and burned Madurai; so,
“Look, I too will burn something in return!”
and Panchali Devi burns a newspaper.
Even after propping a spine into their backs,
the fool-crowd, it seems, lies hunchbacked!
A backwater full of ignorant folk—
and on its panchayat board, most of them are the same!

★

“I shall place this whole village on a leaf
and finish eating it, cooked and uncooked alike—
this is my decision!”
Even when that Maya says so,
the yes-men are ready still
to support her and fall at her feet!
A backwater full of ignorant folk—
and on its panchayat board, most of them are the same!

Declaring contradiction itself to be scripture,
they say the drumstick tree is fit for building a house!
They say that if a wall of wax candles is raised as a fort,
fire itself will flee in fear and hide!
“I shall commit a thousand sins,” she says—
“God is on my side!” No, not even that:

<!-- scan 437 -->

“I myself am that God!” she says!
A backwater full of ignorant folk—
and on its panchayat board, most of them are the same!

★

When everyone stands like hand-raising dolls,
and lifts left hand and right hand together,
would wicked Maya's delight not boil over?
Before it collapses like a long wall that cannot stand,
even the unlearned must learn—
so that wickedness may swiftly be destroyed
on the path of righteousness!
A backwater full of ignorant folk—
and on its panchayat board, most of them are the same!

## Translator note

`மாயை` is kept as the personified “Maya,” allowing both the ordinary sense of illusion/deception and the poem's political personification to remain active. The Panchali/Kannagi, wax-wall, drumstick-tree and self-deification passages are source satire and are translated as such rather than rationalised or fact-checked.
