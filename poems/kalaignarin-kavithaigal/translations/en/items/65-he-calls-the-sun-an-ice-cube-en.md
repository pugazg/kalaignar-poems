# He Calls the Sun an Ice Cube!

<!-- scan 429 -->

He calls the Sun an ice cube!
Sibling,
I am supposedly weeping
that the government may be lost!
*Ananda Vikatan* laughs!
Ah—what satisfaction for it!
“Even if the sceptre is snatched away,
I have not the slightest worry—
no one can snatch
the writing-pen from my hand!”
I thundered at the Muthamil Peravai.
To elder-scholar Vikatan,
it seems that sounded like *Mukhari*,
a lamenting raga!

★ ★ ★

“So long as the writing-pen
remains in his hand,
Karunanidhi will not grieve
even if the sceptre goes”—

<!-- scan 430 -->

that was the line
poet Kannadasan roared many times;
at an award ceremony for artists,
I merely said it again
with pride in stately Tamil.

★ ★ ★

They say I spoke in a trembling voice—
as though I declared myself ready
to take blows like a *thavil*;
as though, uttering an analogy
in the tone of one being punished,
I went away with my head hanging—
this week the high-quality Vikatan,
counting that worthy,
has offered a criticism!
Even in its editorial it abuses me
and lays down a grammar
for supreme courage!

★ ★ ★

Because the Prime Minister said
the days when Chennai ordered Delhi
were gone,
I did not, it seems,
snarl and pounce!
Vikatan prods me!
Only if I had in fact issued such orders
would there be cause to rage:
“How can this be said?”

★ ★ ★

<!-- scan 431 -->

For some reason Vikatan has forgotten—
during the Emergency,
Delhi sent an emissary
to my own house:
if I abandoned opposition to the Emergency,
no hardships would approach,
no defamation case would be filed,
and the government’s life
would be extended by one year—
thus they tried to kindle desire!
Had I yielded,
for a year in MISA prisons
five hundred of my precious dear ones
would not have suffered!
The government too would have continued—
but one thing:
honour would have gone!

★ ★ ★

Even if crores of enemies cry,
“Come, join us,”
we are those who raise our shoulders
and say, “I shall not touch it”—
we remember not only
Paavendar’s song that showed this,
but his path as well!
Vikatan mocks us!
The contradiction
between imitation and truth—

<!-- scan 432 -->

if that gentleman who laughs in delight
would only think,
he would understand!

★ ★ ★

In the election of ’67,
Anna, Rajaji, Quaid-e-Millath,
Ma. Po. Si., and
P. Ramamurthi who stood in our alliance—
was it not Vikatan
that drew them all seated on a donkey
in a cartoon
and laughed?
The fragrance of camphor,
the firmness of the Kazhagam—
he cannot so easily understand them!
That is why
he calls the Sun an ice cube!

## Translator note

The poem is a direct rebuttal to *Ananda Vikatan*. `செங்கோல் / எழுதுகோல்` is preserved as sceptre/pen; `முகாரி` as the Mukhari raga; and the *thavil* image remains literal. The Emergency/MISA and 1967-cartoon passages are translated as Karunanidhi's source recollections, not independently fact-checked here. The closing camphor line carries the familiar donkey/camphor proverb resonance after the source's donkey-cartoon reference. The contents witness shifts the title to second-person `என்கிறாய்!`; it remains separate from canonical respectful third-person `என்கின்றார்!`.
