# Akam Creations

<!-- scan 261 -->

Presiding poetic address by
Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi
at the *kaviyarangam* on the theme
“Akam Creations,”
held on 13 July 1974
at the Muthamizh festival organised
by students of the medical colleges of Madras city.

Greetings.
To those who have come
to make waxing-moon Tamil grow,
to the unwavering tigers of self-respect,
and to Kaviyarasu Kannadasan — greetings.

Greetings too
to those who diagnose the country —
who know its pulse,
and with medicine remove the people's ills
and protect them.

Amid the thousand problems
that dwell within,
they ask me to preside
over a poetry assembly called “Akam Creations.”
These medical students,
trained to explain that even dirt
lodged beneath a fingernail
can injure the health of the body,
are troubling me —
and I have agreed.

In a time when students
holding a festival for Tamil
has itself become a great wonder —

<!-- scan 262 -->

if those learning the art of medicine
prepare a feast for Tamil,
we must bow our heads,
we must join our hands in respect.

Watering the useless weed called strike,
ruining oneself instead of drinking
education that is like milk;
young women with eyes like silk,
while pulling at a sari itself becomes a trade of decay;
going in one bus
and setting another bus on fire —
leaving all this aside,
you have gathered
to make noble Tamil grow.
Long live! Long live!

I too was once a student.
I always walked in such a way
that no condition arose
for my parents to be ashamed.

When I studied,
a love throbbed within me —
a love for Tamil!
Tamil work tasted sweet to me
like bitten sugarcane.

That rare Tamil Nadu Tamil Students' Forum
which I founded in Arur in those days
received a waterfall-like Tamil blessing
from Bharathidasan,
whose stride was that of a lion.
For that festival to flourish,
he sent, with affection,
this greeting:

> In cool-groved Tiruvarur,
> behold the Tamil Nadu Tamil Students' Forum!

<!-- scan 263 -->

> Let it live with generosity and truth!
> Let it become medicine for the people's awakening!
> Those of Tiruvarur are not men of festering wounds —
> behold their tiger-youth,
> shoulders swelling with strength!
> Behold, the pride of Tamil lions has risen,
> searching for the hostile crowd to tear apart!
> In great and fertile Tamil Nadu,
> if non-Tamils stretch out their tails,
> behold — a kick is what they shall receive!

That battle-call of the revolutionary poet —
a poetic thunder sounded by books of *puram* and *akam*,
the great thunder of modern Tamil,
the singular thunder of surging youth!

I think of those days;
I feed on their joy through memory.
Will such a day not come again?
Will our dear students not open their eyes and see?
Will they not join one formation
to guard golden Tamil?
For my heart, aching as it counts these thoughts,
I would call this festival
an apt medicine.

The ancient Tamils divided literature
into creations of *akam*
and creations of *puram*.
*Igambaram* is neither Tamil
nor something belonging to Tamils!
*Akam* and *puram* —
those are Tamil,
and belong to Tamils.
What literature can stand as equal
to the skill of the Tamil
who divided experience into *tiṇai* and wrote it so?

<!-- scan 264 -->

> Beginning with *kaikkilai*
> and ending with *peruntiṇai*,
> the seven modes first declared
> are called the seven *tiṇais*.

Thus speaks the Tolkappiyam,
of undying fame.

Once I debated with Anbazhagan:
“Which is greater — *akam* or *puram*?”
Arignar Anna interrupted
and gave an explanation:

> What can be shared is *puram*, thambi.
> What cannot be shared is *akam*, thambi!

That explanation of Anna
is itself a lamp for the realm of *akam*.

Because the Pandavas and Panchali
did not understand
the dignity of cultured *akam*,
they shared the sphere of *akam*
among five men!
That is a fault of their tradition.
To praise it happily as culture —
that is the fault of those without reason.

Though *akam* and *puram* are only two,
see how they remain
never even a millet-grain apart.
A warrior returns with a spear wound;
his beloved gives medicine
with eyes that themselves shoot spears.

Even if they lie together
like fish within a pond,
if the battlefield calls,
they climb the bank and rise.
Even after placing eye within eye
and tasting the art of union,
if evil threatens the soil,
the warrior clan rises.

To portray the honour of that clan
and pour a beautiful rain of poetry,
the owners of the word-clan have gathered here.
For those who slander the good clan,

<!-- scan 265 -->

for tongues made scarred by abuse,
let the challenge of the mother-clan,
the battle-*parani* of the Tamil clan,
be heard!
Let it tear open
the deaf drum of ears that refuse to hear!

In *akam*,
the hero,
the heroine,
the nurse,
the male friend,
the mother,
the town,
the moon,
the breeze,
the female friend —
all stand as companions,
and Tamil spreads its peacock-feathers and dances.
There are poets here
to give verses that defeat enmity
and win the garland of victory.

In these days
when some headless people
call themselves “leader,”
let us hear a song
about the enduring leader of *akam*.

Is the heroine one
who lies there merely thinking of “fate”?
No! No!
To change her fate,
she makes her friend a messenger,
makes even the nurse an accomplice,
and waits to welcome the lover
carried in by the breeze,
under the moon that stands and burns her.

The male friend is never a traitor
in an *akam* poem!
There are traitors in *puram* poems —
and their treachery becomes visible
right in the opening *puram* itself!

<!-- scan 266 -->

Love does not hide and vanish
because townspeople may blame it.
But today,
in front of the entire town,
on the beach,
inside a car,
in a public hall —
what justice is there
in this mischief of love?
Do they think the townspeople
all possess disabled eyes?
Or do lovers imagine
that because they themselves close their eyes,
the whole world too has closed its eyes?

Making room for love,
making room for duty,
upholding dignity,
and living with discipline —
that is an *akam* creation.
Living with family planning —
that is today's *akam* creation!

If, thinking “creation” in *akam*,
one goes on creating
six, seven, eight —
then what grows from that creation
is only panic!

I said it knowingly.
I said it so it may be understood.
I said it from experience.
Understand it well!

❀

## Translator notes

- `அகம்` (*akam*) is retained in the English title and body because it names the classical Tamil interior/love domain opposed to `புறம்` (*puram*), not merely the everyday sense “inside.”
- `அகத்துறைப் படைப்புகள்` is therefore rendered **Akam Creations**. The final passage deliberately exploits `படைப்பு` (“creation/producing”) for both literary creation and producing children; the neighbouring `பதைப்பு` (“panic/agitation”) is a sound-play that cannot be reproduced cleanly in English.
- The Tolkappiyam quotation and the seven-*tiṇai* terminology are kept source-facing rather than replaced by modern literary categories.
- Anna's `பகிர்ந்து கொள்ளக்கூடியது புறம் / பகிர்ந்து கொள்ள முடியாதது அகம்` explanation is translated as the poem presents it; the Panchali/Pandava criticism is likewise source rhetoric, not an external interpretation supplied by the translator.
- The early student-movement and Bharathidasan material is retained as Karunanidhi's first-person institutional memory; Phase 4 does not fact-check those source claims.
- The poem's closing family-planning satire, including its sexual/public-behaviour humour, is preserved rather than softened into generic advice.
