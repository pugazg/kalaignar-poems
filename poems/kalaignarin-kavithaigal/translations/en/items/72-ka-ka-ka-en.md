# Kā, Kā, Kā!

<!-- scan 448 -->

Kā, kā, kā—
brother, did you hear that song
in *Parasakthi*?
Udumalaiyar's song,
a lesson even for rationalists!
For the crowd that beats its chest for wages,
changes track for gain—
that song is a blast!

Kā, kā, kā—
that is the old song, brother;
now hear the new song!

Kā, kā, kā—
now they're all catching *kākkā*;
is it such a terrible fault
to point it out?
Put up a “cut-out”—*kākkā!*
Cut away traditions and throw them out—*kākkā!*
Print thunderous advertisements in the papers,
as though trampling people underfoot—*kākkā!*
Occupy the Speaker's chair—*kākkā!*
In the middle of the Assembly,
fall at this person's feet or that person's—*kākkā!*
Shout in the House,
“My caste is a higher caste!”—*kākkā!*
For the Mahamaham bath too—*kākkā!*
Even if a hundred people died there—*kākkā!*

<!-- scan 449 -->

Even if ministers eat rice off the ground—*kākkā!*
Even if white fly-whisks wave
and neem-leaf garments are tied on—*kākkā!*
Do *anga-pradakshinam*—*kākkā!*
Even if the price of rice rises unjustly—*kākkā!*
For bus-fare hikes—*kākkā!*
Even if every price reaches the summit—*kākkā!*
For plunder—*kākkā!*
For killings—*kākkā!*
For brutal highway robbery—*kākkā!*
Even if crores upon crores are piled up—*kākkā!*

Brother, how can I tell you the cruelty?
Now *kākkā* even sets fire to policy itself!
Again and again invoking the names
of Periyar and Anna,
*kākkā* lifts up those who stage deceit!

Now the stork pecks even at the fish
called “reservation”;
and for this too,
*kākkā* shouts “Jai!”
and hails “J”!

## Translator note

The poem depends on layered Tamil sound-play. `கா` is the crow's caw; `காக்கா` evokes “crow” while `காக்கா பிடித்தல்` is colloquial sycophancy/currying favour, and the repeated refrain also sounds like “protect.” The English therefore retains *kā / kākkā* rather than pretending the wordplay has a single equivalent. The closing `ஜே / ஜெ` is preserved as “Jai / J.”
