# Come, Let Us Tear Off the Mask!

<!-- scan 443 -->

They grow matted hair—
open shops of devotion;
with a powerful following
they make even governments
that rule by force bow down!
Women with lightning waists too,
without obstruction,
fall into their illusion!

In olden days they tied up their hair,
built huts and lived as sages.
Even those men, who had not wholly renounced,
kept attachment to women
and fathered children!

Buddha of the Bodhi tree,
Mahavira,
Adi Shankara,
Ramanuja—
they preached what were called lofty principles.
Yet those who claim to have come in their path
have slipped into violence,
and some have planted poisonous seed
in the mire of varnashrama.
Because such people have multiplied,
ethical discipline and ascetic discipline
have withered like a scorched sapling;
social progress has spilled onto the ground
like milk running away!

Whoever says, “Come, I shall show you God,”
and calls you to the bed,
is declared the choicest ascetic,
a sadhu, a swami!
Whoever looses Cupid's arrows
claims compassion;
he says, “I shall show you heaven,”
and wastes women themselves!

As though that were a chapter of spirituality,
with no objection raised,
a crowd of women believes his desire to be worship.
Is being deceived a chapter—or an outrage?

How many mahatmas, how many Sai Babas,
how many miracles have they shown!
Though people know that a magician
can reproduce all of it in a moment on a stage,
what a cruelty that they still become
stubborn fools!

Between God and human beings,
what broker is he?
If one has received divine grace,
why dakshina, offerings and daily worship?
If he has renounced everything,
why rosewater to bathe every limb of his body?
Why a maiden?
Is the sign of having attained nakedness
that he delights in photographing women naked?

In place of staff and water-pot,
there gleam a sword and liquor;

<!-- scan 444 -->

there is a body fed and swollen,
and a guru taking pleasure
while many women massage him.
Seeing all this again and again,
why does the crowd still remain
without clarity of reason?

One ashram for printing counterfeit notes;
another ashram for digging pits
and burying gold.
These small-minded thieves are believed,
worshipped and bowed to—
and correcting the innocent women
who trust them becomes a mighty labour!
When a town is deceived and plundered,
what difference does male or female make?

<!-- scan 445 -->

Those who set fire to a thatched house
are placed upon a chariot
and given a festival!
A shawl is wrapped around a rat snake,
while rational thought is beaten to death!
For promotion,
for piling up mountains of money,
many officials wash the golden feet of swamis!

To end the grief of childlessness,
a woman circles the sacred fig tree
and then goes to this mendicant monastery;
he bestows his “hand-grace,”
covers her body with his own,
turns himself into a husband,
and grants the boon the woman sought!

Murder, robbery, abduction,
sexual assault and outrage—
though such reports arrive in waves every day,
governments exist to embrace and protect the ashram
so that not even one hair falls
from its matted locks!
If ministers themselves become servants of swamis
and sit enthroned without fearing the town's smouldering,
then, to prove that there is no smoke without fire,
fire will spread—spread in every direction!
The deeds of thieves living
behind a screen of divinity will burn away!

Holding a festival for the Revolutionary Poet,
researching new books upon books,
and conducting stirring programmes—
that alone is not enough, sir!
Did not that poet ask with feeling:

> “Is it right to call the idle deceivers
> the very highest saints?”

With that rebellious heart,
let us beat the drum and set out,
like a pride of Tamil lions rising!
Come—
let us tear off the masks
of fraudulent ascetics!

## Translator note

The poem is an explicit rationalist polemic against fraudulent religious authority. Names, caste-order terminology, sexual exploitation and criminal allegations remain source claims and are not softened or independently fact-checked. The closing quoted challenge is retained as a quotation attributed by the poem to the Revolutionary Poet.
