# Give Me Your Heart, Anna

<!-- scan 18 -->

Give Me Your Heart,
Anna

<!-- scan 19 -->

<!-- scan 20 -->

Give Me Your Heart,
Anna

(A tearful poetic tribute offered by
Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi to Perarignar Anna
on Chennai Radio on 9 February 1969.)

More tender than the tenderness of a flower-petal,
a sacred heart — a loving heart —
a mother's heart that gathers one into its embrace! His
flower-petals speak Tamil —
even the three fruits, mango, jackfruit and banana, would be defeated —
his eye-flowers become spear, become sword
if any harm should come to Mother Tamil!

Though his foot-flowers wither, his arduous journey will not stop;
his hand-flowers bind together the younger brothers, the Kazhagam!
Those very flowers forgive enemies
and make their heads bow in shame like ripened paddy!
Let the flower of democracy sway,
let the flower of social equality spread fragrance,
let the mother tongue Tamil itself become the grove of life —
the breeze that comes dancing,

<!-- scan 21 -->

O flower-crown sought by all! O crown of fame! Amid the heaps
of greetings that come seeking you — each day
I shall fly as the bee that comes singing;
for you I shall give up myself; the great leader
celebrated in the song sung by
my one crore Tamil youth....

Listen, as I sing the greatness
of that man of action and valour, who made
the dear lives of still-young youth swell
with Tamil ardour packed into the drum!
The wounds made by the spear that lodged and burned,
he soothed with the healing heat called love,
and with his red-fruit lips applied medicine —
listen, as I sing that grace!

Where is the sword in the broad battle-hand; where the jewelled chest?
Where, where are the shoulders of the patron
who would never turn his back, whoever came at the battlefront —
listen to how he asked the younger brothers!
Listen!

“While Kanchi stands, Kalinga reels —
O love-dazed women, Kazhar-chēnni;
while Kanchi stands, Kalinga is lost —
open, that the field-war may be sung!”

To wake the love-bewildered women from sleep,
he set himself to action and sang —
Kalap-parani... *Kalingathu Parani*,
a praise-parani of immeasurable valour — I,
raising a poetic parani, sang a Kanchipuram Parani;
today I stand singing
a parani of anguish...!

<!-- scan 22 -->

As meaning in poetry, as sweetness in sugarcane,
as light in the sunbeam, as flower in the grove,
as coolness in moonlight, as wealth upon the earth,
as language murmured by a cascading waterfall,
as music mounting the wandering wind,

as foam dancing on the wave-rising sea —
the poet-leader whose gathered art breathes fragrance,
Anna — as I sing his sacred glory,
alas, the tears the Earth-maiden pours!
A flood!
A flood!
A mighty flood!

In nineteen hundred and thirty-eight,
if some sowed the seed for a language revolution in the motherland,
then under the peerless leadership of
the hero of Erode,
our Anna entered the field, clad in Kanchi!

For two or three months he withered in prison,
giving Tamil as the supporting staff,
and by those rare three letters became Anna....
That day, back in fifty-two,
in Chennai I told you —
do you remember?

There is a special glory in three letters,
the fragrance of Muttamil is there...
Three kings, three flags, three fruits —
the Tamils made the three drums resound; the Tamil
`வாழ்வு` — life — they lived has three letters; the
`அன்பு` — love — that is the foundation of that life has three letters...
the `அறிவு` — knowledge — that stands beside love has three letters;

<!-- scan 23 -->

`காதல்` — love — rising among the wise has three letters...
the fierce `வீரம்` — valour — praised by lovers has three letters...
the `களம்` — field — where valour is made to grow has three letters...
the `வெற்றி` — victory — seen upon entering the field has three letters;
and the peace-filled `அண்ணா` — Anna —
who urges us all toward that victory has three letters —
that is what I told you to know!

Tamil thunder to all eight directions,
a rain of words everywhere,
asking for plans so those without means, those without place,
may live —
he circles like a bee, mounting the stage!

If Anna writes,
every paper, every home is Tamil,
the whole land.... the fragrance of newness is there....
“Hey, younger brother — pick up the pen....!”
“Do not sharpen the knife; sharpen your intellect.”
A treasure-chest of thought, a casket of camphor!

“A corpse upon a tree-branch!”
“A spear in a seared wound!”
Could we ever forget those heart-winning phrases?
“On the roadside, the unemployed —
strange thoughts in the hearts of the unemployed —
O King! That is the sign of the times!”
Who but Anna could have such a beautiful gait?
A gait of reason!

“The line rose; the hill fell” —
is that not an editorial Tamil Nadu will never forget!
“If this army loses, which army will win?”
“He who has a younger brother fears no army”....

<!-- scan 24 -->

Even if *Manonmaniam* is the work
that uttered incomparable lines —
was it not Anna who inscribed them in our hearts!
“Even jasmine from the rival's garden has fragrance” —
what beauty, teaching political culture!
“Let us forget — let us forgive” —
what dignity, bearing an opponent's abuse!
Who taught us this?
As one builds a wall and then paints a picture,
Anna said that politics should be conducted
with cultivated grace —
and if, forgetting that,
four or five men sprang about,
Anna saw them and laughed!
Because one mother's womb
could not bear giving birth to everyone,
“Younger brothers born of separate mothers!” he called them,
showing love like fruit-sweetness, sugar-candy, honey-syrup,
and embracing them like a bee settling upon a dewy flower.
The master of speech, Sethu Pillai,
and Somasundara Bharathi — in the battle of words
he made them swoon — poor men; he caught them fast!
The Justice Party, behind the high walls of a fortress —
by his principle that it must not remain
a party of the moneyed caste —
he brought it out into the street!
“Cast away the titles given by the white men,”
he moved the resolution at the Salem conference;
unable to resist
Anna's warlike bearing,

<!-- scan 25 -->

those without principle fell,
like blind men without staffs!
Valiant Anna brought forward
the resolution changing the name to Dravidar Kazhagam
at a conference crowded with the brave!
When Annal Gandhi, following the path of virtue,
removed British rule,
Anna rejected Periyar's declaration calling it a day of sorrow
and began his battle!

A day of joy! A sweet day!
Indians reduced to bone and skin,
by beloved Gandhi's grace today
rose, cutting through the fetter of slavery!
“Let us lift the drum of freedom and make it roar” —
that is what Anna said, that very day....

From that day, in the Anna — Ayya — relationship,
a scratch appeared;
in forty-nine it became a separation,
and like a full moon tearing through cloud and emerging,
he made the Munnetra Kazhagam blossom!

Unable to live together with the Leader,
when he came out with the majority of comrades,
our steadfast Anna said it that day.
It feels as though he said it only yesterday!
“There is no leader for the Munnetra Kazhagam —
my Leader's chair will remain empty! In it,

<!-- scan 26 -->

the Leader himself will come and sit someday....
Until then I shall wait,” he said.

Like a flower garden in bloom
shedding morning dew,
those who had waited and heard the meeting
shed tears.
“Drops of tears! Jewels of the nation's eyes!” he called,
and marshalled them to shed red blood!
Has anyone ever heard it?
Has the world ever told it?
Within eighteen years, a movement,
like a tiger crouching before its leap — the story of its coming to rule?
Not even two years had passed since my Anna took office —
yet he set about so many distinguished deeds!
He undertook the effort
to raise all the rich-field farmers
and heap a beautiful life before them.
The illustrious President who came
to inaugurate the World Tamil Conference said,
“I saw an incomparable sight; I saw grandeur;
never in my life have I seen its like,”
and the town itself acclaimed Anna's fame!
Andhra's Brahmananda Reddy too —
“Ah! You alone are the true Gandhian,”
he worshipfully praised him — not even a year had passed!
“You are making prohibition more rigorous; in many states
they have washed their hands of it and tied up the pot;
you alone are the offspring of the Mahatma,” he said.
If Giri means mountain — is it not a mountain among
those trees!
Our Anna became the cloud embracing the mountain!

<!-- scan 27 -->

This is the explanation of V. V. Giri's greeting.

International scholars stood in praise — that day,
those who had stood as peaks of the South:
what did they say
of Anna?
The Pallavaram savant of ancient Tamil
stood spellbound by Anna's Tamil speech —
a thousand words of praise from that Maraimalai.
Pasumalai Bharathi — Pandiyan in bearing,
whom the whole land calls Navalar — he said:
“To guard the Tamil land felled by men of crookedness,
we have found a leader without stain in his heart,”
and told Anna's greatness that day!

Two thousand years ago, as young as she is today,
our Mother Tamil,
to live for countless crores of years,
gave birth to poets who made thousands of books; yet
our Mother, goddess of the arts,
bore Valluvar as her foremost son.

If flower — it is the lotus;
if king — it is Karikalan;
if book — it is the *Tirukkural* —
he gave that book of virtue,
praised in such words;
all humankind rejoiced!
Along the mountain-edge that touches the sky —
rising within and beyond our sight —
like a poisonous tree, in the cultivated life of Tamils,
alas, doctrines wounded in the back began to blaze!

<!-- scan 28 -->

On Tamil soil where the Kural rang out without seeing castes,
midway came crores upon crores of castes!

Shrines fragrant with knowledge disappeared,
and temples housing a thousand gods took their place.
Creatures with only appetite of mouth, with no feeling for language —
whether Tamil died or lived, what did it matter to them?
On the great land where the path of virtue had stood as the goal,
they sowed other paths; they heaped up errors.
Like a field gone without rain, the Tamil mind was changed;
envy, `avar`, anger, hurtful speech — without those four,
some Tamils went about as though no work could be done!
“For those without wealth, there is no world,” they said,
and in a land that had amassed wealth and flourished — today
darkness gathered and distress planted its flag.
Mother Tamil withered; Mother Tamil sang a song of sorrow;
Mother Tamil danced in burning fire...
She ran... she ran... no path could be found!

Leaving the town of earth, he lives in the town of fame —
the great sovereign of poetry's city, the master of the Kural!
She went to him and said, “Dear son!
I have brought one request.”
“What is it, Mother?” asked the Kural-master.
“In Tamil Nadu, where gold comes wherever one digs,
you must be born again,” she said.
“To dig for gold?” he asked.
“To make the minds and lives of Tamils
golden,” she said.
“What has happened now?” he asked.
She said danger threatened the mountain-like language;
he said the poor had no hut to go to;
not knowing how to raise the rod swiftly and cast it gently,

<!-- scan 29 -->

“the killing staff has risen high,” she said; and told him too
the idle stories told by calves in intellect.

Wiping the eyes that had wept,
sweet-speaking Valluvar asked,
“Mother, where shall I be born?”
Kissing the crown of the worshipful son's head,
with arms like hanging banyan roots, she held him close.
“A ploughed field must not dry for want of seedlings —
not from now on,” she thought;
her whole heart leapt full with joy
like a farmer whose mind is glad.
“To pour gruel into the dried stomach —
O sugar-candy! O honey-syrup! O Tirukkural!
be born in Kanchi!” she said.

He was born as our Anna;
as a king of reason.
As the breeze from Pothigai Hill,
as the meaning of a hymn
that flowers into song,
as the war-song of the ancient Tamils,
as the fresh fragrance of Tamil consciousness,
Anna rose into birth — like
a ray risen in the eastern sky —
and to sing his fame,
I too, the younger brother he nurtured,
taking the Tamil he gave me,
have come here; this is the truth —
they call him Leader, they call him philosopher,
they call him actor, they call him king of drama,
they say he possessed savour-rich power of speech and writing.

<!-- scan 30 -->

They call him human, they call him a jewel, they call him
minister of the State;
they call him mother, guardian of noble speech,
they call him politician — and those who have no time
to say each one separately — let them, in their hearts,
call him by the one word “Anna” —
that is why his mother
gave him the name.

To honour that clan of mothers:
a statue for Avvai,
a statue for Kannagi who nurtured virtue,
a statue for Bharathi of the unbending heart,
a statue for Bharathidasan of the unbowed crown,
a statue for Veeramamunivar,
a statue for Caldwell and Pope,
a statue for Kambar,
a statue for the valiant Tamil who steered the ship,
a statue for Tiruvalluvar, to spread the Kural with love to all eight directions.
Because he placed ten statues — so the world might know
Anna's attachment to Tamil — when
a statue for that Anna himself
was placed in Chennai,
only the raised index finger
stood pointing...
We thought our Anna was issuing an order.
Alas — today alone do we understand
that the one finger meant,
“I shall live only one more year!”
Our Anna... king of hearts....
“You have a younger brother who fears no army,”
you told us;

<!-- scan 31 -->

why did you leave us and continue the great journey....?
We grew in the warmth of the light of your eyes!
Why did you turn all our eyes into pools?
We thought you were our shade; but you have gone
beneath the sea-land seeking shade — is that justice?
Not every pearl beneath the sea is a pearl;
are you sleeping on the shore saying, “I alone am the good pearl, my boy”?
Why did you roll up the tongue that poured out music?
With a movement of your finger,
you made wonders in the world of letters;
why did you fold that finger away?
I have seen the great beauty of you thinking with eyes closed;
today what cruelty is this earth closing over you, barring us from seeing you!
You brought cruelty to an end; why did you go,
leaving us to cruelty?
You said we needed a heart that could bear anything;
where, Anna, is the heart in us to bear even this!
Enough of taking the sea breeze on the shore, Anna!
Rise, our Anna!
You will not come; you will not come;
we know nature's conspiracy, Anna... until
I come seeking the place where you are,
lend me your heart, Anna....
When I come, I shall bring it with me in my hands
and place it at your flower-feet, Anna......
