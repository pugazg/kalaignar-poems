# Let It Whirl as a Battle-Sword!

<!-- scan 420 -->

Treasure from the southern quarter,
that dances in honeyed flowers
and comes making the street fragrant—
gentle southern breeze!
Seven of my siblings,
who loved me across the breadth of the sky
until flesh and life would melt,
clasped a mass of flame
tight as though embracing you!
To guard the language,
in fire that spins and races
until the eyes scatter,
once eight bathed in flame;
today, declaring that Tamils at least hereafter shall rise,
seven of undying fame have leapt!
Koyiladi Brindavan, king of principle;
the resolute youth Manohar in Tiruchi;
tireless worker Arur Kittu;
lion-hearted Muthupandian;
Chennai Mary, one from the clan of mothers;
Kallavi Rajendran, never slack in duty;
and Mel Mayil Jaganathan,
whose eyelids would not flicker even at a charging spear—
why must these young trees
be scorched to death in blazing flame?

<!-- scan 421 -->

Wearing fire-blisters
all across their bodies by choice,
Namakkal’s brave young Ramasamy,
Sikkalur lion Ezhumalai, Ramayya,
Tiruppur tiger Thiruvenkatam, Muthusamy
are receiving treatment—
and proudly declare:
“Would lions fear
a pack of little foxes?”
Though this is not a method we can accept,
when they receive burning fire
as though it were a shower of snow,
we are defeated before them in sacrifice—
we bow,
we weep,
we rise;
let their valour whirl in our hands
as a battle-sword—
let the sharp blades of betrayal
break into dust, into dust!

## Translator note

This is a memorial/political poem about self-immolation and injuries in language agitation. The source itself says `ஏற்கும் முறையல்ல இதுவெனினும்`—“though this is not a method we can accept”—and that qualification is retained. Personal names and the source-visible rhetoric are not historically harmonised in the translation.
