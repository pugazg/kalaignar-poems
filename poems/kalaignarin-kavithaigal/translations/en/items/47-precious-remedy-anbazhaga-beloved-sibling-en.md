# Precious Remedy! Anbazhaga, Beloved Sibling!

<!-- scan 398 -->

My sibling,
it is your sixtieth-jubilee!
A festival for our family —
by the fragrance of Tamil on your tongue
the Kazhagam garden flourishes;
by the power of your speech
all treacherous enmity takes flight;
your army of constructive work guards the Kazhagam
and awakens Tamils from their long sleep!

You are a radiant gem,
a Tamil bell that rings without rest!

Could a pit be allowed
in the life of the Tamils? —
your heart boiled and rose at the thought;
your speech became a new flood!
The breath of a race that acts with valour!
The sweep of a sword that brings forth victory!

Rare Tamil literatures,
arrayed as *akam* and *puram*,
drip honey;
a gathering of poets
longs to drink it from you!

If some little fox-like men

<!-- scan 399 -->

dare to raise some petty argument,
the Professor's sharp spear will split it!
Even an incurable illness will be cured,
and the Tamil people will survive!

We are Periyar's children;
we are Perarignar Anna's younger brothers;
we are the inseparable two-coloured flag —
while this great family leaps
in an ocean of great joy,
precious remedy!
Anbazhaga, beloved sibling!

As you complete sixty in this jubilee,
may you live for many years more,
to see celebration after celebration
one upon another!

## Translator notes

- The canonical title's `அன்பழக` is preserved as the direct address **Anbazhaga**, carrying the personal-name/affection resonance of the source. The contents witness instead reads `அன்புறவு`; the two are not merged.
- `அகம் / புறம்` remains *akam / puram* because the line invokes the classical Tamil interior/exterior literary categories rather than merely “inside/outside.”
- `இருவண்ணக் கொடி` is the movement's “two-coloured flag” image exactly as the poem deploys it; the translation does not expand it into a historical explanation inside the verse.
