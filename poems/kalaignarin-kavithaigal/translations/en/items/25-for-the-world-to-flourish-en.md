# For the World to Flourish

<!-- scan 245 -->

Presiding poetic address by
Kalaignar M. Karunanidhi
at the New-Year *kaviyarangam* broadcast by Madras Radio
on 13 April 1973 under the theme
“Let Us Wish for the World to Flourish.”

You have come to wish
that the world may flourish
through love,
through righteous conduct,
through education,
through agriculture,
through industry,
and through the growth of art.
I too greet you.

Love — **-āl**.  
Righteous conduct — **-āl**.  
Education — **-āl**.  
Agriculture — **-āl**.  
Industry — **-āl**.  
Growth of art — **-āl**.

Since all six headings end in **-āl**,
I have become an *arasu* among the *āl* trees —
a royal tree standing among banyans!

At the birth of the year...

<!-- scan 246 -->

Year... birth...
If stroking a young woman's body
were to produce a birth at once,
the slender woman would soon become thin indeed!
Let the year be born, people of the world;
we do not need another kind of birth every year!

Having driven away Panguni,
Chithirai comes bouncing in to play.
We think her dance will last a month;
then from somewhere another arrives —
Vaikasi — and before we can exclaim,
“Ah, is it you?”
Aadi too has finished her dance.
Month after month the performance continues;
years fall like red-lotus petals,
then bloom again.
We call this the New Year
and string garlands of praise around it.

Ten years ago I was a prison bird,
with plenty of time there
to write many poems and books.
Today I lie like cattle in a shed,
pulled here and there,
my horns sharpened,
and made to stand on all these stages.
Is that good?
I ask the radio people themselves:
why do you refuse to open your mouths?
Even if you leave me untethered,
is your confidence simply that
this is a well-behaved bull that will not butt?

Never mind.
Believing that I will come without being struck
whenever I see Tamil fodder,
you ordered me to take the chair.
I am a bee.
When a flood of honey overflows,
when Tamil flowers spread fragrance,
when every flesh-bound heart melts,
when the sound of the world's Tamil scripture resounds,
when we ask, “Why this pit in life?”
and a path appears to make the poor smile —

<!-- scan 247 -->

when a leaping deer's grace
makes the tiger lose heart and fall,
when a head crawling with lice is no forest,
and the ghost of poverty rises to flee —
then Tamil Nadu itself has risen
to blow the conch.
Let us hear the songs
of the young poets gathered here.

### Through love...

To show how the world can flourish through love,
Valluvar said even one's very bones
belong to others.

Love for you arises from affection —
from your affection...
“Give yourself to me,”
one says to another,
while in a thicket where bees drink flower-milk,
through the heat of desire,
a male and a female speak.
Love lies beneath that too,
but higher still stands
that precious love called a mother's love.

A flower of the bed
once kept a man within her control,
turned him into an unripe fruit,
and then said,
“I want a gift.”
“What?” he asked.
Like a cake cooked without a pan,
she slid closer:
“Will you give it without delay?”
“O sugarcane tied in a bundle,
ask — before you close and open your eyes,
I shall give it,” he said, embracing her.

“Cut out your mother's heart,
bring it here,
and place it in my hand,” she said.
And he went.
His mother,
who had guarded her son like a jewel in a box —

<!-- scan 248 -->

he tore out her heart
and ran back carrying it.
His foot slipped;
he fell.
“Was the blow severe, my son? Alas!”
cried the severed mother's heart,
lying there in the open field —
so the story goes.
Is that not a mother's love?

Mother-love is a love apart,
a golden-fruit love.
There are good people in this world
who do not show even the love of a dog.
May they too live well!

For those with leprosy,
with pus running from their sores,
we build sacred homes.
For poor people whose eyes have lost their light,
we restore sight without charge.
These are temples without equal upon the earth.
God is not in gold or copper;
his refuge is in love.
Know this.

### Through righteous conduct...

Love lays the foundation stone
for building the mansion of righteousness.
Gandhi,
Nehru who followed his path,
and Anna too lived
to protect righteous conduct.
Let us remove the differences
that thought cannot even count,
and make equality dance
in all its colours.

<!-- scan 249 -->

Ownership of the land to the one who tills it;
ownership in industry to the one who labours —
we are putting this voice of rights into action
and laying down true righteousness.
If you see righteousness on the lips
but a little fox wandering in the heart,
it is better to step aside.

### Through education...

Four or five years ago,
if forty-three crores were spent on education,
this year we give one hundred and eight crores
so education may flourish in strength.

If at the beginning
we learn about the world from books,
we can then go on
to read the world itself as a book.
Students must think
of the hardships endured by parents and government,
and of the large sums spent on education;
it is service to avoid crossing limits in agitation,
and a duty to shine
as lamps of the future.

### Through agriculture...

Was it not the farmers
who changed the old condition
when people stood in rice queues,
close to tears?
What a pitiable state our granaries were in then!
Against that,
today rice production alone
stands at fifty-five lakh tons.
This is a government
that protects the farming community,
who rise before the sun and labour,
so that harm does not come to them.

<!-- scan 250 -->

### Through industry...

Across every part
of beautiful Tamil Nadu,
we must see people with work.
Power cuts caused by lack of rain
are not our fault.
The weavers' misery from want of yarn —
we shall quickly seek a way
and remove that hardship.
We shall shine as a government
that protects labour welfare.

### Through the growth of art...

“Tamils are a people of their own —
they possess a character of their own.”
Let us not forget what Namakkal Kavignar said.
Let us not abandon our art,
our sculpture,
our epics.

Only in a heart steeped in art
does the condition of the people appear;
only there does compassion arise.
When someone says “there is none,”
when a poor person's eyes reveal
the sorrow of disappointment,
it is the heart shaped by art
that understands.

For stable government,
there must be rule by art within the heart.
Even if you shout “Kalaignar's rule,”
this rule of art must remain
an administration that never dissolves;
that is why you see a reign of statues
along the seashore,
so it may stand in memory through time.

Anna sleeping in his tomb,
and thousands upon thousands
bowing with tear-filled eyes —
was that not a creation
seen by an artistic heart?

<!-- scan 251 -->

At the city where the Kaveri enters the sea,
we created an art hall
with statues telling Kannagi's story —
that too came from the heart of art.
Even when crushing work breaks my head,
it was artistic passion
that chased me here
to preside over this *kaviyarangam*.

The Pallava king who ruled Kanchi,
the Chola who ruled Thanjavur,
the Chera who ruled Vanji,
and the Pandya who ruled Koodal —
all lived with distinction
through magnificent arts,
and so remain in our thought.

Long live art!
Long live the Tamil mountain!
Long live poetry!
Long live this earth!

With that I call
the line of poets before us.

First —
Arasu Manimekalai.
If I called her simply Manimekalai,
you might think of the renunciant Manimekalai.
So: Arasu Manimekalai.
Her father Arasu
was a friend once very close to me.
Come, jewel,
to offer the gift of love
and turn the fallow land of the heart fertile.

*(Poet Arasu Manimekalai sings on “Through Love.”)*

<!-- scan 252 -->

Next —
friend Ponnadiyan comes to sing of virtue.
There is no time for me to sing of his skill.
Let him sing,
and let us rejoice.

*(Poet Ponnadiyan sings on “Through Righteous Conduct.”)*

Next —
“Let the world receive the joy I have received” —
to show the education he has himself received,
poet Hussain comes with fame reaching the sky.

*(Poet Hussain sings on “Through Education.”)*

Next —
only by labouring in the field
does the grain-head grow,
says Pazhaniyappa Kavirayar as he sings.

*(Poet Sami Pazhaniyappan sings on “Through Agriculture.”)*

Next —
our comrade who makes graceful poetry
will begin with “Through Industry.”
Kallai A. Durai, poet —
tell us a song, ayya!

*(Poet Kallai A. Durai sings on “Through Industry.”)*

The poet Durai who sang today
is to be married tomorrow morning.

<!-- scan 253 -->

I have no time to go and greet him then.
So let all of us together
greet him today!

Next —
to make poetry dance through the growth of art,
to sing Tamil like a swelling flood,
I call Kapilavanan.

*(Poet Kapilavanan sings on “Through the Growth of Art.”)*

### Conclusion

We shall do what we say.
We shall say what we do.
We shall block falsehood.
We shall grow truth.
The smile of the poor —
we shall call that joy.
We shall labour
so that everything is available to everyone.
For the world to flourish,
we shall drive poverty away.

Let this not be something
we sing once a year.
Let us take it up in life
as labour that does not bend.

Greetings.

## Translator notes

- `வையம் தழைக்க` is rendered **For the World to Flourish**; the event's longer theme `வையம் தழைக்க வாழ்த்துவோம்` is retained separately in the context note.
- The opening depends on the Tamil instrumental suffix `-ஆல்` shared by all six themes. The English keeps `-āl` visibly in the wordplay and separately explains the `ஆல்` banyan / `அரசு` royal-tree pun rather than inventing an English equivalent.
- The mother-heart story is preserved as the poem tells it; it is a rhetorical parable inside the source, not an independently authenticated event.
- Education, agriculture, industrial, welfare and artistic-policy figures are translated as source claims without external updating or correction.
- The six-theme structure and every guest-poet hand-off remain explicit so the English does not collapse the original *kaviyarangam* architecture.
