## Translator notes — item 4 `இரணியன்`

1. **Title identity** — the source title is retained as **Hiranyan**. The English does not silently expand it to an outside mythological name or substitute another textual tradition.
2. **Counter-mythic frame** — the poem presents Hiranyan as a Tamil/Dravidian king and treats the Narasinga/Prahlada story as an Aryan conspiracy. These are translated as Kalaignar's source claims; Phase 4 does not harmonise them with another Ramayana/Purana tradition or fact-check them inside the poem.
3. **`பெருந்தகை வேந்து` / `பிறைநுதலான்`** — the source's alternative names for father and son are retained as source-specific identities, with readable glossing where needed; they are not replaced by outside-standardised names.
4. **Three-part Tirukkural play** — `அறத்துப்பால்`, `பொருட்பால்`, `இன்பப்பால்` is preserved as the Virtue/Wealth/Love sequence, including the erotic pivot to the queen.
5. **Tutor wordplay** — `வாள் / வால்`, `வேல் / கருவேல்`, and `களம்` depend on Tamil sound/word association. Tamil forms are kept in the English passage so the satire remains traceable rather than being replaced by unrelated English puns.
6. **Source-form expressions** — the final-cleared text's `ஓதியமிலார்-ஓணான் குஞ்சு-` and `அரி நமோத்து சிந்தமென` are source-sensitive and not silently normalised from an external edition. The English retains them conservatively as source-form/transliterated material where exact lexical expansion would overclaim.
7. **Mythological-sexual catalogue** — the Shiva/devotee-wife, Brahma/Tilottama and Kannan/maidens passages retain their deliberately polemical sexual framing; they are not softened into neutral myth-summary prose.
8. **Nature/idol contrast** — Hiranyan's speech about a nature-worshipping people and an idol entering under the name of God is retained as direct ideological rhetoric.
9. **Magnetic-pillar conspiracy** — the source explicitly describes a magnetised pillar, iron footwear, a concealed lion-disguised attacker and a subsequently sold Narasinga story. English keeps this literal narrative architecture without importing an external explanatory correction.
10. **Closing modernisation** — the final move from Prahlada to "countless Prahladas" in the contemporary country remains a direct political accusation, not a translator gloss.
11. **Closing ornament** — the final `✤` is retained.

## Translator notes — item 5 `வாளி மன்னன்`

1. **Title** — `வாளி மன்னன்` is rendered **King Vali**. The stable Tamil title remains explicit in metadata.
2. **Tara's voice** — Tara is not reduced to a lamenting spouse. Her political accusation, challenge to Sugriva, rebuttal of misogyny, legal authority and final grief are all retained as distinct source voices.
3. **Manu/Aryan polemic** — Hanuman is described by the source as a religious monkey fighting for Manu's order and as an ally of Aryanism. English preserves the source's polemical characterization rather than neutralising it.
4. **Avunan/cave plot** — the source's named `அவுணன்`, staged wrestling match, cave pursuit, boulder-sealing and Sugriva's false mourning/coronation sequence are retained in source order.
5. **Gendered insult/rebuttal** — Sugriva's "brooding hen" insult and Tara's cockerel-chick/dog-gratitude rebuttal remain explicit; the translation does not abridge the exchange for decorum.
6. **Vali's return** — his interruption of the arrest order and statement that he kicked away the sealing boulder remain direct dramatic speech.
7. **Rama/Sita passage** — the source gives Rama an erotic description of Sita and a request framed in bodily hunger. It is translated as the source states it; Phase 4 does not replace it with devotional or epic-standard paraphrase.
8. **`வாளி` wordplay/name explanation** — the source links Vali's special name to `வாளி` in combat. English keeps the Tamil form in the explanation rather than inventing an English etymology.
9. **Hidden-arrow accusation** — Vali's accusation that Rama attacks from concealment, his contrast between front-wounds/honour and hidden attack, and his `தர்மத்தின் தூதனோ?` sarcasm remain intact.
10. **"everything white is milk"** — the proverbial/polemical line about some Tamils believing everything white to be milk is retained rather than rationalised into generic gullibility.
11. **Tara's ending** — `கணவனுடன் சென்றாள்` is rendered conservatively as "went with her husband"; English does not add an explicit mechanism of death absent from the final-cleared Tamil wording.
12. **Rama's final reaction** — the poem closes by saying Rama saw Tamil culture and bowed his head. This remains the source's concluding judgement.
13. **Closing ornament** — the final `✤` is retained.
