## Translator notes — item 2

1. **Title `தென்னவன் காதை`** — rendered **The Tale of the Southerner**. In this poem `தென்னவன்` is Kalaignar's recurring heroic designation for Ravana/the ruler of southern Lanka; the English title keeps the directional-political epithet rather than replacing it with `Ravana`.
2. **Counter-epic standpoint** — the poem deliberately reverses familiar Ramayana moral alignments, presenting Ravana, Muthunagai/Surpanakha, Kumbakarna and Lanka through a Dravidian heroic frame while attacking Rama, Lakshmana, Vibhishana, Sugriva, Hanuman, ritual priests and `Aryans`. English preserves that source rhetoric; Phase 4 does not reconcile it with other Ramayana traditions.
3. **`பூசுரர்கள்`** — retained as **pūsuras**, a source polemical term for priestly/Brahmin figures, because flattening it to neutral `priests` everywhere would erase Kalaignar's satirical diction. The surrounding Puranic/sacrificial context remains explicit.
4. **`இருடிகள்` / ascetic vocabulary** — source forms are translated contextually as sages/ascetics where the line clearly functions that way, without altering the final-cleared Tamil spelling.
5. **Yāzh / veena / musical imagery** — `யாழ்` remains **yāzh**, preserving the specifically Tamil instrument and the poem's contrast between Ravana's music, Mandodari's speech and the later `ruined veena called Sita` image.
6. **Muthunagai episode** — the violent sexual-assault/chastity reversal is translated as Kalaignar states it, including the one-woman/two-monkeys image and the enumerated mutilations. It is not harmonised with an outside epic version.
7. **Sita dialogue and voyage** — the source presents Sita as verbally inviting Ravana and voluntarily boarding after Ravana refuses to touch her. English preserves the quotation boundaries and accusatory counter-narrative as source rhetoric, not as an independent historical claim.
8. **`தர்ப்பைகளின் சூழ்ச்சி`** — rendered **the scheming of the darbha-grass men**. `தர்ப்பை` is retained as ritual metonym/satire rather than silently converted into a neutral institutional label.
9. **Fifth-column vocabulary** — `ஐந்தாம்படை` is translated **fifth-column force** for Hanuman and the betrayal motif around Vibhishana; the politically charged metaphor is retained.
10. **Kumbakarna** — the source couples his familiar sleep identity with `சிந்தனை உறக்கத்தில் வல்லவனாம்`; English preserves the deliberately odd `mighty in the sleep of thought` rather than smoothing away the wordplay.
11. **`அம்புவியில் / அம்பு வில்லை` passage** — English keeps the earth/bow/arrow rhetorical turn without claiming to reproduce every Tamil sound-play. The important source force is Kumbakarna's anger at hidden/ranged fighting.
12. **Ravana's battlefield speech** — sexualised imagery, accusations against priests/Aryans, the Sita-successor claim and language-oath remain explicit. They are translated as the speaker's polemical argument, not fact-checked or neutralised.
13. **Shared-Tamil casualty line** — `தமிழினத்தார் - ஒரு குலத்தார்!` is retained as the poem's own tragic observation that the dead on both sides are Tamils of one clan, even within its larger adversarial rhetoric.
14. **Final betrayal** — Vibhishana's spear from behind, `பார்ப்பனத் தாளுடையான்`, Ravana's last speech and the people's collapse are retained as the source's closing accusation. No external Ramayana combat sequence replaces it.
15. **Illustration/ornament** — the line illustration on scan 38 and the closing printer's ornament on scan 42 are represented as structural notes.

## Translator notes — item 3

1. **Title** — `இந்திரஜித்` is rendered **Indrajit**, while `மேகநாதன்` within the poem is rendered **Meghanada**. The poem itself narrates the transition from the childhood name to the earned Indrajit name after defeating Indra.
2. **Family register** — the opening domestic sequence intentionally mixes heroic exaggeration with parental tenderness, bodily humour, child-talk, Muttamil imagery and bedchamber romance. English keeps both registers instead of turning the passage into solemn epic prose.
3. **`பாலி`** — retained as **pāli** in the wedding-house sprouting-grain simile. The culturally specific ceremonial term is not silently replaced by a generic botanical image.
4. **Childhood exaggeration** — the lions, iron pillar, fortress and literary-Tamil hall are translated as Kalaignar narrates them; Phase 4 does not rationalise heroic hyperbole.
5. **Mandodari's portrait of Sulochana** — the extended carved-sandalwood, flame-lip, rainbow-brow, blue-gem-eye and Tamil-fragrant-mouth sequence is retained as accumulative praise. The large reclining-figure illustration on scan 47 remains a structural note.
6. **`புலி நகம்`** — the lover's line about a neck without `tiger-claw` is translated literally. It is not silently identified as a particular modern ornament/ritual object beyond what the source states.
7. **Naga king / Sulochana sequence** — the enemy-daughter identity, mutual-love dialogue, carrying across the fortress wall, demand for her return and the Naga king's stored grievance are kept in the source's order so the final beheading has its internal motive.
8. **Hanuman punishment** — the source's tail/hide punitive wording is translated conservatively as punishment and expulsion; it is not rewritten to match a different Ramayana burning-Lanka sequence.
9. **`குகனோடு “எழுவரான”`** — the quoted source allusion is kept as **“became seven with Guha”** rather than silently expanded or corrected from an outside text. Its function here is part of the attack on Vibhishana.
10. **`நாகாக்கும் இலக்கணம்`** — English preserves the tongue/grammar rhetoric around Vibhishana's proposal to negotiate, followed by Indrajit's honour-based rejection.
11. **Gendered martial rhetoric** — the lines saying women's tears should restrain men's deceit rather than battlefield departure are translated as written. They are not softened into contemporary paraphrase.
12. **`பத்தரை மாற்று`** — rendered **ten-and-a-half-touch pure gold**, retaining the traditional purity/assay metaphor used as an intimate address to Sulochana. English does not pretend the Tamil `மாற்று` resonance can be reproduced exactly.
13. **`முத்திரை ஒன்று`** — rendered **one “seal”** and left deliberately separate from the purity metaphor. The immediate romantic farewell suggests a parting token, but the English does not silently force the word into `kiss` when the final-cleared source says `முத்திரை`.
14. **Nikumbala** — the camp, secret weapons and Vibhishana's intelligence are retained as the poem's military-betrayal mechanism. English does not import the ritual framing of another epic witness.
15. **Chariot betrayal** — the source explicitly makes the charioteer an insider, removes the wheel linchpins and has Vibhishana sever Indrajit's hand. The English follows this internal sequence exactly.
16. **Final combat** — Indrajit's spear-in-mouth fighting, Sulochana's arrival, Lakshmana's arrow, the Naga king's revenge-beheading and the head falling into Sulochana's hands are retained without harmonisation.
17. **Final `ஆஎன்`** — the final-cleared source carries the fused form `ஆஎன்`. English renders its evident lamenting force as **Alas** in the closing Mandodari line, while leaving the Tamil layer untouched. Any lexical correction of `ஆஎன்` would require a Tamil reopen.
18. **Closing ornament** — the source's centered `✤` is retained.
